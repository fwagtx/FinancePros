The mark

Three candles, three shows a day

The icon is three rising candlesticks, the basic building block of every market chart. There are three because the channel posts three times a day. The amber candle shows which show is on, so the icon doubles as a schedule.

7:30 AM ET

Morning Brief

Pre-market movers, overnight world news and what to watch today.

12:30 PM ET

Midday Pulse

One big story explained simply, such as why oil is up 4% today.

5:00 PM ET

Closing Bell

How the day ended, the winners and losers, and one world story that matters for money.

The primary logo uses the Closing Bell arrangement (two white candles and one amber), which reads as a rising chart. The single-candle versions are used only on segment covers and video bumpers.

Color

Ticker navy and signal amber

The palette comes from old stock ticker boards: amber lights on a dark board. Navy does most of the work, amber marks what matters, and teal and coral only ever mean up and down. Click a swatch to copy its hex code.

Motion & sound

Everything moves like a market

A small set of motion rules keeps every video recognizable, even when templates fill it in automatically. Nothing bounces or spins. Things rise, draw and slide like a ticker.

  • The open: candles rise0.9 s

    The three candles grow from the baseline one after another, timed to the three-bell sting. Every video starts this way after the hook.

  • Text enters like a ticker250 ms · ease-out

    Headlines and cards slide in from the right and exit to the left, the direction a ticker tape moves.

  • Charts draw themselves800 ms

    Lines draw left to right, then the endpoint dot pops and the change label appears.

  • Numbers count up600 ms

    Key figures roll up from zero in Martian Mono, so the digits stay still while they change.

  • Cut every 3 to 5 secondsPace

    A new visual at least every 5 seconds keeps people watching. Cuts land on the narrator's pauses.

Sonic logo: the three bells

Three rising bell tones, one for each candle, about a second long. It plays under the logo open and at the end card. Over time, people should know it's FinancePros TV from the sound alone. This demo is a simple synthesized version. The final version should be produced by a sound designer or with a royalty-free sound tool.

Music bed: calm, low electronic pulse at around 100 BPM. Keep it 18 dB below the voice.

The beats

  1. 0:00–0:03
    Hook

    The headline is on screen before anything else, and the narrator says the news in one sentence. No logo first, because people decide to keep watching in the first 2 seconds.

  2. 0:03–0:05
    Bumper

    The candles rise with the three-bell sting and the segment name appears. Two seconds, never longer.

  3. 0:05–0:20
    What happened

    The chart draws, the key number counts up and the ticker strip runs.

  4. 0:20–0:40
    Why it matters

    One or two scenes, such as an animated world map, a simple explainer card or a "what this means for you" example.

  5. 0:40–0:52
    What to watch

    The next date, number or event to keep an eye on.

  6. 0:52–1:00
    Sign-off

    End card with the primary lockup, "Follow for the next bell", the sources and the disclaimer. The three bells play again.

Voice

A calm friend who reads the financial pages for you

The same AI narrator voice is used in every video, so people recognize the channel with their eyes closed. Scripts follow the rules below every time. These rules can go straight into the script-writing prompt.

Narrator

Tone
Warm, clear and confident. A newsreader who smiles.
Pace
160 to 175 words per minute, which is about 150 to 170 words per episode.
Pitch
Mid to low, steady. The energy lifts slightly on the hook.
Brand name
Say "Finance Pros T-V".
Numbers
Round them for the ear: "about 4 percent", not "4.13 percent". The exact figure goes on screen.
Consistency
One voice, locked. Never switch voices between episodes.

Writing rules

SayDon't say
"Oil rose about 4 percent today.""Oil is absolutely MOONING."
"Here's what that could mean for gas prices.""You need to buy this now."
"According to the Labor Department…""Everyone knows…"
"Inflation, meaning prices rising over time, cooled.""CPI printed soft on a core basis."
"Stocks fell after the news.""Stocks crashed!!!" (unless they really did)
"Both parties say…" (neutral on politics)Opinions on politicians or parties

One story per episode. Explain at most one jargon term per episode, in five words or fewer. Name the source for every number. Never predict prices or tell people what to buy or sell.
