# Research brief: 529 plans: how college savings accounts work
Slot: 2026-12-24-explainer (posts Thu Dec 24, 2026, 12 PM CT). Researched Oct 25, 2026. Evergreen: every figure carries its year/source.

## Angle / hook
No breaking news needed (evergreen). Surprise fact: leftover 529 money is not stuck; since 2024 (SECURE 2.0) up to $35,000 lifetime can roll to the beneficiary's Roth IRA, and since July 2025 (OBBBA) K-12 use was broadened and the K-12 cap doubles to $20,000/yr starting 2026.
Do NOT repeat Roth/401(k) topics in depth; the Roth rollover is one payoff line only.

## Core facts (federal)
- 529 = state-sponsored qualified tuition program (IRC Section 529). No federal deduction for contributions. Earnings grow tax-deferred, and withdrawals are federally tax-free when used for qualified education expenses. (IRS fact sheet / IRS news release IR-2017-163; IRC 529; Empower, Morningstar)
- Qualified higher-ed expenses: required tuition and fees; books, supplies, equipment; computers/software/internet; room and board for students enrolled at least half-time. (IRS IR-2017-163)
- Non-qualified withdrawals: the EARNINGS portion is taxed as ordinary income plus a 10% additional federal tax; possibly state tax too. Exceptions to the 10% (income tax on earnings still applies): tax-free scholarships (up to the scholarship amount), death or disability of beneficiary, US military academy attendance, coordination with AOTC/LLC credits. (Savingforcollege; IRS fact sheet via nvigate.gov; IRS Pub 970)
- State tax: rules vary by state. About 40 states + DC offer a deduction or credit (SoFi 2026); Morningstar counts 33 + DC with deductions. Some only for the home-state plan; CA, HI, KY, NC offer none (SoFi). No blanket claim. Some states do not treat K-12 as qualified for state tax (Morningstar).

## K-12 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025)
- K-12 annual federal cap: $10,000 per beneficiary (since TCJA 2018) -> $20,000 per beneficiary starting tax year 2026 (taxable years beginning after Dec 31, 2025). (Voya plan summary; Weaver; Surgent CPE; JPMorgan/Chase "new 529 plan rules 2026")
- Expanded K-12 expenses (distributions after July 4, 2025): curriculum and instructional materials, books, online materials, tutoring (qualified tutor), standardized test fees, dual-enrollment fees, educational therapies for students with disabilities. Also postsecondary credential programs. (Voya; Weaver; WV Treasury; SC Treasurer)
- State conformity varies (federal limit only).

## 529-to-Roth IRA rollover (SECURE 2.0, effective 2024)
- Account open at least 15 years; contributions (and their earnings) from the last 5 years not eligible; $35,000 lifetime max per beneficiary; each year's rollover counts against the annual IRA limit ($7,500 in 2026, IRS Notice 2025-67, Nov 13, 2025) and requires the beneficiary's earned income. Goes to the BENEFICIARY's Roth IRA. Open questions: whether the 15-year clock resets on beneficiary change (no IRS guidance found). (Vanguard; T. Rowe Price; Empower; Gusto; KPMG on Notice 2025-67)

## Gifts / contribution limits
- 2026 annual gift tax exclusion: $19,000 per recipient (same as 2025); $38,000 for married couples splitting gifts. (Morgan Lewis Oct 2025, citing IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32)
- 5-year election ("superfunding"): up to 5 years of exclusion at once: $95,000 per donor / $190,000 per married couple per beneficiary in 2026, elected on Form 709. (Empower; Savingforcollege; Vanguard)
- No federal annual contribution limit; each state sets an aggregate per-beneficiary cap, about $235,000 (e.g., Georgia) to just over $600,000; many around $500,000. (SoFi 2026; Kiplinger; NerdWallet; Savingforcollege)

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled "example"; not a return prediction)
$100 per month for 18 years (216 months) at an ASSUMED 6% annual return, compounded monthly:
FV = 100 x ((1 + 0.06/12)^216 - 1) / (0.06/12) = 100 x 387.353 = $38,735 (rounded)
Contributed: 100 x 216 = $21,600. Earnings: $38,735 - $21,600 = $17,135.
If used for qualified expenses: $0 federal tax on the $17,135. If withdrawn non-qualified: the $17,135 is taxed as income plus a 10% additional tax = $1,714 (10% x 17,135 = 1,713.5).

## The surprising fact
Unused 529 money can move up to $35,000 (lifetime) into the beneficiary's Roth IRA, if the account is 15+ years old (SECURE 2.0).
