# 2026-12-07-hot research brief: Money myth - "The Federal Reserve is funded by taxpayers"

Posts Mon Dec 7, 2026. Evergreen; every figure tagged with its date/period. Researched Oct 8, 2026.

## Core facts
- **No congressional appropriations.** Fed FAQ: the Board "is not funded by congressional appropriations"; "The Federal Reserve does not receive funding through the congressional budgetary process." (federalreserve.gov FAQs)
- **Where the money comes from.** "The Fed's income comes primarily from the interest on government securities that it has acquired through open market operations." (SF Fed, Dr. Econ). Also fees for services to banks (payments, check clearing, ACH): "income from services of $457 million" in 2021 (Fed press release, Jan 14, 2022).
- **Leftover goes to Treasury.** Federal Reserve Act requires Reserve Banks to remit excess earnings to the Treasury after operating expenses, dividends and surplus (Fed press release Jan 14, 2022).
- **Profitable every year from 1914 until 2022** (DOJ Office of Legal Counsel opinion, Nov 2025).

## Losses and the deferred asset
- Rising rates in 2022: Fed's interest expense (interest on reserve balances paid to banks + overnight reverse repo paid mostly to money funds) exceeded interest income. Most Reserve Banks halted remittances in **September 2022** (Fed release Jan 13, 2023; St. Louis Fed Nov 2023).
- H.4.1 note (Oct 1, 2026 release): "Negative amounts represent the cumulative deferred asset position, which is incurred during a period when earnings are not sufficient to provide for the cost of operations, payment of dividends, and maintaining surplus. The deferred asset is the amount of net earnings that the Federal Reserve Banks need to realize before remittances to the U.S. Treasury resume."
- Fed Jan 2023: deferred asset "has no implications for the Federal Reserve's conduct of monetary policy or its ability to meet its financial obligations."
- **2025 (audited, released Mar 25, 2026):** interest income $155.3B; interest expense $167.4B (~$147.7B on reserve balances, ~$19.7B on overnight reverse repos); comprehensive loss $19.6B (Reuters), operating loss $18.7B (Bloomberg); deferred asset $243.5B at end 2025. (Wolf Street, Bloomberg Law, Reuters/Kitco, First Trust)
- 2024 loss ~$77.5B; 2023 ~$114.6B (Reuters).
- July 2026 Monetary Policy Report: deferred asset down $7B since early January to ~$236B; Reserve Banks that cleared their own deferred asset have remitted ~$6B to Treasury in 2026.
- **Latest H.4.1 (released Oct 1, 2026, Wednesday Sep 30, 2026):** "Earnings remittances due to the U.S. Treasury" total **-$233,139 million** (= deferred asset ~$233B). Per bank: NY -134,824; Richmond -40,854; Chicago -23,475; SF -14,049; Cleveland -9,608; Boston -5,353; Philadelphia -3,555; KC -1,388; Minneapolis -262; Atlanta +135; St. Louis +24; Dallas +69 (positive = remitting again). Will change weekly; phrase "about $233B as of Sept 30, 2026".

## Worked example (math)
- 2025: $155.3B interest earned - $167.4B interest paid = -$12.1B net interest; after operating costs etc., loss ~$19B. Deferred asset end-2025 $243.5B -> Sep 30, 2026 $233.1B: shrank ~$10B in 9 months (net income turning slightly positive).
- At 2021's pace of ~$107B a year, $233B would take a bit over two years of profits to clear (illustrative only; actual earnings are far lower now).

## Surprising fact
The Fed lost money for three straight years (2023-2025) and taxpayers were never billed: the loss is booked as an IOU-like "deferred asset" against its own future profits; the Treasury simply stops receiving checks. Indirect cost: forgone remittances mean less revenue for the Treasury (CBO has noted remittances count as federal revenue).

## Neutrality note
No takes on Fed independence or proposals to stop paying interest on reserves.
