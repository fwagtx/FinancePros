# Fact-check: 2026-12-25-business (How movers price a move)

Checked: Oct 26, 2026, for a Fri Dec 25, 2026 2 PM CT post. Line indexes are **0-based** (they index `lines[]`).

## Verified
- **Released value: 60 cents per pound per article.** Correct per FMCSA Protect Your Move (valuation pages, and the "Understanding Valuation" PDF from Mar 2023). It is free. The video says "per item", which is fine as a plain-language swap for "per article".
- **The FMCSA stereo example:** a 10-lb, $1,000 stereo gets at most $6.00 (10 x $0.60 = $6). This is FMCSA's own example, and the math checks out.
- **Released value is opt-in.** FMCSA says the shipper must sign a specific statement on the bill of lading to choose it. Without that, the shipment moves at **Full Value Protection, the default**, and the shipper pays its valuation charge. Under full value the mover may repair, replace, or pay cash for the repair cost or current market replacement value. **The script never says this** (see Issue 1).
- **Pricing models:** local moves are usually billed by the hour per crew, and interstate moves by weight and distance. Consumer guides from 2025-26 back this up (Fixr, MoveAdvisor, Bellhop; per-mover rates run about $25-$50+/hr). "Usually" hedges it correctly.
- **Worked example:** 3 x $45 x 5 = $675 is correct. It is labeled "Example", and $45/hr per mover is a reasonable assumption.
- **Extra charges** (stairs, long carry, shuttle, packing) are the standard extra services listed in FMCSA's moving materials. OK.
- **Binding vs non-binding:** 49 CFR 375.403 (binding estimate, set price) and 375.405 (non-binding estimate, written, and final charges can exceed it). The 110% delivery rule is in **375.407(a)**: the mover must release the goods once the shipper pays up to 110% of the non-binding estimate. The balance is deferred at least 30 days (375.703). Note that the 110% rule is in 375.407, not 375.405. Wording issue in Issue 2.
- **Hostage-load complaints 364/409/495/899 (2017-2020):** these match AARP (2021, "phony movers"), quoting FMCSA spokesman Duane DeBruyne. Since an FMCSA spokesman is the source, "per federal regulators" is acceptable. The same article also reports **932 complaints through July 25, 2021**, which is already more than all of 2020. See Issue 3.
- **All on-screen values match the spoken ones.** No time words would be wrong on Dec 25. No advice, price predictions or politics. The cover title "How Movers Price It" is paid off by the video. No keep:true flags. All compare values are 7 characters or fewer.

## Issues

### 1. Hook and coverage lines are misleading (MUST FIX)
"Movers lose your $1,000 stereo. The free coverage pays 6 bucks." This implies the free coverage is what you get by default. In fact released value applies only if the shipper opts in, in writing; full value is the default. Line 6 ("Full value costs extra") also makes full value sound like the add-on. As written, the video's core claim misleads viewers.

**Line 0 (hook):**
- text: `One signature can turn a $1,000 stereo into 6 bucks.`
- say: `One signature can turn a thousand-dollar stereo into six bucks.` (10 words)
- scene.title: `1 signature: [6 bucks]` (22 characters). The existing title "Lost stereo: [6 bucks]" can also stay. prop, label and value stay as they are.

**Line 5:**
- text: `Released value coverage is free, but only if you choose it in writing. It pays 60 cents per pound per item.`
- say: `Released value coverage is free, but only if you choose it in writing. It pays sixty cents per pound per item.`
- scene stays the same. Optionally change sub to `opt-in, per item` (16 characters).

**Line 6:** remove "Full value costs extra." (it moves to line 7).
- text: `Federal regulators' example: a 10-pound, $1,000 stereo gets 6 bucks.`
- say: `Federal regulators' example: a ten-pound, thousand-dollar stereo gets six bucks.`

### 2. The 110% wording is imprecise (fix)
Line 4 says "capping what's due at delivery at 110%" but never says 110% of what.
- text: `Interstate estimates are binding, a set price, or non-binding, where at most 110% of the estimate is due at delivery.`
- say: `Interstate estimates are binding, a set price, or non-binding, where at most a hundred and ten percent of the estimate is due at delivery.`
- The split items are fine. "Max 110% due" is 12 characters.
