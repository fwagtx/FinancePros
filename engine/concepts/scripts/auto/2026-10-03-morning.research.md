## The story: hiring nearly stalls, and pay growth falls behind prices
### Headline numbers (BLS Employment Situation, September 2026, released Fri Oct 2, 2026, 8:30 a.m. ET)
- Nonfarm payrolls: +29,000 in September 2026 (BLS; CNBC; CNN; Bloomberg).
- Forecast: 84,000 (Dow Jones economists' survey, per CNBC). Other forecast ranges cited: 84,000-95,000 (Babypips).
- Unemployment rate: 4.2%, up from 4.1% in August; forecast was 4.1% (CNBC; CNN; BLS). BLS: rate has stayed in a 4.1%-4.3% range since March; the rise was largely from more people joining the labor force.
- Prior 12 months averaged +45,000 jobs a month (BLS).
- Revisions: July revised to -10,000 (from +21,000); August revised to +133,000 (from +162,000). Combined: 60,000 fewer jobs than previously reported (BLS; CNBC).
- Health care +17,000 (12-month avg +33,000). Government employment -17,000 (BLS table, via search summary; lower confidence, not used).

### Pay vs prices
- Average hourly earnings, private nonfarm: +5 cents (+0.1%) to $37.81 in September 2026 (BLS; Hoodline).
- Over 12 months: +3.0%, down from 3.1% in August; forecast 3.2%; lowest since May 2021; fourth straight monthly slowdown (CNBC; Hoodline; wichitaliberty summary; Commondreams "5-year low").
- CPI-U, August 2026 (latest available, released Sept 11, 2026): +3.4% over 12 months; core 2.4%; energy +16.3%; gasoline +3.9% in August (BLS CPI release).
- So the average hourly wage (+3.0%) is growing slower than consumer prices (+3.4%, August). Note the months differ (Sept wages vs Aug CPI); September CPI not yet out.

### Worked example (hypothetical, own math)
- $50,000 salary x 3.0% = $1,500 raise. $50,000 x 3.4% = $1,700 needed to match prices. Gap = $200 a year.
