## Facts (picked story) - BLS "U.S. Import and Export Price Indexes - September 2026", released Oct 16, 2026
- All U.S. import prices: +1.1% in September 2026 (one month); +8.1% from September 2025 to September 2026, the largest 12-month advance since +8.8% for the year ended July 2022.
- Fuel import prices: +4.8% in September; +34.5% over 12 months. Petroleum +35.4% over the year; natural gas import prices +96.5% over the year.
- Nonfuel import prices: +0.7% in September; +6.0% over 12 months, the largest over-the-year increase since +7.2% in April 2022. Nonfuel prices have not declined month-to-month since August 2025.
- Capital goods import prices: +0.9% in September (second straight month), +8.4% over the year. BLS: computers, peripherals and semiconductors increased amid strong AI-related investment demand.
- September rise also driven by nonfuel industrial supplies, consumer goods excluding autos, and autos/parts.
- Economists expected roughly +0.8% to +1.0% for the month (Yournews/TheStreet; secondary, not used on screen).
- August monthly revised to +0.5% from +0.7%.
- Import price indexes exclude tariffs/duties (they measure what U.S. buyers pay foreign sellers).
- TheStreet headline "AI-related costs rise the most since 1987" - NOT confirmed in BLS text; not used.

## Context
- Import prices are a pipeline measure: costs U.S. importers (companies, retailers) pay before goods reach shelves. They moved from slightly negative in January 2026 to +8.1% by September.
- Energy is the largest driver (fuel +34.5%), but nonfuel prices (+6.0%) are also at a 4-year high, so it's not only oil.
- AI investment demand is lifting prices of imported computers and chips (capital goods +8.4%).

## What it means for a normal person
- Higher costs paid by importers can be passed along in gas, heating, electronics and store prices (mechanism, not a forecast).
- Adds to the broader picture of elevated inflation (Sept CPI was covered separately).

- October import price report (BLS, mid-November), next CPI.
