# Research brief: Who pays for your credit card rewards (How They Make Money, posts Sat Nov 28, 2026)

## Hook / surprising fact
- US merchants paid **$198.25 billion** in credit, debit and prepaid card processing fees in **2025**, a record, up 5.9% from $187.20B in 2024 (Nilson Report, March 2026; confirmed via NRF/Merchants Payments Coalition release and HBSDealer / Hardware Connection coverage). Credit cards were about $157.8B of the 2025 total.
- The surprise: rewards are largely funded by those merchant fees, and a 2010 Boston Fed study estimated cash-paying households end up subsidizing card-paying households.

## Breakdown and rates
- 2025: Visa/Mastercard credit card swipe fees $118.8B (vs $111.16B in 2024); average Visa/Mastercard credit swipe fee rate **2.36%** of the transaction in 2025 (Nilson via Merchants Payments Coalition/NRF).
- 2024: Visa/MC credit $111.16B, Visa/MC debit $33.39B, American Express $28.42B (Nilson, Mar 2025).
- Interchange (paid to the card-issuing bank) is the largest component of the merchant fee; network and acquirer fees are the rest. Published Visa/MC rewards-credit interchange tiers run roughly 1.5%-3.5% depending on card and merchant type.

## Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
- $100 purchase x 2.36% (2025 average Visa/MC credit swipe fee rate) = **$2.36** paid by the store.
- A 2% cash-back card returns $100 x 2% = **$2.00** to the cardholder.
- Gap: $2.36 - $2.00 = $0.36 left for the bank, network and processor (illustrative; actual split varies).

## Company example: American Express (FY2025 10-K, filed Feb 2026; Q4 2025 earnings release Jan 30, 2026)
- Discount revenue (fees merchants pay Amex) 2025: **$37,401M (~$37.4B)**, up 6%, its largest revenue line.
- Card Member rewards expense 2025: **$18,409M (~$18.4B)**, up 11% from $16,599M in 2024.
- Card Member rewards expense series ($M): 2019 10,439; 2020 8,041; 2021 11,007; 2022 14,002; 2023 ~15,400; 2024 16,599; 2025 18,409 (Amex 10-Ks FY2019-FY2025).
- Total 2025 revenue ~$72B.

## CFPB credit card rewards report (Issue Spotlight, May 2024)
- Consumers earned more than $40B in rewards on major general-purpose credit cards in 2022, up over 50% from 2019.
- Average rewards value rose from 1.4 cents per dollar spent (2019) to 1.6 cents (2022).
- Over 1,200 rewards complaints in 2023.

## Boston Fed study (dated research finding)
- Schuh, Shy, Stavins, "Who Gains and Who Loses from Credit Card Payments? Theory and Calibrations," Federal Reserve Bank of Boston Public Policy Discussion Paper No. 10-03 (2010).
- Model estimate: on average each cash-using household pays **$149** a year to card-using households, and each card-using household receives **$1,133** a year from cash users. Because card spending and rewards rise with income, it is also a regressive transfer. Mechanism: merchants largely set one price for all payment methods, so card fees are built into prices paid by everyone.

## Regulation II (debit, context only)
- Since 2011, large-bank (>$10B assets) debit interchange cap: 21 cents + 0.05% of the transaction + 1 cent fraud-prevention adjustment.
- Oct 25, 2023: Fed proposed lowering it to 14.4 cents + 0.04% + 1.3 cents (not finalized as of research).
- August 2025: a federal court (North Dakota) ruled against the cap; the ruling was stayed pending the Fed's appeal (single secondary source; treat as weak, not used in script). This is one reason debit cards rarely carry big rewards.

## News angle (dated, neutral)
- November 2025: Visa and Mastercard announced a settlement with merchants over swipe fees (estimated ~$38B in value over its term).
- June 9, 2026: US District Judge Brian Cogan (Brooklyn) granted **preliminary approval**, calling it "fair, reasonable, and adequate" (Reuters, Payments Dive, June 2026).
- Terms: lowers combined average credit interchange by 0.1 percentage point for five years; caps standard consumer card rates at 1.25% (sources differ on 5 vs 8 years); lets merchants decline whole categories (commercial, premium consumer incl. many rewards cards, standard consumer) and adds surcharge rights.
- Opposition: NRF and other merchant groups oppose; ~978 merchants/groups filed objections Sept 10, 2026. Final approval pending as of research date (Oct 2, 2026).
