# 2026-10-10-hot research brief: Money myth "Pay off all debt before investing"

Format: Money Myth (myth, then fact). Education only: compare interest rates and employer 401(k) match math. No advice.

## Key facts (with dates)

1. Credit card interest rate, accounts assessed interest (people who carry a balance and are charged interest): **22.15% in Q2 2026**, up from 21.52% in Q1 2026. Federal Reserve G.19 Consumer Credit (released August 2026). Across ALL card accounts the Q2 2026 average was 20.94%.
   - Definition (Fed): annualized ratio of total finance charges to average daily balances on which finance charges were assessed; excludes accounts that paid no interest.
   - Record: 23.37% in Q3 2024 (Fed G.19, reported by LendingTree/others).
2. Federal Direct Subsidized/Unsubsidized loans for undergraduates first disbursed July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027: **6.52% fixed** (2025-26: 6.39%). Set from the May 12, 2026 10-year Treasury auction high yield (4.468%, rounded to 4.47%) plus a statutory add-on. Source: Federal Student Aid electronic announcement, June 4, 2026.
3. Employer 401(k) match (Vanguard, How America Saves 2026, data for 2025):
   - Most common single formula: **50% of employee contributions up to 6% of pay** (a 3% of pay maximum employer contribution); appears in about 12% of plans offering a match. Vanguard administered 100+ distinct formulas.
   - Average maximum promised match: **4.7% of pay** (record); median 4.0%.
   - Roughly 86% of plans offer a match (secondary sources citing Vanguard).
   - Employer match money can be subject to a vesting schedule (you may forfeit unvested match if you leave early). General plan rule; varies by plan.

## Worked example (hypothetical; assumptions stated)
- Salary $50,000. Match formula: 50% of contributions up to 6% of pay (the most common Vanguard formula).
- Employee puts in 6%: 0.06 x $50,000 = **$3,000**.
- Employer adds 50%: 0.5 x $3,000 = **$1,500** (3% of pay). That is an immediate +50% on the $3,000, before any market gain or loss, if fully vested.
- Same $3,000 against a credit card balance at 22.15% APR: 0.2215 x $3,000 = **$664.50 a year** in interest avoided (simple, one-year; with monthly compounding about $736).
- Same $3,000 against a 6.52% federal student loan: 0.0652 x $3,000 = **$195.60 a year** in interest avoided.
- Ignores taxes (pre-tax contributions), vesting, and market returns. Illustration only.

## The surprising fact
The most common employer match adds 50 cents per dollar immediately, a bigger one-time bump than even a year of 22% card interest, so "pay off all debt first" can mean passing up match money. But not all debt is equal: 22% card debt and a 6.52% student loan are very different math.

## Framing (education only)
- Myth: every debt must be gone before any investing.
- Fact: people compare each debt's interest rate with the employer match and expected returns; high-rate debt costs a lot, low-rate debt less; match money is instant but may vest.
- No instructions to viewers; no return predictions.
