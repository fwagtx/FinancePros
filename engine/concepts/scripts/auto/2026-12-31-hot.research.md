# Research brief: 2026-12-31-hot (Hot Take, Money Myth)
Topic: Money myth: claiming Social Security early doesn't change your monthly check.
Posts Thursday, December 31, 2026 (evergreen; every number dated).

## Myth vs fact
- MYTH: Claiming at 62 just starts the same check sooner.
- FACT (SSA rules): For anyone born in 1960 or later, full retirement age (FRA) is 67. Claiming at 62 pays 70% of the
  full benefit, a 30% cut. The reduction is permanent: it does not reset to the full amount at 67.
  Annual cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) still apply to the reduced check.
- Reduction formula (SSA): 5/9 of 1% per month for the first 36 months before FRA, plus 5/12 of 1% per month for
  each additional month. 60 months early (age 62) = 20% + 10% = 30%.
- Delayed retirement credits (SSA): 8% per year (2/3 of 1% per month) past FRA, for people born 1943 or later,
  stopping at 70. FRA 67 -> 3 years of credits -> 124% at 70. Waiting past 70 adds nothing.

## Timely numbers (dated)
- 2027 COLA: 3.6%, announced by SSA on Wednesday, October 14, 2026; applies to benefits payable from January 2027
  (SSI increase paid December 31, 2026). Covers about 76 million people. 2026 COLA was 2.8%.
- Average retired-worker benefit (SSA estimate): about $2,095/month before the COLA -> about $2,170/month in
  January 2027 (about +$75).
- 2027 earnings test (if claiming before FRA): $25,560/yr (up from $24,480 in 2026); $1 withheld per $2 over.
  Year reaching FRA: $67,920 (up from $65,160); $1 per $3 over. No limit from FRA on. (Withheld benefits are
  credited back via a recalculation at FRA.)
- 2027 taxable maximum: $192,300.

## Worked example (our math)
Full benefit at 67 = $2,000/month (hypothetical, before COLAs).
- Claim at 62: $2,000 x 0.70 = $1,400/month (-$600/month, permanently).
- Claim at 70: $2,000 x 1.24 = $2,480/month.
- Gap between 62 and 70 checks: $2,480 - $1,400 = $1,080/month.
- Break-even 62 vs 67 (our math, ignores COLAs, taxes, investment returns): by 67 the early claimer has
  60 x $1,400 = $84,000. The later claimer gets $600/month more after that: $84,000 / $600 = 140 months
  = 11 years 8 months -> about age 78 and 8 months (roughly 79).
- Break-even 67 vs 70 (our math): 36 x $2,000 = $72,000 forgone; / $480 = 150 months -> age 82.5.
- COLAs apply as a percentage, so the gap grows in dollars: 3.6% of $1,400 = $50.40 vs 3.6% of $2,000 = $72.

## The surprising fact
The 30% cut at 62 is for life. It does not bounce back to the full amount at 67, and every future COLA is a
percentage of the smaller check.

## Trade-offs (neutral, no advice)
Claiming early means more checks over more years; claiming later means bigger checks. Which pays more in total
depends on how long someone lives, health, other income, work plans (earnings test), and spousal benefits.
Neither choice is "right" for everyone; SSA's my Social Security account shows personal estimates.
