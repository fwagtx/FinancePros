Topic: how to file your taxes for the first time. Checked October 3, 2026 (18 searches). Line numbers are 0-based indexes into `lines`.

## Verified (no change needed)
- **2026 standard deduction** $16,100 single / $32,200 MFJ; **single brackets** 10% to $12,400, 12% to $50,400, 22% to $105,700; **max EITC** $8,231 for 3+ kids. Source: IRS IR-2025-103 / Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (lines 13-14, 37-38, 45, 51-53).
- **Standard deduction chart** (lines 39-41): 6,350 / 12,000 / 12,200 / 12,400 / 12,550 / 12,950 / 13,850 / 14,600 / 15,750 (the 2025 law's figure) / 16,100 all match. "Nearly doubled": 12,000 / 6,350 = 1.89x.
- **Refund and e-file figures, week ending April 24, 2026**: average refund $3,268 (+11.0%). E-filed returns 139,221,000 out of 142,225,000 received = 97.9%, so "about 98%" holds (lines 1, 66).
- **Math**: $3,268 / 12 = $272.33 (line 3). Worked example: $40,200 - $16,100 = $24,100. 10% of $12,400 = $1,240. 12% of $11,700 = $1,404. Total tax $2,644. Effective rate 6.58%, so "6.6%" holds. - **Line 56 caveat is right**: in the IRS tax table, $24,100 falls in the $24,100-24,150 row, which is computed at the midpoint ($24,125). That gives $2,647, so a real return would show a $453 refund. The script already says it "can land a few dollars off."

## Issues
1. **Line 13, filing threshold (factual gap for the core audience).** "If you're single and under 65, the line is gross income of $16,100" holds only if nobody can claim you as a dependent. Many first-time filers are dependents. A dependent has a much lower threshold: for example, more than $1,350 of unearned income, or earned income above the $16,100 standard deduction. Line 22 mentions dependents only as changing the deduction.
   - **Fix:** "If you're single, under 65, and nobody can claim you as a dependent, the line is gross income of $16,100. Dependents have lower lines." Make the matching change to the stat sub, e.g. "Single, under 65, not a dependent" (shorten as needed).
2. **Line 31, attribution.** "The IRS says enter $0 or create a PIN." The IRS guidance is to enter $0 as prior-year AGI if you didn't file last year. The Self-Select PIN is your signature, not an alternative to the AGI; the "or create a PIN" wording comes from software vendors.
   - **Fix:** text and say "Never filed? The IRS says enter $0." Change the scene sub to "Prior-year AGI".
3. **Line 83, on-screen text differs from the spoken line, and the text is inaccurate.** Text: "especially after a move, a marriage or a divorce." Spoken: "after a marriage or a divorce." Moving does not change filing status.
   - **Fix:** delete "a move," from the text.
4. **Line 69, time word.** "As of October 2026" will read stale on Dec 2.
   - **Fix:** "As of this video, no comeback for the 2027 season has been announced." Change the board to "Direct File for 2027: not announced". Re-check Direct File news just before posting.
5. **Lines 2, 95 and the hook (line 0), overstatement.** "For most people, that's tax they overpaid" and "It was mostly an interest-free loan" are unsourced. A large share of refund dollars come from refundable credits (EITC, the refundable part of the child tax credit), which are not overpaid withholding. The 2026 average was also raised by retroactive 2025-law tax cuts that withholding didn't reflect.
   - **Fix:** line 2 should say "For many people, much of that is tax they overpaid from their paychecks...". Line 95 should say "...wasn't a gift. Much of it was money withheld from paychecks, returned with no interest."
6. **Line 0, hook strength.** "Your tax refund isn't a bonus. It's your money." is true in spirit and short (about 2.5 seconds). But it's a familiar slogan, and as worded it doesn't make the viewer need the answer. The concrete number only arrives in line 1.
   - **Stronger:** "The average tax refund this year was $3,268, and most of it was never a bonus." Or: "The average refund was $3,268. Here's whose money that really was." Note the "most" caveat in issue 5; "much of it" is safer.
7. **Lines 1, 66, 100: newer data exists.** IRS stats for the week ending May 8, 2026 show an average refund of $3,276 (+11.5%) and 141.0M e-filed returns. The April 24 figures are accurate as dated, but not the latest.
   - **Fix (optional):** keep the figures with their dates, or update to May 8 ($3,276; 141.0M) everywhere: the cover, post copy, lines 0-3, 66 and 95.
8. **Line 4, stale news framing.** The headline "IRS ends Direct File program", dated Nov 3, 2025, is a year old and paraphrases the email. The email said "will not be available in Filing Season 2026."
   - **Fix:** headline "Direct File: not available since 2026". Also, "one free IRS tool from 2025 is gone" reads as if this were new. Better: "one free IRS tool still isn't back."
9. **Line 45, minor.** "The biggest is the Earned Income Tax Credit." EITC is the largest refundable credit by dollars for low-income workers, but "biggest" is ambiguous.
   - **Fix:** "One of the biggest is..." or "The biggest refundable credit for low-income workers is..."
10. **Lines 64-66, minor.** The timeline mixes full-year counts (1986, 1990, 2005) with a part-year count (through April 24, 2026). The label "Apr 2026" covers it; acceptable.
11. **Lines 88-92, style note.** The checklist is inherently how-to, but it is worded as a description ("a filing method picked") rather than as commands, and line 96 stays balanced on the W-4. Acceptable for an education guide. Lines 88 and 89 both use `reveal: 1`; line 88 could use `reveal: 0`.

No political opinion, price prediction or product endorsement found. The cover and title ("Your Refund Isn't a Bonus", $3,268) are paid off in lines 1-3 and 95. Narration reads naturally aloud.
