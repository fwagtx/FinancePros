# Research brief: The tragedy of the commons (2027-01-10 explainer)

Evergreen. No news hook needed; the "news" anchor is historical (1992 cod moratorium) plus a dated 2026 update.
Note: page fetching was blocked in this session (DFO, Wikipedia, Canadian Encyclopedia hosts not allowed); facts come from web-search
snippets plus one Firecrawl read of the DFO 2018 stock assessment PDF.

## Core idea
- Tragedy of the commons: a resource open to everyone and owned by no one (fishery, pasture, an oak full of acorns) gets overused,
  because each user keeps the full gain from taking a bit more but shares the cost with everyone.
- Named by Garrett Hardin (biologist, UC Santa Barbara), "The Tragedy of the Commons", Science, Vol. 162, No. 3859, Dec. 13, 1968,
  pp. 1243-1248, DOI 10.1126/science.162.3859.1243. Exact quote (p. 1244): "Freedom in a commons brings ruin to all."

## Real-world case: Northern cod (NAFO Divisions 2J3KL, off Newfoundland and Labrador)
- DFO 2018 Stock Assessment (Can. Sci. Advis. Sec. Sci. Advis. Rep. 2018/038), exact text: "Catches of Northern cod increased during the
  1960s to a peak of over 800,000 t in 1968, declined steadily to a low of 140,000 t in 1978, increased to about 240,000 t through much
  of the 1980s, and then declined rapidly in the early 1990s in advance of a moratorium on directed fishing in 1992."
- Same report, Table A1 (reported landings, thousand t): 1998 = 5; 1999 = 9; 2000/01 = 5; 2001/02 = 7; 2002/03 = 4; 2003-06 avg = 1;
  2006/07 = 3; 2007/08 = 3; 2008/09 = 3; 2015/16 = 10; 2016/17 = 13. Averages: 1962-76 = 502; 1977-91 = 212.
- 1988 catch: 266,000 t ("total catches reached 266,000 t in 1988 and decreased to 240,000 t in 1989") per a CAFSAC advisory document
  in the DFO library (search snippet; not read in full -> moderately uncertain).
- Moratorium announced by Fisheries Minister John Crosbie, July 2, 1992 (Canadian Encyclopedia; CBC).
- Jobs: about 30,000 people in Newfoundland and Labrador out of work, about 12% of the province's labour force (Heritage NL);
  Canadian Encyclopedia: some 40,000 fishers and related workers overall, nearly 30,000 in N.L. Other estimates range 20,000-42,000.
  Widely called the largest industrial layoff in Canadian history. USE: "about 30,000 in Newfoundland and Labrador".
- Causes: overfishing is the main cause cited; environmental factors are also debated. Script avoids a single-cause claim beyond the
  commons incentive.
- Reopening: commercial fishery reopened in 2024 with an 18,000 t TAC; 2025 TAC 38,000 t (Minister Joanne Thompson, June 2025);
  June 12, 2026: TAC raised 55% to 59,000 t; DFO's April 1, 2026 assessment put the stock in the "healthy zone" for the first time since
  1992 (about 70% probability, under new benchmarks). Conservation groups (Oceana Canada, Oceans North) say recovery is fragile.

## Worked example (our math, illustrative)
- 10 squirrels share one oak. One squirrel takes 5 extra acorns -> gains +5, all his.
- Suppose overpicking costs the oak 10 acorns of next year's crop. Split across 10 squirrels = 10 / 10 = 1 acorn each.
- Greedy squirrel: +5 gain - 1 cost = +4 net. So every squirrel has the same incentive -> all overpick -> oak loses
  10 x 10 = 100 acorns next year, each squirrel net 5 - 10 = -5.
- Real-data math: 1968 800,000 t -> 1998 5,000 t: (800 - 5) / 800 = 99.4% drop (our math).

## The surprising fact
- Elinor Ostrom shared the 2009 Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences (with Oliver Williamson) "for her analysis of economic
  governance, especially the commons" - the first woman to win the economics prize. She showed communities often manage commons well
  without full privatization or central control (Swiss and Japanese village commons, Spanish and Philippine irrigation, fisheries).
- Ostrom's design principles (Governing the Commons, 1990), simplified: clear boundaries (who's in), users help make the rules,
  monitoring, graduated sanctions (fines that rise), cheap conflict resolution, rules fit local conditions, right to organize,
  nested enterprises for big systems.
