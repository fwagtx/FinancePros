## Claims verified
1. **Ford Credit, Q2 2008, $2.1B.** Confirmed. Ford Credit's Q2 2008 8-K and 10-Q report a $2.1B pre-tax impairment of North America operating leases. It came from lower auction values for full-size trucks and traditional SUVs as fuel prices rose. Line 5 text, say and news scene all match. OK.
2. **Moody's 75% vs ~10%.** The numbers are confirmed. The source is Moody's Analytics, "Increased uncertainty for auto leases": "The return rates are usually quite high, around 75%... lessors have experienced much lower return rates, reaching 10% in mid-2022." The article is filed under moodysanalytics.com/articles/pa/**2023**/, so it was published in 2023 about mid-2022 data. The on-screen sub "Moody's, 2022" makes it look like a 2022 publication (see fix 1). The spoken "per Moody's" is fine.
3. **GM Financial $25.0B.** Confirmed. GM's FY2025 10-K shows GM Financial estimated residual value of $24,981M at Dec 31, 2025 ($23,492M in 2024), which rounds to $25.0B. The 1% sensitivity is also confirmed: a 1% relative change in estimated residual values gives about $250M of total depreciation impact over the remaining lease terms ($174M of it in 2026). The 10-K frames this as depreciation, not a one-time loss (see fix 3).
4. **Who sets the residual and who bears the loss.** The script never says who sets residuals, so that part is fine. "The lessor carries the resale risk" (yt_desc) holds for standard U.S. closed-end leases, but it ignores residual-value insurance and open-end or commercial leases. That is minor. One optional hedge is below.
5. **"Drivers bought at the old residual price and kept the gain" (line 7).** This is broadly true, but it overgeneralizes. Not every driver bought out; about 10% still returned cars, and dealers and third parties also captured buyout equity. Optional softening is below (fix 2).
