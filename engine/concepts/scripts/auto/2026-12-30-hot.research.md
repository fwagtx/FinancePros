# Research brief: 2026-12-30-hot (Hot Take, Scam Alert) - fake movers that hold your stuff hostage
Researched Oct 31, 2026 for an evergreen short posting Dec 30, 2026. No news hook used (evergreen).
Note: fmcsa.dot.gov and transportation.gov could not be fetched directly in this session (network block); figures below
were confirmed through web search snippets of those pages plus reputable outlets. Avoids overlap with the Dec 25 short
(no hourly vs weight pricing, binding/non-binding estimates, 110% rule, or 60-cents/lb released value).

## How the scam works ("hostage load")
- A mover gives a low estimate, then substantially raises the price once items are loaded; if the customer refuses to pay,
  the mover holds the belongings hostage and in many cases drives off with them. (Arizona Attorney General, AG Mayes/BBB moving-scam warning)
- DOT Inspector General Senate statement: hostage-load schemes in which movers threaten to auction goods unless customers
  pay added charges; OIG opened 36 household-goods fraud investigations since 2006. (DOT-OIG statement to Senate Commerce)
- Brooklyn case (DOJ EDNY): drivers threatened to hold goods hostage unless customers paid extra fees "sometimes double or
  triple the original estimated cost."

## Key numbers (with dates)
- Hostage-load complaints filed with DOT (FMCSA spokesman, via AARP, Aug 2021):
  2017: 364 | 2018: 409 | 2019: 495 | 2020: 899 | 2021 through July 25: 932 (already more than all of 2020).
  CHARTABLE SERIES: 2017-2020 (evenly spaced, full years).
- Our math: 899 / 364 = 2.47, so complaints about 2.5x in three years (2017 -> 2020).
- FMCSA Operation Protect Your Move report (2024, published Jul 2025): total complaints peaked in 2021, hostage complaints
  peaked in 2022 and have declined since.
- Operation Protect Your Move 2023 (Mar 20 - Apr 7, 2023): 97 investigations; 494 household goods complaints addressed,
  205 of them hostage complaints. (FMCSA OPYM final report, Apr 2024)
- Operation Protect Your Move 2024 (Apr 1-19, 2024): 380 household goods complaints addressed (252 non-hostage, 128 hostage).
  (FMCSA OPYM report 2024)
- FMCSA Administrator Robin Hutcheson (Transport Topics): in 2022 FMCSA received more than 7,500 complaints against movers
  and brokers, more than double 2015.
- Enforcement case: US Attorney, Southern District of Ohio: a racketeering enterprise using 13 rogue moving companies;
  identified victims lost more than $2.4 million. Leaders: Andrey Shuklin (78 months; scheme April 2013 - July 2018) and
  Serghei Verlan (72 months; pleaded guilty Aug 2022). Employees gave low estimates, then raised prices after goods were
  loaded; court order described holding goods hostage until inflated prices were paid. (DOJ SDOH releases; WMAR2; Journal-News)
- Older case: Tasheen Pickett (J and P Moving), sentenced Jan 6, 2016 to 48 months, $427,809.85 restitution; 34 hostage-load victims. (DOJ / DOT-OIG)

## Penalties / rules
- 49 U.S.C. 14915: anyone found holding a household goods shipment hostage is liable for a civil penalty of not less than
  $10,000 for each violation; each day may be a separate violation. Inflation-adjusted minimum per day: $15,846 (49 CFR 1022.4, 2025 figure).
- FMCSA may suspend a carrier's or broker's registration for 12 to 36 months for a hostage load. (FMCSA hostage-load policy, Federal Register)

## FMCSA Protect Your Move red flags / protections
- Red flag: mover demands cash or a large deposit before the move. (FMCSA Protect Your Move red flags page)
- Red flag: no on-site (in-home or virtual) survey; estimate given sight unseen by phone or online. (FMCSA brochure)
- Red flag: mover doesn't give you "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move" (required by federal rules) and the
  "Ready to Move" brochure. (FMCSA)
- Check the mover's USDOT number and registration in FMCSA's company search (protectyourmove / FMCSA site).
- File complaints with FMCSA's National Consumer Complaint Database (nccdb.fmcsa.dot.gov, 1-888-368-7238).

## BBB figures (supporting, regional)
- BBB Wisconsin (2025): 21,280+ mover inquiries on BBB.org; median reported moving-scam loss $532 (BBB Scam Tracker).
- BBB (2023 data): 5,918 complaints filed with BBB against moving companies; 1.5 million inquiries.
- AARP cites BBB data: reported losses average $754 per incident.

## Worked example (our math)
Growth in hostage complaints 2017 -> 2020: 899 - 364 = 535 more; 899 / 364 = 2.47x (about two and a half times).

## Surprising fact
Federal law sets a minimum civil penalty of $10,000 for each violation of holding goods hostage, and each day can count
as a separate violation, yet complaints still more than doubled from 2017 to 2020.
