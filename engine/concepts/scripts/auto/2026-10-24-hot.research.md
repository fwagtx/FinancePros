# Research brief: Scam alert: card skimmers at ATMs (Hot Take, posts Oct 24, 2026)

- FBI ("Skimming"): skimming costs financial institutions and consumers more than $1B each year. Devices on ATMs, POS terminals and gas pumps capture card data; hidden cameras or keypad overlays capture PINs.
- FICO Card Alert Service: compromised US debit cards were 315,000+ in 2023, 231,000+ in 2024 and 243,000+ in 2025 (+5%). Most compromises happen at non-bank ATMs; bank ATMs are about 27% of compromise locations.
- US Secret Service (Jan 2026): in 2025, 22 operations inspected nearly 60,000 terminals, gas pumps and ATMs and removed 411 skimming devices, preventing an estimated $428.1M in potential losses.
- Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.6, CFPB), debit and ATM cards. CORRECTION from fact-check: the $50 and $500 tiers apply only when the card or PIN is LOST OR STOLEN. For skimming (data copied, card still in hand), only 1005.6(b)(3) applies: $0 if reported within 60 days of the statement. Lost or stolen tiers:
  - Report within 2 business days of learning of the loss: liability capped at $50.
  - Report later, but within 60 days of the statement: capped at $500.
  - After 60 days you may get nothing back for transfers made after that window.
- Shimmers: thin chip-reader inserts (FBI; Secret Service).
- Worked example: $2,000 drained. Report within 2 business days: you can lose at most $50. Report within 60 days of the statement: at most $500.
- Chartable: compromised debit cards, 2023 315K, 2024 231K, 2025 243K (FICO).
