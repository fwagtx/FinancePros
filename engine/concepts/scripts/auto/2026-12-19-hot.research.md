# 2026-12-19-hot research: Scam alert: romance scams that end with a "medical emergency"

Format: Scam Alert (Hot Take). Posts Saturday, December 19, 2026. Figures phrased with their year so they hold on that date.
No crypto-investment / pig-butchering angle (covered Oct 17); no credit freeze; no "fraud team questions".

## Key facts (with dates)

1. FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report (released April 2026): Confidence/Romance fraud = $929,287,469 in reported losses
   (about $929 million) from 23,159 complaints in 2025. Losses up about 38% from 2024. Fifth-largest loss category.
   Total IC3 2025 losses about $20.9 billion from 1,008,597 complaints. People 60+ reported about $584 million of the
   romance/confidence losses (secondary). Sources: IC3 2025 report crime-type table as quoted by The Register (May 5, 2026),
   SOCRadar, EPCOR (Apr 21, 2026). Primary PDF on ic3.gov could not be fetched (proxy), so confirmed via several write-ups.
2. FBI IC3 confidence/romance losses by year (chartable series, $ millions):
   - 2021: $956M (956,039,739) - Comparitech citing IC3
   - 2022: $736M (735,882,192; 19,021 victims) - FBI Memphis field office release; IC3 brochure
   - 2023: about $653M (652.5M) - Review42 citing IC3 (secondary; weaker)
   - 2024: about $672M - secondary directory; consistent with 2025's "+38%" ($929M / 1.38 = $673M)
   - 2025: $929M - IC3 2025 report
   Math: 929 / 672 = 1.38, a 38% increase from 2024 to 2025.
3. FTC Consumer Sentinel, first nine months of 2025 (Jan-Sep): 55,604 romance scam reports, up 22% from same period of 2024,
   reported losses more than $1.16 billion. Median loss $2,218 in Q3 2025. Sources: Central Oregon Daily ("$1.16B lost to
   romance scams in 2025, FTC says"), Global Dating Insights (Feb 26, 2026), both citing FTC Consumer Sentinel.
4. FTC full-year 2025: a May 2026 FTC consumer alert ("New trends in reports about imposter scams") reportedly says romance
   losses rose 22% to "$1.48 million" (apparent typo for billion) and "$2,020 per person". WEAKLY SOURCED (page blocked;
   wording inconsistent). Not used in the script.
   FTC overall 2025: about 3 million fraud reports, $15.9 billion lost (FTC testimony to Joint Economic Committee, March 25, 2026).
5. FTC Data Spotlight "Romance scammers' favorite lies exposed" (Feb 2023, 2022 data): nearly 70,000 people reported romance
   scams in 2022, $1.3 billion lost, median $4,400. The most common lie: "I or someone close to me is sick, hurt or in jail"
   (24% of reports describing a lie). Others: crypto investment, "I'm in the military far away", "I can help you invest",
   "we've never met but let's talk about marriage", "I've come into a lot of money". Source: FTC romance-spotlight-2023.pdf,
   The Register (Feb 10, 2023), CBS Detroit.
6. Payment methods (FTC spotlight, 2022 data): gift cards were the MOST FREQUENTLY reported payment method (24% of people who
   lost money); cryptocurrency and bank wires carried the MOST money (together more than 60% of losses).
   The FTC's 2021 spotlight: people most often reported paying by wire transfer or gift cards; asks "sometimes begin with a
   story about a medical emergency".
7. FTC guidance (paraphrase, not a quote): only scammers ask you to pay with gift cards, wire transfers or cryptocurrency, and
   once the money is sent it is usually gone. FTC: never send money to someone you haven't met in person; scammers cite
   emergencies and hospital bills (FTC consumer alert Feb 2018; FTC blog Feb 2020 "It's not true love if they ask for money").
   In script, phrased as facts ("the FTC says only scammers..."), not instructions.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, labeled on screen)
How the "medical emergency" pitch escalates (illustrative amounts):
- Weeks 1-5: daily texts, always a reason no video call.
- Week 6: "ER visit" - $500 in gift cards.
- Week 7: "surgery deposit" - $2,500 by wire.
- Week 8: "hospital bill" - $6,000.
Total: $500 + $2,500 + $6,000 = $9,000.
For scale: that is about 4 times the FTC's Q3 2025 median romance loss ($9,000 / $2,218 = 4.06).

## The surprising fact
"Sick, hurt or in jail" was the single most common lie in FTC romance-scam reports (2022 data) - more common than the
military or crypto stories - and gift cards are the most reported way people paid.
