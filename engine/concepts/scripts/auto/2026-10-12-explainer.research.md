# Research: Student loans - how interest capitalization works

## Definitions / rules (confirmed)
- Capitalization = unpaid accrued interest is added to the loan's principal; from then on interest is charged on the larger balance (studentaid.gov servicer pages - Edfinancial, Aidvantage; Sallie Mae).
- Federal student loans use simple daily interest: (principal x rate) / 365.25 = daily interest. Interest is charged on principal only, NOT on accrued interest, until a capitalization event folds it into principal (Edfinancial/studentaid.gov "Payments, Interest, and Fees"; Credible).
- Direct Unsubsidized Loans accrue interest while in school, during the 6-month grace period and most deferments/forbearances. Direct Subsidized Loans: government pays interest in school/grace/deferment (studentaid.gov; Yale, BYU financial aid pages).
- Department of Education final rule, Federal Register Nov 1, 2022 (87 FR 65904), effective July 1, 2023: eliminates all instances of interest capitalization not required by statute, "such as the first time a borrower enters repayment, upon exiting a forbearance, and leaving any income-driven repayment plan besides Income-Based Repayment." (Federal Register; NASFAA; FSA Partners.) Also no longer on default or failure to recertify for PAYE/ICR (Tate Law summary).
- Still capitalizes (statutory) on federal loans: end of a deferment on loans without subsidized interest (e.g., unsubsidized), leaving/losing eligibility in IBR, and consolidation (outstanding interest becomes principal of the new Direct Consolidation Loan). (Tate Law; Student Loan Planner; Federal Register rule)
- Private loans set their own terms. Example: Sallie Mae capitalizes unpaid interest when the separation/grace period ends, when a deferment ends and when a forbearance ends (Sallie Mae loan terms pages).
- RAP (Repayment Assistance Plan), created by the 2025 reconciliation law (OBBBA), launched July 1, 2026; on RAP, unpaid monthly interest after a full on-time payment is waived rather than added to the balance (ed.gov fact sheet; SoFi; Credible). The April 2026 RAP final rule did not reverse the 2023 capitalization rule (Tate Law) - weaker source, not used in script.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled): $10,000 Direct Unsubsidized, 6.52%, no payments for 4 years in school
- Yearly interest: 10,000 x 0.0652 = $652
- Daily: 10,000 x 0.0652 / 365.25 = $1.785; 4 years = 1,461 days -> 1.785... x 1461 = $2,608.00 (= 652 x 4)
- Balance owed: 10,000 principal + 2,608 accrued interest = $12,608
- If that $2,608 capitalizes (e.g., consolidation, a deferment ending, or a private loan at end of grace with the same rate): new principal $12,608; yearly interest 12,608 x 0.0652 = $822.04 (about $822)
- Difference: 822.04 - 652 = $170.04 a year (about $170) extra, just from interest now earning interest.
- Under current federal rules, simply entering repayment after the grace period does NOT capitalize; the $2,608 stays as separate unpaid interest (simple interest).

## Surprising fact
- Unpaid interest on federal loans normally doesn't earn interest; capitalization is the moment it starts to. Since July 2023, most of the old federal triggers (entering repayment, leaving forbearance) are gone.
