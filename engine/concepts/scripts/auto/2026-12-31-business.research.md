# Research brief: How golf courses make money
Show: How They Make Money (posts Thu Dec 31, 2026, 2 PM Central). Evergreen: every number carries a date or period.
No timely news hook needed; the angle is the revenue mix (golf itself is a small slice at private clubs) plus public-course volume.
Note: there is no current public US golf-course operator filing a 10-K with a course revenue split (ClubCorp/Invited went private in 2017; its last 10-K is FY2016). We use the club managers' association (CMAA) annual Finance and Operations data, NGF rounds data, and an official city golf enterprise (San Diego).
Research note: web fetch to ngf.org, cmaa.org, sec.gov was blocked in this session; figures below come from search results quoting those primary pages. Flagged where a number is derived.

## Key facts
1. Private club revenue mix (CMAA Board Brief, Jan/Feb 2023, reporting the CMAA 2022 Finance and Operations Report data, fiscal 2022):
   - Membership dues: 49.5% of club operating revenue
   - Food, beverage and social events: 24.7%
   - Golf operations and golf shop: 13.5%
   - All other operating revenue: 12.3%
   - Sum = 100.0%.
   - Context: CMAA Board Brief Jan 2021: dues about 49% of income 2017-2019; 2020 report: typical respondent about 54% from dues (48% in 2018 and 2019). CMAA Board Brief Jan 2026: dues about 50% of operating revenue at clubs with golf, about 40% at clubs without golf. Pre-pandemic F&B and social about 30% (2018, 2019) per CMAA Mar/Apr 2022 Board Brief.
   - Older 10-K cross-check: ClubCorp Holdings 10-Q, 24 weeks ended June 14, 2016, same-store golf and country clubs: dues $184.4M of $372.3M revenue (our math: about 50%); F&B $83.1M (about 22%). FY2016 10-K: same-store GCC revenue +2.0% ($16.5M); golf operations revenue -0.7% ($1.2M), mostly weather; same-store Adjusted EBITDA +4.5%, credited to higher-margin dues.
   - Private club fees (secondary, attributed to GGA Partners/CMAA Club Leader's Perspective 2024): average initiation fee about $58,000 (median $34,000); average annual operating dues about $10,700 (median $9,100). NOT used in script (secondary source).
2. US rounds played (National Golf Foundation, Golf Datatech data):
   - 2020: 502 million (NGF via Golf Digest; NGF Graffis Report: +14% year over year despite spring shutdowns)
   - 2021: record at the time; about 529 million (DERIVED: NGF says 2023's 531M eclipsed the 2021 high by 0.3% -> 531/1.003 = 529.4)
   - 2022: 3.7% below 2021 (NGF 2022 wrap-up; Golfdom) -> about 510 million (DERIVED: 529.4 x 0.963 = 509.8; consistent with Golf Datatech's +4.2% for 2023 -> 531/1.042 = 509.6)
   - 2023: 531 million (NGF, record)
   - 2024: 545 million (NGF Graffis Report 2025; record, +2.2% vs 2023 per NGF)
   - 2025: 1.2% above 2024 record (NGF December 2025 national rounds played, published Feb 2026); our math about 551.5 million (not a published total; not used on screen). NGF: sixth straight year above 500 million; record "with 2K fewer courses" than the mid-2000s.
3. Supply: NGF: since 2006 closures have outpaced openings every year; net decline about 2,200 18-hole equivalents (roughly 13%) since 2006; about 90 18-HEQ closures in 2024, fewest since 2004.
4. Worked example, public courses: City of San Diego Golf Division (Torrey Pines North and South, Balboa Park 9 and 18, Mission Bay), fiscal year 2025 (July 2024-June 2025):
   - Rounds: 427,508 (FY2024: 419,843), +1.8% (City 2025 Golf Division Business Plan Update, Sept 2025)
   - Gross revenue: record $41.4 million (FY2024: $39.4M) (Municipal Golf Committee minutes, July 17, 2025; Times of San Diego). One Sept 2025 summary lists about $42.3M; $41.4M is better supported.
   - Revenue includes green fees, cart fees, driving range, golf shop merchandise, Torrey Pines golf shop lease, food and beverage at all three courses (City memo to Councilmember Elo Rivera, 2026). 9.9% of gross revenue goes to the city General Fund. About $13.4M estimated net to capital projects. FY2025 ending fund balance $55.1M.
   - OUR MATH: $41,400,000 / 427,508 rounds = $96.84 per round (all revenue, not just green fees).

## Surprising fact
At private clubs, golf itself (golf operations plus the golf shop) was just 13.5% of operating revenue in 2022; dues were 49.5% and food, drinks and events 24.7% (CMAA).
