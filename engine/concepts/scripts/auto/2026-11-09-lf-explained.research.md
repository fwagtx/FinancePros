# Research brief: How private equity is buying everything (2026-11-09-lf-explained)

Posts Monday, November 9, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its date or period. Neutral: present evidence on both sides; no advice, no predictions.

## Hook / surprising facts
- PitchBook, 2025 Annual US PE Breakdown (Jan 2026): US PE firms did more than 9,000 deals in 2025; full-year US PE deal value about $1.2 trillion, second-highest on record after 2021. 150 deals over $1 billion made up about 52% of value (a record share). 9,000 / 365 = about 25 deals a day. (PitchBook report page + PitchBook post on X, Jan 2026)

Distributions to LPs below 15% of NAV for four straight years. (Bain via caproasia.com Apr 16, 2026; economymiddleeast.com; cfo.com; Skadden Apr 2026)
- Largest 2025 buyout per Bain: Electronic Arts take-private ($56.6B in Bain's count). EA/consortium release (Sept 29, 2025): about $55B enterprise value, PIF + Silver Lake + Affinity Partners, about $20B of debt committed by JPMorgan; $210 per share.

## How a PE fund works (typical structure; industry standard descriptions)
- General partner (GP) = the PE firm; it raises a fund, picks companies, runs them, sells them. Limited partners (LPs) = investors: pension funds, endowments, insurers, sovereign wealth funds, wealthy families.
- Typical "2 and 20": annual management fee typically 1.5%-2% of committed/invested capital; carried interest typically 20% of profits, usually after a preferred return (hurdle) typically 8% a year. Funds typically run about 10 years.
- Leveraged buyout: fund puts in equity, borrows the rest; the debt sits on the bought company's balance sheet, not the fund's.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, round numbers, ignores fees, interest and taxes)
- Buy company for $100M: $40M fund equity + $60M debt (loan on the company).
- Upside: over 5 years profits pay debt down to $40M; company sells for $150M. Equity = $150M - $40M = $110M. $110M / $40M = 2.75x. All-cash buyer: $150M / $100M = 1.5x.
- Carry: profit to fund = $110M - $40M = $70M; 20% carry = $14M to GP; LPs keep $56M profit (simplified, ignoring hurdle/catch-up).
- Downside: profits stall, debt stays $60M, company sells for $80M. Equity = $80M - $60M = $20M: half of the $40M is gone (-50%). All-cash buyer: $80M / $100M = lose 20%.

## Where PE has been buying (documented)
- Home services: nearly 800 HVAC, plumbing and electrical firms acquired by PE since 2022 (PitchBook data, Wall Street Journal, Oct 12, 2024).
- Dental: 16.1% of US dentists affiliated with a dental support organization in 2024, vs 7.2% in 2015 (ADA Health Policy Institute). PE-affiliated dentists 6.6% (2015) to 12.8% (2021) (ADA-affiliated study in Health Affairs, reported ADA News Aug 2024).
- Vet clinics: FTC in June 2022 required JAB Consumer Partners (PE firm) to divest clinics as a condition of its $1.1B SAGE Veterinary Partners deal and its $1.65B Ethos Veterinary Health deal; final order Oct 2022 (FTC press releases).
- Housing: GAO-24-106643 (May 22, 2024): 32 institutional investors owned about 450,000 single-family homes by 2022; investors with 1,000+ homes owned about 3% of single-family rentals nationally; about 25% of Atlanta single-family rentals.
- Hospitals: Steward Health Care (owned by Cerberus 2010-2020) filed Chapter 11 on May 6, 2024; 31 hospitals in 10 states; over $9B liabilities, $6.6B of them lease liabilities to Medical Properties Trust after sale-leasebacks (MPT SEC filings, 2024). Cerberus sold its stake in 2020.
- Software/games: EA take-private (above).
- Sports: NFL owners voted 31-1 in August 2024 to let approved PE funds buy up to 10% of a team (CNBC, ESPN, Aug 27, 2024). NBA, NHL, MLS cap PE at 30% of a team (Akin Gump summary).
- Healthcare: Bain says global healthcare PE deal value hit a record $190B in 2025 (Bain press release 2026).

## What research says
Case for / mixed:
- Davis, Haltiwanger, Handley, Lerner, Lipsius, Miranda (NBER 2019; Management Science 2025), US buyouts 1980-2013: labor productivity rises 8% over two years vs controls; employment falls 13% in public-to-private buyouts but rises 13% in private-to-private buyouts; earnings per worker fall 1.7%.
- Bernstein & Sheen (Review of Financial Studies, 2016): restaurants become cleaner, safer and better maintained after PE buyouts (fewer health-inspection violations).
- Harris, Jenkinson & Kaplan (Journal of Finance, 2014): US buyout funds beat the S&P 500 by 20%-27% over a fund's life, more than 3% a year (PME).
- Gao, Kim & Sevilir (Journal of Financial Economics, 2025): PE-acquired hospitals raised operating income (7.4% in summaries), no evidence of higher mortality or readmissions, no significant change in inpatient prices; patient satisfaction declined; administrative job cuts.
Case against:
- Gupta, Howell, Yannelis & Gupta (Review of Financial Studies, 2024): PE ownership of nursing homes raised short-term patient mortality by about 11%; staffing and care compliance fell; taxpayer spending per patient episode rose.
- Kannan, Bruch & Song (JAMA, Dec 26, 2023): Medicare patients at PE-acquired hospitals had a 25.4% increase in hospital-acquired conditions (falls, central-line bloodstream infections) vs matched controls.
- Kannan, Bruch & Song et al. (Annals of Internal Medicine, Sept 2025): hospital-wide full-time employees fell 11.6% and salary spending 16.6% vs controls; ED deaths up 13.4% (7 more per 10,000 visits).
- Phalippou (Journal of Investing, 2020): since about 2006, US PE net returns about matched public stock indexes (~11% a year); about $230B in carried interest on funds raised 2006-2015.
