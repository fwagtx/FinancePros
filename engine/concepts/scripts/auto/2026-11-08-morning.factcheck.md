Problem: TEXT says "34.2%" but SAY says "about thirty-four percent".
- TEXT: `Gas did a lot of that, up 34.2% in a year.` (unchanged)

### 2. Line 7: misleading timing (required fix)
Problem: "Friday brings wholesale prices, up 6.3% in a year" sounds like Friday's report will show 6.3%. That number is September's, released Oct 15. Friday's October number is not known yet, and implying it reads like a prediction.
- TEXT: `Friday brings wholesale prices. September's were up 6.3% in a year, the most since 2022.`
- SAY: `Friday brings wholesale prices. September's were up six point three percent in a year, the most since twenty twenty-two.`
- SCENE: unchanged.
- Effect: +2 spoken words.
- Source: BLS TED (PPI +6.3% in September, largest since Dec 2022); BLS PPI schedule (Oct data on Nov 13).

### 3. Line 8: tighten the comparison and offset the word count (recommended)
Problem: the CPI release has both a monthly and a yearly figure. A raise is a yearly number, so the line should point to the yearly rate. Dropping "The takeaway:" makes room for the words added in lines 5 and 7.
- TEXT: `Compare your raise to Tuesday's yearly rate. Beat it, your real pay grew. Miss it, it shrank.`
- SAY: `Compare your raise to Tuesday's yearly rate. Beat it, your real pay grew. Miss it, it shrank.`
- SCENE: unchanged.
