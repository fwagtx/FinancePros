# Research brief: Why American Express charges merchants more (How They Make Money)

## Business model (the surprise)
- Amex is a "closed-loop" network: where it both issues the card and acquires the merchant, it sees both ends of the transaction. Its 10-K says this "closed-loop" is what "distinguishes our network from the bankcard networks" (FY2011 10-K wording; FY2025 10-K/annual report describe an integrated platform: card issuer, merchant acquirer, network; closed loop called the foundation of its Membership Model).
- Visa and Mastercard are open-loop/four-party: banks issue the cards, other banks/processors sign merchants, Visa/MC run the network in between. (Bankrate; Practical Ecommerce; Motley Fool explainers.)
- So on Amex-issued cards the merchant fee ("discount revenue") stays with Amex rather than going mostly to a separate issuing bank.
- FY2025 10-K: discount revenue is "our largest revenue source" and "primarily represents the amount we earn and retain from the merchant payable for facilitating transactions between Card Members and merchants on payment products issued by American Express."
- Supreme Court, Ohio v. American Express (No. 16-1454, decided June 25, 2018, 5-4): treated card networks as two-sided platforms; syllabus says Amex charges merchants higher fees than rivals to fund cardholder rewards. (Chanrobles syllabus reproduction; Willamette, Skadden, Ropes & Gray summaries.) No exact quote verified, so paraphrase only.
- Amex merchant pricing varies by merchant/industry; it does not publish one current average merchant rate. Last published averages: 2.48% (2014), 2.51% (2013) (FY2014 10-K). Not used as current.

## FY2025 numbers (American Express FY2025 10-K / annual report, Q4 2025 release)
- Discount revenue 2025: $37,401 million (~$37.4B), up 6% from $35,192M in 2024.
- Total revenues net of interest expense 2025: $72,229M (~$72.2B), up 10% from $65,949M.
- Discount revenue share: 37,401 / 72,229 = 51.8% (~52%, "about half").
- Net interest income 2025: $17,364M (~$17.4B); 2024 $15,543M.
- Net card fees 2025: $9,993M (~$10.0B); 2024 $8,449M; 2023 $7,255M.
- Net income 2025: $10.8B (vs $10.1B 2024).
- Billed business 2025: $1,669.8B (~$1.67 trillion), up 8% from $1,550.9B.
- Average proprietary basic Card Member spending 2025: $25,453 (2024: $24,608), Q4 2025 tables.
- 12.5M new proprietary cards acquired in 2025 (Digital Transactions; not used).

## Worked example (rough ratio, labeled)
Discount revenue / billed business, 2025: $37.401B / $1,669.8B = 0.0224 = about 2.2 cents per dollar charged.
Caveat: rough ratio only, not Amex's merchant rate (billed business includes cards issued by network partners and some revenue items are netted).

## Surprising fact
Amex makes more than twice as much from merchant fees ($37.4B) as from net interest ($17.4B) in 2025, the opposite of a typical card-issuing bank. Discount revenue alone is about half of all revenue.
