# Research brief: How Anheuser-Busch Makes Money (AB InBev), long-form, posts Jan 26, 2027

Evergreen. No breaking-news hook needed; every company number is tagged with its fiscal year (AB InBev's fiscal year = calendar year). Most recent annual results: FY2025 (full-year release dated Feb 11-12, 2026; 20-F filed after). FY2026 results are not out before posting (expected ~Feb 2027), so FY2025 is the latest full year.

Note on sourcing: sec.gov, ab-inbev.com, businesswire.com and nasdaq.com could not be fetched from this session (network blocked). ## Core FY2025 numbers (AB InBev FY2025 results, Feb 2026)
- Revenue FY2025: $59,320 million (about $59.3B), down 0.8% reported (currency); organic revenue +2.0%.
- Volume FY2025: 561.1 million hectoliters (561,100 thousand hl), down 2.3% (beer -2.6%, non-beer -0.4%).
- Revenue per hectoliter FY2025: +4.4% (organic), driven by "revenue management and premiumization".
- Normalized EBITDA FY2025: $21,223 million (about $21.2B), +4.9% organic; margin 35.8% (+101 bps).
- Free cash flow FY2025: $11.3 billion. Underlying EPS $3.73 (FY2024: $3.53).
- Net debt / normalized EBITDA: 2.87x at Dec 31, 2025 (2.89x at Dec 31, 2024).
- Dividend: FY2025 total 1.15 EUR/share (0.15 interim + 1.00 final proposed). $6B buyback announced Oct 30, 2025.
- 2026 outlook (company): EBITDA growth 4-8% organic (we do NOT use this; no predictions).
- Above-core (premium) portfolio = 35% of FY2025 revenue. Megabrands revenue +4.1% in FY2025. 20 billion-dollar beer brands. Corona and Budweiser = #1 and #2 most valuable beer brands (Kantar BrandZ, per release). Portfolio of "more than 500 brands" (annualreport.ab-inbev.com).
- Corona revenue +8.3% outside Mexico in FY2025, double-digit volume growth in 30 markets.
- No-alcohol beer portfolio revenue +34% in FY2025, led by Corona Cero globally and Michelob Ultra Zero in the US. (Release also has a "high-twenties" phrase elsewhere; 34% is the repeated headline number.) Balanced choices (low-carb, gluten-free, NA, etc.) revenue +8.9%.
- Beyond Beer (Cutwater, Nutrl etc.): ~3% of revenue; Q3 2025 revenue +27%. (Full-year % not confirmed -> say "about 3% of revenue" only if used, attributed.)

## Regions (fourth quarter 2025, from FY2025 release; full-year regional EBITDA not retrievable)
- North America Q4 2025: revenue $3.235B, normalized EBITDA $906M -> our math margin ~28%.
- Middle Americas (Mexico, Colombia, etc.) Q4 2025: revenue $4.927B, EBITDA $2.508B -> our math ~51%. Record volumes, Colombia cited.
- South America (Brazil etc.) Q4 2025: revenue $3.645B, EBITDA $1.321B -> our math ~36%.
- Brazil FY2025: revenue +1.0%, revenue/hl +5.4%.
- North America FY2025 volume ~-3.0%.

## Worked example (OUR MATH, FY2025)
- Revenue per hectoliter: $59,320M / 561.1M hl = $105.72 per hl (about $106).
- 12-oz can = 354.88 mL = 0.0035488 hl. Revenue per can-equivalent: 105.72 x 0.0035488 = $0.3752 -> about 38 cents.
- Normalized EBITDA per hl: $21,223M / 561.1M hl = $37.82. Per can: 37.82 x 0.0035488 = $0.1342 -> about 13 cents.
- Can-equivalents per year: 56.11 billion liters / 0.35488 L = about 158 billion 12-oz cans' worth (all drinks, incl. non-beer).
- Caveat: AB InBev's reported revenue is net of excise taxes; the shelf price also includes distributor and retailer markups and sales taxes. So the brewer sees about 38 cents of a can that costs the shopper much more.
- Mix caveat: average across all countries, brands and package sizes; not the price of any one product.

## Costs
- Key inputs: barley/malt, aluminum cans, glass, freight. Q1 2025 had cost-of-sales tailwinds; later 2025-2026 coverage (Reuters via HL/Finimize) cites higher commodity, packaging and freight costs.
- US aluminum tariff doubled to 50% in summer 2025 (The Drinks Business, Jan 2026). CEO said 2025 hedges cushioned it (kelo/inside.beer, 2025).
- Jan 6, 2026: AB InBev exercised option to buy back the 49.9% stake in its US metal container plants (7 plants, 6 states) for about $3B, the same ~$3B it got selling the stake to Apollo-led investors in 2020 to pay down debt (Bloomberg, Food Dive, TimesLive, Jan 2026).

## History / deals
- July 13, 2008: InBev agreed to buy Anheuser-Busch for $70/share cash, about $52B; closed Nov 2008 -> Anheuser-Busch InBev (PBS, SEC 8-K).
- SABMiller: final offer GBP 45/share, valuing SABMiller at about GBP 79B; completed Oct 10, 2016 (BeverageDaily; AB InBev 20-F FY2016).
- Net debt: $42.2B (Dec 31, 2015) -> $108.0B (Dec 31, 2016) (AB InBev FY2016 results, 6-K).

## 2023 Bud Light boycott (business facts only)
- April 2023 social-media promotion for Bud Light triggered a boycott (Wikipedia, PBS, Fortune).
- Bud Light lost the top-selling US beer spot to Modelo Especial (Constellation Brands) in May 2023 (Fortune May 31 & Aug 23, 2023; CBS).
- Anheuser-Busch US revenue FY2023 -9.5%; sales-to-retailers -12.7%, depletions -11.9% (AB InBev FY2023; Brewbound Feb 2024).
- Q2 2023 US normalized EBITDA down more than 28% (company, via Fortune/AOL).
- AB InBev US share ~38.3% at end of 2023 (company, via Investing.com).
- Group FY2023 revenue still rose 7.8% to $59.38B, all-time high (FY2023 release).
- Sept 2025: Michelob Ultra became top-selling US beer by volume (Circana, 52 weeks to Sept 14, 2025), passing Modelo; Modelo still #1 in dollar sales YTD (ABC/6abc, Fox Business, CNN, VinePair).

## The surprising fact
Sales volume FELL 2.3% in FY2025, yet EBITDA rose 4.9% and margin hit 35.8%: the company makes more by charging more per hectoliter (+4.4%) and selling pricier brands, not by selling more beer. And the brewer collects only about 38 cents of revenue per can-equivalent.
