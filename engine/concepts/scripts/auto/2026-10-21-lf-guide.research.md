# Research brief: How to buy a car without overpaying (2026-10-21 lf-guide)

Researched September 30 / October 1, 2026. Direct fetches of coxautoinc.com, prnewswire.com, ncua.gov were blocked by the proxy (Firecrawl out of credits); figures are confirmed via search results quoting the official releases, cross-checked where noted. ## Timely angle
- KBB (Cox Automotive), Sept 10, 2026: average new-vehicle transaction price (ATP) $50,089 in August 2026, up 0.5% from July and 1.9% from August 2025; the first month of 2026 above $50,000 (Dec 2025 had set a record). Average MSRP $51,852 (Aug 2026). Incentives 6.5% of ATP (6.6% July, 7.2% Aug 2025).
  - Derived: average buyer paid $1,763 below average sticker ($51,852 - $50,089), about 3.4%.
- California CARS Act (SB 766) took effect October 1, 2026 (Press Democrat Sept 24, 2026; CBT News; Ginsburg Law Group; Calif. Credit Union League): 3-day right to cancel used-car purchases/leases priced $50,000 or less; ends if driven more than 400 miles; dealers may charge a capped restocking fee; total price must be disclosed in ads; add-ons must be disclosed as optional; bans add-ons that give no benefit (e.g., oil change on an EV).
- IRS/Treasury final regulations on car loan interest deduction (TD 10054), published Federal Register Sept 8, 2026 (2026-18219).

## Rules and limits (official where possible)
- FTC: the Cooling-Off Rule does not cover cars; "federal law doesn't require dealers to give you three days to cancel"; some states require a right to cancel. Used Car Rule: dealers must post a Buyers Guide on every used car, saying whether it has a warranty or is sold "As Is - No Dealer Warranty". (consumer.ftc.gov "Buying a Used Car From a Dealer"; "Buyer's Remorse: The FTC's Cooling-Off Rule May Help")
- FTC CARS Rule (add-on / total price rule, finalized Jan 2024) was vacated by the Fifth Circuit on Jan 27, 2025 (procedural: no ANPRM); FTC formally withdrew it effective Feb 12, 2026. So no federal add-on rule; state laws (like California's) fill in. (ca5.uscourts.gov opinion No. 24-60013; Holland & Knight; Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor)
- CFPB: "A buy rate is the interest rate that a financial institution quotes to the dealer"; the rate offered to the buyer may be higher to compensate the dealer; the rate is negotiable. (consumerfinance.gov Ask CFPB "What is a buy rate for an auto loan?", "Can I negotiate...") Lender caps on dealer markup are commonly 2-2.5 points (industry sources; Fifth Third settlement capped at 1.25 pts for terms of 5 yrs or less). On screen we only use a labeled 2-point example.
- CFPB: multiple auto-loan credit inquiries within 14 to 45 days (depending on scoring model) generally count as a single inquiry.
- Doc fees: California caps the dealer document processing charge at $85 (dealers in DMV's private industry partner program; $70 otherwise), Vehicle Code 4456.5 / CA DMV handbook. SB 791 (raise to up to $260) was vetoed Oct 13, 2025 (CalMatters; leginfo). Florida has no cap; CarEdge 2026 analysis puts the Florida average around $999 (secondary source; used as "no cap" only). New York cap $175 (secondary).
- Sales tax and trade-ins: most states tax price minus trade-in; California taxes the full price (no trade-in credit). Other states weakly sourced, not used.
- Car loan interest deduction (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, sec. 163(h)(4); IRS final regs Sept 2026): up to $10,000/yr of interest, tax years 2025-2028; loan originated after Dec 31, 2024; NEW vehicle, final assembly in the US, under 14,000 lb, personal use, secured by first lien; leases and used cars don't qualify; available whether or not you itemize; VIN reported on the return; phases out by $200 per $1,000 of MAGI over $100,000 ($200,000 joint), gone at $150,000/$250,000; interest attributable to negative equity and other nonqualifying amounts is not deductible. (IRS IRB 2026-39; Federal Register 2026-18219; EY; KBB; Kiplinger)

## Worked examples (standard amortization, fixed rate, no fees; all labeled examples)
1. Dealer rate markup: $35,000, 60 months. 6.35% -> $682.36/mo, interest $5,941.53. 8.35% (2-point markup) -> $715.55/mo, interest $7,933.06. Difference $33.19/mo, $1,991.53 total (~$2,000).
2. Out-the-door example: price $38,000 + add-ons $1,500 + doc fee $500 + 6% sales tax on price $2,280 + title/registration $400 = $42,680.
3. Financed add-ons: $1,500 at 6.35% for 72 months -> $1,807.77 total paid ($307.77 interest).
4. Trade-in tax credit (state that taxes the difference, 6% example): $38,000 price, $15,000 trade -> tax on $23,000 = $1,380 vs $2,280 on full price -> $900 saved.
5. Rolling negative equity: $6,884 added at 6.35% for 72 months -> +$115.23/mo, $1,412.46 interest on that slice; total paid $8,296.46.
6. Interest deduction: $35,000 at 6.35%, 60 months -> first-12-month interest $2,045.77. Deduction value roughly $245 (12% bracket) or $450 (22% bracket). It's a deduction, not a credit.

## Surprising fact
In August 2026 the average new car sold for $50,089, about $1,763 under the average sticker; but the price is only one of four numbers in the deal (price, rate, trade, add-ons), and a 2-point dealer rate markup on a $35,000 loan (example) costs about $2,000, more than that whole discount.
