# Research brief: How to make a budget that actually works (long-form guide, posts Oct 21, 2026)

## Headline data
- BEA Personal Income and Outlays, August 2026 (released Sep 30, 2026): personal saving rate 4.1% of disposable income (July 2026: 4.6%).
- Federal Reserve SHED, "Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025" (May 13, 2026): 63% of adults would cover a $400 emergency expense with cash or its equivalent (unchanged from 2024; peak 68% in 2021). So about 37% would not.

## Where the money goes (BLS Consumer Expenditures 2024, released Dec 19, 2025)
- Average annual expenditures: $78,535 per consumer unit. Average income before taxes: $104,207.
- Shares of spending: housing 33.4%, transportation 17.0%, food 12.9%, personal insurance and pensions 12.5%, healthcare 7.9%, entertainment 4.6%.
- Approximate dollars (share x $78,535): housing ~$26,231, transportation ~$13,351, food ~$10,131.
- Chartable series, average annual expenditures: 2019 $63,036 | 2020 $61,332 | 2021 $66,928 | 2022 $72,967 | 2023 $77,280 | 2024 $78,535.
- The 2025 data was delayed and adjusted after the 2025 lapse in appropriations (BLS MLR 2026), so 2024 is the latest full release.

## Methods
- 50/30/20: from "All Your Worth" (2005) by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi. Of after-tax income: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings or debt payoff.
- Zero-based budget: income minus every assigned dollar = 0.
- Pay yourself first: move savings automatically on payday.
- Sinking funds for irregular bills.

## Worked examples (hypothetical)
- Take-home $4,500 a month: 50% = $2,250 needs, 30% = $1,350 wants, 20% = $900 savings.
- Irregular bills: car insurance $1,800 a year / 12 = $150 a month; holidays $900 / 12 = $75 a month. Total $225 a month.
- Saving $25 a week: 16 weeks = $400 (the SHED benchmark).
