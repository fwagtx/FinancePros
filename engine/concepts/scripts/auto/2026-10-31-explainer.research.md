# Research brief: Parlays, why they pay big and win rarely (posts Oct 31, 2026)

## Definition
- A parlay combines two or more bets ("legs") into one ticket. Every leg must win or the whole ticket loses. Payouts multiply leg by leg.
- Same-game parlay (SGP): legs all from one game.

## Worked example (exact math, standard -110 odds)
- A -110 bet: risk $110 to win $100. Decimal odds = 1 + 100/110 = 21/11 = 1.909.
- Straight bet on a true 50/50 at -110: expected return = 0.5 x 1.909 = 0.9545 -> bettor loses about 4.5% on average (house edge 4.55%).
- 4-leg parlay, every leg -110 and a true coin flip:
  - Chance all 4 win = 0.5^4 = 1/16 = 6.25%.
  - Book pays 1.909^4 = 13.28x -> $10 returns $132.83 ($122.83 profit), about +1228 or roughly 12-to-1.
  - A fair payout for 1-in-16 would be 16x -> $160 return ($150 profit), 15-to-1.
  - Expected return per $10 = 132.83 / 16 = $8.30 -> average loss $1.70 per $10 = about 17% house edge.
- Edge by legs (all -110 coin flips): 1 leg 4.5%, 2 legs 8.9%, 3 legs 13.0%, 4 legs 17.0%, 5 legs 20.8%, 6 legs 24.4%.
- The surprise: the book's cut compounds with each leg. Four legs at 4.5% each is not 4.5%; it's about 17%.

## Real-world hold data
- Colorado Division of Gaming data, 13 months to July 2026 (July 2025-July 2026): sportsbooks kept 15.51% of parlay money versus 6.46% of everything else. Parlays were about 29% of dollars wagered and just under half of what books kept. (reported from Colorado Division of Gaming monthly summaries; Colorado reports parlays on a separate line)
- Industry-wide: parlays/SGPs hold roughly 15-30% versus 4-6% for straight bets (industry trackers).
