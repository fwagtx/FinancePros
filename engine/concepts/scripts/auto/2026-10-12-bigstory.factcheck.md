Checked by an independent fact-checker on Oct 12, 2026, using about 17 web searches. Direct fetches of npr.org, bls.gov, dol.gov and reuters.com were blocked, so these results rely on search-result excerpts of the official pages and of NPR member-station syndications.

## Claim-by-claim verification

| # | Claim | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | "Nearly 4 in 10 run out of jobless benefits before finding work" | VERIFIED | NPR (Scott Horsley, "It's taking longer to find a new job after losing one," Oct 11, 2026) leads with "nearly four in ten... exhaust their unemployment benefits before they land a new job." The DOL UI data dashboard (updated Sept 11, 2026) shows a regular-program exhaustion rate of **39.27%** for the 12 months ending 08/31/2026. "Nearly 4 in 10" is honest rounding. |
| L1 | NPR report dated Oct 11 | VERIFIED | Oct 11, 2026 was a Sunday. "Oddly, layoffs are rare" overstates what the data show. Low claims mean layoffs are *low*, not rare. |
| L2 | Weekly claims "near a 57-year low," Reuters, Oct 8 | PARTLY VERIFIED | The DOL release of Oct 8 puts initial claims at 197,000 for the week ended Oct 3. The low was 189,000 in mid-July, the lowest since 1969, which is 57 years. That makes "near a 57-year low" accurate. **I could not confirm a Reuters article** (reuters.com is blocked to search). Bloomberg, TheStreet and Trading Economics carried the numbers. The safer source credit is "Labor Dept, Oct 8, 2026." |
| L3 | Median duration of unemployment was 11.5 weeks in Sept 2026 | **NOT VERIFIED** | No search result shows a September median. One BLS A-12 snippet with no column headers ends in 11.4. | L4 | 1.9M long-term unemployed, 27.1% of the unemployed | VERIFIED | BLS Employment Situation for Sept 2026 (released Oct 2): "essentially unchanged at 1.9 million... 27.1 percent." KPMG gives 1.94M. |
| L5 | Colorado up to six months, North Carolina 12 weeks | VERIFIED | CDLE: "up to 26 weeks." NPR syndications: Colorado "six months," North Carolina "less than three months." The verified alternative is the NC maximum of **$350/week** (several 2026 guides; NPR's NC subject is "capped at $350 a week"). 12 x 350 = **$4,200**. |
| L7 | "People planning for a layoff check..." | STYLE/ADVICE RISK | This reads as a soft instruction, and `mood: happy` is off-tone. |

- **Awkward narration:** In L1, "NPR's October eleventh report" is clunky out loud.
- **Time words:** "September" and the Oct 11/Oct 8 dates are correct for an Oct 12 post. "Sunday" also works.
- **Politics/advice:** The script is neutral and gives no price predictions. L7 is borderline instruction (fix below).
