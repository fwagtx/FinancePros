# Research brief: What a credit score actually predicts, and why it isn't wealth
Posts Fri Jan 8, 2027 (evergreen). Researched Nov 9, 2026. Prior video (Nov 7, 2026) covered "salary isn't in your score" + five FICO factors; this one is about what the score PREDICTS and score vs net worth.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. What a FICO score predicts: FICO's bureau risk scores are built to predict the likelihood a consumer goes 90 days past due or worse on any credit obligation in the 24 months after the score is pulled (myFICO "Understanding FICO Scores" booklet; Equifax FICO Risk Score product page; Experian Insights "credit risk scores" explainer). Definition of the model target, not a guarantee for any person.
2. Scale: base FICO scores run 300 to 850; higher score = lower predicted risk (myFICO / FICO).
3. Average FICO score (FICO, April readings unless noted):
   - Apr 2015: 695 (FICO, via NGPF Aug 2015)
   - Apr 2017: 700 (FICO blog "US Average FICO Score Hits 700")
   - Apr 2019: 706 (myFICO blog "Average U.S. FICO Score Ticks Up to 706")
   - Apr 2021: 716 (FICO blog "Average U.S. FICO Score Stays Steady at 716" / Bankrate)
   - Apr 2023: 718, record high (FICO blog "Average U.S. FICO Score at 718"; CNBC Oct 30, 2023)
   - Apr 2025: 715, down from 717 in Apr 2024; student loan delinquency reporting resumed (FICO blog "Student Loan Delinquencies Lower the Average FICO Score to 715"; BusinessWire Apr 16, 2025)
   - Oct 2025: 714 (FICO Score Credit Insights report, March 2026); still 714 in Fall 2026 edition released Aug 25, 2026 (BusinessWire via Finviz)
4. No score without credit history: FICO minimum scoring criteria require at least one account open 6+ months (myFICO FAQ "FICO score requirements"; Golden 1 CU help). Savings, checking, investment balances and income are NOT on credit reports or in the score (myFICO "What's not in your FICO score"; Chase; Money Management International).
5. Credit invisible (CFPB): 2015 report estimated 26 million (11%) in 2010. CFPB June 2025 technical correction: corrected 2010 figure 5.8% (13.5 million); December 2020: 2.7% of adults, about 7.0 million consumers credit invisible; scored records rose from 81.6% (2010) to 87.5% (2020). Unscored records ~25 million (~10%). (CFPB "Technical correction and update to the CFPB's credit invisibles estimate", June 2025; American Banker; AFSA; Orrick InfoBytes June 27, 2025)
6. Net worth = assets minus debts (standard definition, Federal Reserve SCF).

## Surprising fact
A person with $1 million in savings who has never borrowed can have NO FICO score, while someone with negative net worth who pays every bill on time can score high. The score measures debt-repayment risk, not wealth.

## Worked example (our math, hypothetical people)
- Alex: assets $1,000,000 savings; debts $0. Net worth = 1,000,000 - 0 = $1,000,000. No credit accounts -> no FICO score.
- Sam: assets $60,000 (car + savings); debts $95,000 (student loans + car loan). Net worth = 60,000 - 95,000 = -$35,000. Pays on time -> can score high.
