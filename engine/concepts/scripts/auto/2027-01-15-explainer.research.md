# Research brief: Free trials that auto-renew, and how they're designed to be forgotten
Prepared Mon Nov 16, 2026, for the explainer posting Fri Jan 15, 2027, at 12:00 PM CT. The video is evergreen, so every number below carries a date.
Education only. No advice and no predictions.

## Timely angle
There's no single news hook for that week. The evergreen angle is that the federal click-to-cancel rule is gone, but enforcement goes on under ROSCA, Amazon's Prime settlement refunds are still being paid in fall 2026, and states like California have their own laws.

## The hook fact (confirmed)
- In its June 2023 complaint, the FTC said Amazon internally called its Prime cancellation flow "Iliad", after Homer's epic. The FTC described it as a **4-page, 6-click, 15-option** process, while enrolling took **1-2 clicks**. The FTC says Amazon changed the flow in 2023 after FTC pressure. Amazon neither admitted nor denied the allegations.
  (Sources: FTC complaint as reported by Courthouse News, The Register, and Fast Company; former FTC commissioner Bedoya quoted by Malwarebytes.)

## Legal status as of Nov 2026
- **The FTC "Click-to-Cancel" rule** (amended Negative Option Rule, finalized Oct 2024) was vacated in full by the **8th Circuit on July 8, 2025**, on procedural grounds (Magnuson-Moss rulemaking steps).
- In **Feb 2026** the FTC restored the old, narrow pre-notification Negative Option Rule. That older rule doesn't reach most automatic renewals or free-trial conversions.
- On **March 11, 2026**, the FTC issued an **Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM)**. Comments were due Apr 13, 2026 (docket FTC-2026-0265). It asks whether to cover auto-renewals, continuity plans and trial conversions, and whether to revive parts of click-to-cancel. **As of Nov 2026 I found no proposed rule (NPRM)**, so the rule is still at an early stage.
  (Sources: Gibson Dunn; Jones Day (May 2026); Kelley Drye; Wiley; Sidley; Goodwin (Feb 2026); Legal500.)
- **ROSCA** (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, 2010) still applies. Before billing, a seller must clearly disclose the material terms and get the buyer's express informed consent, and it must offer a simple way to stop recurring charges. The FTC keeps enforcing it together with Section 5 (Gibson Dunn; Goodwin).
- **The Amazon Prime settlement** was announced **Sept 25, 2025** and totals **$2.5B**: a **$1B civil penalty** (the largest ever in a case involving an FTC rule violation) plus **$1.5B in refunds** for about **35 million** consumers. The claims were under the FTC Act and ROSCA. Amazon didn't admit or deny them, and the case settled days into the jury trial (Jurist; Alston & Bird; Goodwin; Variety; TheWrap).
  - **Sept 2026:** the court approved a revised order that raised the refund cap from $51 to **$200**, with automatic payments starting Oct 1, 2026 (FTC press release, Sept 2026; News4Jax).
- **California AB 2863** amends the Automatic Renewal Law and took **effect July 1, 2025**. It covers free-to-pay conversions and requires express affirmative consent. For online sign-ups, the business must offer a click-to-cancel option, and retention offers are allowed only if canceling stays immediate. It also requires **annual reminders** and clear notice of price changes. It applies to contracts entered, amended or extended on or after July 1, 2025 (Davis Wright Tremaine; JD Supra; Mondaq; Latham).

## Survey data (dated)
- **C+R Research, spring 2022** (1,000 US consumers, Apr 22 to May 2, 2022): people guessed **$86 a month** on subscriptions, but itemized it came to **$219** a month, a gap of **$133**. **42%** had kept paying for a subscription they'd stopped using, 74% said recurring charges are easy to forget, and 72% used auto-pay (NBC/CNBC syndication, Fox 6, Motley Fool, C+R blog).
- **CNET/YouGov 2026** (Apr 29 to May 1, 2026; 2,522 US adults): the average was **$111 a month** ($1,332 a year) on subscriptions, up 23% from **$90** in 2025. People spent **$252 a year** (about $21 a month) on **unused** subscriptions, up from $204-205 in 2025 (WFSB, July 16, 2026; Scripps "Don't Waste Your Money" stations; BGR).
- **CNET/YouGov 2025:** $90 a month, $1,080 a year, and $205 a year unused (The Desk, Cord Cutters News). **CNET 2024:** $91 a month.
- **Free-trial surveys (older and varied, not used on screen):** In a 2017 CreditCards.com survey, 48% said they'd forgotten to cancel a free trial before an auto-renewal charge. In Bankrate's 2019 survey, 59% of free-trial users were later charged against their will. In Bankrate's Jan 2022 survey (YouGov, 2,497 adults), 51% of adults who'd had a subscription reported unwanted charges.

## Worked example (our math)
A forgotten plan at $14.99 a month, the same price as Prime monthly since 2022, comes to $14.99 x 12 = **$179.88 a year**. That's about $180.
Cross-check: CNET's $252 a year in unused subscriptions works out to $252 / 12 = $21 a month.

## The surprising fact
The FTC says Amazon's own name for its Prime cancel flow was "Iliad", the epic about a ten-year war. Separately, in the C+R survey people underestimated their subscription spending by about 2.5x ($219 / $86 = 2.55, our math).
