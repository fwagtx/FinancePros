# Research brief: Why is a box of diapers so expensive? (Hot Take, posts Wed Jan 6, 2027, 6 PM CT)

Evergreen angle (no breaking-news hook used): retail diaper prices rose about a third from 2019 to 2023, factory prices jumped after 2021, and the inputs are oil-linked. Plus the surprise: SNAP can't buy diapers.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)

1. Retail price per pack (NIQ via Fox Business, Aug 2023): average US disposable diaper package was $16.54 in 2019 and $21.90 "now" (article ~Aug 2023). Our math: 21.90 / 16.54 = 1.324 -> about 32% higher. [S1]
   - Corroboration (different data provider): Circana data cited by Bloomberg (Feb 8, 2024): retail price of a pack of diapers rose 35% from 2019 to 2023. [S2]
   - Caveat: Fox article date is inferred (Breitbart Aug 17, 2023 cites it); NIQ original not seen.

2. Factory (producer) prices: BLS PPI by Commodity, "Disposable diapers and similar disposable products, purchased sanitary paper stock and wadding" (WPU091501234, index Dec 2007=100, not seasonally adjusted), via FRED, last updated Oct 15, 2026. [S3]
   - Flat for years: 111.2 every month from Aug 2018 through Jun 2021.
   - Jumped to 117.625 in Sep 2021; 123.291 by Oct 2022; 129.038 in Oct 2025 through Feb 2026 (peak 129.172 Mar-Apr 2026); 125.189 Jun-Sep 2026.
   - Our math: Jan 2026 / Jan 2021 = 129.038 / 111.2 = 1.160 -> about 16% higher.
   - Note: the series covers diapers AND similar disposable products (broader than baby diapers).

3. Why costs rose: superabsorbent polymer (SAP), the gel in diapers, is made mainly from acrylic acid, which is produced by oxidizing propylene, a petrochemical (naphtha/oil-gas derived). Sanyo Chemical (SAP maker) 2018 price notice: naphtha and propylene price hikes raised acrylic acid, "primary raw material of SAP", plus utility and transport costs. Nippon Shokubai: SAP business directly affected by naphtha raw material prices. [S4][S5]
   - Fluff (wood) pulp is the other absorbent core material (University at Buffalo engineering course page on diapers). [S6]

4. Company price increases: On Oct 19, 2021, Procter & Gamble (maker of Pampers) said it had been raising prices on product lines including baby care, to offset higher commodity and freight costs (AP). [S7] Lines up with the PPI jump in Sep 2021. (A claimed 2022 7% P&G/K-C diaper hike could NOT be confirmed with a dated source; not used.)

5. Market structure: category "long dominated" by P&G's Pampers and Kimberly-Clark's Huggies (Modern Retail, Sep 23, 2025). Exact current shares not confirmed (older figures: P&G ~44-45%, K-C ~35-37% in mid-2010s per Trefis/Market Realist) - NOT used on screen.

6. Diaper need: NDBN Diaper Check 2023 (YouGov survey, Apr 21-May 18, 2023): 47% of US families with young children reported diaper need (struggled to afford enough diapers); was about 1 in 3 in 2010. [S8] (2024 Diaper Check reportedly 46% - secondary source.)

7. Cost per month: NDBN Fast Facts (June 2023): infants need up to 12 diapers a day, costing "$80 to $100 or more per month per baby". [S9]
   - Worked example (our math): $80 x 12 months = $960 a year; $100 x 12 = $1,200 a year.

8. The surprise: SNAP cannot be used for diapers. USDA FNS SNAP retailer notice on allowable items lists "Baby bottles/diapers/wipes" among items retailers must not accept SNAP for (page updated Nov 24, 2025); non-food items are ineligible. [S10] WIC is a food/nutrition program and does not provide diapers (secondary sources only; not used on screen).

9. Sales tax: counts conflict (about 19-26 states exempt as of 2025 depending on source); Nevada (Jan 1, 2025), Missouri (Aug 28, 2025), Alabama (Sep 1, 2025) added exemptions. NOT used (uncertain count).

10. CPI: diapers are not a standalone CPI item; BLS ELI AF012 "Infants' and toddlers' underwear and diapers" sits in infants' and toddlers' apparel (BLS CE/quality-adjustment tables). Not charted; used PPI instead.

## Worked example (our math)
- NIQ pack: $21.90 / $16.54 = 1.324 -> +32% (2019 to 2023).
- PPI: 129.038 / 111.2 = 1.160 -> +16% (Jan 2021 to Jan 2026).
- Yearly diaper cost: $80/month x 12 = $960 per year (low end of NDBN range).

## Surprising fact
SNAP benefits can't buy diapers (USDA lists diapers as a non-allowable item).
