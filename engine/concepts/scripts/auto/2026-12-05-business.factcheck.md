# Fact-check: 2026-12-05-business (vertical short, posts Dec 5, 2026)
Checked: October 6, 2026. Topic: how affiliate links pay creators (Amazon Associates example).

## Sources verified
- Amazon Associates Program Standard Commission Income Statement (affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ, page copy cached 2026-10-06). Table 1: Luxury Beauty / Luxury Stores Beauty / Amazon Explore 10%; Digital & Physical Music, Handmade, Digital Videos 5%; Physical Books, Kitchen, Automotive 4.5%; Kindle/Fire/Echo/Ring devices, Apparel, Shoes, Jewelry, Watches, Luggage, Handbags, Luxury Stores Fashion 4%; Toys, Furniture, Home, Home Improvement, Lawn & Garden, Pets, Headphones, Beauty, Musical Instruments, Business & Industrial, Outdoors, Tools, Sports, Baby, Amazon Coins 3%; PC, PC Components, DVD & Blu-Ray 2.5%; Televisions, Digital Video Games 2%; Amazon Fresh, Physical Video Games & Consoles, Grocery, Health & Personal Care 1%; Gift Cards, Wireless plans, Alcohol, etc. 0%; Coach 0%; All Other Categories 4%. Highest fixed rate = 10% (Amazon Games 20% no longer listed).
- 2026 reporting (Adweek, eMarketer, Shopifreaks, Hello Partner, May 2026): Amazon cut negotiated/premium publisher rates by up to 50% (some from 10% down to 4-5%) and cut bonuses; these are custom publisher deals, not the public standard table.
- Associates Operating Agreement (updated Oct 15, 2025), Section 5: "You must clearly and prominently state the following, or any substantially similar statement previously allowed under this Agreement ...: 'As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.'" Quote is word-for-word correct and correctly attributed.
- Session rule (Amazon Associates help/agreement): session begins on click and ends at the first of 24 hours or an order being placed; items added to cart in that window still count if ordered within 89 days of the click. Accurate.
- FTC: updated Endorsement Guides announced June 29, 2023 (Federal Register July 26, 2023), 16 CFR Part 255. Sec. 255.5: an unexpected material connection "must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously." Guides are advisory interpretations of the FTC Act, so "should" is a fair, not overstated, framing.
- Price: affiliate commissions come out of the merchant's marketing budget; the listed price is not changed by the link ("usually the same" is fair, hedged).
- Other programs: digital-product networks (e.g., ClickBank) commonly pay 50-75%; many SaaS/digital programs pay 20-50%. So "up to 10%" is Amazon-specific, not an affiliate-wide ceiling.
- Math (python): $200 x 4% = $8.00. Correct.

## Issues
1. **Line 3 (FACT, major): "4% for all other categories" misrepresents the table.** In Amazon's table, "All Other Categories" is only the leftover bucket for categories not named. Most everyday categories are listed separately below 4%: toys, home, beauty, sports, baby 3%; PCs 2.5%; TVs 2%; grocery, health and personal care 1%. Books, kitchen and automotive get 4.5%. The three-tier list (10 / 4 / 0) suggests almost everything pays 4%, which is wrong.
   Fix (text and say): "Amazon's standard rates run from zero on gift cards to ten percent on luxury beauty. Lots of everyday categories pay one to four and a half percent." Scene list: ["Lux beauty: 10%", "Books: 4.5%", "Toys, home: 3%", "Grocery: 1%", "Gift cards: 0%"] (reveal 5). Or keep three items but call the middle one "Most: 1-4.5%".
2. **Post ig (FACT): "10% luxury beauty, 4% all other categories, 0% gift cards"** has the same error. Fix: "Amazon standard rates (Oct 2026) run from 0% (gift cards) to 10% (luxury beauty); many categories pay 1-4.5%." The yt_desc wording ("from 0% on gift cards to 10% on luxury beauty") is accurate. Keep it.
3. **Line 0 hook / cover / post titles (OVERGENERALIZATION): "That link pays creators up to ten percent."** On its own it reads as a cap for all affiliate links, but digital-product programs often pay 20-75%. And 10% is Amazon's top rate, which only luxury beauty and Amazon Explore get. Fix: "An Amazon link can pay a creator up to ten percent of your order." (about 2.5 s). Stronger, still true: "That Amazon link? The creator gets a cut of your order, up to ten percent." Update post.title, ig and tiktok first lines to say "An Amazon link pays creators up to 10% of your order."
4. **Cover title "Your Click Pays Them" (minor accuracy):** a click alone earns nothing; the creator gets paid only on a qualifying purchase. Fix: "Your Order Pays Them" or "Your Cart Pays Them".
5. **Line 7 (attribution/overclaim, moderate): "Amazon requires this exact line, so when you see it, that link pays the creator."** The quote is verbatim and correctly sourced. But the agreement also allows "any substantially similar statement previously allowed," so "exact" overstates it. And the line is a blanket disclosure: it means the creator is an Associate whose Amazon links can earn on qualifying purchases, not that a particular link pays. Fix: "Amazon makes creators post this line, so when you see it, their Amazon links can earn them a cut."
6. **Line 4 (hypothetical labeling, minor):** It is labeled "Example", which is good. But 4% only applies to certain categories, and commission is figured on qualifying revenue (excluding tax and shipping). Fix: text "Example: a $200 order in a 4% category pays the creator $8. ($200 x 4% = $8)". Say "For example, a two hundred dollar order in a four percent category pays the creator eight dollars." Optionally add sub "before tax & shipping". Values match spoken ($200 / $8).
7. **Line 6 (framing, OK):** "should clearly disclose" is fair for advisory Guides. To match the Guides' wording: "say creators should disclose paid links clearly and conspicuously." "Updated in 2023" is correct (June 29, 2023). No change needed.
8. **Line 5 (OK):** "Usually the same" is hedged and sourced. Compare values "$200" and "-$8" are 7 characters or fewer. The on-screen and spoken values match.
9. **Line 3 time word "as of October 2026":** This is accurate for the table read on Oct 6, 2026, and still reads fine on Dec 5, 2026 as a dated statement. Re-check the table before posting, since Amazon changed publisher rates in 2026.
10. **Line 2 (OK):** 24-hour window and 89-day cart rule are accurate. Optional nuance: the window also closes once you place an order.
