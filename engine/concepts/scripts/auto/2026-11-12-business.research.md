# Research brief: How hospital systems make money buying doctor practices
Slot 2026-11-12-business (How They Make Money, posts Thu Nov 12, 2026). Researched Sep 30 / Oct 1, 2026.

Note: hospital systems are mostly nonprofits with no single 10-K covering this; numbers come from official CMS rules, the PAI-Avalere workforce study and Kaufman Hall (Vizient) benchmarks.

## Key facts (with dates)
- As of Jan 1, 2026, 82.0% of US physicians were employed by hospitals/health systems or other corporate entities: 59.7% by hospitals, 22.3% by other corporate entities; just 18% in physician-owned practices. (PAI-Avalere Health report, 2018-2026, released Sep 2026; Medical Economics, Becker's, HealthExec)
- Hospital-employed physicians: 219,712 (2018) -> 400,938 (Jan 2026), +181,200; 44,000 added 2024-2026. 63.9% of practices owned by hospitals/corporate entities (vs 29.8% in 2018). (PAI-Avalere 2026; Medical Economics)
- As of Jan 1, 2024: 77.6% employed by hospitals or corporate entities; 55.1% by health systems. (PAI-Avalere 2024; Advisory Board, Healthcare Dive)
- Jan 2019: ~62%; Jan 2022: ~74% (73.9%) employed by hospital or corporate entity. (PAI-Avalere 2022 release; Medical Economics, PCC)
- Kaufman Hall Physician Flash Report Q2 2026: median investment/subsidy (i.e., loss on the medical group) per physician FTE = $314,231 (Q2 2025: $313,330); per provider FTE incl. APPs $235,908. Data on 200,000+ employed physicians/APPs. Kaufman Hall reports these as annualized figures. (Kaufman Hall/Vizient; Becker's ASC "The $314K physician investment")
- Facility fees: in a hospital outpatient department (HOPD), Medicare pays the physician fee AND a separate facility fee under OPPS; in an independent office it pays one PFS amount. CBO: Medicare pays on average ~2.5x more for many identical services in HOPDs. (Bipartisan Policy Center, citing CBO)
- CMS example (CY2026 OPPS rule, final Nov 21, 2025): chemotherapy IV infusion, up to 1 hour: $119 under the Physician Fee Schedule vs $341 under OPPS (186% more). CMS finalized paying the PFS-equivalent rate (40% of OPPS) for drug administration at excepted off-campus provider-based departments starting 2026; est. -$290M in 2026. (CMS rule; California Hospital Association summary; Federal Register 2025-20907)
- Off-campus provider-based departments opened after Nov 2, 2015 have been paid a PFS-equivalent rate (40% of OPPS) since 2018 (Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015 sec. 603).
- CY2027 OPPS proposed rule, released July 2, 2026: proposes extending site-neutral (40% of OPPS) pay to imaging without contrast at excepted off-campus PBDs; rural sole community hospitals exempt. (Holland & Knight; Advis; America's Essential Hospitals)
- Referrals ("downstream revenue"): Merritt Hawkins 2019 Physician Inpatient/Outpatient Revenue Survey: physicians generated an average of nearly $2.4M a year in net revenue for affiliated hospitals (2018 data), up from $1.5M in 2016. Family physicians $2.1M; cardiovascular surgeons ~$3.7M. (Fierce Healthcare, Healthcare Dive, Feb 2019)

## Worked example
CMS example: $341 (hospital outpatient rate) - $119 (office rate) = $222 more for the same infusion. $341 / $119 = 2.87, about 2.9 times. ($222/$119 = 186.6% more, matches CMS's "186%".)

## Surprise fact
Hospitals typically lose money on the doctors' offices themselves (median ~$314K per physician, Q2 2026), and count on facility fees and referrals for tests, surgery and hospital stays to make it back.
