# Research brief: Why billionaires can have low taxable income (slot 2026-11-11-hot)
Researched Oct 1, 2026. Posts Nov 11, 2026. Angle: income vs. wealth + the realization rule + one dated estimate (with critics).
Distinct from the Oct 5 long-form on "buy, borrow, die": no borrowing/step-up content here.

## Core concept
- The federal income tax taxes INCOME, not wealth. A rise in the value of an asset you still hold (an unrealized or "paper" gain) is not taxable income.
- Realization rule: a capital gain is generally taxed when you sell. IRS Topic 409: "When you sell a capital asset, the difference between the adjusted basis in the asset and the amount you realized from the sale is a capital gain or a capital loss." Held > 1 year = long-term (lower rates may apply).
- Moore v. United States (decided June 20, 2024, 7-2): Court upheld the one-time Mandatory Repatriation Tax but did NOT decide whether realization is a constitutional requirement for an income tax ("issues for another day"). Barrett/Alito (concurring) and Thomas/Gorsuch (dissent) said realization is required. Sources: CRS LSB11185; RSM; DLA Piper; Holland & Knight (June/July 2024).

## Key estimates (dated)
1. White House CEA/OMB (Greg Leiserson, CEA; Danny Yagan, OMB), released Sept 23, 2021: the 400 wealthiest families paid an average federal individual income tax rate of 8.2% over 2010-2018, on about $1.8 trillion of income, where "income" INCLUDES estimated unrealized capital gains (built from Forbes 400 data). Sources: OMB blog (via UCSB American Presidency Project), CNBC Sept 23, 2021, UPI Sept 23, 2021.
2. ProPublica, June 8, 2021 ("The Secret IRS Files"): the 25 richest Americans' wealth rose $401 billion from 2014 to 2018; they paid $13.6 billion in federal income taxes; ProPublica's "true tax rate" (taxes paid / wealth growth) = 3.4%. Based on leaked IRS data. Sources: CBS News, NPR (June 8, 2021).
   Check: 13.6 / 401 = 3.39% -> 3.4%.

## Critics' objection (must be noted)
- Tax Foundation (Sept 2021, "Latest White House Report Tells Incomplete Story on Average Tax Rates for Wealthy"): the CEA figure counts paper gains as income and fails to account for corporate income taxes paid at the company level.
- Cato Institute (June 2021): wealth growth is not income; standard measures from CBO, Tax Policy Center, JCT and IRS show people at the top pay higher average rates than people in the middle. AEI, NTU similar.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- A founder holds shares that rise $1,000,000,000 in value in a year; she sells none and draws a $1,000,000 salary.
- Wealth gain: $1,000,000,000 (unrealized -> not taxable income).
- Income subject to income tax: the $1,000,000 salary (before deductions); taxable gain on the shares = $0 until sold.
- Gain-to-salary ratio: 1,000,000,000 / 1,000,000 = 1,000x.

## Surprising fact
- Under the standard definition, a billion-dollar rise in someone's net worth can add $0 to taxable income, legally, as long as nothing is sold.
