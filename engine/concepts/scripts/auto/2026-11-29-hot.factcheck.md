## Issues

1. **Hook is too long and gives the answer away (L1). Moderate.** The line is true and specific. But at 10 words it runs about 3.5-4 seconds at normal speed, which misses the under-3-second rule. It also states only the obvious upside of 5% down, with no tension, so the viewer has no reason to need the answer. Make it 8 words and end on an open loop that the payment and interest lines then pay off:
   - text: `5% down: $64,365 less cash on the August 2026 median home. The catch?`
   - say: `Five percent down saves sixty-four grand. The catch?`
   - scene: unchanged (`title "[$64,365] less cash"`, label `Aug 2026`, value `5% down`, mood `shock`)
2. **L3 ties the down payment to the interest rate. Moderate (logic or misleading).** The say line "At seven point two eight percent, that's about eighty-six thousand down..." and the title "Down payment (7.28% rate)" suggest the rate sets the down payment. It doesn't. The down payment is just a percentage of the price. Move the rate to L4, where it matters.
   - L3 text: `Down payment on $429,100: $85,820 at 20% vs $21,455 at 5%.`
   - L3 say: `Down payment: about eighty-six thousand versus about twenty-one thousand.`
   - L3 scene.title: `Down payment needed` (left and right values unchanged; both are 7 characters or fewer)
   - L4 text: `Monthly principal and interest, 30 yrs at 7.28% (Freddie Mac, Oct 1, 2026): $2,349 vs $2,789.`
   - L4 say: `At seven point two eight percent, the payment flips: about two thousand three hundred fifty versus two thousand seven hundred ninety.`
   - L4 scene: unchanged
3. **L8 spoken claim goes slightly beyond the data. Minor.** A 10% median means at least half of first-time buyers put down 10% or less. That does not strictly prove "most first-time buyers skip twenty percent".
   - L8 say: `The typical first-time buyer skips twenty percent anyway: ten percent median in twenty twenty-five.`
   - L8 text and scene: unchanged
4. **The data will be stale by November 29. Minor. The wording is dated, so nothing will be false.**
   - NAR's September existing-home sales (out about late October) and October existing-home sales (out about mid-to-late November) will both be public. All mentions of the price say "August 2026", which is fine.
   - About 8 newer PMMS readings will be out. "7.28% ... Oct 1, 2026" is dated on screen and in the post. The spoken L4 rate has no date, but the caption next to it does. That is acceptable. Optional: re-run the numbers on the latest PMMS before you schedule.
   - **The NAR 2026 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers normally comes out in early November** (2025: Nov 4; 2024: Nov 4; 2022: Nov 3). By the posting date, the "10%" chart end point and "repeat buyers 23%" will probably be last year's numbers. The text says "NAR's 2025 profile", so the claim stays true. But consider adding the 2026 point if it is out before posting. Keep the chart end label matched to the last point.
5. **The post's source line is ambiguous. Minor.** IG says "Sources: NAR (Sept 2026, 2025 Profile)", which reads as September data. Replace it with: `Sources: NAR (August 2026 existing-home sales, released Sept 10, 2026; 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers); Freddie Mac PMMS (Oct 1, 2026); CFPB.`
6. **Unstated assumption in the example. Minor.** The example uses the same 7.28% rate for both loans. PMMS rates assume 20% down and excellent credit, and a 5% down loan can price a little higher. The extra interest also assumes the loan is held all 30 years with no refinance or prepayment. L6 already says "Example". Optional L6 scene.sub: `Same rate, held 30 yrs, before PMI` (if it fits the sub limit; otherwise keep the current one).
7. **TikTok caption leaves out "P&I". Nit.** "$440 more a month" is P&I only, and the real gap is larger with PMI. Suggested: `5% down vs 20% on a $429,100 home: $64,365 less cash, $440 more a month before PMI. Not financial advice. #moneytok #personalfinance #mortgage #financeprostv`
