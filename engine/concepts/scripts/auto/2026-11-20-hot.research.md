# 2026-11-20-hot research: Scam alert - fake class action settlement claims

Format: Hot Take / Scam Alert. Evergreen; every number dated. Red flags described factually; protective points framed as what official sources say. No private individuals named.

## Core facts (dated)
- FTC Consumer Sentinel, 2025 data (released March and June 2026): about 3 million fraud reports; record **$15.9 billion** in reported fraud losses in 2025, up from $12.5 billion in 2024 (about +27%). [FTC testimony/press, PYMNTS, CNBC]
- Imposter scams were the most-reported fraud category for the 5th straight year in 2025: **$3.5 billion** reported lost; about 1 million imposter reports; about 80% reported no loss, the other 20% lost the $3.5B. Business impersonators nearly $1B (bank impersonators highest); government impersonators about $920M. [FTC press release June 2026; CNBC June 26, 2026]
- Note: the FTC does not publish a separate "fake class action settlement" loss category. Do NOT state a dollar figure specific to settlement scams. Fake settlement notices are a form of impersonation (of a claims administrator, court, company, or the FTC).

## Timely angle: Amazon Prime FTC settlement
- Sept 2025: FTC secured a $2.5 billion settlement with Amazon over Prime enrollment/cancellation allegations ($1B civil penalty, $1.5B for refunds). Amazon did not admit wrongdoing (settlement). [FTC press release Sept 2025]
- Amazon began automatic payments Nov 2025; as of Sept 2026, more than **$845 million** paid. [FTC press release Sept 2026]
- Sept 2026: FTC announced additional payments; max per eligible customer raised from **$51 to $200**; earlier recipients may get an additional **$149** ($51 + $149 = $200). Automatic payments to more customers (used 11-20 Prime benefits in a year) starting Oct 1, 2026, via PayPal, Venmo, or mailed check; no claim form needed for automatic payments. [FTC press release Sept 2026; CNBC Select; Yahoo Finance]
- FTC Amazon refunds page: "No one from the FTC or Amazon will ask you for money to get a refund." FTC says it is not contacting people about refunds in the Amazon matter, and only scammers promise special access or a guaranteed refund. [ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds/amazon-refunds; Snopes; news reports Sept 2026]

## How real settlements / refunds work (official guidance)
- FTC Refund Programs FAQ: the FTC never requires people to pay money or give account information (bank account, Social Security number) to get a refund; anyone asking for a fee to release a refund is a scammer. Legit FTC refunds come as checks, PayPal/Venmo payments, or via a claim form on an official site listed at ftc.gov/refunds. [ftc.gov refund FAQ; consumer.ftc.gov]
- Court class actions: a judge approves the settlement; a court-approved claims administrator runs the official settlement website; the administrator is paid from the settlement fund or by the defendant, so class members pay nothing to file. Real notices name the case, the court, the administrator and the official settlement site. [BBB warnings 2026 via local outlets (KYMA Mar 4, 2026; WALB Jul 9, 2026; WGEM Jul 26, 2026); legal-industry explainers]
- BBB (2026 warnings): fake class action settlement notices are proliferating and getting more convincing. Red flags: requests for Social Security number or bank account, upfront "processing fees," vague case details, unusually large payout claims, bad grammar, sender addresses or links that don't match the company or court, gift cards/crypto, pressure to act now. BBB says to report to ReportFraud.ftc.gov and BBB Scam Tracker. (No BBB Scam Tracker numeric data specific to settlement scams found.)
- Washington AG has issued a consumer alert on cons claiming you've received a lawsuit settlement payment (date not confirmed; not used on screen).

## Surprising fact
- FTC staff report, Sept 2019, "Consumers and Class Actions" (149 settlements): median claims rate only **9%** (weighted mean 4%); consumers often called real notices "spam," "scam," or "phishing." Real notices look like spam, which is exactly what scammers exploit.

## Worked example (exact math)
- Amazon Prime refund: earlier max $51 + additional $149 = $200 max per eligible customer (FTC, Sept 2026).
- Imposter share of 2025 fraud losses: 3.5 / 15.9 = 22.0%, about 1 in 5 dollars.
- Amazon refunds paid vs fund: $845M / $1.5B = 56% paid as of Sept 2026.
