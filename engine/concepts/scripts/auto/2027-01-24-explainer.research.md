# Research brief: Why some states have no income tax (and how they pay for things)
Made Nov 25, 2026; posts Sun Jan 24, 2027. Evergreen. State law can change: every list is "as of 2026".

## Timely angle
None needed (evergreen). Light hook: early-year tax season. Don't use "this week".

## Core facts (with dates)
- As of 2026 (Tax Foundation, "2026 State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets", published Feb 17, 2026, updated Feb 19, 2026):
  - 8 states have no individual income tax: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Wyoming.
  - Washington taxes capital gains income only (graduated, top rate 9% on capital gains over $1 million); it does not tax wages.
  - 42 states levy a broad-based income tax: 41 tax wages and salaries, Washington taxes capital gains only.
  - So 9 states do not tax wage income as of 2026 (8 + Washington).
- New Hampshire: Interest & Dividends Tax (RSA 77) repealed for taxable periods beginning after Dec 31, 2024 (NH Dept. of Revenue Administration FAQ). Confirmed: fully repealed as of Jan 1, 2025. NH never taxed wages.
- Tennessee: Hall income tax (on investment income) fully phased out in 2021 (Tax Foundation index history; secondary).

### Texas
- Texas Comptroller: sales tax is the largest source of state funding, 58% of all tax collections (fiscal 2025 releases). FY2025 sales tax revenue $49.06 billion; all-funds tax collections $84.2 billion (FY2025 revenue release, Sept 2025).
- Property taxes: no statewide property tax; local governments only. Tax Foundation 2025 property tax report (2023 ACS data): effective rate on owner-occupied homes about 1.36%, 7th highest (seen via search summary; Tax Foundation page couldn't be fetched directly — UNCERTAIN, not used in script).

### Alaska
- No statewide sales tax (local only, avg 1.82%) and no individual income tax.
- Oil + Permanent Fund: Legislative Fiscal Analyst FY2025 budget overview: DOR projected petroleum at 33% of unrestricted general fund (UGF) revenue in FY2025; oil is no longer the largest UGF source (Permanent Fund earnings are), but the most volatile.
- Alaska Permanent Fund Corporation transferred $3.5 billion to the state in FY2024 via the Percent of Market Value draw; FY2025 draw $3.7 billion (OMB FY2026 budget component).
- Permanent Fund Dividend per resident (Alaska DOR PFD Division summary table + DOR releases):
  - 2021: $1,114
  - 2022: $3,284 ($2,622 PFD + $662 energy relief, ACFR 2023)
  - 2023: $1,312
  - 2024: $1,702 ($1,403.83 PFD + $298.17 energy relief)
  - 2025: $1,000 (DOR release Sept 19, 2025)
  - 2026: $1,000 (DOR release Sept 28, 2026; payments starting Oct 1, 2026)

### Nevada
- Legislative Appropriations Brief 2025 (2025-27 biennium General Fund forecast): sales tax ~$3,713.4M (30.8%), gaming taxes ~$2,087.5M (17.3%), Modified Business Tax ~$1,736.6M (14.4%).

### Washington
- Business & Occupation (B&O) tax: a gross receipts tax paid by the business (not collected from customers); rate depends on activity (DOR: retailing 0.471%, wholesaling/manufacturing 0.484%; services higher tiers, recent changes — exact current service rates UNCERTAIN, not used in script).

## Total tax burden (Tax Foundation, "State and Local Tax Burdens, Calendar Year 2022", published April 2022)
- Lowest: Alaska 4.6%, Wyoming 7.5%, Tennessee 7.6%. Highest: New York 15.9%, Connecticut 15.4%, Hawaii 14.1%. Washington 10.7% (rank 30).
- Note: an older-vintage estimate; not used in script.

## Tax Foundation 2027 State Tax Competitiveness Index (published Nov 5, 2026)
- Top 5 overall: Wyoming, South Dakota, Alaska, Florida, Texas. Washington ranks 47th (sales tax component 50th). (Context only — this is a structure index, not a burden measure.)

## Worked example (our math)
$40,000 of taxable spending in a year:
- At Tennessee's 9.61% average combined rate (July 2026): 40,000 x 0.0961 = $3,844
- At the 7.53% US average (July 2026): 40,000 x 0.0753 = $3,012
- Difference: $832 a year. (Simplification: real taxable spending, exemptions such as groceries, and local rates vary.)

## The surprising fact
"No income tax" is not "no tax": Tennessee and Washington, both without wage taxes, have the 2nd and 3rd highest combined sales tax rates in the US (July 2026). And Alaska actually pays residents ($1,000 each in 2026).

## Neutrality
Explain mechanics only. No claim that one system is better; no suggestion to move.
