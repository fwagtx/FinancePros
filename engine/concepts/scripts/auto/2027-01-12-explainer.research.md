# Research brief: Negotiating rent at renewal, what gives you leverage
Show: Explainer (vertical short), posts Tuesday January 12, 2027. Evergreen: every number carries its date.
Researched November 13, 2026.

## Angle / hook
The surprising fact: at big apartment landlords, people who renew are paying MORE while new tenants pay LESS.
MAA (Mid-America Apartment Communities, a large apartment REIT), Q3 2026 (company release, Oct 28, 2026):
- Effective new lease rate growth: -6.0% (new tenant's rent vs the prior lease on the same unit)
- Effective renewal lease rate growth: +5.3% (up 80 basis points year over year)
- Effective blended lease rate growth: +0.4%
- Resident turnover (same store, as of Sept 30, 2026): 39.6%, "historically low"; company cites "record retention"
Context (trade press, not used on screen): Equity Residential Q2 2026 reportedly new leases -0.7% vs renewals +5.2%;
LeaseLock/RealPage put 2026 renewal growth around 3.5% vs move-in growth about 1.9%. Pattern: renewals carry rent growth.
Why it matters: landlords price renewals assuming moving is a hassle, but a vacancy costs them money.
No single news event this week; the "news" is the market backdrop (vacancy highs, concessions), which is evergreen-safe with dates.

## Leverage fact 1: empty units (official, chartable)

- Rental vacancy rate Q3 2026: 7.6% (vs 7.3% in Q2 2026, 7.1% in Q3 2025). Not seasonally adjusted.
- Median asking rent for vacant-for-rent units, Q3 2026: $1,541.
- Next release: February 2, 2027 (after posting date of Jan 12, 2027, so Q3 2026 stays the latest).

## Leverage fact 2: deals for new renters
Zillow, April 2026 rental report / May 27, 2026 release: 39.8% of rental listings on Zillow offered a concession
(free rent, waived fees, discounted move-in costs), up 5 points from a year earlier; more than double pre-pandemic.
Later Zillow readings: 39.7% (June 2026), 39.8% (August 2026, per Zillow release). Local spread is huge:
Denver 68.3%, Charlotte 66.6%, Dallas 64.2% vs New York 18.4%, Buffalo 11.1% (April 2026).

## Leverage fact 3: a vacant unit costs the landlord
Apartment List, October 2026 market recap: the median vacant unit took 34 days to lease in September 2026.
Landlord turnover costs (cleaning, repairs, marketing) are often quoted around $1,500-$4,000 per unit (NAA surveys and
trade press) but figures vary and I could not confirm a single primary number, so they are NOT used on screen.

## Worked example (our math, illustrative)
Rent = $1,541 (Census median asking rent, Q3 2026). Renewal raise = 5.3% (MAA Q3 2026 renewal growth).
- Monthly raise: 1,541 x 0.053 = $81.67
- Yearly raise: 81.67 x 12 = $980.04 -> about $980
Vacancy cost: daily rent = 1,541 x 12 / 365 = $50.66
- 34 days empty: 50.66 x 34 = $1,722.44 -> about $1,722 (unrounded daily rate gives $1,722.54)
Takeaway: about 34 empty days cost the landlord about $1,722 in lost rent, more than the whole first year of a
5.3% raise ($980), before any turnover costs. That is the renter's leverage. (Not a guarantee: a landlord can refuse,
and local markets differ widely.)

## Takeaway for script
Leverage at renewal = (1) local vacancy, (2) deals offered to new renters, (3) what it costs the landlord to refill.
Education only, no advice; frame as "where leverage comes from", note the landlord can still say no.
