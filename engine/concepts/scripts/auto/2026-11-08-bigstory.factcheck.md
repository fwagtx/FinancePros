# Fact-check: 2026-11-08 Big Story (IRS 2027 inflation adjustments)

Checked Sun Nov 8, 2026, about 1 PM CT, for a 4 PM CT post. irs.gov, journalofaccountancy.com and taxfoundation.org could not be fetched from this session, so I checked through search results from several outlets.

## Verified claims (no change needed)
- Release: the IRS put out IR-2026-133 and Rev. Proc. 2026-35 on Thu Nov 5, 2026. They adjust almost 70 provisions for tax year 2027 and generally apply to returns filed in 2028. "Set Thursday by the IRS" is correct for a Sunday post. (Search snippets citing IRS IR-2026-133, CPA Practice Advisor 11/05/2026, Accounting Today, The Tax Adviser, KPMG TaxNewsFlash, NBC Miami)
- Standard deduction for 2027: $16,600 for single filers (up $500 from $16,100) and $33,200 for married filing jointly (up $1,000 from $32,200). Head of household is $24,925. (CPA Practice Advisor, NBC Miami/NBC Connecticut, political.org 11/05/2026)
- "Brackets rise an average of 3.2%, rates stay the same": NerdWallet says the 2027 inflation adjustments were "set at 3.2%". My own check of the thresholds agrees: $12,400 to $12,800 is +3.23%, $50,400 to $52,025 is +3.22%, and $105,700 to $109,125 is +3.24%. The rates stay at 10/12/22/24/32/35/37%. (NerdWallet tax-changes-2027, KTVU, Fox LA)
- 2027 single brackets: the 10% bracket ends at $12,800 and the 12% bracket ends at $52,025. (Kiplinger "2027 tax brackets are set", Fox LA, Thomson Reuters Checkpoint)
- 2026 single brackets (Rev. Proc. 2025-32): the 10% bracket ends at $12,400 and the 12% bracket ends at $50,400. The 22% bracket for married filing jointly starts at $100,800, which is twice $50,400. (Tax Foundation 2026 brackets, CPA Practice Advisor 10/09/2025, worked example $12,400 x 10% = $1,240)
- "Our math" $68: 2026 taxable income is $43,900, so tax is 1,240 + 0.12 x 31,500 = $5,020. 2027 taxable income is $43,400, so tax is 1,280 + 0.12 x 30,600 = $4,952. The difference is $68. All inputs are confirmed. The stat sub "fed income tax" correctly limits the claim to federal income tax.
- Chart of the single standard deduction, 2018-2027: 12,000 / 12,200 / 12,400 / 12,550 / 12,950 / 13,850 / 14,600 / 15,750 / 16,100 / 16,600. The 2025 figure of $15,750 is the amount after the July 2025 law (One Big Beautiful Bill) raised it from $15,000. IRS 2026 release and search results confirm it. 2018-2024 match the IRS figures for each year.
- Hook "Your first $16,600 next year? Federal income tax-free.": this is fair. It holds for anyone who takes the standard deduction, because single and married-filing-separately filers get exactly $16,600 and joint and head-of-household filers get more. "Federal income tax" is precise, since payroll and state taxes still apply. It has 12 spoken words, within the limit of 12.
- Timing line 8 (2027 income, filed in 2028): correct.
