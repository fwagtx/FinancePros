# Fact-check: 2026-10-30 The Big Story (Exxon/Chevron Q3 refining windfall)

Checked Fri Oct 30, 2026, ~1:00 PM CT. Direct fetches of sec.gov, qz.com and finance.yahoo.com were blocked in this session, so verification relies on agreeing web-search excerpts (Exxon release/8-K, Reuters via Investing.com, Quartz, Chartmill, GuruFocus, Motley Fool, Chevron 8-K, EIA weekly table, OilPriceAPI).

## Verified claims
- **Exxon Q3 2026 GAAP earnings: $15.4B ($3.72/sh), compared with $7.5B ($1.76/sh) in Q3 2025.** 15.4 / 7.5 = 2.05x, so "doubled" holds on a GAAP basis. Adjusted earnings were $17.5B ($4.23/sh). The script uses GAAP throughout, so lines[1], lines[2], the cover and the post fields are consistent.
- **Segments (GAAP): Energy Products about $7.2B (7,169M) vs Upstream about $7.1B** (Exxon release table; Quartz citing Reuters). The adjusted figures also show refining ahead ($9.05B vs $7.3B), so the claim holds either way.
  - Labelling "Refining" with the sub "Energy Products" is accurate, because Energy Products is Exxon's refining and fuels segment.
  - Labelling "Oil & gas" with the sub "Upstream" is accurate.
- **"First time in at least six years, per Reuters."** Quartz cites Reuters for this. A Motley Fool piece published today also says "first time in 6 years". Supported.
- **Cause: the Iran conflict plus Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries squeezing fuel supply.** The Reuters wrap ("war-driven refining surge") and the Zacks/Yahoo coverage both make this link. The attribution is fine.
- **Chevron downstream (refining and marketing): $4,931M in Q3 2026, compared with $1,137M in Q3 2025** (Chevron 8-K). "$1.1 to $4.9 billion in a year" is accurate. Chevron itself credits higher margins on refined product sales.
- **EIA diesel: $6.352/gal for the week of Oct 26, 2026** (released Oct 27). That is up 7.6c week over week and $2.634 above a year earlier. Text, say and scene all match: "$6.35", "six thirty-five", "+$2.63".
- **CEO Darren Woods** called the improvements "lasting improvements, not simply the result of favorable prices and margins" (Exxon release). **Analysts** flag that the beat leans on margins inflated by geopolitical supply disruptions. Supported.
- **Hook:** "Diesel averages six thirty-five a gallon. Exxon's profit just doubled." Both facts are true and current; the results came out today, so "just" is fine. Placing them side by side implies a link, which matches what the sources say: the fuel supply squeeze lifted refining margins. The hook does not claim that Exxon set diesel prices. OK.
- **Other fields:** The header and cover pay off in the video. The post fields (title, ig, tiktok, yt_desc) have consistent numbers and dates and carry no advice or predictions. The politics are neutral; Trump's price-gouging comments are not mentioned, which is fine.
