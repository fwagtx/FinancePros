# Research brief: What a dividend is, explained with acorns (posts Oct 19, 2026)

## Definition (SEC Investor.gov)
- A dividend is a payment a company makes to shareholders, usually out of earnings. Many US companies pay quarterly. Dividends are not guaranteed: the board can raise, cut or stop them.
- Ex-dividend date: buy on or after it and you don't get the next dividend. On the ex-dividend date the share price typically drops by about the dividend amount, so the dividend isn't free money.

## Numbers
- S&P 500 dividend yield: about 1.06% as of Sep 28, 2026 (GuruFocus), versus a long-term average of about 1.6% (other trackers show 1.04%). Spoken as "about one percent".
- Chartable series: S&P 500 dividends per share, calendar years (S&P Dow Jones Indices, Howard Silverblatt, Jan 7, 2026 release): 2022 $66.92 | 2023 $70.30 | 2024 $74.83 | 2025 $78.92. 2025 was the 16th straight annual increase, up 5.5%.
- US common dividend increases totaled $46.4B in 2025 (S&P DJI).

## Worked example (hypothetical)
- 100 shares x $0.50 per quarter = $50 per quarter; x 4 = $200 a year.
- Yield = annual dividend / price = $2.00 / $50 = 4%.
