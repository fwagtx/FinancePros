# Fact-check: 2026-10-14-hot ("Landlord Sold? Your Lease Stays")

Checked 2026-10-01 for posting 2026-10-14. Script JSON only. Math recomputed in python.

## Verified
- L1/L2: A sale does not end a fixed-term lease; the buyer takes the property subject to it (general rule across US states). "In most states" is fine. One edge case: a rare lease clause that ends the lease on sale.
- L3: 35% of households rent. Census HVS Q2 2026 homeownership rate is 65.0%, and 100 - 65.0 = 35.0. The Q3 release comes in late October, so Q2 is still the latest data on Oct 14. Not stale.
- L4: The hypothetical ($1,800 rent, 8 months left) is labeled "Example". Same rent and same end date fit the general rule.
- L5: Cal. Civ. Code 1950.5(h) requires the old landlord to transfer the deposit to the successor, or return it to the tenant, and to notify the tenant. What gets transferred or returned is the amount left after lawful deductions.
- L7: PTFA. A bona fide tenant can finish the lease, or gets at least 90 days' notice (90 days if month-to-month, or if the buyer will live in the unit). "Generally" covers the exceptions.

## Issues
1. **L6 (text/say/scene) and post.ig: legal claim broader than the law (wrong fact).** Under 1950.5(j), when the old owner skips the transfer, successors are jointly and severally liable for "the security, or that portion thereof to which the tenant is entitled." That is the amount after lawful deductions, not automatically the full $2,000. Also, 1950.5(j)(3) shields a successor who in good faith believed the deposit was transferred or returned. "Can still owe you the full $2,000" overstates this.
   Fix: "The surprise: if the old owner skips that, the new owner can be on the hook for your deposit too." Scene: label "New owner can share liability", value "Deposit", sub "Calif. Civ. Code 1950.5(j)". In post.ig, add "in many cases" and "minus lawful deductions".
2. **L8 (text/say/split scene): broader than the law (wrong fact).** It says month-to-month renters "can get a notice to leave, on a timeline set by state law." But in California (Tenant Protection Act, Civ. Code 1946.2, for tenants of 12+ months), Oregon, New Jersey, Washington and many cities with just-cause rules, a sale alone is not a lawful reason to end a tenancy. Exceptions are narrow, such as owner move-in. Also, the line comes right after the foreclosure line, and there month-to-month tenants get the federal 90 days, not a state timeline.
   Fix: "The catch: month-to-month renters have less protection. In many states the new owner can give notice, but some states and cities require a legal reason." Change the right column to "Can get notice in many states" / "Some places need just cause".
3. **L7 scene sub "federal PTFA, since 2018": wrong date.** The PTFA was enacted May 2009, expired end of 2014, and was restored permanently in May 2018. Fix: "federal PTFA (2009, permanent 2018)".
4. **L5: minor precision.** Say "transfer what's left of it, minus lawful deductions, or refund you". The money scene shows $2,000 moving in full and implies no deductions; add sub "minus lawful deductions" or keep the "example" label on both sides.
5. **L3 say: awkward and weak link.** "That matters, since about thirty-five percent of US households rent" reads stiffly out loud. Fix: "About one in three US households rents." That also matches the on-screen 35%. Technically it is the share of occupied units that are renter-occupied, which is fine for a general audience.
6. **L9: close to an instruction-style takeaway.** "Renters check three things" is a "do this" list in descriptive form. Acceptable, but safer: "A sale letter usually names three things: the new owner, where rent goes, and who holds the deposit."
7. **L2 text vs say:** "the lease" on screen vs "your lease" spoken. No number differs; trivial.
8. **Opening (L1):** True and specific, about 3 seconds. It works. Optional stronger version: "Landlord sold your building? Your lease didn't end." That hits viewers who are in that situation right now.

No other numbers on screen differ from the narration. No price predictions or political opinion. The cover and title are paid off for fixed-term leases. Time words are fine apart from issue 3.
