# Research brief: How to choose a health insurance plan (The Guide, long-form, posts Wed Dec 16, 2026)

Written Oct 17, 2026. All figures phrased to hold on Dec 16, 2026. Education, not advice: no plan is recommended.

## Timely angle (on posting date)
- Dec 16, 2026 is inside ACA Marketplace open enrollment for 2027 coverage.
  - HealthCare.gov states: open enrollment Nov 1, 2026 - Jan 15, 2027. Enroll by Dec 15 for coverage starting Jan 1, 2027; enroll Dec 16 - Jan 15 for coverage starting Feb 1, 2027. (HealthCare.gov dates page as summarized in search results; HealthCare Insider 2027 deadlines FAQ.)
  - So on the posting day, the Jan 1 deadline on HealthCare.gov has just passed; Feb 1 coverage is still available through Jan 15.
  - State-run exchanges can set their own dates (e.g. California, New Jersey, New York, DC through Jan 31 per HealthCare Insider). Phrase as "some state-run exchanges have different dates".
  - The 2025 Marketplace Integrity rule would have ended open enrollment by Dec 31 starting with 2027 coverage; a federal district court vacated that provision in June 2026 (HFMA; Baldwin; SHVS litigation tracker July 24, 2026; Maryland Health Benefit Exchange 2027 OEP dates memo). Appeal status unclear -> do NOT make this a script claim beyond "check your state's deadline".
- Most employer open enrollments for Jan 1 plan years run in the fall, so by mid-December most are over. Outside open enrollment, a qualifying life event (marriage, birth/adoption, losing other coverage) opens a special enrollment window: generally at least 30 days for employer plans (HIPAA special enrollment, 29 CFR 2590.701-6), generally 60 days on the Marketplace (KFF; HealthCare.gov).

## 2027 rules and limits (labeled by year)
- ACA maximum out-of-pocket (in-network essential health benefits, non-grandfathered plans) for 2027: $12,000 self-only / $24,000 family. 2026: $10,600 / $21,200. About a 13.2% increase. Released by HHS/CMS Jan 29, 2026 (premium adjustment percentage guidance, 1.8916224814). Sources: NFP, Milliman, HUB International, Baldwin, Hylant, NIS Benefits (all citing CMS guidance).
- IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-24 (released May 29, 2026), 2027 HSA/HDHP figures:
  - HSA contribution limit: $4,500 self-only / $9,000 family (2026: $4,400 / $8,750). Catch-up age 55+: $1,000 (not indexed).
  - HDHP minimum deductible: $1,750 self-only / $3,500 family.
  - HDHP maximum out-of-pocket: $8,700 self-only / $17,400 family.
  - Sources: IFEBP (June 1, 2026), Lockton, Mercer, Lawley, Keiter CPA, 401k Specialist.
- Premiums do NOT count toward the out-of-pocket maximum (CMS/HealthCare.gov glossary; consistent across sources).
- One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025): starting Jan 1, 2026, bronze and catastrophic plans available on an Exchange are treated as HSA-compatible even if they don't meet the usual HDHP tests; IRS Notice 2026-5 (Dec 9, 2025) confirms, including off-Exchange versions. Telehealth safe harbor made permanent; certain direct primary care arrangements allowed. (KPMG, RSM, NFP, Foley summaries of Notice 2026-5.) THE SURPRISING FACT for many viewers.
- Marketplace metal tiers by actuarial value (average share of covered costs the plan pays for a standard population): Bronze 60%, Silver 70%, Gold 80%, Platinum 90%. Cost-sharing reductions (lower deductibles/copays) are only available on Silver plans, for eligible incomes (up to 250% of the federal poverty level). (Pennie; Maryland Health Connection; Anthem; HealthCare.gov metal categories.)
- ACA preventive services covered with no cost sharing in network (HealthCare.gov preventive care page). No Surprises Act (in effect since Jan 2022) bars most surprise out-of-network bills for emergency care and certain care at in-network facilities (CMS). Standard, stable rules.
- Plan types (HealthCare.gov glossary): HMO = usually in-network only, often needs referrals from a primary care doctor; PPO = can go out of network for more money, no referral usually; EPO = in-network only except emergencies, no referral usually; POS = in-between, referrals, some out-of-network coverage.
- Definitions: premium (monthly price), deductible (what you pay before the plan starts paying for most care), copay (flat fee), coinsurance (your % share after deductible), out-of-pocket max (the most you pay for covered in-network care in a plan year, excluding premiums).
- HSA: owned by the account holder, money rolls over year to year, contributions tax-deductible (or pre-tax via payroll), growth and qualified medical withdrawals tax-free (IRS Publication 969). Must be enrolled in an HSA-eligible plan to contribute.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL employer plans, single coverage, 2027, in-network, no employer HSA deposit)
- Plan A (low deductible): premium $150/month = $1,800/yr; deductible $500; then 20% coinsurance; out-of-pocket max $3,000.
- Plan B (HSA-eligible HDHP): premium $50/month = $600/yr; deductible $2,000 (above the 2027 $1,750 minimum); then 20%; out-of-pocket max $5,000 (under the 2027 $8,700 HDHP cap and $12,000 ACA cap).
- Premium gap: $1,200/yr in B's favor.
- Year 1, low use ($400 of non-preventive care billed at network rates):
  - A: 1,800 + 400 = $2,200. B: 600 + 400 = $1,000. B costs $1,200 less.
- Year 2, middle ($5,000 of care):
  - A: 500 deductible + 20% x 4,500 = 900 -> 1,400 out of pocket; total 1,800 + 1,400 = $3,200.
  - B: 2,000 deductible + 20% x 3,000 = 600 -> 2,600; total 600 + 2,600 = $3,200. Tie.
  - (With these made-up numbers the totals stay tied from $2,000 to $13,000 of care, because B's extra $1,500 deductible minus A's $300 coinsurance on that slice equals the $1,200 premium gap.)
- Year 3, high use ($40,000 surgery):
  - A: 500 + 20% x 39,500 = 8,400 -> capped at 3,000; total 1,800 + 3,000 = $4,800.
  - B: 2,000 + 20% x 38,000 = 9,600 -> capped at 5,000; total 600 + 5,000 = $5,600. A costs $800 less.
- Worst case formula: annual premiums + out-of-pocket max. A: 4,800; B: 5,600.
- Variation: if an employer deposited a hypothetical $500 into B's HSA, B's high-use year nets to $5,100 and the low-use year to $500 (illustration only).
- Tax note: HSA contributions reduce taxable income; a hypothetical $1,200 deposit at a 22% federal bracket saves $264 in federal income tax (1,200 x 0.22 = 264). Label hypothetical.

## Steps in order (for chapters)
1. Know your window and your options (employer open enrollment, Marketplace Nov 1-Jan 15 with Dec 15 for Jan 1, life events).
2. Learn the five numbers: premium, deductible, copay, coinsurance, out-of-pocket max (2027 caps).
3. Check the network: HMO/PPO/EPO/POS; your doctors, hospital, prescriptions (formulary).
4. Run the math: premiums + expected costs, and worst case = premiums + OOP max; three-year example.
5. HSAs and metal tiers: HSA limits 2027, bronze HSA-eligible since 2026, silver CSRs.
6. Common mistakes + recap checklist: choosing by premium only, forgetting premiums aren't in the OOP max, not checking drugs/doctors, missing deadlines, ignoring family deductible/embedded limits, out-of-network costs.

## Hook
"The cheapest health plan can cost you more." (8 words) Paid off by the worked example (low-premium plan costs $800 more in the high-use year, $1,200 less in the low-use year).
