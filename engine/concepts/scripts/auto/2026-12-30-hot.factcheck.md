# Fact-check: 2026-12-30-hot (Scam alert: hostage moving loads)
Checked Oct 31, 2026 for an evergreen post on Dec 30, 2026. The claims were checked with independent web searches; the writer's brief was used for leads only. The FMCSA PDF could not be fetched directly (proxy 403), so FMCSA figures rest on matching search excerpts.

## Verified (no change needed)
- Hostage-load complaints to DOT: 364 (2017), 409 (2018), 495 (2019), 899 (2020). Source: FMCSA spokesman Duane DeBruyne, via AARP, Aug 2021. The chart points, the "899" end label, the spoken rounding ("about 360" / "about 900") and the "federal data via AARP" attribution are all correct.
- OPYM 2024 (Apr 1-19, 2024): 62 investigations; 380 HHG complaints addressed = 252 non-hostage + 128 hostage. Source: FMCSA OPYM 2024 report. The 2023 operation addressed 205 hostage complaints.
- S.D. Ohio case (Shuklin/Verlan): scheme ran April 2013 to July 2018; "identified victims lost more than $2.4 million total" (USAO SDOH sentencing releases). Prosecutors say the companies refused to return goods until customers paid inflated prices.
- 49 U.S.C. 14915(a): a civil penalty of "not less than $10,000 for each violation" for holding a household goods shipment hostage.
- FMCSA red flags: cash or a large deposit; no on-site (in-home or virtual) survey; no "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move" booklet; check the USDOT number; file complaints with the NCCDB. The script gives no advice beyond FMCSA's own guidance and makes no price predictions or political statements. No time words go stale on Dec 30, 2026.

## Issues

### 1. cover (value) - misleading pairing (MUST FIX)
Problem: The cover reads "Held for Ransom" / "PAY" / "$2.4M". That implies $2.4M was paid as ransom, or that hostage loads overall cost $2.4M. The $2.4M is the total fraud loss of one racketeering ring. Prosecutors' figure covers the whole scheme: inflated cubic-foot pricing, fees and goods never returned. It is not ransom money specifically, and it is one case, not a national total.
Replacement (uses the strongest verified national figure, which line 2 pays off):
"cover": { "title": "Held for Ransom", "prop": "box", "label": "COMPLAINTS", "value": "899" }
(Alternative if the label must stay short: "label": "2020", "value": "899". Another option: "label": "FINE", "value": "$10K+", which line 7 pays off.)

### 2. lines[3] - "peaked in 2022" not verifiable (MUST FIX)
Problem: No search result confirms that FMCSA said hostage complaints peaked in 2022. Only the writer's notes make that claim. AARP shows 932 by Jul 25, 2021, so the series was still rising. The "but" also sets up a contrast the data can't support: 128 is the number of complaints one 3-week sweep addressed, not a yearly total. The stat label "Hostage complaints" with tone "down" implies 128 is the national count and that it fell.
Replacement:
- text: "And in 2024, a three-week federal sweep still took on 128 hostage complaints."
- say: "And in twenty twenty-four, a three-week federal sweep still took on a hundred twenty-eight hostage complaints."
- scene: { "t": "stat", "label": "Hostage cases in sweep", "value": "128", "tone": "flat", "sub": "FMCSA sweep, Apr 2024", "mood": "think" }
(If "flat" is not a valid tone, keep "down" only if the label clearly reads as a sweep count, as above.)

### 3. lines[4] - "ring of 13 movers" (FIX)
Problem: Sources disagree on the count. The DOJ release says "at least 12 affiliated moving companies", the DOJ case page and some news say 13, and other reports say 14. There were 12 defendants, so "13 movers" also reads as 13 people. The $2.4M is losses to "identified victims".
Replacement:
- text: "One ring running more than a dozen moving companies, from 2013 to 2018, cost victims over $2.4 million, per federal prosecutors."
- say: "One ring running more than a dozen moving companies, from twenty thirteen to twenty eighteen, cost victims over two point four million dollars, per federal prosecutors."
- scene unchanged (headline "$2.4M moving scam" is accurate).

### 4. lines[7] - penalty wording (FIX)
Problem: The statute sets a civil penalty, and only for someone "found" holding a shipment hostage. "Fine" usually means a criminal fine, and "carries" makes the penalty sound automatic.
Replacement:
- text: "Movers found holding goods hostage face a federal civil penalty of at least $10,000 per violation."
- say: "Movers found holding goods hostage face a federal civil penalty of at least ten thousand dollars per violation."
- scene: { "t": "stat", "label": "Civil penalty, min.", "value": "$10,000", "tone": "up", "sub": "49 U.S.C. 14915", "mood": "up" }

### 5. lines[5] - on-screen item differs from narration (minor)
Problem: The narration says "cash or a big deposit", but the list item reads "Cash deposit". FMCSA's red flag is cash OR a large deposit.
Replacement: "items": ["Cash or big deposit", "No home survey", "No rights booklet"]

### 6. post.ig - unverified peak claim and company count (MUST FIX, mirrors issues 2 and 3)
Replace:
"FMCSA says they peaked in 2022; its April 2024 sweep still addressed 128."
with:
"FMCSA's April 2024 enforcement sweep still addressed 128 hostage complaints."
Replace:
"Ohio federal prosecutors said one ring of 13 rogue movers (2013-2018) cost victims over $2.4 million."
with:
"Ohio federal prosecutors said one ring running more than a dozen rogue moving companies (2013-2018) cost identified victims over $2.4 million."
