# Research: How nail salons make money on thin margins

Note: no public company is a pure-play US nail salon chain, so there is no 10-K. Numbers come from the US Census Bureau (Service Annual Survey, via FRED) and BLS. Industry rules of thumb are labeled as such. Timely news angle: none found; video is evergreen.

## Data recency
- SAS was folded into the Census Annual Integrated Economic Survey (AIES; 2023 data Feb 2026, 2024 data Sept 3, 2026). 3 searches could not confirm AIES publishes NAICS 812113; script labels 2022 as latest in the Census Service Annual Survey and does not splice surveys.

## Framing
- Aggregate: employer salons kept about 22 cents per revenue dollar before income taxes in 2022 ((12,853-10,073)/12,853 = 21.6%). Not thin in aggregate; the $30 example is a hypothetical tighter single-salon case.

## Industry totals: US employer nail salons (NAICS 812113), Census Service Annual Survey (via FRED)
Covers nail salons with at least one paid employee (employer firms, all establishments). Millions of dollars, annual, not seasonally adjusted. 2022 is the latest year in this series (described as the highest on record).
- Revenue 2022: $12,853M (about $12.9B)  [FRED REVEF812113ALLEST]
- Expenses 2022: $10,073M (about $10.1B)  [FRED NSEAEEF32812113]; 2021 expenses $8,468M
- Expenses / revenue 2022 = 10,073 / 12,853 = 0.784 -> about 78 cents of every revenue dollar went to operating expenses. Revenue minus expenses = $2,780M (about 21.6% of revenue), before income taxes. (Aggregate for employer salons only; it is not a margin for any single salon, and for unincorporated businesses owner pay is not an expense.)

## Chartable series: employer nail salon revenue, Census SAS (via FRED REVEF812113ALLEST)
- 2018: $7,257M (single secondary confirmation; not charted)
- 2019: $8,200M -> $8.2B
- 2020: $6,408M -> $6.4B (pandemic dip; an earlier vintage reported about $6.3B, down ~24%, since revised)
- 2021: $10,889M -> $10.9B
- 2022: $12,853M -> $12.9B

## Labor (biggest cost)
- USED: BLS OEWS May 2025 (per fact-check): manicurists and pedicurists median $17.19/hr ($35,760/yr).
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook: manicurists and pedicurists median pay $34,660/yr, $16.66/hr, May 2024; 210,100 jobs in 2024; projected +7% 2024-2034.
- (Weakly sourced, secondary sites only: OEWS May 2025 median $17.19/hr, $35,760/yr, 152,770 wage-and-salary jobs. Not used.)

## Industry rules of thumb (NOT official data; label as rule of thumb)
- Service commission for nail techs commonly 40%-60% of service revenue, with 45%-50% the usual anchor (Zenoti; Mirelle Inspo commission benchmark).
- Pay models: hourly ($10-$18/hr + tips), commission (40-60%), or booth/chair rental ($200-$600/week flat) (Zenoti).
- Retail commission to techs typically 10%-15% of product sales (Zenoti).
- Profit margins quoted by industry advisory sites: roughly 10%-25% net, varying by size (dojobusiness, KwickOS). Vary widely; not official.
- NAILS Magazine Big Book average prices (survey year not confirmed): manicure $22, basic pedicure $35, full set of gels $51. Not used on screen as a dated fact.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL; stated assumptions)
- $30 manicure (assumed price).
- Tech commission 50% (rule of thumb, within 40-60%): $30 x 0.50 = $15.
- Supplies (polish, files, liners): $3 (assumption).
- Share of rent, utilities, insurance, software for that service slot: $8 (assumption).
- Left for the salon: 30 - 15 - 3 - 8 = $4 -> 4 / 30 = 13.3% (about 13%).
- One extra client in the same chair during hours already paid for: rent is fixed, so extra = 30 - 15 - 3 = $12.

## Surprising fact
- In 2022, US employer nail salons spent about 78 cents of every revenue dollar on operating expenses ($10.1B of $12.9B), per Census.
- Revenue grew from $6.4B in 2020 to $12.9B in 2022 (roughly doubled).
