# Research brief: How to get out of student loan debt: the options
Researched Nov 14, 2026 for posting Wed Jan 13, 2027. Evergreen; every number dated. Note: ed.gov / studentaid.gov fetches are blocked from this environment; rules confirmed via search snippets of official pages (ed.gov press releases, FSA Partners, NY Fed, CRS) plus reputable outlets (NPR, CNBC, ABC, NerdWallet, NASFAA, Kiplinger, The College Investor). Items marked FLAG need the fact-checker's attention.

## News angle
No single news event on the posting date. The angle: federal student loan rules changed more in 2025-2026 than in a decade (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025, which the Education Department calls the "Working Families Tax Cuts Act"): RAP launched July 1, 2026; SAVE is ending; older IDR plans closing; new borrowing caps. Plus the NY Fed's Q3 2026 data (released Nov 10, 2026): 10.9% of student debt balances 90+ days delinquent.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. Total student debt (NY Fed Household Debt and Credit report, Q3 2026, released Nov 10, 2026): $1.656 trillion (about $1.66T), up $5 billion in the quarter. Share of balances 90+ days delinquent or in default: 10.9% in Q3 2026, up from 10.6% in Q2 2026. (newyorkfed.org press release 2026/20261110; The College Investor; ABA Banking Journal Nov 2026)
   - Q3 2025: $1.65T; 9.4% seriously delinquent (NY Fed Nov 5, 2025).
   - Missed payments not reported from 2020Q2-2024Q4 (pandemic pause/on-ramp) began appearing on credit reports in 2025 (NY Fed).
2. Federal portfolio (FSA Data Center, 2026 updates): about 42.3-42.6 million recipients, about $1.7 trillion in federal student loans (FSA Partners electronic announcements June 23 and Sept 22, 2026). So the vast majority of student debt is federal.
3. Federal vs private: Federal loans (Direct Loans from the Education Department) come with income-driven plans, PSLF, deferment/forbearance, death and disability discharge. Private loans (banks, credit unions, online lenders) follow the lender's contract; none of the federal programs apply. Check which you have at studentaid.gov (federal) and your credit report (private shows up too).
4. Federal loan interest rates, loans first disbursed July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 (FSA GENERAL-26-33, June 4, 2026; Federal Register): undergrad Direct 6.52% (2025-26: 6.39%), grad unsubsidized 8.07%, PLUS 9.07%. Fixed for life of the loan.
5. Repayment plans after July 1, 2026 (OBBBA; ed.gov fact sheet; WTOP/Kiplinger/NPR June 2026):
   - New loans made on/after July 1, 2026: only two plans: Tiered Standard and RAP. Consolidating on/after July 1, 2026 also limits you to these two (The College Investor, TICAS).
   - Tiered Standard (new borrowers): fixed payment; term by total balance: under $25,000 = 10 years; $25,000-$50,000 = 15 years; $50,000-$100,000 = 20 years; over $100,000 = 25 years. Default plan if you don't choose. No forgiveness timeline.
   - RAP (Repayment Assistance Plan), launched July 1, 2026 (about 46,000 applications on day one, per Under Secretary Nicholas Kent): payment = a percentage of total AGI by bracket: AGI up to $10,000 -> $10/month flat; $10,001-$20,000 = 1%; $20,001-$30,000 = 2%; ... each $10,000 step adds 1 point; $90,001-$100,000 = 9%; over $100,000 = 10%. Annual amount / 12, minus $50 per dependent; minimum payment $10/month (no $0 payments, unlike older IDR plans). On-time payments: unpaid monthly interest is waived (not added to balance); if your payment reduces principal by less than $50, the government matches up to $50 of principal. Forgiveness after 360 qualifying payments (30 years). RAP counts for PSLF. (ed.gov fact sheet; NerdWallet; The College Investor; CNBC/NBC; Tate Law) FLAG: exact tier boundaries from secondary sources; confirm on studentaid.gov.
   - Parent PLUS loans are not eligible for RAP (NerdWallet, Phila.gov June 2026).
   - Existing borrowers (loans before July 1, 2026): can still use IBR (amended; partial financial hardship requirement dropped) or RAP. PAYE and ICR close; borrowers must leave by July 1, 2028 (sources say June 30/July 1, 2028) and are moved to IBR or RAP. Old standard 10-year plan stays for existing loans. Switching to RAP may bar returning to IBR/PAYE/ICR (FLAG: one source).
   - IBR payment: 10% of discretionary income (borrowed on/after July 1, 2014) or 15% (earlier), forgiveness after 20 or 25 years.
6. SAVE plan ending: Education Dept announced a settlement with Missouri in Dec 2025 to end SAVE (no new enrollments, deny pending applications, move borrowers to legal plans); 8th Circuit directed approval around March 9-10, 2026. About 7 to 7.5 million borrowers were in SAVE forbearance (interest accruing since Aug 1, 2025). Servicers sent notices starting July 1, 2026 with 90 days to choose a plan; most deadlines around Sept 30, 2026, but some servicers (Nelnet) sent notices in waves through end of 2026, so some deadlines may fall in early 2027. If you don't choose, you're placed in a standard plan. SAVE forbearance months generally don't count toward PSLF/IDR forgiveness. (NPR Dec 2025; AACOM; NASFAA; Inquirer Apr 2026; The College Investor; Nelnet/MOHELA) FLAG: deadline could fall near/after Jan 13, 2027 for some; script says "check your servicer notice".
7. Borrowing limits from July 1, 2026 (OBBBA; Stanford, NBC/CNBC, schools): Grad PLUS eliminated for new borrowers (grandfather for those enrolled with a loan as of June 30, 2026). Parent PLUS capped at $20,000 per student per year, $65,000 lifetime per student. Lifetime federal cap $257,500 (excluding Parent PLUS). (Grad annual caps $20,500 / professional $50,000 not used in script.)
8. PSLF: forgiveness of remaining Direct Loan balance after 120 qualifying monthly payments (10 years, need not be consecutive) while working full-time for a government organization or 501(c)(3) nonprofit, on a qualifying plan (IDR incl. RAP, or 10-year standard). PSLF forgiveness is federally tax-free. Through April 2026: about 1.254 million borrowers, about $93.4 billion forgiven (CRS LSB11472, Aug 2026; Brookings: 1.2M+, $90.6B by Jan 2026).
   - 2025-26 rule change: ED final rule (Oct 31, 2025) to exclude employers with a "substantial illegal purpose", effective July 1, 2026, was vacated by federal courts on June 30, 2026 (Orrick, DWT July 2026, CRS). ED dropped its appeals in Oct 2026 (NASFAA "Trump Administration Abandons PSLF Final Rule"; AACOM Nov 2, 2026); one states' appeal may technically remain on the docket. Net: employer rules unchanged.
9. Taxes: IDR forgiveness became federally taxable again starting Jan 1, 2026 (the 2021 ARPA exclusion expired Dec 31, 2025). PSLF stays tax-free. OBBBA made death and disability discharges permanently tax-free. (NASFAA Jan 2026; MEFA; Kiplinger)
10. Default: federal loans default after 270 days of missed payments. Consequences: whole balance due, credit damage, tax refund offset, wage garnishment (up to 15% of disposable pay, 30 days' notice), Social Security offset.
    - Status 2026: ED planned to restart garnishment notices Jan 2026 (NPR Dec 23, 2025), then on Jan 16, 2026 announced a delay of involuntary collections (administrative wage garnishment and Treasury offset) (ed.gov press release; CNBC, ABC, Axios Jan 16, 2026). As of Oct 2026 secondary sources say ED garnishment remains paused, no restart date; Treasury is taking over defaulted-loan collection under a March 2026 agreement; defaults still reported to credit bureaus. FLAG: status may change before Jan 13, 2027; script says "paused in January 2026... check current status", never says it is paused now.
    - Ways out: (a) Rehabilitation: 9 voluntary, on-time payments within 10 consecutive months, amount based on income; removes the default record from the credit report (late payments before stay); currently allowed once; OBBBA allows twice starting July 1, 2027 (NerdWallet, Tate Law, schools). (b) Consolidation into a Direct Consolidation Loan (with an IDR/RAP plan or 3 on-time payments first). (c) Pay in full.
11. Consolidation: Direct Consolidation Loan is free via studentaid.gov; new rate = weighted average of old rates, rounded up to nearest 1/8 of a percent; simplifies to one payment; it does NOT lower the rate. Consolidating after July 1, 2026 limits plans to RAP/Tiered Standard (FLAG check).
12. Refinancing: a private lender pays off loans with a new private loan, possibly at a lower rate. Refinancing federal loans is one-way: you lose IDR/RAP, PSLF, federal forbearance/deferment, and federal discharge options. Can make sense to compare for private loans.
13. Scams (FTC consumer.ftc.gov; FTC June 2025 alert): it's illegal for debt relief companies to charge before they help; never pay an upfront fee; never share your FSA ID; no one can promise fast forgiveness; everything the scammers sell (plan changes, consolidation, PSLF forms) is free at studentaid.gov or from your servicer. Report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
14. No prepayment penalty on federal or private student loans (Higher Education Opportunity Act 2008 for private; federal by law). Extra payments go to fees/interest first, then principal; tell the servicer which loan to apply extras to.

## Worked examples (our math)
A. Avalanche vs snowball (simulation, monthly interest, our math):
   - Loan A: federal $4,000 at 5.50%, minimum $43.41 (10-year amortization)
   - Loan B: federal $9,000 at 6.53%, minimum $102.33
   - Loan C: private $7,000 at 11.00%, minimum $96.43
   - Total $20,000; minimums $242.17; budget = minimums + $200 = $442.17/month (about $442).
   - Minimums only for 10 years: total interest about $9,060.
   - Avalanche (C 11% -> B -> A): debt-free in 53 months; total interest $3,327.38; first loan (private) gone month 27.
   - Snowball (A $4,000 -> C $7,000 -> B $9,000): debt-free in 54 months; total interest $3,839.57; first loan gone month 18.
   - Difference: $512.19 (about $510) less interest with avalanche, one month sooner. Snowball gets first win 9 months earlier.
B. RAP payment (our math, using brackets above): AGI $45,000 -> 4% -> $1,800/yr / 12 = $150/month. With one dependent: $150 - $50 = $100/month. At AGI $8,000: $10/month.
C. RAP principal match (illustrative): payment $150, interest due $160 -> $10 of unpaid interest waived; principal reduced $0 by borrower, so the government adds up to $50 toward principal (balance falls $50). Keep it qualitative-ish in script: "interest waived, principal drops at least $50 if on time".
D. Consolidation rate: $4,000 at 5.50% + $9,000 at 6.53% -> weighted average = (220 + 587.70)/13,000 = 6.213%, rounded up to nearest 1/8 = 6.25%. (our math)

## Surprising fact
More than one in ten student loan dollars (10.9% of balances) was 90+ days delinquent or in default in Q3 2026 (NY Fed). Second: under RAP, the smallest payment is $10, not $0. Third: refinancing federal loans is a one-way door.
