## Pick: November 2026 Consumer Confidence (Conference Board, released Nov 24, 2026)
Facts (Conference Board release via PR Newswire "US Consumer Confidence Surged in November", Nov 24, 2026; CNBC; FXStreet; Advisor Perspectives; TheStreet; Korea Times/AP-style wire; MLQ):
- Consumer Confidence Index (1985=100): 103.3 in November 2026, up 11.4 points from 91.9 in October (October revised from 91.7).
- Highest reading since January 2025 (stockanalysis, Advisor Perspectives, Quartz); beat every forecast in a survey of economists (TheStreet). Consensus differs by source (Dow Jones 92.2 per CNBC; ~92 per Anadolu) - do not use a consensus number.
- Present Situation Index: up 13.7 points to 132.8.
- Expectations Index (6-month outlook for income, business, jobs): up 9.9 points to 83.6. First reading above 80 this year; best since end of 2024 (Bloomberg, via search snippet). The Conference Board has said readings below 80 signal a recession ahead.
- Jobs "plentiful": 30.8% (from 26.2%). Jobs "hard to get": 18.4% (from 21.4%). (Korea Times; CNBC gap 12.4 points)
- Average 12-month inflation expectations: fell for a second straight month to 5.7%, still elevated (MLQ, CNBC).
- Conference Board senior economist Fergus McCormick: confidence rallied, "potentially due to lower uncertainty following the midterm elections"; respondents still expressed frustration with high prices and rising costs. (paraphrase; exact wording unverified)
- Survey cutoff: Nov 17 (preliminary, Nov 1-17 field period).
- Economists note confidence does not closely track actual spending (CNBC coverage).

Chartable series (Conference Board CCI, each month as revised in the following month's release):
Jun 2026 92.2 | Jul 90.2 | Aug 88.6 | Sep 84.2 (initially 81.9, lowest since 2014) | Oct 91.9 | Nov 103.3
(Sources: Conference Board monthly releases Jun-Nov 2026 via search snippets; CNBC Sept 29, 2026.)

What it means: Mood is the best in almost two years and the forward-looking gauge left the recession-warning zone, with better job views. But people still expect prices up 5.7% over the next year, and confidence is not the same as spending or income.

What to watch: December Conference Board release (Dec 29, 2026, last Tuesday), holiday spending data, whether inflation expectations keep falling.
