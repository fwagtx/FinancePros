# Research brief: What a credit score actually measures (Acorn Economics explainer)
Researched October 2, 2026. Posts November 30, 2026. Evergreen; every figure dated.

## Core facts
1. FICO Score factor weights (myFICO, current as of 2026; widely republished by Amex, Sallie Mae, banks):
   - Payment history 35%
   - Amounts owed 30% (includes credit utilization)
   - Length of credit history 15%
   - New credit 10%
   - Credit mix 10%
   Total 100%. myFICO notes weights are for the general population and vary by person.
2. Base FICO Score range: 300 to 850; higher means lower credit risk (myFICO "What is a FICO Score").
3. Score is calculated only from information in the credit report.
4. NOT in a FICO Score (myFICO "What's not in your credit score"; Chase; Experian): income/salary, occupation, title, employer, employment history,
   savings/checking balances, investments, where you live, age, race, marital status. Lenders may consider income/job separately.
   CFPB says the same in substance (its page was blocked to fetch; myFICO + Chase + Experian are the two-plus sources).
5. Latest average US FICO Score: 714. FICO Fall '26 Credit Insights report (released Aug 25, 2026): average "held steady at 714, unchanged since
   October 2025 but down one point from a year ago." Spring '26 report (Mar 24, 2026): dipped to 714. April 2025: 715 (FICO, Apr 16, 2025).
   48.1% of consumers with a FICO Score had 750+ (Fall '26 report).
6. Lenders use FICO in about 90% of US lending decisions (CNBC Select citing FICO).

## The surprise fact
Income counts for 0% of a FICO Score. A squirrel with a giant stash and a squirrel with none get scored only on how they handle borrowed acorns.

## Worked example (credit utilization, exact math)
Utilization = balance / credit limit (share of available credit in use).
$600 balance / $2,000 limit = 0.30 = 30%. (Hypothetical example.)
