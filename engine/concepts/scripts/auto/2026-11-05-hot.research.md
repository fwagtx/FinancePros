# Research brief: 2026-11-05-hot (Hot Take, Scam Alert)
Topic: fake inheritance and "unclaimed funds" letters vs. real state unclaimed-property programs.
Researched October 1, 2026. Posts November 5, 2026. Note: direct page fetches of .gov sites were blocked by the network proxy; facts below are confirmed through search results quoting the official pages (exact-phrase searches used for quotes).

## Timely angle
- July 9, 2026: FTC consumer alert "Unclaimed life insurance money? It's a scam" (consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/07/...). Local coverage July 2026: WFTV/WDBO (Orlando), Orange County FL newsroom, Denver7, DRG News (July 17, 2026), ConsumerAffairs.
- Earlier FTC alerts on the same scam: Aug 2022 ("Contacted about a long-lost relative's inheritance? Hold on a minute"), Aug 2023 ("Did you get a letter from a 'lawyer' about cashing in on someone else's life insurance policy?"), June 2024 ("Contacted about long-lost relative's life insurance policy or an inheritance? It's a scam"). It is a recurring scam.

## How the scam works (FTC, July 2026 and earlier alerts; USPIS)
- Official-looking letter from a supposed law firm: a deceased client with the same last name left an unclaimed life insurance policy / inheritance worth millions; no heirs found.
- Offers to split the money between the recipient, the "law firm" and one or more charities; 2022 version asks for secrecy and contact by email immediately.
- After contact: asks for personal/financial info (Social Security, bank account numbers) and "fees" for taxes, legal documents, wire transfers or bank clearance. The money never existed.
- USPIS: scammers say the inheritance is hard to access due to government regulations, taxes or bank restrictions; a second or third scammer may pose as a banker, lawyer or tax agent.

## Exact quotes (word-for-word, confirmed by exact-phrase search)
- USPIS (Inheritance Scams page, uspis.gov/news/scam-article/inheritance-scams): "Legitimate law firms or executors of wills do not request you to pay a fee to find out about your share of an estate."
- FTC consumer alert (Aug 2022 "Contacted about a long-lost relative's inheritance? Hold on a minute"; same line repeated in later alerts): "Never send money or information to a stranger who promises big rewards."
- FTC (paraphrase only, wording varies across reprints; NOT used as a quote): the letter is not from a lawyer, it's from a scammer.

## The real thing: state unclaimed-property programs
- NAUPA (National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators) = the unclaimed property programs of all 50 states, DC and Puerto Rico. Sites: unclaimed.org; MissingMoney.com (official NAUPA/NAST multi-state search).
- NAUPA FY2024 Annual Report (released Oct 29, 2024; FY = July 1, 2023 to June 30, 2024; latest available, no FY2025 report found as of Oct 2026):
  - $4.49 billion returned to rightful owners in FY2024.
  - Nearly $70 billion in unclaimed property owed to almost 33 million Americans; about 1 in 7 people have unclaimed property.
  - Average claim paid $2,080.00; median claim paid $144.30.
- Free: unclaimed.org "There is no fee to search for and claim your unclaimed property—if you use your official state program." MissingMoney.com "never charges a third-party fee." Private "finders" may charge a percentage.

## Chartable data series (NAUPA, total returned to owners, $ billions, by fiscal year)
- FY2019: $3.14B (NAUPA FY20 report context)
- FY2020: $2.87B (NAUPA FY20 Annual Report; NAST blog)
- FY2022: "more than $4 billion" (NAUPA, approximate)
- FY2023: "over $5 billion" (NAUPA, approximate; one source says $5.4B, unconfirmed)
- FY2024: $4.49B (NAUPA FY24 Annual Report)
Caveat: FY2021 not found and FY2022/23 only approximate, so the script uses stat scenes rather than a chart.
Alternate series (FTC Consumer Sentinel reported fraud losses): 2022 $8.8B, 2023 $10.0B, 2024 $12.5B, 2025 $15.9B (used in an earlier FinancePros short).

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- Median real claim FY2024: $144.30. Example: if a "processing fee" of $200 were charged to release a claim of that size, the owner would be $200 - $144.30 = $55.70 worse off. USPIS notes a finder's fee can exceed the amount claimed. Official state search and claim: $0.
- Average vs median: $2,080.00 / $144.30 = about 14.4x, so a few large claims pull the average up; the typical claim is small.

## Surprising fact
- The typical (median) real unclaimed-property claim paid in FY2024 was $144.30, not millions, while scam letters promise millions from a stranger.
