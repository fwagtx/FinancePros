# Fact-check: 2027-01-25-hot "Why is a hearing aid so expensive?" (posts Jan 25, 2027, 6 PM CT)

## Verified claims (independent web search)
- **Prescription pair average $3,432 (private pay, includes Costco), OTC pair average $502**: HearingTracker, "How much do hearing aids cost in 2026?" (Abram Bailey, AuD; published Jan 11, 2026, updated Jul 31, 2026). The figures come from its **2025** reader survey: 1,733 US responses, 879 US pair purchases with a reported price. Two independent clinic sites (audiologyassociates.org, calhearing.com) cite the same 2025 survey with the same figures. This is one authoritative survey with plain attribution, so the script now uses it.
- **Big Five (WS Audiology, Demant, GN, Starkey, Sonova) ~90% global / 84% US**: American Economic Liberties Project report (dated Oct 2025, file 20251003), quoted by The Capitol Forum in 2026. GMInsights separately puts the top five at about 87.5% of global revenue in 2025, which supports "about 90%". The estimate dates from **2025**, not 2026, so the script was fixed.
- **Original Medicare doesn't cover hearing aids or exams for fitting them**: Medicare.gov/coverage/hearing-aids. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer hearing benefits, and the post captions now say so.
- **FDA OTC rule**: the final rule was issued Aug 17, 2022 and took effect Oct 17, 2022. It covers adults 18+ with *perceived* mild-to-moderate loss (Federal Register 2022-17230; FDA final rule documents). Verified.
- **$199-$999 OTC launch range (old script)**: this was Walmart's launch range, as reported by Axios, NBC and The Hill on Oct 17, 2022. Other retailers' ranges (Walgreens $799, Hy-Vee $499.99-$999.99) came from the White House fact sheet. Crediting the range as a whole to "the White House" was loose, and the range itself was 2022 launch pricing. The script now uses the more recent $502 survey average instead.
- **Bundled services**: HearingTracker and the National Academies (2016) both describe prescription prices as typically bundling evaluation, fitting and follow-up care. The script now says "often bundled", which is accurate.
