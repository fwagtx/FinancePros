## Picked story: 39 years ago today, Black Monday. What it would look like on today's Dow.
Facts (1987):
- Monday, October 19, 1987: the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 508 points (508.32), or 22.6%, to 1,738.74. Largest one-day percentage drop in Dow history. S&P 500 fell 20.47%. (CNN Money Oct 19 2017; CNBC; Wikipedia "Black Monday (1987)"; RBC "crash of 1987 by the numbers")
- Pre-crash record close: 2,722.42 on August 25, 1987 (Christian Science Monitor, Aug 1989; Benzinga dates it Aug 26). By the Oct 19 close the Dow was ~36% below that peak (1,738.74 / 2,722.42 = -36.1%, computed).
- Year-end closes: Dec 31, 1987: 1,938.83; Dec 30, 1988: 2,168.57 (historical table, fedprimerate.com).
- Dow recovered its Black Monday losses (back above the Oct 16, 1987 close) on Jan 24, 1989 (~15 months) (CNN Money 2017 / RBC).
- New record close: 2,734.64 on Thursday, August 24, 1989, up 56.53 that day (Salina Journal Aug 25, 1989 via newspapers.com; CNN/RBC say "closed at 2,735 on Aug 24, 1989"). Crash (Oct 1987) to new high (Aug 1989) = about 22 months; peak to new peak about 2 years.
- No single agreed-upon cause; program/computerized trading was widely blamed at the time.
- Circuit breakers: first put in place after the 1987 crash (SEC/Investor.gov bulletin). Current market-wide rules (2012 revision): based on the S&P 500 vs the prior day's close: Level 1 -7% and Level 2 -13% halt all trading 15 minutes (if before 3:25 p.m. ET); Level 3 -20% halts trading for the rest of the day. Before 2012: 10/20/30% of the Dow. (investor.gov, sec.gov)
- So the S&P's 20.47% drop of Oct 19, 1987 would hit today's Level 3 threshold.

Facts (now):
- Friday, Oct 16, 2026 closes (AP via ABC News/Yahoo/KENS5): Dow 52,002.41 (-0.3%, +0.7% for the week); S&P 500 7,659.44 (-1.1%; -1.9% for week); Nasdaq 26,140.03 (-2.1%; -4.5% for week, worst week since June). Chipmakers led the selloff. (One PR Newswire commentary had different numbers; majority used.)
- Same 22.6% drop applied to 52,002.41 = 11,752.5 points (computed) -> "about 11,750 points".
- Early Monday Oct 19, 2026: stock futures rose led by a tech rebound (CNBC live blog: Nasdaq 100 futures +1.1%, S&P 500 futures +0.5%, Dow futures ~flat; AOL Sunday night: S&P futures +0.72%, Nasdaq 100 futures +1.2%). Futures are early indications and can change before the open; numbers differ by time, so say only "futures rose early Monday".
- Some commentary outlets floated "Black Monday" worries; a market commentator called them "overdone" (AOL) - weakly sourced, not used.
- Last week's Nasdaq loss over 5 days (4.5%) vs Dow's 1987 one-day loss (22.6%): about 5x (computed: 22.6/4.5 = 5.0).
