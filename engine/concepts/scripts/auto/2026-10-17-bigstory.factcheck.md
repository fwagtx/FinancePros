# Fact-check: 2026-10-17-bigstory (WTI above $100)

Checked Sat Oct 17, 2026, about 1 PM CT. Posts today at 4 PM CT. I read only the raw.json.

## Verified claims
- **WTI settle on Fri Oct 16, 2026: $101.41.** CNBC says WTI "rose 3.5% to settle at $101.41". EnergyNow/Reuters says it was up $3.44 (3.51%) from Thursday's $97.97, and Macrotrends also lists $101.41. **$101.71 is not supported.** I found no source for it, so keep $101.41.
- **Weekly gain "more than 10%": correct.** EnergyNow puts last Friday's close at $91.85, a gain of $9.56 (+10.41%) for the week. My own math: 101.41/91.85 − 1 = 10.41%. One caveat: midday CNBC pieces said "8% for the week so far". Those are intraday figures. The settled figure is 10.4%.
- **"At least 9 ships attacked near Hormuz since Oct 11": correct.** CNBC (Oct 16) says "at least nine commercial ships have come under attack near Hormuz since Sunday", and Oct 11, 2026 was a Sunday.
- **Kpler ~70%: correct.** CNBC (Oct 16) says shipments were 9.4M bpd in the week ended Wednesday, about 70% of the pre-war average, "according to data provided by... Kpler". Note that Kpler uses its own baseline (about 13.4M bpd implied), which differs from the EIA's 20M bpd. This is fine as long as the figure stays attributed to Kpler, which it is.
- **WTI Oct 2025 average $60.26: supported, but it is the World Bank series.** FIPI cites the World Bank for $60.26. The EIA/FRED Cushing spot average is $60.89. Both round to "about $60", so the spoken line is fine. The on-screen $60.26 should be labeled as World Bank (fix below).
- **AAA September 2026 average $4.33, a September record: correct.** AAA's Oct 1 release says $4.33 is "50 cents higher than the previous September record of $3.83 set in 2023."
- **"Today" check: OK.** No line says "today" or "this week" about Friday's close. Every reference is dated "October 16" or "Oct 16".
- **Math and lengths: OK.** All fields are within limits. Compare values are $60.26 (6 characters) and $101.41 (7). The tiktok text is 147 characters, post.title is 46, and the longest board line is 20.
- **Other:** There are no keep:true lines. Every on-screen number matches the spoken one. There is no advice and no political opinion.

## Issues and fixes

1. **Line 3: unattributed and oversimplified cause (REQUIRED).** "The trigger:" presents the ship attacks as the only cause. CNBC also points to a large US inventory draw tied to Gulf Coast shutdowns after Hurricane Isaias. Attribute the cause and drop "the trigger."
   - text → `A big driver, per CNBC: at least 9 ships attacked near the Strait of Hormuz since October 11.`
   - say → `A big driver, per CNBC? At least nine ships have been attacked near the Strait of Hormuz since October eleventh.`

2. **Post copy: same causal framing (REQUIRED).**
   - ig: replace `up more than 10% for the week, after at least 9 ships were attacked near the Strait of Hormuz since Oct 11.` with `up more than 10% for the week. CNBC tied the jump to attacks on at least 9 ships near the Strait of Hormuz since Oct 11 and a big drop in US crude stockpiles.`
   - yt_desc: replace `up more than 10% for the week, after at least nine ships were attacked near the Strait of Hormuz and flows fell` with `up more than 10% for the week. CNBC tied the move to attacks on at least nine ships near the Strait of Hormuz, while Kpler data showed flows fell`
   - tiktok: replace `after Hormuz ship attacks.` with `amid Hormuz ship attacks.` (the result is 147 characters, within the 150 limit).

3. **Line 5: label the data source for $60.26 (recommended).**
   - left.sub `monthly average` → `World Bank avg` (14 characters).

4. **Line 8: general claim with no source (recommended).** "Pump prices tend to follow crude weeks later" is reasonable as a general pattern, but "weeks later" is too precise to state as fact. Change it to:
   - text → `Pump prices usually follow crude with a lag, so the number to watch is whether oil stays above $100.`
   - say → `Pump prices usually follow crude with a lag, so the number to watch is whether oil stays above a hundred dollars.`

5. **Line 6: optional context.** The prior September record could go in the sub field, for example `Prior record: $3.83` (19 characters, which is over the 18 limit). Better to leave the sub as is and add it to the spoken line: `...a record for that month, beating three eighty-three in 2023, says triple A.`

6. **Hook: OK.** "US oil is back above $100 a barrel." is true and takes under 3 seconds to say. "Back" is accurate, because WTI was above $100 earlier in 2026 (about $114 on Apr 6). Optional stronger version: `US oil just jumped back above $100 a barrel.`

7. **Line 2: wording nit.** "Closed" is acceptable. "Settled" is more precise and matches the post copy: `It settled at $101.41 on October 16...`
