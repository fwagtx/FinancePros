# Research: How airlines price seats (why the same flight costs different amounts)

## Mechanism
- Revenue management: each cabin's seats are split into fare classes / "fare buckets", identified by single-letter booking codes (e.g., Y, B, M, H). The legacy system allows up to 26 letter codes. When the cheapest bucket sells out, it closes and the next, pricier one opens. Airlines reopen or close buckets based on demand forecasts, competitor fares, and booking pace. (AltexSoft; OAG; Wikipedia "Airfare")
- So two people in identical economy seats can pay very different fares depending on when they booked and which bucket was open.

## Company numbers: Delta Air Lines 2025 Form 10-K (fiscal 2025) and Q4 2025 release (Jan 2026)
- 2025 ticket revenue: main cabin $23.391B; premium products $22.097B.
- Q4 2025: premium $5.70B (+9%) exceeded main cabin $5.62B (-7%) for the first time.
- Remuneration from American Express in 2025: $8.2B (Delta expects ~$10B over the next few years).
- Premium + diverse revenue streams: 60% of operating revenue in 2025 (57% in 2024).

## Chartable real series: BTS average US domestic itinerary fare, inflation-adjusted to 2025 dollars (BTS release, Apr 2026)
- 1995: $618 (start of records)
- 2000: $634 (highest year)
- 2022: $416 (recent high)
- 2024: $394
- 2025: $387
- Unadjusted 2025: $387 (2024: $384).

## Worked example (hypothetical 150-seat economy cabin)
- 50 seats at $150 = $7,500; 50 at $250 = $12,500; 50 at $400 = $20,000. Total = $40,000.
- If all 150 sold at the lowest $150: 150 x 150 = $22,500.
- Difference: $17,500 more from the same seats.

## Surprising fact
- Delta's credit card deal with American Express paid it $8.2B in 2025.
