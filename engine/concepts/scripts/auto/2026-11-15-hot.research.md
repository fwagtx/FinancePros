# Research: The latte factor - real math or myth?

## Origin
- "Latte factor" popularized by David Bach in "The Automatic Millionaire" (2004); updated in "The Latte Factor" (2019, with John David Mann). Bach says it's a metaphor for any small, regular spending you could redirect to savings, not literally coffee. (Wikipedia; CNBC Nov 26, 2019)
- Critics (e.g., Ramit Sethi, "I Will Teach You to Be Rich") argue the "big wins" (housing, cars, income) matter far more. (CNBC Nov 26, 2019)

## Worked example (hypothetical, exact math)
- $5 a day x 365 days = $1,825 a year.
- If invested each year at a hypothetical 7% annual return for 30 years (end-of-year contributions, annual compounding):
  FV = 1,825 x ((1.07^30 - 1) / 0.07) = 1,825 x 94.4608 = $172,391 (about $172,000).
- Total actually put in: 1,825 x 30 = $54,750. The rest (~$117,641) is growth - IF the return happens (not guaranteed; hypothetical).

## Big-ticket comparison (BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey, 2024, released Dec 2025)
- Average annual household spending in 2024: $78,535.
- Housing: $26,266 (33.4% of spending). Transportation: 17.0%. Food: 12.9%.
- A 10% cut in average housing cost = $2,627 a year, more than the $1,825 latte habit.

## Chartable real series: BLS average price, coffee, 100% ground roast, per pound, US city average (APU0000717311), January values
- Jan 2021: $4.59
- Jan 2023: $6.36
- Jan 2024: $6.12
- Jan 2025: $7.02
- Jan 2026: $9.366 (record high for the series back to 1980, per StoneX/BLS)
- Latest: Aug 2026 $9.299 (BLS, released Sept 11, 2026)

## Surprising fact
- Even the home-brew alternative got pricier: ground coffee roughly doubled from $4.59/lb (Jan 2021) to $9.37/lb (Jan 2026).

## Verdict for the video
- The math is real (compounding works), but it's small next to big fixed costs. Both matter; big costs move more.
