# 2026-12-09-lf-guide: research brief
Topic: Your end-of-year money checklist: taxes, benefits, accounts and goals
Posts Wednesday, December 9, 2026, 4 PM Central. Researched Saturday, October 10, 2026. Deadlines framed as December 31, 2026 and April 15, 2027.

## Hook / news angle
- No single breaking story. The strongest true hook is about use-it-or-lose-it money:
  EBRI "Vital Statistics on Flexible Spending Accounts, 2022: Forfeitures on the Rise" (published May 2024; 3.2 million accounts):
  roughly HALF of FSA account holders forfeited money to their employer in 2022; average forfeiture $441 (up from $339 in 2019);
  total about $1.4 billion across accounts reviewed (vs $1.08 billion in 2019). Younger workers more likely to forfeit.
  About one-third of accounts in the database were pure use-it-or-lose-it. (Latest EBRI figures I can confirm are 2022.)
- Hook line: "In 2022, about half of FSA holders forfeited their own money." (11 words). Payoff: chapter on FSA rules with the math.
- Surprising fact: the money is gone on a fixed date, and the rules differ by plan (carryover vs grace period vs neither).
- Note: IRS has NOT announced 2027 limits as of Oct 10, 2026 (projections only: Milliman ~$25,000-$25,500). Do not cite 2027 limits.

## Official 2026 numbers
Retirement (IRS Notice 2025-67, IRS news release Nov 13, 2025):
- 401(k)/403(b)/governmental 457/TSP employee deferral: $24,500 (2025: $23,500).
- Catch-up age 50+: $8,000 (total $32,500).
- Ages 60, 61, 62, 63 "super catch-up": $11,250 (total $35,750).
- IRA limit: $7,500 (2025: $7,000); IRA catch-up 50+: $1,100 (total $8,600).
- Roth catch-up rule (SECURE 2.0 sec. 603): starting Jan 1, 2026, workers whose prior-year (2025) FICA/Social Security wages from that employer exceeded $150,000 must make catch-up contributions as Roth (after-tax). Final regulations Sept 15, 2025 (most provisions apply 2027; 2026 is good-faith compliance, but the requirement itself is in effect for 2026). Plans without a Roth option may not allow those workers catch-ups. Sources: CAPTRUST, Fidelity sponsor guide.
- Deadlines: employee 401(k) deferrals must come through payroll by Dec 31 (no retroactive deferrals; Invesco, PensionBee). IRA and HSA contributions for tax year 2026 can be made until April 15, 2027 (filing deadline).

HSA (Rev. Proc. 2025-19, May 1, 2025):
- 2026: $4,400 self-only, $8,750 family; catch-up age 55+: $1,000.
- HDHP 2026 minimum deductible $1,700 self / $3,400 family.

Health FSA (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, Oct 9, 2025):
- 2026 salary reduction limit: $3,400 (up $100).
- Carryover max: $680 from a 2026 plan year into 2027 (20% of limit; $660 from 2025 into 2026). Employer may set lower.
- Grace period alternative: up to 2.5 months after plan year end (March 15 for a calendar-year plan).
- A plan may offer carryover OR grace period, not both, or neither (IRS IR-2015-126; Pub 969).
Dependent care FSA:
- OBBBA (signed July 4, 2025) raised the limit to $7,500 ($3,750 married filing separately) for tax years beginning 2026; it had been $5,000 since 1986. Employers not required to adopt the higher limit (Hill Ward Henderson advisory).

RMDs:
- Annual RMD due Dec 31; first RMD can wait until April 1 of the year after turning 73 (then two in one year). NBC/CNBC on IRS notice; Kiplinger.
- Missed RMD excise tax 25% (SECURE 2.0, down from 50%), 10% if corrected within the correction window (Morningstar, CalCPA, Bricker). Form 5329.

Capital gains / loss harvesting (Rev. Proc. 2025-32):
- 2026 0% long-term capital gains rate: taxable income up to $49,450 single, $98,900 married filing jointly. 15% up to $545,500 single. (Kiplinger, Experian, Trustate.)
- Wash sale (IRC 1091): loss disallowed if substantially identical security bought within 30 days before or after sale (61-day window); disallowed loss added to basis of replacement shares. Applies in taxable accounts.
- Net capital loss deductible against ordinary income up to $3,000/yr ($1,500 MFS); excess carries forward indefinitely (IRC 1211(b), IRS Topic 409).

Gifts (Rev. Proc. 2025-32): annual gift exclusion 2026: $19,000 per recipient (same as 2025). Married couples splitting: $38,000.

Standard deduction 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32): $16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly (head of household $24,150). Additional age-65/blind: $2,050 single, $1,650 per married spouse.
OBBBA 2026 changes relevant at year-end:
- New non-itemizer charitable deduction starting 2026: up to $1,000 single / $2,000 joint for cash gifts to qualifying public charities (Franklin Templeton, Eaton Vance, DAFgiving360).
- Itemizers: 0.5% of AGI floor on charitable deductions starting 2026 (AGI $200,000 -> first $1,000 not deductible). 37% bracket itemized benefit capped at 35%. 60% AGI cash limit made permanent.
- SALT cap: $40,400 for 2026 (was $40,000 in 2025), rises 1%/yr through 2029, phases down above $505,000 MAGI (not below $10,000); reverts to $10,000 in 2030.
- Senior deduction: up to $6,000 per person 65+, 2025-2028, phases out above $75,000 MAGI single / $150,000 joint.
- Trump Accounts: contributions allowed starting July 4, 2026; up to $5,000/yr from individuals/employers (employer up to $2,500 excluded from employee income); $1,000 federal pilot deposit for U.S.-citizen children born Jan 1, 2025-Dec 31, 2028; elect via Form 4547. IRS: more than 4 million children signed up, more than 1 million with pilot elections, as of March 31, 2026.

Accounts:
- Free credit reports weekly from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion at AnnualCreditReport.com, made permanent (FTC, NH Banking Dept, Nasdaq, Sept 2023). Checking your own report does not affect your score (FTC).
- Beneficiary designations on 401(k)s, IRAs, life insurance generally pass outside a will (general rule; describe, don't overclaim).

## Worked examples (hypothetical, labeled)
1. FSA leftover: elected $3,400 for 2026, spent $2,500 by mid-December -> $900 left.
   - Carryover plan: $680 carries into 2027, $900 - $680 = $220 forfeited.
   - Grace-period plan: until March 15, 2027 to incur expenses for the full $900.
   - Neither: all $900 forfeited at plan year end.
2. 401(k) max at age 61: $24,500 + $11,250 = $35,750. Age 55: $24,500 + $8,000 = $32,500.
3. 0% gains room: single, $54,100 wages, standard deduction $16,100 -> taxable $38,000. Room under $49,450 = $11,450 of long-term gains taxed at 0% federally (state tax may apply).
4. Loss harvesting: $2,000 realized gain, $7,000 realized loss -> net loss $5,000; $3,000 deducted against ordinary income in 2026; $2,000 carries forward to 2027.
5. Charitable (non-itemizer, 2026): single gives $1,000 cash to a qualifying charity -> $1,000 deduction; in the 22% bracket that's $220 less federal tax (1,000 x 0.22).
