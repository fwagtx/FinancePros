# Research brief: Scam alert - fake IRS calls, texts and letters (posts Jan 27, 2027)

Angle: tax season (filing season opens late January) is peak time for IRS impersonation. Evergreen; no "this week" news peg used.

## How the real IRS contacts you (IRS.gov guidance, current as of 2026)
- The IRS "normally initiates contact with taxpayers through mail delivered by the United States Postal Service." IRS employees may call or visit to discuss audits or collections, but not without first trying to notify by mail. (IRS: "Avoid scams: Know the facts on how the IRS contacts taxpayers")
- The IRS and its private collection agencies will never call to demand immediate payment using a specific method such as a prepaid debit card, gift card or wire transfer. The IRS never accepts gift cards as payment. (IRS Tax Tip 2025-06; IRS "how the IRS contacts taxpayers")
- The IRS will not threaten to bring in police or have you arrested for not paying. (IRS)
- The IRS does not initiate contact by email, text messages or social media to request personal or financial information. (IRS phishing page, irs.gov/phishing)
- Federal agencies never request payment by wire, crypto or gift card (AARP; consistent with FTC guidance).

## How to verify
- Log into your IRS Online Account at IRS.gov to see balances and notices.
- Call the IRS using the phone number printed on the official notice / IRS.gov, never a number from a call or text.

## How to report
- Fake IRS emails: forward to phishing@irs.gov (with headers). Texts: send details to phishing@irs.gov (subject "Text"), and forward the text to 7726 (SPAM) so the carrier can block it. (irs.gov/phishing)
- Phone impersonation: TIGTA, 800-366-4484 / tigta.gov.
- All fraud: FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

## IRS "Dirty Dozen" 2026 (IR-2026-30, released March 5, 2026, "National Slam the Scam Day")
- Includes IRS impersonation by email and text (phishing/smishing), and AI-enabled phone impersonation (robocalls, spoofed caller ID); also misleading social media tax advice, ghost preparers, fake charities, offer-in-compromise mills, identity theft targeting IRS online accounts, fabricated wage/withholding claims, spear-phishing of tax pros. New in 2026: abusive undistributed long-term capital gains claims (Form 2439). (IRS; Journal of Accountancy, Mar 2026; Accounting Today)

## Other numbers
- Imposter scams 2025: about $3.5 billion reported lost, up nearly 20%; nearly 1 in 3 fraud reports were imposter scams (FTC press release, June 2026; BleepingComputer, Bitdefender).
- Investment scams 2025: about $7.9 billion, nearly half of losses (FTC testimony, Mar 2026).
- Government-impersonation-specific losses: reported figures in coverage were inconsistent (about $789M for 2024 per FTC; 2025 numbers unclear) - NOT used in script.

## Worked example (our math)
Growth 2022 to 2025: $15.9B / $8.8B = 1.807 -> about 81% more in three years.

## Surprising fact
The IRS never takes gift cards as payment, and its first contact is normally a paper letter, yet scammers now use AI robocalls and spoofed caller ID that can show "IRS" on your phone (Dirty Dozen 2026).
