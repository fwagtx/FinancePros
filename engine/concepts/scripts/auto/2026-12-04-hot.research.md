# 2026-12-04-hot research brief: lending money to family that never gets repaid
Researched Oct 5, 2026. Education, not legal or tax advice.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. Unpaid loans are common
   - LendingTree 2025 Friends and Money Report (QuestionPro online survey, 2,000 US consumers 18-79, July 14-15, 2025): 77% have lent money to a friend; of those, 32% did NOT get their money back the last time; 19% said it hurt the friendship. 36% have lost a friendship over money. (Note: this stat is about friends, not family specifically.)
   - Bankrate 2025 Financial Taboos Survey: ~70% of US adults have lent money or footed a group expense expecting repayment; 55% of them had at least one negative consequence: lost money 44%, damaged relationship 26%, lower credit score 7%.
   - Bankrate Dec 2021 survey (friends and family assistance): 38% lost money, 23% damaged relationship.
   - LendingTree (older survey): 31% say a loved one owes them money; median $400.
2. Loan vs gift (IRS Topic 453, Pub. 550): to deduct a bad debt you must show you intended at the time to make a loan, not a gift. If you lend to a relative or friend with the understanding they may not repay, it is a gift, not a loan, and not deductible.
3. Forgiving a loan = a gift. 2026 annual gift tax exclusion: $19,000 per recipient (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32; Kiplinger, OSU Farm Office). Gifts above that to one person in a year require Form 709 (gift tax return); couples can split for $38,000.
4. Nonbusiness bad debt: must be TOTALLY worthless (no partial deduction); deductible only in the year it becomes worthless; must show reasonable collection steps; reported as a SHORT-TERM CAPITAL LOSS on Form 8949 Part I with a detailed statement (debt description, amount, due date, debtor name and family relationship, collection efforts, why worthless). (IRS Topic 453; Pub 550.) IRS scrutinizes family loans.
5. Capital loss limit (IRS Topic 409): net capital loss offsets up to $3,000 of other income per year ($1,500 married filing separately); excess carries forward.
6. 2026 brackets unchanged: 10/12/22/24/32/35/37% (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, Oct 9, 2025). 22% single: taxable income over $50,400.
7. Below-market loans (IRC 7872): loans charging less than the Applicable Federal Rate (published monthly by IRS) can create imputed interest income for the lender. $10,000 de minimis exception for gift loans between individuals (not used for income-producing assets). $10,000-$100,000: imputed interest capped at borrower's net investment income; zero if that is $1,000 or less. October 2026 short-term AFR: 3.23% annual (Rev. Rul. 2026-19) -- changes monthly, not used on screen.
8. Small claims court limits vary by state: about $2,500 (Kentucky) to $25,000 (Tennessee, Delaware) per secondary 2026 compilations (not used with numbers on screen).

## Worked example (hypothetical)
$5,000 documented loan to a relative becomes totally worthless; lender has no other capital gains; 22% bracket.
- Year 1: deduct $3,000 against ordinary income -> $3,000 x 22% = $660 saved.
- Year 2: $2,000 carryforward (assuming no gains, same bracket) -> $2,000 x 22% = $440.
- Total $1,100 over two years. (Note: the full $5,000 x 22% = $1,100 in one year would need $2,000+ of capital gains to offset.)

## Surprising fact
The IRS treats a "loan" you never expected back as a gift: no deduction. And even a real bad debt is only a capital loss, capped at $3,000 a year against income.
