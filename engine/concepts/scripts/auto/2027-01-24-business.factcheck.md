# Fact-check: 2027-01-24-business (Affirm merchant fees)

Firecrawl was out of credits, so I checked everything with WebSearch. The search snippets quote Affirm's 10-K filings and shareholder letters. sec.gov and finviz could not be fetched directly.

## Verified facts
- FY2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) merchant network revenue was $1,149,932K, about $1.15B, up 30% from $882,658K (FY26 10-K and Q4 FY26 shareholder letter). Correct.
- FY2026 GMV was $50.2B, up 37% (Q4 FY26 results coverage). Correct.
- Merchant network revenue as a share of GMV was 2.3% in FY26, down from 2.4% (FY26 10-K MD&A). Our math gives 1,149.9 / 50,200 = 2.29%. Correct.
- Chart points against the 10-Ks: FY21 $379.6M ($379,551K), FY22 $458.5M ($458,511K), FY23 $507.6M ($507,600K), FY24 $674.6M ($674,607K), FY25 $882.7M ($882,658K), FY26 $1,149.9M. All correct, and the points are evenly spaced one year apart.
- 10-K wording: "we generally earn larger merchant fees on 0% APR financing products and financial products with longer term lengths." The script's "generally bigger on 0% APR" is accurate. "Lower on interest-bearing ones" is the implied contrast, not a direct quote, and the "generally" hedge covers it. The Affirm shareholder letter adds that about 95% of 0% APR monthly-installment GMV at integrated merchants is merchant-funded. This supports the hook's "Often, the store pays for it."
- Merchant value claims (bigger orders, conversion, new shoppers) appear on Affirm's business pages: a 60% AOV lift claim, conversion lift via Stripe, and "reach new and repeat users." The script attributes them to Affirm ("Affirm says"). OK.
- Worked example: 2.3% x $2,000 = $46, and $2,000 - $46 = $1,954. The math is correct.
- Staleness: FY26 is still Affirm's latest full fiscal year on Jan 24, 2027. Fiscal Q1 FY27 is out, and the Q2 report is expected in early February. Nothing stale, and there are no relative time words ("this year", "recently").
- No advice, no price predictions, no political opinion. The cover "Who Pays Affirm? 2.3%" is paid off by lines 1-3 and 8.
