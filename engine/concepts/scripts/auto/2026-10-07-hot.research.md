# Research brief: Why is a bag of coffee so expensive at the grocery store? (2026-10-07-hot)

Researched Wednesday, September 30, 2026. Posts Wednesday, October 7, 2026. Direct fetches of BLS/FRED are blocked from this environment, so every number below was confirmed through search results quoting the official BLS series, cross-checked across outlets.

## Timely angle
- The latest BLS average price (August 2026, released September 11, 2026) puts ground coffee at $9.299 a pound. It is still the latest figure on the posting date, because September 2026 CPI data comes out October 14, 2026.
- Store prices are still near record highs even though arabica bean futures fell from their 2025 peaks. That gap is the hook: "why is it still so expensive?"

## Key facts and numbers (with dates)
- BLS average price, coffee, 100% ground roast, all sizes, per pound, U.S. city average (series APU0000717311). It covers ground, regular-caffeine coffee sold in cans or plastic containers:
  - August 2026: $9.299 (July 2026: $9.317, down 0.2%). Source: BLS via FRED/usinflationcalculator; Economy Insights.
  - August 2025: $8.872, a record at the time. August 2024: $6.31. Source: NewsNation/BLS ("record $8.87 per pound in August, up from $6.31 a year earlier").
  - All-time record: $9.72 in April 2026, the highest since BLS began the series in 1980. March 2026 was $9.60. Source: Daily Coffee News, May 15, 2026 (citing BLS).
- CPI coffee index: retail coffee prices were 6.1% higher in August 2026 than in August 2025. Nonalcoholic beverages were up 3.7%. Source: USDA ERS Food Price Outlook.
- Annual averages: $6.16 in 2023, $6.322 in 2024, $5.89 in 2022, $3.14 in 1980. Source: usinflationcalculator (BLS).
- More than 99% of the coffee Americans drink is imported. Hawaii and Puerto Rico supply less than 1%. Source: National Coffee Association (aboutcoffee.org); USDA.
- Brazil grows about 35% of the world's coffee: 63 million 60-kg bags in 2025/26. Source: USDA FAS "Coffee: World Markets and Trade" (via Visual Capitalist).
- Brazil's drought and erratic weather cut the 2025/26 arabica crop. Conab forecast arabica down 12.4% to 34.7 million bags. Source: DTN, Nasdaq/Barchart.
- ICE arabica futures topped $4 a pound in 2025: a record near $4.30 in February 2025 and about $4.38 in October 2025, depending on the source. Source: Nasdaq/Barchart, Trading Economics. On September 30, 2026 they were about $2.95/lb (Barchart); that number is volatile and NOT used in the script.
- Tariffs: a 50% U.S. tariff on Brazilian goods, coffee included, took effect August 6, 2025 (10% baseline plus a 40% extra). On November 20, 2025, an executive order removed the 40% tariff on Brazilian coffee, beef and other farm goods, retroactive to November 13, 2025. Sources: Royal Coffee, Algrano, Daily Coffee News (Nov 21, 2025), Fortune, CBS News, Supply Chain Dive.
- Lag: retail coffee prices trail the commodity market, often by months. Roasters lock in beans ahead of time with forward contracts and hedges, so costs locked in near the 2024-2025 peak are still reaching shelves. Sources: StoneX insight "Retail Prices Stay Elevated as Futures Fall"; Bean Box.
- Note: BLS published no October 2025 data because of the federal government shutdown, which leaves a gap in the monthly series.

## Worked examples
- Two-year change: ($9.299 - $6.31) / $6.31 = $2.989 / $6.31 = 0.4737, so about 47% higher in August 2026 than in August 2024.
- A 12-ounce bag at the August 2026 average: 12/16 = 0.75 lb; 0.75 x $9.299 = $6.974, about $6.97.

## The surprising fact
- The United States grows less than 1% of the coffee it drinks, so almost every bag depends on weather, trade policy and prices overseas. And shelf prices can stay high for months after bean prices fall, because roasters bought their beans ahead of time.
