# 2027-01-11-hot research brief
Topic: Money myth: "Paying off a loan early always boosts your credit score." Posts Mon Jan 11, 2027 (evergreen, no news peg).
Angle: how it works (education, not advice). Distinct from the Jan 8 explainer ("credit score isn't wealth"): no score-range or average-score material reused.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. FICO score factor weights (myFICO "What's in your credit score?", page modified Oct 12, 2026):
   payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%.
   "Amounts owed refers to how much debt you carry in total and how much of your available credit you are using."
2. THE SURPRISE (myFICO FAQ "Can paying off installment loans cause a FICO Score to drop?", modified Jul 7, 2025), exact quote:
   "analysis of credit data shows that having a low installment loan balance to loan amount ratio is even less risky than having
   no active installment loans at all. As a result, paying off the last of your active installment loans can result in a loss of points."
   Also: FICO "weigh[s] the amounts paid down and balances of ... installment loans (such as auto or student loans) against the original
   loan amounts"; paying down the balance "may have a positive impact"; and "even after a consumer has paid off their installment debt(s),
   it is still possible to have a very high FICO Score, by actively and responsibly managing other types of accounts."
3. Experian ("Why Did My Credit Score Drop When I Paid Off a Loan?", May 11, 2019): "If the loan you paid off was your only installment
   account, you might lose some points because you no longer have a mix of different types of open accounts." Experian describes any drop
   as likely temporary and paying off a loan as generally not having much impact either way.
4. Experian (same page): "If you repaid the loan in full and never missed a payment, the credit bureaus will keep the account on your
   credit report for up to 10 years after the account is closed."
5. Equifax ("Why your credit scores may drop after paying off debt"): paying off your only installment credit could reduce credit mix
   (10% of FICO score); if the closed account was your oldest, it could affect length of credit history; the drop is unlikely to be
   permanent; lenders report on a delay (improvements often seen 30 to 45 days after payoff).
6. Revolving vs installment: utilization (balance / credit limit on cards) sits in "amounts owed" (30%). Experian says many scores use
   the most recently reported utilization, so lowering card balances can help fairly quickly; Experian suggests keeping utilization
   under 30%. Equifax: paying off a card AND closing it can raise utilization (lost limit). So paying down revolving balances typically
   helps; paying off the last installment loan can cause a small, temporary dip.

## Worked example (OUR MATH, hypothetical, simple-interest loan, no prepayment fee)
$10,000 auto loan, 7.00% APR, 60 months. Monthly rate r = 0.07/12 = 0.0058333.
Payment = P*r / (1 - (1+r)^-60) = 10,000 * 0.0058333 / (1 - 1.0058333^-60) = $198.01.
Total paid = 198.01 * 60 = $11,880.60, so total interest = $1,880.60 (about $1,881).
Balance after each year: yr1 $8,269.02, yr2 $6,412.91, yr3 $4,422.62, yr4 $2,288.45, yr5 $0.
Interest paid in first 24 months = $1,165.20. If the $6,412.91 balance is paid off at month 24, the remaining scheduled interest
skipped = 198.01*36 - 6,412.91 = $715.52 (about $716). At month 36: balance $4,422.62, interest skipped $329.67.
Caveats: some loans carry prepayment penalties or precomputed interest; then savings differ. The 7% APR is an illustrative assumption.

## Uncertain / not used
- Size of the dip: no official number; forum anecdotes (myFICO forums) report roughly 5 to 20+ points. Not stated in script.
- Whether closed loans still count toward "credit mix" is debated on forums; myFICO's official explanation centers on the
  installment balance-to-loan-amount ratio (no active installment loan). Script says "can cost some points" only.
- Different score models (VantageScore, other FICO versions) may treat payoff differently.
