## Chosen story: Bank of America owes $1.2B for failing at its anti-money-laundering reporting job
Announced Tuesday, October 13, 2026 by the U.S. Treasury (FinCEN, OCC, OFAC).

### Facts
- FinCEN assessed a $1.2 billion civil penalty against Bank of America (Oct 13, 2026). (FinCEN release; Treasury press release sb0657; CNBC, CNN, ABC/AP)
- OCC civil penalty: $750 million, the largest ever issued by the OCC; credited against (counted toward) the FinCEN penalty, not added on top. (CNBC; CNN; Doctor of Credit; NBC)
- OFAC: separate $11.6 million settlement over 475 apparent sanctions violations. (CNN/CNBC)
- Total about $1.21 billion. (UPI; CU Today)
- Treasury: the bank missed reporting deadlines on (failed to report or reported late) more than $5 billion in suspicious transactions; more than $1 billion implicated national-security-related and other priority illicit-finance risks, including Chinese money-laundering networks and southwest-border illicit finance. (Treasury release; CNBC; IBTimes; Daily Hodl)
- Treasury said the bank relied heavily on tips from law enforcement and other banks to spot suspicious activity. (Treasury release, via coverage)
- Bank of America admitted it willfully violated the Bank Secrecy Act (failing to maintain a compliant AML program, do required due diligence on foreign correspondent accounts, and file suspicious activity reports). (FinCEN resolution, via CNBC/NBC)
- Must hire a third party for a "lookback" to identify and report suspicious transactions that went undetected. (Treasury/consent order coverage)
- Ranking: third-largest FinCEN penalty in history, behind Binance (Nov 2023, $3.4B) and TD Bank (Oct 2024, $1.3B, then the largest against a depository institution). (Newsweek; Daily Caller; FinCEN TD Bank release)
- No accompanying Justice Department action (unlike Binance and TD Bank). (Newsweek summary)
- BofA: Q3 2026 results include a $1.2 billion non-tax-deductible charge, reducing net income by approximately 17 cents per share. Q3 results due ~6:45 AM ET Oct 14, call 8:30 AM ET. (CNBC; BofA newsroom)
- Bank had been under a December 2024 regulatory (OCC) order; regulators said problems persisted. (NBC/coverage)
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the biggest institutions are not above the law (exact wording varies by outlet; NOT used as a quote).
- Caveat: findings are compliance-system failures; they do not establish that every flagged transaction was criminal.

### Context: what the reporting rules are (Bank Secrecy Act)
- Suspicious Activity Report (SAR): banks must file with FinCEN when they suspect possible crimes/money laundering; bank-level floor generally $5,000 when a suspect is identified. Filed confidentially. (SanctionsScanner, CertFuel; 31 CFR 1020.320)
- Currency Transaction Report (CTR): banks must file for cash transactions of more than $10,000 in one business day (aggregated per person), whether or not anything is suspicious; due within 15 days. Threshold unchanged since 1972. (Verafin, Sardine, CertFuel; 31 CFR 1010.311)
- Splitting cash to dodge the $10,000 report ("structuring") is itself illegal / triggers a SAR.
- STREAMLINE Act (introduced Oct 21, 2025) would raise CTR to $30,000; not enacted per latest sources found (Aug-Sep 2026). So $10,000 is current.

### Chartable series
- FinCEN largest penalties: Binance 2023 $3.4B; TD Bank 2024 $1.3B; Bank of America 2026 $1.2B (only 3 points; use timeline, not chart).

### What it means for a normal person
- The bank pays the penalty from its own earnings (charge to Q3). The rules behind it are the same ones behind routine reports on your own cash: move more than $10,000 in cash in a day and the bank files a CTR automatically; reports are paperwork, not accusations.
