1. **Line 21: Nvidia CEO exit wrongly timed and loaded.** "Investors were also digesting a leadership shock... sudden exit" suggests it happened this week. Huang resigned on Thu Nov 12, and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO.
   Fixed text/say: "It was also Nvidia's first report since longtime CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12th. Finance chief Colette Kress is interim CEO." News source changed to "CNBC, Nov 12, 2026".
2. **Line 31: unverifiable quote card.** The exact wording "further action to raise the target range for the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate" could not be confirmed. Reports paraphrase it as "almost all participants judged that raising the target range further would likely be appropriate."
   Fixed: the quote scene is replaced with a board ("Almost all:" / "more hikes likely"), and the line now reads: "The minutes say almost all officials judged that raising rates further would likely be appropriate."
   Line 32 now reads: "In plain English: under their main forecast, almost every official at the table expects more rate hikes." Its board is "Translation:" / "rates may rise".
3. **Line 35: quote not exact.** Only the fragment "a material source of inflation risk" is confirmed as a direct quote, with "many officials/participants" outside the quote marks. The quote card now shows just that fragment. The line now says "...many officials called the AI buildout a material source of inflation risk."
4. **Line 37: wrong claim.** The script said "Fed staff ... flagged a risk in how some AI companies finance each other." The minutes say "Most participants observed that AI-related debt had grown rapidly and that much of this growth had taken place through complex and opaque financing structures." So it was participants, not staff, and it was about debt, not companies financing each other.
   Fixed: "Most officials also noted that AI-related debt has grown fast, often through complex and opaque financing. That's a risk if the payoff disappoints." Board: "AI debt:" / "a new Fed worry".
5. **Line 43: misleading "first time since 2007."** The 10-year traded above 5% intraday in October 2023 (5.02%), and Euronews notes the Sept 14, 2026 touch was the "highest since October 2023". The 2007 claim holds only for **closes**: none from 2008 to 2025.
   Fixed: "In September, the 10-year closed above 5% for the first time since 2007." Headline: "10-yr closes over 5%". The Fortune Sept 22 source could not be found, so the source is now Trading Economics, Sept 15, 2026.
6. **Line 49: small timeline error.** The rate hit 7.40% on Oct 8 and held at 7.40% on Oct 15. The four weekly declines came after that (Oct 22 to Nov 12).
   Fixed: "It hit 7.40% on October 8th, held there a week, then fell four weeks in a row, down to 7.11%." Timeline label changed to "7.40% high".
7. **Lines 17-18: Nvidia consensus.** The reported consensus was $109.15B (others: $108.7-109.3B), and "LSEG poll" could not be confirmed.
   Fixed: the line says "roughly $109.2 billion", the on-screen value is "$109.2B" with sub "Analyst avg", and the beat is now "more than $6.5 billion" ($115.8B - $109.15B = $6.65B).
8. **Line 20: "slipped" understated the drop.** Wednesday's drop was 2.9%. The line now says "fell almost 3% on Wednesday". Thursday's early gain then fall is confirmed: up as much as ~4.7% early, then closed down 2.7-3.3% at $145.98.
9. **Line 27: Fed chart label.** The 2026 point is the current rate, not a year-end value. The line now says "...since 2021, plus where it is now...".
10. **Line 29: inflation data could read as stale.** The October CPI (released Nov 10) had no annual rate because of the shutdown data gap. That leaves September's 3.7% as the latest yearly reading. The line now says "In September, the latest yearly reading, prices were up 3.7%..."

12. **Line 89: faulty cause.** "Bond yields stayed near 5%, so the 30-year mortgage rate jumped" gives a flat yield as the cause of a jump. It now reads "With bond yields near 5%, the 30-year mortgage rate jumped to 7.28%..."
13. **Line 67: Walmart refunds wording.** Walmart had received "substantially all" of about $2.9B (all but ~$100M) and was already cutting prices on ~11,000 items. The line now says "...it had received nearly all of about $2.9 billion in refunds, and was using them to cut prices."
