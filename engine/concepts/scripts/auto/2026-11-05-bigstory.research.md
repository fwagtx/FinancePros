## The story: mortgage rates fall for a 3rd week, but you still pay more than a year ago
Facts (Freddie Mac PMMS release, Thursday Nov 5, 2026, 12:00 ET):
- 30-year fixed averaged 7.18% as of Nov 5, 2026, down from 7.24% last week. A year ago: 6.22%.
- 15-year fixed averaged 6.57%, down from 6.60% last week. A year ago: 5.50%.
- Third consecutive weekly decline (release headline: "Mortgage Rates Decline for Third Consecutive Week to 7.18%").
- Quote, Sam Khater, Freddie Mac chief economist: "Mortgage rates continued their downward trajectory this week, improving the environment for prospective homebuyers against a backdrop of strong economic growth."
- PMMS = conventional, conforming, fully amortizing purchase loans, 20% down, excellent credit.

Context (AP, Nov 5, 2026):
- Rates had been rising since late February, when the U.S. war with Iran began, pushing up oil and inflation; plus bond-market volatility.
- 30-year average reached 7.4% early last month (early October 2026), its highest level in nearly three years.
- In late February the 30-year briefly dipped to 5.98%, its lowest since late 2022.
- AP: the rise since then has added roughly $300 to $350 to the monthly cost on a $400,000 home loan.
- 10-year Treasury yield: 4.84% at midday Thursday (lowest since early September); surged above 5.30% in early October (highest since 2002); 3.97% before the war.
- Home sales stalled; existing-home sales essentially flat last year at a 30-year low.
- Rates generally follow the 10-year Treasury yield.

Our math (principal + interest, $400,000 30-year loan):
- 7.18%: about $2,710/month ($2,709.74)
- 6.22%: about $2,455/month ($2,455.07)
- 5.98%: about $2,393/month
- 7.4%: about $2,770/month
- 7.18% vs 6.22%: about $255/month more, about $3,056/year more.

## What it means for a normal person
- Falling three weeks in a row is real relief from the October peak, but the typical rate is still about a point above a year ago, which on a $400,000 loan is roughly $255 more a month (our math).
- Your actual rate depends on credit, down payment, and loan type; the survey assumes 20% down and excellent credit.
