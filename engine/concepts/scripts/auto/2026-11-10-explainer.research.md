# Research brief: How the IRS's yearly limit changes work: 401(k), IRA and tax brackets (posts Nov 10, 2026)

## Status note (as of Oct 1, 2026)
- The IRS had NOT yet announced 2027 retirement limits (usually early-mid November; 2026 limits came Nov 13, 2025) or 2027 tax brackets (Rev. Proc. usually Oct/Nov; 2026's was Rev. Proc. 2025-32, Oct 2025). Projections (Milliman: 401(k) $25,500 in 2027) are NOT official. Script therefore uses official 2026 figures and explains the mechanism; viewers are pointed to IRS.gov for 2027.

## Official 2026 figures (IRS)
- 401(k)/403(b)/most 457 elective deferral limit: $24,500 (2025: $23,500). Catch-up 50+: $8,000 (2025: $7,500). Ages 60-63 catch-up (SECURE 2.0): $11,250 -> $35,750 total.
- IRA limit: $7,500 (2025: $7,000); IRA catch-up 50+: $1,100 (now inflation-indexed).
- Standard deduction 2026: $16,100 single / $32,200 married filing jointly (Rev. Proc. 2025-32).
- Tax brackets are adjusted for inflation each year (chained CPI) so the same real income isn't pushed into higher brackets ("bracket creep"); retirement limits are indexed and rounded down (401(k) in $500 steps), which is why some years don't change.

## Worked example
Maxing the 2026 401(k) limit with 26 biweekly paychecks: 24,500 / 26 = $942.31 per paycheck.
