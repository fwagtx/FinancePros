# Research brief: How student debt got so big (Explained, long-form, posts Jan 18, 2027)

Researched Nov 19, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its date. Note: newyorkfed.org, collegeboard.org and FRED could not be fetched directly from this session (network); figures were confirmed through web search of the official releases and multiple outlets quoting them.

## News angle (dated, not "this week")
- NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report, Q3 2026 (released Nov 10, 2026): student loans $1.656 trillion (~$1.66T), up $5B on the quarter and only $3B over the year (smallest rise of any category). Credit cards $1.285T (~$1.29T, record). Total household debt $19.1T (+$334B). Share of student loan balances 90+ days delinquent: 10.9% (up from 10.6% in Q2 2026). Flow into serious delinquency 7.72% (vs 14.26% in Q3 2025) - a different measure, do not mix.
- So as of Q3 2026, Americans owe more on student loans ($1.66T) than on credit cards ($1.29T). NY Fed: student debt passed credit cards in Q2 2010 and became the second-largest household debt after mortgages.

## Chartable series 1: student debt, NY Fed (Q4 of each year, trillions)
2012 0.966 | 2014 1.16 | 2016 1.31 | 2018 1.46 | 2020 1.56 | 2022 1.60 | 2024 1.62 ; latest Q3 2026 1.656
(NY Fed press releases: Q4 2012 "$966 billion"; Feb 2015; Feb 2017; Feb 2019 "$1.46 trillion"; Feb 2021 "$1.56 trillion"; Feb 2023 "$1.60 trillion"; Q4 2024 report "$1.62 trillion".)
- Our math: 2012 to Q3 2026: $0.966T -> $1.66T = about 1.7x. Fast growth 2012-2020 (+$0.6T in 8 years), nearly flat 2020-2026 (pause, then forgiveness programs, slower borrowing).
- NY Fed staff report: student debt grew ~14% a year on average in its growth period; before 2008 it was the smallest household debt category.
- Fed Board G.19 (different, broader measure incl. all holders): student loans owned and securitized $481.0B in Q1 2006 -> $1.84T in Dec 2025 (SLOASM 1,841,667.69 million). Our math: about 3.8x. Do NOT mix with NY Fed numbers.

- CPI: College Board's constant dollars are CPI-adjusted, i.e. this already removes general inflation. (Direct BLS CPI index values could not be confirmed this session - not used on screen.)

## Federal portfolio (Federal Student Aid data center)
- March 31, 2026 (FSA EA, June 23, 2026): $1.7239 trillion outstanding federal loans, 42.6 million recipients. Our math: average ~ $40,467 per borrower (~$40,500).
- June 30, 2026 (FSA EA, Sept 22, 2026): ~42.3 million recipients, >$1.7T; about 9.3 million borrowers in default holding $234B (~14% of the $1.64T ED-managed portfolio) (Newsweek, ACA International, College Investor citing FSA).
- Federal total > NY Fed total because measures differ (NY Fed = credit-report data on individuals; FSA = federal portfolio incl. Parent PLUS etc.).

## Policy history (what each does; neutral)
- 1958 National Defense Education Act: first federal student loans (NDSL, later Perkins).
- Nov 8, 1965: LBJ signs the Higher Education Act. Title IV created the Guaranteed Student Loan program: private lenders' loans guaranteed by the government (LBJ Library; Bush Center).
- Bankruptcy: before 1976 student loans discharged like other unsecured debt; 1976 law required "undue hardship" during first 5 years of repayment (later 7); 1998 Higher Education Amendments removed the time limit for government loans; 2005 BAPCPA extended the exception to private student loans (CRS RS22699; Saving for College; Tate Law).
- Deficit Reduction Act of 2005 (Higher Education Reconciliation Act): created Grad PLUS, letting grad students borrow up to cost of attendance minus other aid; effective with the 2006-07 year (July 1, 2006) (Third Way memo; ED GEN-06-02). (Exact first-availability date: high confidence but not quoted by a primary source in search.)
- Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act of 2010 (SAFRA), signed March 30, 2010: from July 1, 2010, all new federal loans are Direct Loans from the Education Department; FFEL (bank-made, government-guaranteed) ended (CRS R41127).
- Payment pause: March 2020 (first pause); interest 0% through Aug 2023; interest resumed Sept 1, 2023; payments due from October 2023 (ED, via Axios/ABC, 2023). Roughly 3.5 years.
- SAVE plan (2023 income-driven plan): blocked in court; Education Department-Missouri settlement (Dec 2025) to end it; Eighth Circuit judgment March 9, 2026 directing approval; district court judgment March 10, 2026. Borrowers being moved to other plans through late 2026 (notices from July 1, 2026; 90 days to choose). Nov 18, 2026: judge denied preliminary injunction in Havens v. Dept of Education (transition continues). Exact vacatur wording: somewhat uncertain - say "ended in court in March 2026".
- One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025), effective July 1, 2026 for new borrowers:
  - Grad PLUS ends for new borrowers (3-year/program-completion carve-out for those already borrowing).
  - Graduate: $20,500/yr, $100,000 aggregate; professional: $50,000/yr, $200,000 aggregate.
  - Parent PLUS: $20,000/yr and $65,000 total per dependent student.
  - Lifetime federal limit $257,500 (excluding Parent PLUS).
  - Undergrad limits unchanged: dependent $31,000 aggregate, independent $57,500.
  - Repayment: loans first disbursed on/after July 1, 2026 get only the Tiered Standard Plan or the new Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP). RAP: payment 1%-10% of AGI, $10/month minimum, unpaid monthly interest waived for on-time payments, remaining balance forgiven after 360 qualifying payments (30 years). RAP live July 1, 2026. Other legacy IDR plans close by July 1, 2028; IBR continues for older loans.
  (Harvard SFS, Iowa State, UIC, Saving for College, ed.gov fact sheet, College Investor.)

## Interest rates 2026-27 (FSA EA June 4, 2026; fixed for life of loan; loans first disbursed July 1, 2026-June 30, 2027)
- Undergrad Direct Sub/Unsub 6.52% (2025-26: 6.39%), grad unsub 8.07%, PLUS 9.07%. Set from May 12, 2026 10-year Treasury auction (4.468%) + 2.05 add-on for undergrad.

## Worked example (our math, standard amortization M = P*r/(1-(1+r)^-n))
- $30,000 at 6.52% fixed, 10 years (120 payments), r = 0.0652/12 = 0.0054333
- M = $340.95/month; total paid $40,913.92 (~$40,914); interest $10,913.92 (~$10,914) i.e. about 36% extra.
- First month's interest: 30,000 x 0.0652 / 12 = $163.00 (about half the first payment).
- Same loan over 20 years: $224.03/month, interest $23,766 (more than double the 10-year interest).
- (Federal loans technically use simple daily interest; amortization formula is the standard approximation.)

## Surprising facts
1. Student debt ($1.66T) is bigger than all US credit card debt ($1.29T), Q3 2026, NY Fed.
2. Since 2010 the federal government itself is the lender for new federal loans.
3. Public 4-year sticker price about doubled after inflation 1995-96 to 2025-26, but fell 7% after inflation in the last decade - while the debt pile barely grew 2022-2026.
