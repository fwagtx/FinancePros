# Research brief: Broker fees, paying someone to find your apartment
Explainer short. Posts Thu Jan 14, 2027, 12:00 PM CT. Researched Sun Nov 15, 2026. Evergreen: every number is dated.

## Timely angle
No breaking news needed. Hook is evergreen: New York City's old norm of tenant-paid broker fees up to 15% of a year's rent, and the rule change ("whoever hires the broker pays") that NYC (June 11, 2025) and Massachusetts (Aug 1, 2025) adopted. StreetEasy and city data now cover the first 6-12 months after the change.

## Core definitions and rules (confirmed)
- Broker fee: a one-time commission paid to a real estate agent/broker for a rental. In NYC before June 11, 2025, tenants were commonly charged it even when the landlord hired the broker.
- Typical size in NYC (pre-FARE): one month's rent up to 15% of annual rent (ABC7 NY, Dwell, Council Member Abreu/THE CITY explainer, June 2024). StreetEasy uses "typically about 12% of annual rent" in its methodology (StreetEasy, Dec 9, 2024 and Dec 18, 2025). NYC law did not cap fees before or after.
- NYC FARE Act (Fairness in Apartment Rental Expenses Act) = Local Law 119 of 2024. Passed by City Council Nov 13, 2024. Took effect June 11, 2025 (NYC DCWP FAQ).
  - A broker who represents the landlord (including one listing with the landlord's permission) cannot charge the tenant a broker fee.
  - No one can require a tenant to hire a broker to rent an apartment.
  - Tenants may still choose to hire their own broker and pay that broker.
  - All fees tenants must pay must be disclosed in listings and in an itemized written disclosure signed before the lease.
  - Enforced by NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP); violators face civil penalties and may owe restitution. Report via 311.
  - A federal judge denied REBNY's request for an injunction, so the law took effect (CBS NY / ABC7, June 2025).
- Massachusetts (statewide, includes Boston): starting Aug 1, 2025, renters can't be required to pay a broker's fee unless they hired the broker themselves; whoever hires the broker pays (Boston.gov Office of Housing Stability; Mass.gov FAQ). Passed as part of the FY26 state budget signed July 4, 2025 (JD Supra/Mondaq legal summaries). It is a state law, NOT a Boston city ordinance. Overcharged tenants may recover damages under Ch. 93A (legal summaries). NBC10 Boston (2026) reported some listings still appear to demand tenant-paid fees a year later.

## Key numbers (dated)
- StreetEasy, Dec 18, 2025 (six-month review):
  - FARE Act associated with ~1.1% higher average asking rent on broker-represented units. Net effective average asking rent Nov 2025: $3,559 vs. estimated $3,521 without the law, a $38/month difference.
  - Renters save an average $5,454 at lease signing in exchange.
  - Upfront costs on fee listings averaged $13,076 in Jan-May 2025 (first month + deposit + 12% fee).
  - 38% of surveyed renters (Sept 10-22, 2025, n=773) reported a rights violation July-Sept 2025, most often a fee for a broker they didn't hire.
  - Had landlords passed the full fee (~12% of annual rent) into rent, average asking rent would have been $3,948, 11% higher.
- StreetEasy May 2026 report (via CRE Daily, June 2026): rent effect still ~1.1% (~$46/month); average upfront savings $5,862; citywide median asking rent $4,199 in May 2026, +7.3% y/y. (Secondary reporting; not used on screen.)
- Openigloo survey (via CRE Daily/Real Deal): share of renters paying broker fees fell from 31% before the law to 15% after. (Survey data; not used on screen.)
- Enforcement, first year (The Real Deal, June 10, 2026, citing THE CITY / DCWP data): 2,000+ complaints and inquiries since June 2025; 74 summonses alleging 100 violations; ~$27,000 in penalties; ~$15,000 in broker-fee refunds to renters.
- StreetEasy, Dec 9, 2024: FARE Act projected to cut upfront cost on fee listings 41.8%, from about $12,951 to $7,537. 46.8% of NYC rentals charged a broker fee (Jan-Sept 2024, per StreetEasy pre-law piece).

## Worked example ("our math")
Illustrative rent: $3,500/month (close to StreetEasy's $3,559 Nov 2025 average for broker-represented units).
- Annual rent: $3,500 x 12 = $42,000
- 12% fee: $42,000 x 0.12 = $5,040
- 15% fee: $42,000 x 0.15 = $6,300
- One month's fee: $3,500
- Upfront with 12% fee: $3,500 (first month) + $3,500 (deposit) + $5,040 = $12,040; without the fee: $7,000.
- StreetEasy trade-off (their numbers): $38/month higher rent x 12 = $456 a year (our math) vs. $5,454 saved at signing.

## Surprising fact
In NYC, tenants routinely paid a fee to a broker they never hired: the landlord's agent. StreetEasy estimates the law's rent effect at ~$38/month, far less than the ~12% fee would be if fully baked into rent ($3,948 vs $3,559).

## Neutrality notes
Present it as a rule change in who pays. Critics (REBNY, some landlords) argued costs would shift into rent; StreetEasy data estimates a 1.1% rent effect. No position taken.
