# Research brief: The 25x rule: where the retirement 'magic number' comes from
Video posts Tuesday, January 5, 2027 (written November 6, 2026). Evergreen; every number dated. Education only, not advice.

## Timely angle
No hard news peg needed. Soft peg: Morningstar publishes its safe-withdrawal-rate estimate each late fall/December (latest confirmed as of Nov 2026: 3.9%, December 2025 report for 2026 retirees). A new estimate may appear in Dec 2026 before posting; script dates the figure as "December 2025 report" so it stays true.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. The math: 25x = 1 / 0.04. If you withdraw 4% of a portfolio in year one, the portfolio must be 25 times that withdrawal. (Arithmetic.)
2. Origin: William P. Bengen (financial planner), "Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data," Journal of Financial Planning, October 1994. Tested rolling 30-year retirements using US historical returns from 1926, with a 50/50 stocks/bonds (large-cap stocks / intermediate Treasuries) portfolio, first-year withdrawal then raised with inflation each year. (Wikipedia "4% rule"; Bankrate; Moneywise; Yahoo Finance.)
3. Worst case: a retiree starting October 1968 could sustain about 4.15% for 30 years; Bengen rounded down to 4% (Bankrate, Money.com, Wikipedia; some outlets say ~4.2%).
4. Trinity study: Cooley, Hubbard & Walz (Trinity University, San Antonio), "Retirement Savings: Choosing a Withdrawal Rate That Is Sustainable," AAII Journal, Feb 1998; US data 1926-1995; found 4% inflation-adjusted withdrawals over 30 years succeeded in the large majority (~95%) of historical periods for stock-heavy mixes; ignored taxes and fees. Popularized the "4% rule". (Wikipedia "Trinity study"; Retirement Researcher.)
5. Bengen update: book "A Richer Retirement: Supercharging the 4% Rule to Spend More and Enjoy More," published August 2025; raises his worst-case 30-year rate to 4.7% using a more diversified portfolio (adds small/mid-cap and international stocks). Bengen calls 4.7% a worst-case figure. (Money.com, Bankrate, Morningstar.com.au, Boldin, Fa-mag.) Our math: 1 / 0.047 = about 21.3x.
6. Morningstar (forward-looking, 30 years, 90% probability of success, fixed inflation-adjusted withdrawals, balanced portfolio): 3.9% in its December 2025 report for 2026 retirees, up from 3.7% the year before (Kiplinger; ThinkAdvisor Dec 3, 2025; Motley Fool Dec 10, 2025; Boldin). 30-year to 40-year horizon cuts it to ~3.2% (secondary coverage, not used on screen).
7. Social Security: average retired-worker benefit estimated at $2,071/month in January 2026 after the 2.8% COLA (up from $2,015) per SSA COLA fact sheet as reported by Axios (Oct 24, 2025) and PlanAdviser. Our math: $2,071 x 12 = $24,852/yr, about $25,000.

## Worked example (our math)
- Spend $40,000/yr: $40,000 x 25 = $1,000,000 (equivalently $40,000 / 0.04).
- Subtract Social Security first: average $2,071/mo (Jan 2026) x 12 = $24,852, call it about $25,000.
- Gap: $40,000 - $25,000 = $15,000; $15,000 x 25 = $375,000.
- Same idea: the multiple applies only to spending your portfolio must cover.

## The surprising fact
The "magic number" isn't magic: it's just 1 divided by 4%, from one planner's 1994 backtest whose worst start date (Oct 1968) survived about 4.15%. And Social Security can shrink the target a lot: $1,000,000 becomes $375,000 in our example.

## Caveats
- Rule of thumb from US history, not a guarantee; future returns may differ.
- Ignores taxes and investment fees (Trinity explicitly excluded them).
- Sequence-of-returns risk: bad markets in the first years hurt most (1968 retirees).
- Assumes 30 years; longer retirements need a lower rate (bigger multiple).
- Education only; no advice.
