# Research brief: How car leasing companies make money on residual value
Slot: 2026-12-20-business (posts Sun Dec 20, 2026, 2 PM CT; evergreen, no "this week" claims)
Written Oct 21, 2026.

## How a lease makes money (the model)
- A lessor (a captive like Ford Credit, GM Financial, Toyota Financial Services, or a bank like Ally) buys the car and owns it. At signing it sets a **residual value**: its estimate of what the car will be worth at lease end. The customer pays for the depreciation (price minus residual) plus a finance charge (**money factor**), plus fees.
- **Money factor x 2,400 ~ APR** (approximation; JD Power, Omni Calculator). Monthly rent charge = (cap cost + residual) x money factor (standard formula; num8ers calculator example consistent).
- Fees (vary by lessor/contract): acquisition fee roughly **$595-$1,095** (Edmunds data, via Bankrate); disposition fee about **$300-$400** (Edmunds via AOL; KBB ~$350); excess mileage typically **10-25 cents/mile** (Consumer Reports), 15-30 cents (Finn). Excess wear charges also apply.
- At lease end the lessor either collects the residual (customer buys) or takes the car back and sells it, mostly at auction (remarketing). Ford Credit 10-K: residual risk = the chance that what it recovers on returned vehicles is less than the residual it estimated at origination; the gap between book value and sale proceeds is booked as a depreciation adjustment at disposal.
- **Subsidized ("supported") residuals**: automakers pay their captive to raise the contractual residual above the captive's standard estimate, which lowers the monthly payment (Ally 10-K FY2023 calls it residual support; GMAC FY2008 10-K: manufacturer pays present value of expected support at origination, trued up at termination; Santander Consumer FY2016 10-K calls it a form of subvention). It's a sales tool: the automaker absorbs part of the residual risk.

## Worked example (hypothetical; assumptions stated)
- $40,000 cap cost, 36 months, 58% residual, money factor 0.0025 (~6% APR = 0.0025 x 2,400). No taxes/fees.
- Residual: $40,000 x 0.58 = **$23,200**
- Depreciation: $40,000 - $23,200 = **$16,800**; per month $16,800 / 36 = **$466.67 (about $467)**
- Rent charge: ($40,000 + $23,200) x 0.0025 = $63,200 x 0.0025 = **$158.00/month**
- Base payment: $466.67 + $158.00 = **$624.67/month** (before tax and fees)
- Context: Experian average new lease payment **$617** in Q2 2026 (up 0.7% y/y).
- Lessor's bet: if the car sells for $21,000 at end, it loses $2,200 vs residual on that car (example). If it's worth $28,000, the lessee can buy at $23,200 and keep the $4,800 upside (example).

## Risk side 1: used prices fall (2008)
- Q2 2008: Ford Credit took a **$2.1 billion** pre-tax impairment charge on North America operating leases, mainly full-size trucks and traditional SUVs, after high fuel prices and a weak economy cut auction values. Carrying value of impaired assets cut from $11.5B to $9.4B. Ford Credit's Q2 2008 pre-tax loss was $2.38B (vs $112M profit a year earlier); excluding the charge, a $294M loss. (Ford Credit Q2 2008 10-Q, July 2008 8-K/earnings release; Auto Remarketing "Ford Credit Hit with Steep Charge on Lease-End Residuals"; FY2008 10-K)

## Risk side 2: used prices spike (2021-22) - the surprising part
- Normally most leased cars come back: Moody's (2022) said return rates are usually around **75%**, but with used prices jumping, lessors saw return rates fall to about **10% by mid-2022**. Ford Credit CFO (2022 JPMorgan conference): "Typically, our return rate is about 80 percent." Ford Authority: Ford Credit return rate fell from 34% in Q2 2021 to 9% in Q2 2022.
- Customers bought cars at the residual (set years earlier) and kept the gain; lessors still got the residual, but missed the resale upside on cars that never came back. GM Financial's FY2021 10-K: in 2021, prices at termination generally exceeded contractual residuals; GMF raised residual estimates at Dec 31, 2021. Q3 2021 10-Q: gains on lease terminations $0.4B (Q3) and $1.6B (9 months 2021) - on the cars that did come back.
- Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index: record 236.3 in January 2022 (Cox/Auto Remarketing; later series revisions show 257.7 - DO NOT chart index levels, series was rebased). Up 46.6% in 2021, +14.2% 2020; fell ~15% in 2022, ~7% in 2023; +0.4% in 2024. September 2026: **205.9**, down 0.6% y/y (Cox Automotive, Oct 7, 2026); Cox forecasts ~+0.2% for 2026.

## Now: returns are climbing again
- GM Financial (FY2025 10-K, filed early 2026): estimated residual value of leased vehicles **$25.0 billion** at Dec 31, 2025; leased vehicles, net **$33.7B** (vs $31.6B a year earlier). Sensitivity: a 1% (relative) change in estimated residual values = about **$250 million** of depreciation over the remaining portfolio life ($174M in 2026, $60M in 2027). No material impairment indicators at year-end 2025.
- Wards Auto (Jan 2026): GM Financial's Q4 2025 return rate 33% vs 27% a year earlier and 77% in Q4 2019; used prices for more models near or below contracted residuals. (single trade source)
- Ford Credit FY2025 10-K: a 100 bp change in future auction values moves projected lifetime depreciation about $50M; U.S. Ford/Lincoln is 73% of net investment in operating leases.

## Lease share of new vehicles
- Experian Q2 2026: new-vehicle leasing **23.75%** of the market vs 24.04% in Q2 2025 (Experian press release, via experianplc.com newsroom). Honda 12.71% of new leases, Toyota 9.9%, Ford 6.57% (Ford Authority citing Experian).

## Surprising fact
In mid-2022 only about 1 in 10 leased cars was returned (Moody's), versus about 3 in 4 normally: drivers bought their cars at the old residual price and kept the gain.
