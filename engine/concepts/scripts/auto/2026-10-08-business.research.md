# Research brief: How banks make money from your checking account
Show: How They Make Money | Posts Thursday, October 8, 2026, 2 PM Central | Researched September 30, 2026

## Timely angle
- On September 16, 2026 the FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 0.25 point to 3.75%-4.00% (first hike since 2023). The Fed Board raised the interest rate on reserve balances (IORB) to 3.90%, effective September 17, 2026. (Fed implementation note Sept 16, 2026; Raisin; Chase; Advisor Perspectives; The Money Overview.) Next FOMC meeting is after the Oct 8 posting date, so 3.90% is still current on posting.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. FDIC national rate for interest checking: 0.07% (as of September 21, 2026; $2,500 product tier). National rate cap: 4.38%. (FDIC National Rates and Rate Caps; confirmed via US News/Fool quoting FDIC, 0.07% also as of August 17, 2026.)
2. IORB: 3.90% effective September 17, 2026 (Federal Reserve implementation note, Sept 16, 2026; FRED IORB series).
3. Ratio: 3.90 / 0.07 = 55.7, so the Fed's overnight rate to banks is more than 55 times the average interest checking rate.
4. Reserve requirement ratios: 0% since March 26, 2020 (announced March 15, 2020; Federal Reserve Reserve Requirements page; Federal Register 2020-05806). Banks are not required to hold any share of deposits in reserve.
5. JPMorgan Chase (largest US bank) 10-K for fiscal 2025, as of December 31, 2025:
   - Total deposits: $2,559,320 million (~$2.56 trillion)
   - Noninterest-bearing deposits: $620,399 million ($620.4 billion) = $583,342M US offices + $37,057M non-US offices
6. JPMorgan full-year 2025 net interest income: $95.4 billion reported (managed basis $95.868B), up 3%. Net income 2025: $57.0 billion. (4Q25 earnings release / FY2025 10-K)

## Worked example (exact math)
$5,000 kept in interest checking for one year:
- At the FDIC average 0.07%: 5,000 x 0.0007 = $3.50 to the customer
- Same $5,000 parked at the Fed at 3.90%: 5,000 x 0.039 = $195.00 to the bank
- Gap: 195.00 - 3.50 = $191.50 (simple interest, rates held constant for illustration)

## The surprising fact
The Fed pays banks more than 55x the average checking rate just to leave cash parked overnight, with no lending risk, and banks have had no reserve requirement since March 2020. JPMorgan alone held $620.4 billion of deposits paying zero interest at end-2025.

## Other ways (context, no numbers used)
- Account fees (monthly maintenance, overdraft/NSF) and debit card interchange (swipe fees paid by merchants). Reg II debit cap (21 cents + 0.05% + 1 cent) is in legal limbo: N.D. district court vacated it Aug 6, 2025 but stayed the vacatur pending appeal; a Kentucky court upheld it. Avoid citing specific interchange numbers.
