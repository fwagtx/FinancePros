## Facts (picked story) - Netflix Q3 2026, shareholder letter dated Oct 20, 2026 (SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1)
- Released Tuesday, October 20, 2026, after the US market close.
- Q3 2026 revenue: $12.9B in the letter ("rose 12% year over year to $12.9B, consistent with our forecast"); +11% F/X neutral. StockTitan gives $12.87B (single secondary source for the exact figure; use $12.9B on screen). Analysts (Zacks/Hudson Labs consensus) ~$12.87-12.88B -> "in line", not a beat (Variety headline: "Q3 Revenue Meets Wall Street Estimates").
- Netflix's own Q3 guidance (July 16, 2026 letter): $12.86B revenue, EPS $0.82, op margin 33.2%.
- Operating income ~$4.5B, +37% y/y; operating margin 34.6% vs 28.2% in Q3 2025 (Q3'25 was depressed by a ~$619M Brazil tax charge). Netflix says margin was ahead of forecast "primarily due to the timing of expenses" plus slight revenue upside.
- Diluted EPS $0.87 vs $0.59 in Q3 2025 (+49%); LSEG consensus $0.82 (Yahoo/Benzinga).
- Growth drivers per Netflix: mainly subscriptions (membership growth and price increases), plus advertising and favorable currency. Churn improved y/y globally and in the US.
- Ads: Netflix lowered its 2026 ad revenue expectation to about $2.7B from about $3B (a ~$300M, ~10% cut), citing a slightly slower than expected rollout of some ad product capabilities; co-CEO Greg Peters cited delays in rollout and partner integrations (MediaPost, AdNews, PPC Land, The Next Web, Variety, Yahoo Finance). Netflix still expects ad revenue growth above 80% in 2026; 2025 ad revenue was more than $1.5B (more than doubled in 2025). Q2 2026 ad revenue was ~$618M (S&P Global, July).
- Q4 2026 guidance: revenue ~$13.44B (letter: $13.4B, ~+12%) vs consensus ~$13.51B (Yahoo Finance); Q4 operating margin 26.0% including ~120 basis-point hit from recent headcount reductions.
- Full-year 2026 revenue outlook: $51.1B (+13%), op margin 31.5% (prior range $51.0-51.4B).
- Stock: shares fell after hours (reports range ~3% to ~6%) - NOT used on screen (after-hours, will be stale by 10 AM).
- Context: revenue growth 12% is the slowest in nearly three years (Reuters via search summary; weakly sourced, not used).
