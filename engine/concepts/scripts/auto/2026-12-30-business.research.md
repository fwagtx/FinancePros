# Research brief: How Epic makes money giving Fortnite away free
Show: How They Make Money (posts Wed Dec 30, 2026, 2 PM Central). Evergreen: every number carries a date.
Epic Games is PRIVATE: no 10-K. Numbers come only from court records (Epic v. Apple, N.D. Cal. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR), the FTC, and Epic's own official pages/announcements. No stock angle.

Avoid overlap: Dec 28 short = Canva freemium ("9 in 10 users pay nothing"); Dec 29 short = consoles sold at a loss (used Epic v. Apple Xbox testimony). Our angle: a made-up currency with no marginal cost, cosmetics, the Battle Pass loop, the March 2026 V-Bucks repricing, and the creator economy.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. Fortnite revenue (Epic internal financial presentation, Jan 2020, made public as an exhibit around the Epic v. Apple trial, reported May 2021 by The Verge, PC Gamer, TechSpot, Complex, NME):
   - 2018: about $5.48 billion; 2019: about $3.71 billion; total about $9.1-9.2 billion over 2018-2019.
   - (Some outlets mix in a ~$5.5B two-year profit figure; one outlier (Techrecipe) gives different numbers. We use revenue $5.48B / $3.71B.)
2. Freemium model, court findings (Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, Rule 52 order, Sept 10, 2021, Dkt. 812):
   - Battle Royale "is available to download and play free of charge," but "players can make in-app purchases for digital content, including digital avatars, costumes, dance moves, and other cosmetic items."
   - In-app items "function as forms of self-expression, including cosmetic enhancements or 'skins' ... dance moves known as 'emotes'."
   - Tim Sweeney testimony (May 2021), quoted in the order: "V-Bucks themselves don't have a marginal cost." (Trial Tr. (Sweeney) 190:14-16)
   - Other revenue: Fortnite Crew subscription (since Dec 2020), Save the World paid mode, retail codes, cross-promotions/ads, console hardware bundles, brand licensing (cosmetics of licensed brands), merchandise licensing.
   - Epic pays platform commissions (standard 30%) on Fortnite transactions through Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo eShop and Google Play; extra payments to Sony based on cross-platform play time.
   - Fortnite on iOS earned "more than $700 million" (Mar 2018-Jul 2020); 93% of Fortnite revenue came from non-iOS platforms; only 13.2% of paying users bought on iOS.
   - Fortnite had "over 400 hundred [sic] million registered players" (as of trial, 2021).
   - Epic Games Store: 88/12 split, which the court found "below-cost"; store expected to lose money for years (2021 finding).
3. V-Bucks repricing (official, fortnite.com, posted Mar 10, 2026, effective Mar 19, 2026; "The cost of running Fortnite has gone up a lot and we're raising prices to help pay the bills."):
   - $8.99 pack: 1,000 V-Bucks -> 800 V-Bucks. $22.99: 2,800 -> 2,400. $36.99: 5,000 -> 4,500. $89.99: 13,500 -> 12,500.
   - Battle Pass now costs 800 V-Bucks and awards 800 V-Bucks on completion (previously cost 1,000 and could earn 1,000 + 500 bonus).
   - Fortnite Crew V-Bucks grant: 1,000 -> 800.
   - 20% back in Epic Rewards when bought through Epic's payment system (PC, iOS, Android, web).
4. Creator economy: Creator Economy 2.0 (Epic, March 2023) sets aside 40% of Fortnite's net revenue (V-Bucks, real-money transactions, Fortnite Crew) for monthly engagement payouts to island creators (Epic's own islands also take part).
   - Epic said at State of Unreal (Unreal Fest Chicago, June 17, 2026; reported by GamesBeat) it has paid "over $1 billion" to UEFN creators since March 2023. In May 2026, 47% of player hours were in creator-built islands (35% in May 2025).
   - Earlier: $320 million paid over the 12 months to March 2024 (GDC 2024 State of Unreal, via Game World Observer).
5. FTC (Dec 19, 2022): Epic agreed to pay $520 million total: $275 million civil penalty (COPPA, children's privacy) + $245 million refunds over "dark patterns" and billing practices (e.g., purchases charged without confirmation). FTC called the $245M its largest refund in a gaming case. Epic said it was resolving concerns over "past designs of the Fortnite item shop and refund systems."
6. Unreal Engine: 5% royalty on worldwide gross revenue above the first $1 million per product (Unreal Engine license/FAQ, unrealengine.com). Reported reduction to 3.5% from Jan 1, 2025 for games also launched on the Epic Games Store ("Launch Everywhere with Epic", MediaPost 2024).
7. Epic Games Store: 88/12; since June 2025 developers keep 100% of the first $1 million net revenue per product per year (Epic Games Store news, June 3, 2025).
8. Disney: $1.5 billion equity investment in Epic announced Feb 7, 2024 (Disney earnings call; CNBC, ABC), plus a Disney universe connected to Fortnite. Valuation not disclosed.

## Worked example (OUR MATH), from the official March 2026 price table
- Before Mar 19, 2026: $8.99 / 1,000 V-Bucks = $0.00899 = about 0.90 cents per V-Buck.
- From Mar 19, 2026: $8.99 / 800 V-Bucks = $0.0112375 = about 1.12 cents per V-Buck.
- 1.12375 / 0.899 = 1.25 -> each V-Buck costs 25% more for the same $8.99.
- Battle Pass: 800 V-Bucks = exactly one $8.99 pack; finishing it pays back 800 V-Bucks = the next pass. A player who finishes every pass pays real money once.
- Platform cut (illustrative, our math): if bought through a store with the standard 30% commission, $8.99 x 30% = about $2.70 to the platform, about $6.29 to Epic.

## Surprising fact
The product Epic sells most of costs it almost nothing to make: Sweeney testified "V-Bucks themselves don't have a marginal cost." A free game earned about $9.1B in 2018-2019.
