The executive summary says "exclusively using cash, savings, or a credit card paid off at the next statement." So 37% would not. - Line 48: Bankrate 2026 Annual Emergency Savings Report (Feb 2026): "nearly 1 in 4 (24%)" have no emergency savings. Confirmed only through secondary coverage, and the attribution is worded correctly. It is acceptable but has the weakest sourcing in the script.
- Lines 54-57: The FDIC limit is $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category, and the NCUA standard is the same. Stocks, bonds, mutual funds and crypto are not insured, even when bought through a bank. Correct.
- Line 72: Fed G.19 rate on accounts assessed interest: 21.52% in Q1 2026, 22.15% in Q2 2026, and 22.36% reported for Aug 2026. "About 22% in 2026" holds for Dec 30.
- Lines 65-68 (PLESA): created by SECURE 2.0 (2022) and allowed since 2024. The employer chooses whether to offer one, contributions are Roth (after-tax), withdrawals are allowed at least monthly, and the first 4 withdrawals per plan year are fee-free. The $2,600 figure for 2026 comes from IRS Notice 2025-67 (up from $2,500). It caps the balance attributable to contributions, not annual contributions (see fix 3).
- Lines 76-77: SECURE 2.0 emergency personal expense distribution since 2024. It is up to $1,000 a year, exempt from the 10% additional tax and still taxable. Correct.
- CFPB paraphrases (lines 11, 31) match the CFPB definition and its tip to size the goal from past unexpected expenses. No starter amount is attributed to the CFPB. Good.
- Math: 3 x $2,500 = $7,500 and 6 x $2,500 = $15,000. $25/wk gives $500 at 20 weeks, $1,000 at 40 and $1,300 at 52. $25/7 = $3.57/day. At $50/wk, $1,000 takes 20 weeks. $100 x 12 = $1,200. $1,000 x 0.38% = $3.80. $1,000 x 22% / 12 = $18.33, and about $220 for a year at simple interest. All correct.
- No product picks and no personalized advice. The script uses "common rule of thumb" and "one common approach" framing, and lines 9 and 91 carry the disclaimer. The text matches the spoken line on every line checked; the numbers are only written differently in each.

### 3. lines[67] + post.yt_desc: "$2,600 in contributions for 2026" is mis-described and may be stale
Problems:
(a) The $2,600 is a cap on the account balance attributable to your contributions, not an annual contribution limit. Once you withdraw, you can contribute again. Employers can also set a lower cap.
(b) The IRS normally announces next year's indexed limits between late October and mid-November. No 2027 figure had been published as of Oct 23, 2026. By Dec 30 a 2027 PLESA cap will probably exist, so a bare "for 2026" looks out of date the day the video posts. Name the year, mention the inflation adjustment, and ideally swap in the 2027 number if the IRS has published it by the edit lock.
