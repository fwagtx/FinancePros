# Research brief: FDIC insurance: what's covered and what isn't (Explainer, posts Oct 26, 2026)

- FDIC standard maximum deposit insurance: $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category (FDIC.gov).
- Covered: checking, savings, money market deposit accounts, CDs, cashier's checks and money orders issued by the bank.
- Not covered (FDIC "Financial Products That Are Not Insured"): stocks, bonds, mutual funds, crypto assets, life insurance policies, annuities, municipal securities, safe deposit boxes and their contents, and US Treasury bills/bonds/notes. Treasuries are backed by the full faith and credit of the US government instead. This holds even when the products are bought at an insured bank.
- Joint accounts: each co-owner is insured up to $250,000 for joint accounts, so a 2-owner joint account is covered up to $500,000.
- Synapse (2024): the fintech middleware firm collapsed. More than 100,000 customers with about $265M were locked out, with a $65-95M shortfall. The FDIC said deposit insurance does not cover the failure of nonbanks; pass-through coverage only protects against failure of the underlying bank (CNBC, July 2 and Nov 22, 2024).
- Worked example: one owner with $300,000 at one bank in one category: $250,000 insured, $50,000 not.
- Surprising fact: safe deposit box contents are not FDIC insured.
