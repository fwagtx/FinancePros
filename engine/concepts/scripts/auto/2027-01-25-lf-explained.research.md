## Gold as money (history)
- Gold Reserve Act, signed Jan 30, 1934: statutory price raised from $20.67 to $35/oz (Federal Reserve History; Wikipedia).
- Bretton Woods (1944): currencies pegged to the dollar, dollar convertible to gold at $35 for foreign governments (state.gov Office of the Historian).
- Aug 9, 1971: Switzerland left the Bretton Woods system (Wikipedia, Nixon shock).
- Aug 15, 1971: Nixon TV address. Exact quote: "I have directed Secretary Connally to suspend temporarily the convertibility of the dollar into gold or other reserve assets" (Nixon Foundation transcript; gold.org archive; Wikipedia). By 1973 the "temporary" suspension was permanent; major currencies floated (state.gov).
- Jan 21, 1980: London fix $850. Not surpassed nominally until Jan 3, 2008 ($865.35): ~28 years. Inflation-adjusted 1980 peak not surpassed until 2025 (Bloomberg Sept 11, 2025): ~45 years.
- Our math: $35 (1971) -> $850 (Jan 1980) = 24.3x.
- 1999 low: about $252 (Aug 25, 1999 close $252.35, JM Bullion; LBMA historic low $252.80 July 1999). Washington Agreement on Gold, Sept 26, 1999: European central banks capped sales at 400 t/yr (2,000 t over 5 years) (WGC; LBMA).
- Sept 2011 peak about $1,920 intraday ($1,923 LBMA PM) (USAGold; others).

## What moves the price
1. Real interest rates = nominal rate minus inflation. Gold pays no interest; higher real rates raise the opportunity cost (WGC; SSGA 2025). Example: $10,000 at 4% = $400/yr; gold = $0.
2. US dollar: gold priced in dollars; weaker dollar -> more dollars per ounce, cheaper for non-dollar buyers. Dollar index fell ~9.4% in 2025, worst since 2017 (Bloomberg; NPR Jan 4, 2026).
3. Fear/geopolitics: safe-haven demand. 2008 avg +25%, 2020 avg +27% (our math from annual averages).
4. Inflation expectations.
- Fed cut three times in 2025 to 3.50-3.75% (NPR, Business Standard).
- Nov 2026: hike bets, dollar near 19-month high -> gold to 14-month low (Reuters via Kitco/NST, Nov 16-18, 2026).

## Ways to own it, with worked examples (our math)
- ETF expense ratios (ETF.com, Nasdaq, 2026): GLD 0.40%, IAU 0.25%, GLDM 0.10%.
- Worked example A (ETF fees): $10,000 held 10 years, gold price flat. Fee reduces holdings each year: 0.40% -> 10,000 x (1 - 0.996^10) = $392.88 (~$393). 0.10% -> 10,000 x (1 - 0.999^10) = $99.55 (~$100). Difference ~$293.
- Worked example B (coin premium): typical 1 oz American Gold Eagle premium ~5.1% over spot (dealer premium survey, Nov 3, 2026; other 2026 sources range 1-8%). At $3,720 spot: 3,720 x 0.051 = $189.72 premium; price $3,909.72 (~$3,910). Selling back at spot needs a 5.1% rise to break even (simplified; real buyback quotes vary).
- Miners: companies, not metal; profits tied to gold but also costs, debt, operations (definitional).

## Risks
- No yield. Long drawdowns (1980-2008 nominal; 2012-2015 avg -30%). Volatility (-33% Jan to Nov 2026).

## Surprising fact
Gold pays zero interest, yet central banks bought 863 t in 2025 and 675 t in the first nine months of 2026, even as the price fell about a third from its January 2026 record. In the 1990s, central banks were sellers.
