# Research brief: Severance pay, how it works and what's negotiable (2026-12-05-explainer, Ticker)

## Core facts (confirmed Oct 6, 2026)
- No federal severance requirement: "There is no requirement in the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) for severance pay. Severance pay is a matter of agreement between an employer and an employee (or the employee's representative)." (US DOL, Severance Pay topic page / elaws FLSA FAQ). EBSA may help if an employer-sponsored severance PLAN failed to pay.
- WARN Act exception (US DOL Plant Closings and Layoffs; elaws WARN advisor): employers with 100 or more employees must give at least 60 calendar days' advance written notice of a plant closing or mass layoff (50+ employees at a single site, with conditions). An employer that fails to give notice owes back pay and benefits for each day of violation, up to 60 days. WARN is a notice rule, not a severance mandate.
- Taxes (IRS Publication 15, 2026, Circular E): severance is wages, subject to Social Security, Medicare, federal income tax withholding and FUTA. Supplemental wages (bonuses, commissions, severance, back pay, etc.) can be withheld at a flat 22%; 37% mandatory on the portion above $1 million to one employee in a calendar year. P.L. 119-21 (2025 law) permanently extended the rates. 22% is withholding, not the final tax owed.
- Release of claims / over-40 rules (EEOC, Q&A: Understanding Waivers of Discrimination Claims in Employee Severance Agreements; 29 CFR 1625.22): to validly waive ADEA age claims, a worker 40+ must get at least 21 days to consider the agreement (at least 45 days if part of a group exit-incentive or termination program), and 7 days after signing to revoke. Material changes to the final offer restart the 21 days. A waiver needs consideration (something extra, like pay) and cannot waive the right to file an EEOC charge or future rights.
- Health coverage (US DOL EBSA COBRA FAQs): after job loss (not gross misconduct), COBRA continuation usually lasts 18 months; plan can charge up to 102% of the full premium (full cost + 2% admin). Employers sometimes pay or subsidize COBRA as part of severance (negotiable term).
- Unemployment: whether severance delays or reduces unemployment benefits depends on state law (NY DOL "Dismissal or severance pay and your UI benefit"; CA EDD treats severance as not wages for UI eligibility; Nolo).
- Commonly negotiated items (education, not advice): amount/weeks, COBRA premium subsidy, health coverage end date, equity vesting/exercise window, non-compete and non-disparagement terms, reference or neutral letter, how the exit is described.

## Benchmarks
- Challenger, Gray & Christmas, 2025 Severance & Salary Benchmarking Report (released Dec. 30, 2024; 2024 data; 13,000+ job seekers, 840 companies): average severance across industries 19.3 weeks in 2024, up from 15.6 weeks the prior year. Individual contributors average under 10 weeks; C-suite 30-50+ weeks. Hybrid formulas (e.g., a base number of weeks plus one week per year of service) are common. (Challenger blog; Becker's Hospital Review.)
- "1-2 weeks per year of service" widely cited as a rule of thumb (Michigan employer survey via DBusiness: 1 week/year most common at 57%, 2 weeks/year 24%; date unclear). WEAK; script treats 2 weeks/year only as a hypothetical assumption.

## Worked example (hypothetical)
- $60,000 salary; assumption: 2 weeks of pay per year of service x 5 years = 10 weeks.
- Weekly pay = 60,000 / 52 = $1,153.85. 10 weeks = $11,538.46 -> about $11,538.
- 22% federal supplemental withholding = 11,538.46 x 0.22 = $2,538.46 -> about $2,538.
- Left after federal withholding = 11,538.46 - 2,538.46 = $9,000.00 (before Social Security 6.2% and Medicare 1.45% = 7.65% = $882.69, and any state tax).

## Surprise fact
- No federal law requires severance at all; it's a contract term. The only federal "pay for layoffs" rule is WARN back pay when a big employer skips the 60-day notice.
