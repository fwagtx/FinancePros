## What the Fed does (definitions)
- Mandate from Congress (1977 Federal Reserve Reform Act): "maximum employment, stable prices, and moderate long-term interest rates"; the first two are called the dual mandate. (St. Louis Fed; Chicago Fed; CRS IF12940.)
- Price stability goal: 2% inflation over the longer run, measured by the PCE price index (Fed statement on longer-run goals).
- FOMC: 12 voting members = 7 Board governors + New York Fed president + 4 of the other 11 Reserve Bank presidents on rotation (federalreserve.gov FAQ; Cleveland Fed). Eight scheduled meetings a year.
- Federal funds rate: the rate banks charge each other to borrow reserve balances, usually overnight. The Fed sets a target range; it does not lend to consumers. (FRED FEDFUNDS notes; CNBC Select; Chase.)
- Transmission: prime rate conventionally = top of fed funds range + 3 points (4.00 + 3 = 7.00). Most credit card APRs are variable = prime + a margin set by the issuer, so they usually move within a billing cycle or two of a Fed change (Boston Fed 2026; CFPB Reg Z index + margin disclosure; primerates).

## Worked example (exact math)
- $5,000 credit card balance on a variable prime-based APR.
- Quarter-point rise (prime 6.75% -> 7.00%) adds 0.25% to the APR: 5,000 x 0.0025 = $12.50 more interest a year (simple, ignoring compounding).

## Surprising fact
- The Fed doesn't lend to you or set your card rate. Its headline rate is the rate banks charge each other overnight, but banks peg the prime rate to it, and your variable-rate debt is priced off prime.
