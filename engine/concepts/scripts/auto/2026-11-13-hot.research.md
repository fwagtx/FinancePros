# Research brief: How casinos and slot machines are designed to win (house edge)
Slot: 2026-11-13-hot (Hot Take, posts Fri Nov 13, 2026). Researched Oct 1, 2026. Evergreen; every number dated.
Note: direct fetch of .gov/UNLV/AGA pages was blocked by the egress proxy; numbers confirmed via search results quoting the official sources, cross-checked where possible.

## Key facts
1. Nevada slot hold, calendar 2025: statewide slot hold 7.15% -> machines paid back 92.85% of what went in (UNLV Center for Gaming Research, December 2025 / 2025 annual report built from Nevada Gaming Control Board data; reported by Las Vegas Review-Journal, GGB, News3LV). A search summary of the NGCB 2025 figures reports 7.13%; small difference likely from rounding/method. Script uses 7.15% (UNLV, explicit with 92.85% payback) and speaks "about seven cents".
   - 2004 statewide hold 5.72% (UNLV) -> slots have gotten tighter (about 26% higher hold).
   - 2025 by area (UNLV): Downtown LV 8.30%, Laughlin 8.22%, Strip 7.90%, statewide 7.15%, Reno 5.49%.
   - 2024 statewide slot win about 7.2% (Review-Journal, NGCB data).
2. Nevada Regulation 14.040 (Nevada Gaming Commission): gaming devices must theoretically pay out not less than 75% of all amounts wagered (each wager). Waivers possible in narrow cases.
3. American Gaming Association, State of the States 2026 (May 2026): US commercial gaming revenue in 2025 = $78.6 billion (78.62), up 9.1%, sixth straight record year. (AGA's February 2026 tracker first reported $78.72B; later report revised to $78.62B. Script uses $78.6B.) Slots generated over $37 billion in 2025, up about 3%; table games just over $10B; land-based casino games $51.06B; iGaming $10.7B+.
4. Roulette math (definition, fixed):
   - American wheel: 38 pockets (1-36, 0, 00). Straight-up bet pays 35 to 1.
   - Expected value per $1 on one number: (1/38)(+35) + (37/38)(-1) = -2/38 = -0.05263 -> house edge 5.26%.
   - European wheel (single zero, 37 pockets): 1/37 = 2.70%.
   - The extra 00 roughly doubles the edge with identical payouts.

## Worked example (hypothetical, exact math)
Assumptions: American roulette, $10 per spin, 100 spins.
- Total wagered = $10 x 100 = $1,000
- Expected loss = $1,000 x 2/38 = $52.63 (52.6316)
- Any single session can win or lose; this is the long-run average.
Slots example (hypothetical): $1,000 played through a machine at the 2025 Nevada statewide hold of 7.15% -> expected casino win $71.50.

## The surprising fact
A 5.26% edge looks tiny, but it applies to every dollar wagered, and money gets re-bet over and over. That small slice, times billions of bets, is why US commercial gaming set a sixth straight record in 2025 ($78.6B). And Nevada slots have gotten tighter: 5.72% hold in 2004 vs 7.15% in 2025.
