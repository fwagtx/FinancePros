# Research brief: Tax refund vs owing: what withholding really means
Slot: 2026-10-08-explainer (posts Thursday, October 8, 2026). Researched Wednesday, September 30, 2026.

## Timely angle
- The 2026 filing season (tax year 2025 returns) produced the biggest average refunds in years. Through the week ending April 17, 2026, the average refund was $3,275, up 11.3% from $2,942 at about the same point in 2025 (week ending April 18, 2025). About 140.2 million individual returns received by then. (IRS filing season statistics, via CNBC Apr 24, 2026; CBS News)
- Through May 8, 2026: 99,138,000 refunds totaling $324.8 billion, average $3,276, 11.5% above the same point in 2025 and the highest for that point in the season in IRS weekly data back to 2009. (IRS filing season statistics, via Fool.com / Tax Foundation reporting)
- Withholding link: after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) added 2025 tax breaks, the IRS announced NO changes to 2025 withholding tables (phased implementation). CNBC: "As a result, many W-2 workers overpaid taxes through the rest of 2025." (IRS newsroom: "IRS announces no changes to individual information returns or withholding tables for 2025 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act"; CNBC Apr 24, 2026)
- The 2026 withholding tables (IRS Publication 15-T, for use in 2026) WERE updated for the law's changes (extended TCJA rates, standard deduction, tips/overtime deductions). So 2026 paychecks and next spring's refunds may look different. (IRS Pub 15-T 2026; Grant Thornton Nov 18, 2025) -- no prediction of refund sizes.

## Core definitions (evergreen)
- Withholding: employer takes federal income tax out of each paycheck and sends it to the IRS in advance, based on the employee's Form W-4 and IRS withholding tables (Pub 15-T).
- At filing, total tax owed is compared with total paid (withholding + estimated payments + refundable credits).
  - Paid more than owed -> refund of the overpayment.
  - Paid less than owed -> balance due.
- A refund is your own overpaid money returned; the IRS pays no interest on it unless the refund is issued more than 45 days after the filing deadline (or filing date if later). (NerdWallet; IRS rules)

## Owing: penalty rules (Form 2210 / IRS Pub 505)
- No underpayment penalty if tax due after withholding and credits is under $1,000.
- Otherwise no penalty if withholding + timely estimates are at least the smaller of 90% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax (110% if prior-year AGI over $150,000; $75,000 married filing separately). (H&R Block; multiple tax guides summarizing IRS rules)
- Underpayment interest rate for individuals: 7% per year, compounded daily, for Q4 2026 (Oct 1 - Dec 31, 2026), unchanged from Q3 2026. (IRS newsroom "Interest rates remain the same for the fourth quarter of 2026"; CPA Practice Advisor Aug 24, 2026)

## Worked example (illustrative numbers)
- Tax bill for the year: $9,000.
- Case A: $12,000 withheld -> $12,000 - $9,000 = $3,000 refund.
- Case B: $8,000 withheld -> $9,000 - $8,000 = $1,000 owed.
- Average 2026 refund as monthly pay: $3,275 / 12 = $272.92, about $273 a month paid in early, interest-free.

## Surprising fact
- A refund is not a bonus or a gift: it is an interest-free loan you made to the government. The 2026 season's bigger refunds were partly from 2025 withholding tables that were not updated for new tax breaks, so many workers had too much withheld.
