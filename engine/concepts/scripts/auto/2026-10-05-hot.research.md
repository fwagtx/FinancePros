# Research brief: What happens if you get a signing bonus and then quit? (2026-10-05-hot)

Format: Money Myth / "hidden cost" Hot Take. Researched September 30 - October 1, 2026.

## Angle / hook
The surprise: if you quit early and have to repay a signing bonus, many agreements ask for the GROSS amount, but you only ever received the NET (after withholding). If the repayment lands in a later calendar year and is $3,000 or less, federal law gives no deduction, so the income tax withheld on that money is effectively lost. You can end up repaying money you never received.

Timely angle (evergreen-safe, give dates): California AB 692 took effect January 1, 2026 and restricts "stay-or-pay" repayment terms; New York's Trapped at Work Act (signed December 19, 2025, amended February 13, 2026) takes effect December 19, 2026 and still allows signing-bonus repayment only when the worker leaves voluntarily.

## Facts (with dates and sources)
1. Clawbacks: a signing bonus is typically conditioned on staying for a period, often one or two years; many clauses prorate (Repayment = Bonus x months remaining / commitment months). Clawbacks often require repaying the gross bonus even though only the net hit your bank account; varies by contract. (adviceonly.com glossary; leonstaff.com; workcontractreview.com; AOL/GOBankingRates) - secondary sources, describe as "often/many".
2. Federal supplemental-wage withholding (bonuses) in 2026: 22% flat rate; 37% on supplemental wages above $1 million in a calendar year. Rates permanent under P.L. 119-21 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act). (IRS Publication 15 (2026))
3. Social Security 6.2% + Medicare 1.45% = 7.65% employee FICA on wages (IRS Pub 15).
4. Repayment same calendar year: just reduce income by the repayment; employer can adjust payroll/W-2. (IRS Pub 525)
5. Repayment in a later year, $3,000 or less: "you can't deduct it" - for tax years after 2017 no miscellaneous itemized deductions. (IRS Publication 525 (2025), "Repayments")
6. Repayment in a later year over $3,000: deduction or IRC Section 1341 claim-of-right credit, whichever gives less tax. (IRS Pub 525)
7. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025) amended Section 67(g) to make the suspension of miscellaneous itemized deductions permanent (it would have returned for 2026). (Congress.gov CRS R48550; Covington; Journal of Accountancy; Kitces)
8. Prior-year wage repayment: employer can recover/refund Social Security and Medicare tax via Form 941-X; it cannot adjust income tax withholding; employee cannot amend (1040-X) to recover income tax. If employer won't, employee can file Form 843 for SS/Medicare. (IRS Publication 15 (2026); Form 941-X instructions)
9. California AB 692 (effective January 1, 2026): repayment of a sign-on bonus allowed only via stand-alone agreement, 5 business days to review, notice of right to consult a lawyer, retention period max 2 years, prorated, no interest, not accelerated on separation. (Pillsbury, Morgan Lewis, Akin Gump, Proskauer)
10. New York Trapped at Work Act: signed December 19, 2025; chapter amendment signed February 13, 2026; effective December 19, 2026; allows repayment of signing bonuses only if employee voluntarily separates (not if terminated other than for misconduct). (Holland & Knight, Davis Wright Tremaine, Littler)

## Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
Assumptions: $5,000 signing bonus, 12-month prorated clawback, repay gross, quit after month 6, repayment made in the calendar year AFTER the bonus was paid, worker in 22% federal bracket, federal withholding at the 22% supplemental rate, FICA 7.65%, state tax ignored.
- Owed: $5,000 x 6/12 = $2,500 (gross)
- Withheld on that $2,500: federal 22% = $550; FICA 7.65% = $191.25
- Actually received of that $2,500: $2,500 - $550 - $191.25 = $1,758.75
- Repay $2,500. Social Security/Medicare $191.25 recoverable (employer 941-X or Form 843).
- Income tax $550: repayment is $3,000 or less, so no deduction -> lost.
- Out of pocket beyond what you received: $2,500 - $1,758.75 = $741.25 before the FICA refund; $550 after it.
- If repaid in the SAME calendar year: payroll reduces wages, so the $550 is not lost.

## Surprising fact
You can repay the gross bonus, including tax money you never received, and below $3,000 in a later year the IRS gives no deduction - a rule the 2025 tax law made permanent.
