# Research brief: Store credit cards: why the checkout discount can cost you more
Posts Wed Jan 27, 2027, 12 PM Central (evergreen). Researched Nov 28, 2026. Education only, not advice.

## News angle
No dated news hook used (evergreen). Bankrate's 2026 retail card study did not show up in search as of Nov 28, 2026, so the latest annual figure is the Sept 2025 study. Holiday store-card sign-ups are a natural backdrop, but the script avoids time words.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. **Average retail card APR: 30.14%** in Bankrate's Retail Cards Study (published Sept 15, 2025). That's down from a record 30.45% in 2024, and it's the second-highest since Bankrate started tracking in 2008. Store-only cards averaged 31.64% and co-branded cards 28.65%. The highest retail card APR was 35.99%, shared by 13 cards. (Bankrate press release, 2025; ChainStoreAge; PaymentsJournal)
2. **All-card average in the same 2025 Bankrate study: 20.12%.** That makes retail about 1.5x the all-card rate and about 10 percentage points higher (30.14 - 20.12 = 10.02; our math).
   - Cross-check (Fed G.19 / FRED TERMCBCCINTNS): commercial bank credit card rate on accounts assessed interest was 21.52% in Feb 2026. For all accounts it was 21.00% in Feb 2026 and 20.94% in May 2026.
   - Bankrate weekly variable-card average: 19.63% as of Sept 30, 2026.
3. **LendingTree cross-check:** the average new store card APR was 30.58% in 2025, against 24.19% for all new cards. LendingTree had 30.78% for 2024 (implied by its 2025 report), 29.31% for 2023 and 26.60% for 2022. Two outlets agree that store cards run above 30%.
4. **CFPB Issue Spotlight, "The High Cost of Retail Credit Cards" (Dec 2024):**
   - 90% of retail cards reported a maximum APR above 30%, versus 38% of non-retail general-purpose cards.
   - Average APR on top retailers' private-label cards: 32.66%.
   - Many private-label cards have a fixed APR, so every cardholder pays the same rate regardless of creditworthiness.
   - Cardholders are more likely to carry a balance and make only the minimum payment. Fees are typically higher.
   - Four large banks issued over 80% of the cards.
   - The CFPB 2025 CARD Act report (Dec 2025) put the average private-label card APR at 31.3% in 2024, the highest since at least 2015.
5. **Hard inquiry:** applying for a store card generates a hard inquiry, typically a drop of 5 points or less, and the hit is temporary (Experian; Equifax/myFICO say the same).
6. **Low limits raise utilization:** in Experian's example, $200 on a $400 store-card limit is 50% utilization, versus 5% on a $4,000 general card. Utilization counts per card and overall, and experts cite keeping it under about 30% (Experian).
7. Deferred interest ("no interest if paid in full") was covered on 2027-01-22. CFPB flags it as a driver of carrying debt. It is not used in this script.

## Worked example (our math, approximate, hypothetical discount)
- The purchase is $500 with a 20% sign-up discount (hypothetical; offers vary). $500 x 0.20 = **$100 saved**, so you pay $400.
- The $400 is carried for one full year at 30.14% APR (Bankrate 2025 average). Simple interest is $400 x 0.3014 = **$120.56, about $121.** That's more than the $100 saved.
- This is a simplification: it ignores payments, which would lower the balance, and monthly compounding, which raises the cost to about $139 if nothing is paid. It is labeled "our math, approx."

## Surprising fact
On a carried balance, one year of interest at the average store-card rate (about $121 on $400) can exceed the whole 20% discount ($100).
