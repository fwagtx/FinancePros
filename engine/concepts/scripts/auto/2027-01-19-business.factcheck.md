| 0 | Medicare pays $7.77 for a CBC (CPT 85025) | $7.77 is the CY2026 CLFS national limit, the most-cited figure (PayerPrice "2026" rate; CodingAhead). One cost-guide page says $7.59, which I could not tie to any CMS year, so I rejected it. The CLFS has been frozen at 2020 rates through 2026, which fits $7.77. **Stale by the post date:** PAMA cuts take effect Jan 1, 2027 (Consolidated Appropriations Act 2026, signed Feb 3, 2026; capped at -15% per test per year for 2027-2029). CMS posted preliminary CY2027 rates Sep 21, 2026, with 1,171 codes below their 2026 rates. Final CY2027 rates were due in November 2026, and I found no code-level CY2027 figure for 85025. So the present-tense "pays $7.77" could be wrong on Jan 19, 2027. FIXED (see below). |

| 1 | Quest FY2025 revenue $11.04B | Confirmed: Feb 10, 2026 release, up 11.8%. |
| 2 | About 244M orders (requisitions) in 2025 | Confirmed as "approximately 244 million" by Quest's 2025 Corporate Responsibility Report, a Reuters-based FY2025 summary and Hudson Labs. The script says "roughly", which is fine. |

| 5 | Medicare pays one national price; insurers negotiate | True. Since PAMA (2018) the CLFS uses single national rates based on weighted medians of private-payer rates. Commercial rates are negotiated. |

But "more orders" alone was incomplete: of the 12.3% volume growth in 2025, 8.9 points came from acquisitions (10-K), so the line now says acquisitions too. |
| 8 | Tiny price x giant volume | A fair conclusion from the numbers. |

## Issues found and fixes applied
1. **Stale price / time-word risk (line 0, post captions).** On Jan 19, 2027 the CY2027 CLFS will be in effect, and 85025 may be cut by up to 15%. Fix: the hook is now past tense ("Medicare paid $7.77"). Line 1 dates it explicitly in speech and text: "That was its 2026 rate." All captions now say "in 2026" / "(CY2026)". The IG caption notes that rates are reset starting in 2027. If CMS's final CY2027 file is checked before posting and 85025 is unchanged at $7.77, the copy is still true.
2. **Weak/slow hook (line 0).** At 11 words it ran over 3 seconds, and "Labs make billions" was a flat statement. New: "Medicare paid seven seventy-seven for a blood count. Labs? Billions." That is 10 spoken words, about 3 s, and sets up a tiny price against billions as a question. "Seven seventy-seven" is kept: "seven dollars and seventy-seven cents" would break the hook budget, and the $7.77 tag is on screen at the same moment. I added no "about": $7.77 is an exact figure.
3. **Spoken vs on-screen mismatches.** Line 1 said "about eleven billion" against a $11.04B on-screen stat. Lines 3 and 6 said "about ten point eight billion" against $10.79B and mixed precision ("about nine point two five"). Now spoken as "eleven point oh four billion", "ten point seven nine billion" and "nine point two five billion".
4. **Incomplete cause (line 7).** Changed to "before acquisitions and more orders pushed it past its old high."
5. **Word budget.** To stay within 140 words, tightened lines 2, 3, 5 and 8 with no change to the facts. Line 2 dropped "that year"; the board still shows "in 2025".
6. Post title and TikTok/IG/YT captions updated to match (dated 2026; IG shows the $44 math).

No advice, price predictions or political opinion. The cover "$7.77 Blood Test" and the header are paid off in the video. The last line is unchanged. check.py: OK, 10 lines, 140 spoken words, hook 10 words.
