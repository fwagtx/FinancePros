# Research brief: How oil prices ripple through everything you buy
Slot: 2026-10-08-lf-world (World Money, long-form). Researched Sept 30, 2026. Posts Oct 8, 2026 (evergreen: date every number).
Companion video (Oct 1) covers the dollar; this one stays on oil -> prices.

## Timely angle (as of late Sept 2026)
- Iran war began Feb 28, 2026. Brent ~$72/bbl on Feb 27, 2026; futures peak $118.35 (Mar 31, 2026) (CNBC timeline Apr 21, 2026; Wikipedia econ impact of 2026 Iran war).
- Dated Brent (physical cargo benchmark) hit $144.42 on Apr 7, 2026, highest since Platts began publishing it in 1987 (Bloomberg / Forbes, Apr 7, 2026).
- Brent settled $102.59 on Sept 29, 2026, down 2.6%; WTI $89.38 (Rio Times / CNBC Sept 29, 2026: Saudi Red Sea exports recovering). Brent closed $105.28 on Sept 28 (CNBC). Oil is volatile: in script say "above $100 in late September 2026" / "about $103 on Sept 29, 2026".
- Strait of Hormuz: ~20 million b/d in 2024, ~20% of global petroleum liquids consumption; 84% of crude/condensate through it went to Asia (EIA, 2025 analysis).
- US regular gasoline (EIA weekly): $4.465/gal on Sept 28, 2026, $1.347 above a year earlier (so ~$3.118 a year earlier). AAA: $4.48 on Sept 24, 2026, "highest the national average has ever been for this time of year".
- Diesel (EIA weekly): record $6.529 week of Sept 21, 2026, highest in EIA weekly records back to 1994; $6.382 on Sept 28, 2026, $2.628 above a year earlier (Logistics Management; weeklydiesel/IndexBox citing EIA).

## CPI, August 2026 (BLS, released Sept 11, 2026)
- All items +3.4% over 12 months. Energy +16.3%. Gasoline +27.4% (up 3.9% in Aug alone). Fuel oil +52.0%. Airline fares +23.4%. Food at home +2.2%. Food away from home +3.4%.
- (Fox Business, IndexBox, USInflationCalculator summarizing BLS.)

## Countries (characters)
- US: net petroleum exporter every year since 2020; 2025 net exports ~2.8 million b/d (EIA). Still pays world price.
- Canada: 63.4% of US crude imports in 2025; ~3.9 million b/d (EIA / Canada Energy Regulator).
- China: world's largest crude importer, record 11.55 million b/d in 2025 (China customs via Reuters/IndexBox).
- Japan: 94% of crude imports from the Middle East in 2025 (METI via Argus).

## What's in a gallon (EIA, 2025)
- Crude oil 51%, refining 14%, distribution & marketing 18%, taxes 17%. Federal tax 18.4 cents/gal; states avg ~33 cents (EIA, 33.27 cents Jan 2026) -> total ~52 cents (fact-check correction).
- Rule of thumb: 42 gallons per barrel, so each $1/bbl change ~ 2.4 cents/gal of crude cost (EIA/NACS).
- A 42-gal barrel yields ~19.6 gal gasoline, ~12.5 gal distillate (diesel/heating oil), ~4.4 gal jet fuel (EIA, 2023 data); refinery gain -> ~45 gal of products.

## Other ripples
- Airlines: fuel was about 25% (25.4%) of airline operating costs in 2025 (IATA, June 2026 outlook).
- Plastics: polyethylene/polypropylene come from oil and gas feedstocks; European PE spot prices roughly doubled at the low end from end-Feb to mid-April 2026 (ICIS, Apr 14, 2026; fact-check correction). Packaging costs feed into shelf prices.
- Fertilizer/food: urea above $850/metric ton in April 2026, up 80% since February, highest since April 2022; Middle East is nearly one-quarter of global urea exports (World Bank blog, Apr 2026 Commodity Markets Outlook).
- Food at home only +2.2% (Aug 2026): food ripples are slower/smaller than gas and airfare (BLS).

## History
- Oct 19, 1973: OAPEC embargo on US; oil roughly quadrupled from $2.90 to $11.65 a barrel by Jan 1974 (Federal Reserve History, "Oil Shock of 1973-74").
- July 11, 2008: WTI record $147.27 intraday; fell to ~$32 by Dec 2008.
- Apr 20, 2020: WTI May contract settled at -$37.63 (CFTC interim report).
- 2022: US gasoline averaged $3.95 for the year (EIA).

## WORKED EXAMPLES
1) Crude math: Brent ~$72 (Feb 27) -> $102.59 (Sept 29): +$30.59 ~ $30. $30 / 42 gal = $0.714; rule of thumb 30 x 2.4 cents = 72 cents per gallon of crude cost alone.
2) Fill-up: 15 gallons x $4.465 = $66.98 (Sept 28, 2026) vs 15 x $3.118 = $46.77 a year earlier -> +$20.21 per fill-up. Once a week for a year: 20.205 x 52 = $1,050.66 (~$1,050) at that gap (illustration, not a forecast).

## Surprise fact
- The US exports more petroleum than it imports (every year since 2020), yet US gas hit ~$4.47 in Sept 2026, because oil is priced on one world market.
