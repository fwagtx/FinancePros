# Research brief: Fractional reserve banking (Acorn Economics, posts Fri Jan 1, 2027)

News angle: none needed (evergreen). Researched Nov 2, 2026.

## Core facts (with dates and sources)
1. Fractional reserve banking: banks keep only part of deposits as reserves (vault cash + balances held at the Federal Reserve) and use the rest to fund loans and other assets. Reserves = vault cash + balances at Federal Reserve Banks (Federal Reserve, "Reserve Requirements" page).
2. Reserve requirement is 0%. The Fed reduced all reserve requirement ratios to 0 percent effective March 26, 2020 (announced March 15, 2020). Before that: 10% on net transaction accounts above the low reserve tranche, 3% on the tranche. Cut removed an estimated ~$200 billion of required reserves. Reason: the Fed operates an "ample reserves" framework. Interim final rule 85 FR 16525 (Mar 24, 2020), final rule 86 FR (Feb 10, 2021), effective Mar 12, 2021.
   Source: federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/reservereq.htm; Federal Register 2020-05806 and 2020-28756.
3. FDIC insurance: standard maximum $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, for each account ownership category. Automatic for deposit accounts (checking, savings, MMDA, CDs) at FDIC-insured banks.
   Source: FDIC deposit insurance FAQ (fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/faq); FDIC "Deposit Insurance at a Glance".
4. Modern view of money creation: Bank of England Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1, "Money creation in the modern economy" (McLeay, Radia, Thomas, Mar 14, 2014): commercial banks create most money by making loans; when a bank makes a loan it credits the borrower's account, creating a new deposit. It says the textbook ideas that banks just lend out saved deposits, or mechanically "multiply" central bank money, are misconceptions. Do NOT present the money multiplier as how banks work today.
   Source: bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy
5. Bank deposits are the bulk of money: BoE 2014 says bank deposits are about 97% of money in circulation in the UK (UK figure, 2014).

## Worked example (our math, illustrative only)
Squirrel deposits 100 acorns. Bank keeps 10 as reserves (10%), lends 90: 100 - 10 = 90 lent. 10 / 100 = 10% kept. (Illustrative; there is no required ratio since 2020; real banks choose their own liquidity under supervision and liquidity rules.)

## The surprising fact
The legal reserve requirement in the US is 0% (since March 26, 2020), yet banks still held about $3.2 trillion at the Fed on average in 2025 (FRED WRESBAL annual average).

## Uncertain / careful
- WRESBAL covers all depository institutions' balances at the Fed (incl. US branches of foreign banks); don't present reserves/deposits ratio as an official stat.
- Exact BoE wording not quoted; paraphrase only ("loans create deposits").
