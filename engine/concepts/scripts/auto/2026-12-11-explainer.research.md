# Research brief: What a dividend is, explained with acorns (Acorn Economics)

## Definition (stable)
- A dividend is a payment a company makes to its shareholders, usually cash, out of profits; set per share and commonly paid quarterly in the US. The board decides whether and how much to pay.
- Dividends are not guaranteed: the board can raise, cut or suspend them.

## Key dates (US, T+1 settlement since May 28, 2024)
- Declaration date: board announces the dividend, the record date and the payment date.
- Ex-dividend date: under T+1, the ex-date is set on the record date (FINRA Rule 11140; NYSE). Buy before the ex-date to receive the dividend; buy on/after it and the seller gets it.
- Record date: the company's list of shareholders of record who will be paid.
- Payment date: when the cash is actually paid.

## Taxes (2026, IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32)
- Qualified dividends are taxed at the long-term capital gains rates: 0%, 15%, 20%.
- 2026 0% ceiling: $49,450 taxable income single / $98,900 married filing jointly; 15% up to $545,500 single / $613,700 MFJ; 20% above. Ordinary (non-qualified) dividends are taxed as ordinary income.

## Dividends can be cut: real dated example
- Intel announced on August 1, 2024 that it would suspend its dividend starting in Q4 2024 (Q2 2024 earnings release / Form 10-Q). Last declared payout: $0.125/share, payable Sept 1, 2024. Intel's 2024 10-K says the CHIPS Act agreement bars dividends for two years.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- 100 shares x $0.50 per share per quarter = $50 per quarter; x 4 quarters = $200 per year.
- Dividend yield = annual dividend per share / share price. Example: $2.00 a year ($0.50 x 4) on a $50 share = 2 / 50 = 4%.
- Assumptions: dividend unchanged all year, price $50, before taxes.

## The surprising fact
- About 4 in 5 S&P 500 companies (409 of them, Q4 2025) pay dividends, and from 1940-2024 dividends averaged about a third (34%) of the index's total return.
