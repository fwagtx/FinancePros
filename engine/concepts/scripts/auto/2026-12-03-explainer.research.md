# Research brief: Lemon laws: what they protect and what they don't
Slot 2026-12-03-explainer (posts Thu Dec 3, 2026, noon CT). Researched Oct 4, 2026. Education, not legal advice.
Note: .gov pages (oag.ca.gov, ag.ny.gov, dca.ca.gov) block direct fetch here; facts confirmed via search results quoting the official pages/statutes, cross-checked with reputable outlets.

## Core facts
1. Every state has a new-car lemon law: all 50 states plus D.C. have lemon laws for new vehicles with substantial defects the maker can't fix (Justia 50-state survey; Nolo; Edmunds). Rules differ state by state.
2. What a lemon is (typical): a defect covered by the warranty that "substantially impairs" use, value or safety, not fixed after a reasonable number of repair attempts. Remedy: manufacturer replaces the vehicle or refunds the purchase price (plus collateral charges, minus a usage allowance) (Minnesota AG "MN Car Laws" ch.1; Nolo).
3. Example presumption, California (Song-Beverly / Tanner Act, Civil Code 1793.22): within 18 months of delivery or 18,000 miles, whichever first, a lemon is presumed if (a) same defect repaired 4+ times and buyer notified maker directly once, or (b) a defect likely to cause death/serious injury repaired 2+ times, or (c) out of service for repair more than 30 cumulative calendar days. (CA leginfo bill text SB-1718; CA AG lemon law page via search; LA County DCBA; McMillan Law Group.)
4. NOT covered (typical): damage from accidents, abuse, neglect, unauthorized modifications; minor defects that don't substantially impair use/value/safety (Minnesota AG handbook; CT OLR report 2009-R-0267).
5. Used cars: generally not covered by state lemon laws. About 10 states have used-car lemon laws: Arizona, Connecticut, Hawaii, Illinois, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Rhode Island (J.D. Power "Used Car Lemon Laws"; iSeeCars; CT OLR 2015-R-0285 used car lemon laws report). Florida: no used-car lemon law (FLHSMV).
   - New York used-car lemon law (NY AG guide): price at least $1,500, bought from NY dealer, more than 18,000 miles/2 years; dealer warranty 90 days/4,000 mi (18,001-36,000 mi), 60 days/3,000 mi (36,001-80,000), 30 days/1,000 mi (80,001-100,000). Presumed lemon after 3 repair attempts or 15+ days out of service. Arbitration through NY AG or court.
6. Federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (1975): applies to consumer products sold with a written warranty, including used cars sold with one; does NOT require anyone to give a warranty; "as is" sales without a warranty are generally outside it (FTC Businessperson's Guide to Federal Warranty Law; Center for Auto Safety). FTC Used Car Rule: dealers must post a Buyers Guide saying "As Is" or warranty (FTC).
7. Attorney fees: California 1794(d): prevailing buyer recovers attorney fees and costs from manufacturer (statute text via search; McMillan Law Group). Magnuson-Moss 15 USC 2310(d)(2): a prevailing consumer "may be allowed by the court to recover" costs including attorney fees (statute). Many state laws vary.
8. Arbitration: many states have manufacturer or state-run arbitration programs; some require it before court; in California, state-certified programs are free to consumers (DCA Arbitration Certification Program; Nolo). Massachusetts: maker can't force its own program (Nolo).

## Surprising fact
Even a winning buyback is not a full refund: California subtracts a usage offset = (miles at first repair attempt for the defect / 120,000) x price paid (Civil Code 1793.2(d)(2)(C); multiple CA lemon-law firm explainers).

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, California rules)
$50,000 car, first taken in for the defect at 6,000 miles.
Offset = 6,000 / 120,000 x $50,000 = 0.05 x $50,000 = $2,500.
(Context: KBB average new-vehicle transaction price was $50,089 in August 2026.)
