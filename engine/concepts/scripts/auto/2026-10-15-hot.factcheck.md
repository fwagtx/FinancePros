# Fact-check: 2026-10-15-hot (Scam alert: fake bank fraud calls)
Checked: October 1, 2026 (posting date Oct 15, 2026). Source read: 2026-10-15-hot.raw.json only.

## Verified claims
- L2 "Move your money to a safe account" = the scam: FCC Consumer Alert on Bank Impersonation Scams (July 21, 2026): "Banks will never call or text and ask you to move your money to protect it." FTC consumer guidance says the same. VERIFIED.
- L3 nearly $1B to business impersonators in 2025, highest losses to bank impersonators: FTC press release (June 2026), "nearly $1 billion to business impersonators with the highest reported losses to bank impersonators" (up from $866M in 2024). VERIFIED.
- L4 imposter scams $3.5B in 2025, $1.2B in 2020: FTC June 2026 release ($3.5B, "nearly tripled since 2020"); FTC 2020 data ($1.2B). 3.5/1.2 = 2.92x, consistent. VERIFIED.
- Chart points: 2020 $1.2B (FTC), 2021 $2.3B (FTC Feb 2022 release; later FTC 2022 release revised it to $2.4B), 2022 $2.6B, 2023 ~$2.7B ("nearly $2.7B"), 2024 $2.95B, 2025 $3.5B. All match FTC figures, see note on 2021.
- L5 script (fraud alert, read back a code, move money): matches FTC description (costliest scams start with a fake security alert, often from a bank; people convinced to move money to "protect" it) and FCC warning not to share verification codes. VERIFIED.
- L6 caller ID spoofing: FCC alert notes fraudsters use caller ID spoofing to pose as banks. VERIFIED.
- L7 example math: $5,000 + $3,000 = $8,000. Correct. Labeled "Example"/"Moved (example)". "Likely for good, says the FTC": FTC says you "probably won't get that money back." Fair paraphrase.
- L8 call the number on the back of your card: consistent with FTC/FCC guidance (FCC says call the number on your statement). Framed descriptively, not as an instruction.

## Issues (all minor)
1. L4 chart, 2021 point = 2.3. The FTC's later (and current) figure for 2021 is $2.4B ("$2.6 billion, up from $2.4 billion in 2021"). Fix: change [2021, 2.3] to [2021, 2.4] so the series uses the latest FTC values.
2. L3 text/say "fake businesses" is slightly inaccurate: the FTC category is business *impersonators* (scammers posing as real businesses). Fix: "In 2025, people reported losing nearly $1 billion to scammers posing as businesses, and fake banks caused the biggest losses." (Stat label already says "Business impersonator losses".)
3. L5 on-screen vs spoken wording differs ("a code to read back, then a transfer to 'protect' your money" vs "read me a code, now move your money to protect it"). Same meaning, no numbers involved; optional to align.
4. Post (ig): "The FTC says banks never call or text asking you to move money to protect it." The verified word-for-word quote is the FCC's (July 2026). Fix: attribute to "The FCC says" (or "FCC and FTC say"), and use the exact quote: "Banks will never call or text and ask you to move your money to protect it."
5. Hook (L1): "Your bank's fraud department will never ask you this." True, specific, creates a strong open loop, about 3 seconds. Acceptable. Optional tighter version: "Your bank will never ask you this." 
6. Stale news / time words: data released June 2026, still the latest annual FTC data on Oct 15; no "this week"/"today" time words. No advice, price predictions or political opinion. Cover "Your Bank Never Asks This" is paid off by L2.

VERDICT: PASS
