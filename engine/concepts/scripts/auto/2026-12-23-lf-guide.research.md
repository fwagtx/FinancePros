## What an index fund is
- Index fund: a mutual fund or ETF that tries to match a market index by holding the stocks in it, instead of a manager picking stocks.
- S&P 500: 500 large U.S. companies picked by an S&P committee; about 80% of U.S. stock market value (Morningstar; PWL Capital). 
- Total U.S. stock market index (e.g. CRSP US Total Market): thousands of stocks (roughly 3,800-4,000 per PWL Capital / ETF.com; say "thousands"), large, mid and small.
- Mutual fund vs ETF: mutual funds are bought/sold once a day at the closing net asset value (NAV); ETFs trade on an exchange all day at market prices that can differ slightly from NAV (SEC Investor Bulletin on ETFs; Wealthspire). Mutual funds may have a minimum initial investment; many brokers let you buy fractional ETF shares (varies by broker - say "many").
- Expense ratio: the yearly cost of owning a fund as a % of assets, taken out of the fund (not billed). 1% = $10 per $1,000 a year (NASAA/Investor.gov). Excludes sales loads and commissions.

## Steps in order
1. Foundation first (education framing): emergency fund. Fed SHED 2025 (May 2026): 63% of adults would cover a $400 emergency with cash or equivalent. High-interest debt: average credit card rate on accounts assessed interest about 22% (Fed G.19, 22.36% August 2026; weak - third-party mirror). Paying off a 22% card is like a guaranteed 22% return on that money; no stock fund promises that. Money needed within a few years usually isn't in stocks because prices can fall.
2. Choose the account:
   - 401(k) with employer match: 2026 employee limit $24,500 (IRS Notice 2025-67, Nov 13, 2025); age 50+ catch-up $8,000. Match = extra money from employer (hypothetical: 50 cents per dollar on first 6% of pay).
   - IRA / Roth IRA: 2026 limit $7,500 total across all IRAs (50+: $8,600). Roth income phase-out 2026: single $153,000-$168,000; married joint $242,000-$252,000.
   - Taxable brokerage account: no contribution limit, no special tax break; dividends and gains taxed.
   - 2027 limits: IRS announces in Nov 2026; check IRS.gov.
3. Open and fund: online brokerage or fund company; need SSN, ID, bank link. SIPC protects customer securities and cash up to $500,000 (including $250,000 cash) if a brokerage firm fails - NOT against market losses (SIPC.org).
   Common mistake: depositing money but never buying a fund - it sits in cash (settlement/sweep). 
4. Pick a broad index fund: check index (S&P 500 vs total market vs international/bond), expense ratio, minimum. Compare average 0.05% (ICI 2025).
5. Automate: automatic contributions each payday; dollar-cost averaging = same amount on a schedule, buys more shares when prices are low. 401(k) payroll deduction is automatic by design.
6. Rebalance: e.g. hypothetical target 80% stocks / 20% bonds; after stocks rise it may drift to 85/15; rebalancing sells/buys or redirects new money back to target. Common: once a year or when drift exceeds a set band. In taxable accounts, selling can trigger taxes; redirecting new money avoids that. Target-date funds rebalance automatically (Nov 18 covered - one line).
7. Stay the course: index funds fall with the market. S&P 500 total return 2008: -37.00%; 2022: -18.11% (S&P DJI via YCharts/Slickcharts). 2009 +26.46%; 2023 +26.29%. Peak-to-trough: closing high 1,565.15 Oct 9, 2007 to 676.53 Mar 9, 2009 (about 57% drop); new record close 1,569.19 on Mar 28, 2013 (UPI, NPR, Reuters, Mar 28, 2013) - about five and a half years.

## Common mistakes
- Money left uninvested in cash after deposit. - Chasing last year's top fund. - Paying high fees for an index-like fund. - Selling in a crash. - Owning several S&P 500 funds thinking it's diversified (they hold the same companies). - Investing money needed soon.
