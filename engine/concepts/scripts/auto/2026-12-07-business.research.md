# Research brief: How market makers make money on the spread (2026-12-07-business, short, evergreen)

Note: sec.gov, finra.org and several news sites are blocked for direct fetch here; figures were confirmed through search results quoting the named official source. Posting date Dec 7, 2026: every figure carries its period.

## How it works (definitions)
- Bid = the price a market maker will pay to buy; ask (offer) = the price it will sell at. Spread = ask minus bid. The market maker stands ready to do both all day and earns the gap when it buys from one customer and sells to another.
- Spread capture works only at huge volume: fractions of a cent per share, millions of shares.
- Inventory risk: between buying and selling, the market maker holds shares; if the price moves against it, the loss can wipe out many trades' worth of spread. Firms hedge and keep holding periods short.
- Payment for order flow (PFOF): market makers pay retail brokers to send them customer orders. Retail orders are attractive because they are small and less likely to be "informed" (less adverse-selection risk).
- SEC Rule 606: brokers must publish quarterly reports on where they route non-directed orders and the payments they receive. Rule 605: market centers publish execution-quality statistics (amendments adopted March 2024; compliance date extended from Dec 14, 2025 to Aug 1, 2026 per Federal Register / law-firm summaries; current status not re-checked, so not used in script).
- PFOF debate (neutral): critics see a conflict of interest (brokers paid to route orders); supporters say it funds $0-commission trading and that market makers give price improvement vs. the quoted spread. The SEC withdrew its 2022/2023 "Order Competition Rule" proposal on June 12, 2025, along with 13 other proposals (Proskauer, Seward & Kissel, NatLawReview).

## Key company numbers
### Virtu Financial (public market maker), FY2025 Form 10-K and Q4 2025 release (Jan 29, 2026)
- Adjusted Net Trading Income (ANTI) 2025: $2,145.3 million, up 34.3% from $1,597.7 million in 2024.
- Average daily ANTI 2025: $8.6 million (248.5 trading days), vs $6.4 million in 2024.
- Trading income, net 2025: $2,436.7 million. Total revenues $3,632.1 million. Net income $912.3 million.
- "Brokerage, exchange, clearance fees and payments for order flow, net" 2025: $769.8 million (combined line; PFOF not broken out).
- Segment (earnings call transcript only): Market Making ANTI $6.7M/day 2025; Execution Services $1.9M/day.
- Latest quarter, Q2 2026 (release July 30, 2026): ANTI $717.9 million, up 26.4% y/y; average daily ANTI $11.6 million; net income $284.9 million.
- Surprising fact (historical, 2014 S-1): Virtu reported only ONE losing trading day out of 1,238 trading days from Jan 1, 2009 to Dec 31, 2013 (Quartz, Finance Magnates quoting the S-1).

## PFOF amounts
- Moody's Ratings (reported by Investment Executive, April 2026), analysis of Rule 606 disclosures of 14 large US brokers, year 2025: PFOF touched 27% of US equity volume and 51% of options volume. Broker PFOF revenue: about $3.0B from options, $1.2B from non-S&P 500 stocks, $0.2B from S&P 500 stocks = about $4.4 billion total (our sum of Moody's components). Called record levels. Webull, Robinhood, TradeStation most reliant.
- S3 aggregation of 606 reports (Global Trading): Q1 2025 market makers paid $1.19B for US retail flow, a record quarter; Citadel Securities largest payer.
- Broker 606 example: Firstrade Q4 2025 report: Virtu Americas pays $0.0015 per share for stocks priced $5.00 or more ($0.000225 below $5).
- Robinhood 606 (Q2 2026, per search summary): payment set at a fixed share of the spread (12.35% during regular hours) - not independently confirmed; not used.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labelled as example)
- Quote: bid $100.00, ask $100.01 -> spread $0.01.
- Market maker buys 1,000,000 shares at $100.00 and sells 1,000,000 at $100.01: 1,000,000 x $0.01 = $10,000 gross spread captured (before PFOF, exchange fees, hedging costs).
- Inventory risk: if it is holding 1,000,000 shares and the price drops 1 cent before it sells, it loses $10,000, the whole day's example capture.
