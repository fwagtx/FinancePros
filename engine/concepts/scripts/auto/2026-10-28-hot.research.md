# 2026-10-28-hot research brief: Scam alert, fake crypto exchange customer support
Researched October 1, 2026. Posts October 28, 2026. Scope: fake support numbers/accounts/reps impersonating exchanges (Coinbase etc.). Not pig-butchering, bank-fraud calls, voice clones or job scams (covered elsewhere).

## Timely angle (news hook)
- Brooklyn District Attorney's Office, Sept 23, 2026: Ronald Spektor, 23, of Sheepshead Bay, Brooklyn, sentenced to an indeterminate 4 to 12 years in prison. He pleaded guilty Sept 2, 2026 to all 31 counts (incl. first-degree grand larceny, first-degree money laundering, first-degree criminal possession of stolen property). Conviction by plea, so not an allegation.
- What he did (Brooklyn DA, charged Dec 19, 2025): posed as a Coinbase representative, told users their assets were at risk from a hacker, convinced them to transfer crypto to a new wallet he controlled, then laundered via swapping/mixing services and crypto gambling sites.
- Amount: nearly $16 million (reported as $15.94M / $15,944,000) from about 100 Coinbase users. Restitution of nearly $16M ordered; forfeiture of >$500,000 in cash, crypto and property.
- Sources: brooklynda.org (Dec 19, 2025 and Sept 23, 2026 releases), crypto.news, Crowdfund Insider, CryptoSlate, Bitcoin.com News (cross-checked).

## Worked example (exact math)
- Average loss per victim (estimate, since victim count is "about 100"): $15,944,000 / 100 = $159,440, about $159K per victim on average.

## Other numbers
- FBI IC3 2025 report: total reported losses $20.877B (up 26%); cryptocurrency-related complaints 181,565 with more than $11B losses, up 22% from 2024; crypto investment fraud $7.228B (do not use - covered by pig-butchering short).
- FTC (press release June 2026, Consumer Sentinel 2025 data): total reported fraud losses $15.9B in 2025 (vs $12.5B in 2024); imposter scams $3.5B in 2025, most-reported category; business impersonators nearly $1B (bank impersonators highest); bank payments then cryptocurrency were the highest aggregate-loss payment methods in 2025.
- Coinbase Form 8-K, May 2025: paid-off overseas support contractors/employees leaked customer data affecting less than 1% of monthly transacting users (69,461 people per later filings); data used to impersonate Coinbase; Coinbase estimated costs of $180M-$400M; no passwords or private keys compromised. (Context only, not used in script.)

## Exact quotes (word-for-word)
- FTC (consumer.ftc.gov, "What To Know About Cryptocurrency and Scams"): "No legitimate business is going to demand you send cryptocurrency in advance – not to buy something, and not to protect your money."
- Coinbase Help Center ("Technical support and impersonation scams"): "no one will ever ask for your password, 2FA codes, or for you to transfer assets to a specific or new address, account, vault, or wallet."
- Massachusetts (mass.gov, "Beware Cryptocurrency Scams"): "Con artists may create fictitious websites or post customer support numbers for you to find while doing an internet search."
- Coinbase (company blog): its only official support handles are @CoinbaseSupport on X and r/CoinbaseSupport on Reddit.

## Surprising fact
- The "support" call that drains the account usually says it is protecting the money: the pitch is "you were hacked, move it to safety". Also, fake "Coinbase support number" pages are seeded across ordinary websites (observed in search results during research, e.g., user-content pages on large sites), matching mass.gov's warning.
