# Fact-check: 2026-11-17-hot (Black Friday price myth)

Checked Oct 2, 2026, for a Nov 17, 2026 post. Only the script JSON was read.

## Verified
- Which? (UK), published Nov 25, 2025 ("Don't believe the hype: Most Black Friday deals the same price or cheaper at other times of the year"): 175 home, tech and health products from **8 UK retailers** on offer on **Black Friday 2024**, with prices tracked for six months on either side. 83% were "cheaper or the same on at least one other date outside of the four-week sales period". On Black Friday itself "there were no deals at all that were at their cheapest of the year". Lines 2-4 and the post are accurate.
- Adobe, US: Black Friday 2025 online spending was $11.8B, a record and up 9.1% from $10.8B. The series 2021 $8.9B, 2022 $9.12B, 2023 $9.8B, 2024 $10.8B and 2025 $11.8B checks out against Adobe figures reported by CNBC, TechCrunch and others. 2021 was slightly below 2020, so the chart is fine but no line should say "every year a record".
- Adobe 2025 Holiday Recap (Jan 2026): "Black Friday was the most discounted day of the season with average discounts across measured categories of -23.7% compared to -22.2% in 2024." TVs peaked at **24.3%** off (24.2% in 2024), and TVs hit their deepest single-day discount on Black Friday. The January full-season recap is the authoritative source, so use 24.3% and not the earlier 22% figure.
- Math: $500 x (1 - 0.243) = $378.50. Correct, and the example is labelled as one.
- Spoken and on-screen numbers match everywhere. Line 6 says "twenty-two point two" without "percent", which is acceptable.
- No political content. No price predictions.
