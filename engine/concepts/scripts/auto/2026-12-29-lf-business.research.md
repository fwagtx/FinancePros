# Research brief: How insurance companies make money: float explained (lf-business, posts Tue Dec 29, 2026)

Prepared Friday, October 30, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its year/period. Scope: US property/casualty (P&C) insurers (car, home, business insurance). Avoid overlap with Dec 8 (Berkshire float) and Oct 26 (why premiums rose): this video is about the INDUSTRY business model. Berkshire: one line max (or none).

## Timely angle (evergreen-safe)
- Verisk/APCIA, Sept 2, 2026: first half of 2026 US P&C combined ratio 92.7 (vs 96.5 in H1 2025); net underwriting gain $31.7B (vs $11.6B H1 2025); net income after tax $77.8B (+53% from $50.9B). Net written premium growth slowed to 2.1% (vs 5.2% in H1 2025) as rate increases moderated. Verisk's Saurabh Khemka: "First-half results should not be mistaken as evidence that underlying risk has diminished." (quote as reported by trade press coverage of the release).
- Q1 2026 combined ratio 92.4 vs 99.2 in Q1 2025 (Verisk/APCIA, June 23, 2026).

## The surprise (hook)
- In 2023, US P&C insurers LOST about $21-22 billion on underwriting (Verisk/APCIA initial estimate $21.1B; later releases cite $21.8B / "$22 billion"), combined ratio 101.6, yet still earned net income after taxes of $35.7 billion (Verisk/APCIA, 2023 full-year release, spring 2024). The investment side (float + surplus) paid for it.
- Same story 2021: $3.8B underwriting loss, combined ratio 99.6, net income $61.9B (Verisk/APCIA 2021 release, spring 2022).

## Two profit engines
1. Underwriting profit = premiums earned - (claims + claim-handling costs + operating expenses incl. commissions). Measured by the combined ratio.
2. Investment income on float: premiums arrive up front, claims are paid later (months for car repairs, years for injury lawsuits). Meanwhile the money is invested, mostly in bonds. That pool of "other people's money" is the float. Insurers also invest their own capital (policyholders' surplus).
- Industry 2025 (AM Best First Look, Mar 23, 2026): underwriting income ~ $60.9B (nearly triple 2024's $22.1B); net investment income +9.1% to $91.4B; pre-tax operating income +43.2% to $153.1B. So even in a great underwriting year, investment income was bigger.
- Verisk/APCIA 2025 (Mar 25, 2026): underwriting gain about $63B (vs $23B in 2024); combined ratio 92.9 (vs 96.6 in 2024); net income after tax $148B (vs $169B in 2024) because realized capital gains fell to $23B from $79B; net written premiums +4.8% to $971B; net earned premiums +6.3% to $953B; policyholders' surplus $1.2 trillion (from $1.1T). Covers ~97.8% of private US P&C business.

## Combined ratio explained
- Combined ratio = (losses + loss adjustment expenses + underwriting expenses) / premiums. Under 100 = underwriting profit; over 100 = underwriting loss. 92.9 means 92.9 cents paid out per $1 of premium -> 7.1 cents underwriting profit.
- AM Best: catastrophe losses added 7.6 points to the 2025 combined ratio (8.8 points in 2024). AM Best: $18.1B favorable reserve development in 2025; accident-year combined ratio ex that 94.9.

## Interest rates boosted investment income
- Federal Reserve kept its target near zero in 2020-21, began raising in March 2022 and reached 5.25%-5.50% in July 2023 (Federal Reserve). Bonds bought after that pay higher coupons.
- Industry net investment income: 2021 $54.3B (Verisk/APCIA, up from $51.7B in 2020); 2023 record $73.9B (AM Best special report, May 2024, tied to higher rates); 2025 $91.4B (AM Best First Look, Mar 2026). Note: 2021 figure is Verisk/APCIA, 2025 is AM Best; different methodologies, label sources on screen.
- Progressive (10-K FY2025): recurring investment income pretax book yield 4.1% in 2025 vs 3.9% in 2024; FTE total return 7.3% (2025) vs 4.6% (2024); 10-K attributes higher recurring investment income to reinvesting cash and higher-coupon bonds.

## Real company: Progressive (FY2025, year ended Dec 31, 2025; 10-K filed Feb 2026 + Dec 2025 results release Jan 28, 2026)
- Net premiums written $83.2B (+$8.8B, +12% vs 2024); net premiums earned +15% (~$81.7B per Fintool summary of release; treat exact earned figure as secondary).
- Companywide combined ratio 87.4 (2025) vs 88.8 (2024) -> 10-K: underwriting profit margin 12.6% vs 11.2%. Long-standing goal: at least a 4% calendar-year underwriting profit, i.e., combined ratio of 96 or better (10-K).
- Investment income $3,583M (2025) vs $2,832M (2024), +26.5% (Dec 2025 release; Zacks).
- Net income ~$11.3B (2025) vs ~$8.5B (2024) (Zacks/Fintool from release).
- Investment portfolio fair value $97.4B at Dec 31, 2025 vs $80.3B at Dec 31, 2024 (10-K).
- Policies in force 38.6M at year-end 2025, +10%.
- OUR MATH (approx): underwriting profit ~ 12.6% x ~$81.7B earned ~ $10.3B, about 3x investment income of $3.6B. Progressive is unusual: underwriting is its bigger engine; the industry overall earns more from investments.

## Worked example (hypothetical, our math; round numbers)
Example insurer collects $2,000 premium on one car policy for a year.
- Normal year: claims and claim-handling $1,400 (70%), expenses/commissions $500 (25%) -> combined ratio 95 -> underwriting profit $100.
- Float: suppose the $1,400 meant for claims sits invested for about a year at 4% -> $56 investment income (1,400 x 0.04 = 56).
- Total: $100 + $56 = $156.
- Bad year: claims $1,540 + $500 expenses = $2,040 -> combined ratio 102 -> underwriting loss $40; investment $1,540 x 4% = $61.60 -> about $22 profit overall. (=$21.60)
Lesson: an insurer can lose on underwriting and still make money; but at combined ratio 110, ($2,200 out) loss $200 > investment income -> loses money.

## Catastrophes
- January 2025 Los Angeles (Palisades and Eaton) fires: Verisk PCS estimated $28B-$35B insured property losses (Jan 22, 2025); Aon $37.5B; KCC $28B, costliest wildfires in US history. Prior record Camp Fire 2018 $12.4B (Verisk).
- 2017: combined ratio 103.7, $23.2B underwriting loss (ISO/PCI) after Harvey, Irma, Maria.
- 2025 still a strong year because the rest of the year had lower cat losses (cats 7.6 points per AM Best).

## Reinsurance (brief)
- Insurers buy insurance for themselves (reinsurance) to cap catastrophe losses. Global reinsurance capital at record levels at year-end 2025 (Aon $785B; Gallagher Re $648B dedicated reinsurance capital - broker measures differ).
- Jan 1, 2026 renewals: risk-adjusted global property-cat reinsurance rates-on-line fell an average 14.7% (Howden Re), largest annual decrease since 2014; AM Best said 10-20% drops and revised its global reinsurance outlook to stable from positive (Jan 20, 2026).

## What it means for a normal person (neutral, no advice)
- Premiums are priced to cover expected claims + expenses; investment income is a cushion, so when rates were near zero, more of the profit had to come from premiums.
- Insurers profit when claims are fewer/smaller than expected; that's why they investigate claims, set deductibles, and price by risk. Not a judgment on any insurer.
- State insurance regulators oversee rates and solvency; insurers keep reserves for future claims.
- Premium growth slowed to 2.1% in H1 2026 (Verisk/APCIA) as rate increases moderated; no predictions.
- Choosing coverage: deductible vs premium trade-off is math; coverage limits matter more in catastrophe years. Describe, never advise.
