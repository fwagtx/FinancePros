# Research brief: How to read your pay stub and W-2 (The Guide, posts Wed Nov 25, 2026)

Researched October 2, 2026. All figures are for tax year 2026 unless labeled. Timely angle: the W-2s for tax year 2026 arrive in early 2027 and are the first to carry new Box 12 codes (TT overtime, TP tips, TA Trump account). Due date to employees: February 1, 2027 (January 31, 2027 is a Sunday).

## Core facts (with sources)

### Payroll (FICA) taxes, 2026
- Social Security tax: 6.2% employee (and 6.2% employer) on wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 (up from $176,100 in 2025, +$8,400, about 4.8%). Max employee Social Security tax 2026: $11,439 (184,500 x 6.2%). Sources: SSA contribution and benefit base (ssa.gov/oact/cola/cbb.html, blocked to fetch; confirmed via The Tax Adviser, Oct 2025; Paycor; Patriot Software).
- Medicare tax: 1.45% employee (and 1.45% employer) on ALL wages, no cap.
- Additional Medicare Tax: extra 0.9% employee-only. Employers must withhold it on wages over $200,000 in a calendar year regardless of filing status. Actual liability thresholds: $200,000 single, $250,000 married filing jointly, $125,000 married filing separately. Source: IRS Topic 560 (via search snippet); Patriot Software.
- Combined employee FICA under the wage base: 7.65% (6.2 + 1.45).

### Retirement and health limits, 2026
- 401(k) employee elective deferral limit: $24,500 (2025: $23,500). Catch-up age 50+: $8,000. Ages 60-63 "super catch-up": $11,250. Source: IRS retirement topics pages, Fidelity, Empower (IRS Notice 2025-67).
- Traditional vs Roth 401(k): traditional deferrals are pre-tax (lower Box 1 now; withdrawals taxed later as income). Roth 401(k) contributions are after-tax (stay in Box 1, code AA); qualified withdrawals are tax-free. Source: IRS Roth comparison / TaxAct, LegalClarity (Box 12 AA).
- HSA limits 2026: $4,400 self-only, $8,750 family, +$1,000 catch-up at 55+. Source: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-19 via Fidelity, Taxstra.

### Federal income tax, 2026
- Standard deduction 2026: $16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly. Source: IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (via NTU, CRS RL34498, Copera).
- Single brackets 2026: 10% on $0-$12,400; 12% on $12,400-$50,400; 22% above that (to next threshold). Source: same.
- Form W-4: redesigned for 2020; no more withholding allowances; five-step form ("Employee's Withholding Certificate"). Employees hired before 2020 don't have to file a new one unless they change withholding. Source: IRS FAQs on the 2020 Form W-4; Paychex.

### W-2 boxes (what each means)
- Box 1: wages, tips, other compensation subject to federal income tax. Reduced by traditional (pre-tax) 401(k) deferrals AND by Section 125 cafeteria-plan deductions (pre-tax health/dental/vision premiums, FSAs) and pre-tax HSA contributions.
- Box 2: federal income tax withheld.
- Box 3: Social Security wages (capped at $184,500 in 2026). Reduced by Section 125 deductions and pre-tax HSA, but NOT by traditional 401(k) deferrals.
- Box 4: Social Security tax withheld (6.2% of Box 3).
- Box 5: Medicare wages (no cap). Same reductions as Box 3. Box 3 and Box 5 usually match unless pay is above the wage base.
- Box 6: Medicare tax withheld (1.45% of Box 5, plus 0.9% over $200,000).
- Box 12 codes: D = elective deferrals to 401(k) (explains why Box 1 is lower than Box 3/5). AA = Roth 401(k) contributions (these stay in Box 1). W = employer HSA contributions including employee pre-tax HSA through a cafeteria plan (excluded from Boxes 1, 3, 5). DD = total cost of employer-sponsored health coverage (employer + employee share), information only, not taxable. Employer match is NOT in Box 12.
- New for tax year 2026 W-2: Box 12 code TT (qualified overtime, premium portion only), TP (qualified tips), TA (employer Trump account contributions); Box 14 split, new Box 14b for Treasury Tipped Occupation Code. Related deductions run 2025-2028. Source: IRS 2026 General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (via TaxBandits, Experian Employer Services, Patriot Software).
- Boxes 15-20: state and local wages and tax.
- Deadline: employers must furnish W-2 for tax year 2026 by February 1, 2027 (Jan 31, 2027 is a Sunday). Source: TaxBandits; IRS W-2/W-3 instructions.
- Wrong W-2: ask the employer for a corrected Form W-2c. If still missing/uncorrected by end of February, the IRS can be contacted (800-829-1040); it sends the employer a letter and provides Form 4852 (substitute W-2). Source: Nolo; IRS guidance (via search).

### Pay stub anatomy
- Gross pay (before anything comes out) -> pre-tax deductions (401(k), Section 125 health premiums, HSA/FSA) -> taxes (federal income tax withholding, Social Security, Medicare, state/local) -> post-tax deductions (Roth 401(k), union dues, garnishments) -> net pay (take-home).
- Each line usually has a "current" column and a "YTD" (year-to-date) column. Last stub of the year's YTD numbers should roughly reconcile to the W-2.
- Pay frequency (BLS, February 2023, private establishments): biweekly 43.0%, weekly 27.0%, semimonthly 19.8%, monthly 10.3%. Biweekly = 26 paychecks a year (occasionally 27). Source: BLS CES "Length of pay period" (bls.gov/ces/publications/length-pay-period.htm).

## Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
Assumptions: $60,000 salary, single, paid biweekly (26 checks), 5% traditional 401(k), $100 pre-tax health premium per check (Section 125), lives in a state with no income tax (e.g., Texas), no other income, standard deduction.
Per paycheck:
- Gross: 60,000 / 26 = $2,307.69
- 401(k) 5%: $115.38
- Health premium: $100.00
- FICA wages: 2,307.69 - 100 = $2,207.69 (401(k) does not reduce it)
- Social Security 6.2%: $136.88
- Medicare 1.45%: $32.01 (FICA total $168.89)
- Federal taxable wages: 2,307.69 - 100 - 115.38 = $2,092.31
- Estimated federal income tax: about $167.23 per check (annual estimate below / 26; actual withholding follows IRS Pub 15-T and the W-4, so it can differ)
- Estimated net pay: 2,307.69 - 115.38 - 100 - 136.88 - 32.01 - 167.23 = $1,756.19
Annual (the W-2):
- Box 1: 60,000 - 3,000 (401k) - 2,600 (health) = $54,400
- Box 3 and Box 5: 60,000 - 2,600 = $57,400
- Box 12 code D: $3,000
- Box 4: 57,400 x 6.2% = $3,558.80; Box 6: 57,400 x 1.45% = $832.30 (sum of rounded per-check amounts may differ by cents)
- Estimated federal tax: taxable income 54,400 - 16,100 = 38,300; tax = 10% x 12,400 ($1,240) + 12% x 25,900 ($3,108) = $4,348.
Why Box 1 < Box 3/5: the $3,000 401(k) skips income tax but not FICA. Box 1 is $3,000 below Box 3/5, and $5,600 below the $60,000 salary.

## The surprising fact
Your W-2 can show three different "wages" (salary $60,000, Box 1 $54,400, Box 3/5 $57,400 in the example) and all of them are correct. Many people think Box 1 is wrong because it isn't their salary.

## Common mistakes
1. Expecting Box 1 to equal salary.
2. Ignoring YTD columns (where you'd spot the Social Security tax stopping at the wage base, a missing 401(k) deferral, or a wrong premium).
3. Not checking name/SSN on the W-2 (mismatch can delay processing).
4. Thinking Box 12 DD is taxable income (it isn't).
5. Old W-4 left unchanged after life changes (marriage, second job) -> withholding off.
6. Two jobs: each employer withholds Social Security up to the wage base; excess Social Security withheld across employers can be claimed as a credit on the tax return (general rule; not verified in this session's searches; omit from script or keep general).
