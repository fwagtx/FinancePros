# Research brief: The pain of paying (why cards make you spend more than cash)
Prepared Nov 23, 2026 for posting Fri Jan 22, 2027. Evergreen; no news hook needed (no timely angle used).

## Key study (the surprising fact)
- Prelec, D. & Simester, D. (2001). "Always Leave Home Without It: A Further Investigation of the Credit-Card Effect on Willingness to Pay." Marketing Letters 12(1), 5-12 (Feb 2001). DOI 10.1023/A:1008196717017.
- Study 1: MBA students in a classroom bid (second-price sealed-bid auction, real transaction) on a pair of sold-out Boston Celtics tickets (Celtics vs Miami Heat, last regular-season game, Boston Garden). Half were told payment would be cash, half credit card.
  - Celtics tickets: cash mean $28.51 (N=31); credit card mean $60.64 (N=33); credit card premium +113%. Medians: cash $25.00, card $41.00.
  - Red Sox tickets: card mean $15.92, premium +76%. Banners: card mean $5.29, premium +59% (not significant).
  - Study 2 (restaurant certificate with known value): the premium did NOT always arise; authors "offer no theory" for its size. -> present as "in one experiment", not universal.
- Our math: $60.64 / $28.51 = 2.127 -> about 2.1x (i.e., +113%).
- Exact year the auction ran is not stated in our sources (paper published 2001; game at the Boston Garden) -> say "a study published in 2001".

## Pain of paying (neuroscience)
- Knutson, Rick, Wimmer, Prelec & Loewenstein (2007), "Neural predictors of purchases," Neuron 53(1):147-156 (Jan 4, 2007). fMRI: excessive prices activated the insula (a region associated with anticipated loss), and insula activation (with nucleus accumbens and mPFC) independently predicted purchase decisions; insula activation preceded decisions not to buy.
- Mazar, Plassmann, Robitaille & Lindner (2016/2017 working paper), "Pain of Paying? A Metaphor Gone Literal": fMRI suggests paying is experienced as affective (emotional) pain, not physical pain.
- Concept: Prelec & Loewenstein (1998) "The Red and the Black" coined "pain of paying"; cards separate buying from paying, dulling the sting.

## Neutral takeaway (framed as following from the research, not advice)
- One idea that follows from the research: before a big card purchase, ask whether you'd pay that amount in cash.
