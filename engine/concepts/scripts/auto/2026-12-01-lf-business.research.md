## The surprising fact (hook)
- In 2025, American Express paid Delta $8.2B (Delta FY2025 results / 10-K). Delta's 2025 GAAP net income was $5,005M (~$5.0B). The card deal brought in more than the whole airline's net profit (by $3.2B).
- American Airlines 2025: cash payments from co-branded credit card and other partners $6.2B (AA FY2025 10-K); net income $111M ($0.17/share) (AA Q4/FY2025 release, Jan 27, 2026; Dallas News).

## Delta and American Express
- Partnership began 1996 (Fortune, Apr 3, 2026; CNBC Apr 2, 2019).
- April 2019: 11-year renewal; exclusive Delta SkyMiles Amex cards through end of 2029 (CNBC, The Points Guy, OMAAT, Apr 2019). 2018 payments: $3.4B; target then "nearly $7B by 2023".
- Remuneration series (Delta earnings releases / 10-Ks):
  - 2018: $3.4B (CNBC Apr 2019, Delta)
  - 2019: $4.1B (2021's $4.0B was "98% restored vs 2019" -> ~ $4.1B; Delta Jan 2022 release)
  - 2020: $2.9B, down 30% (Delta Jan 14, 2021 release)
  - 2021: $4.0B (Delta Jan 2022 release)
  - 2022: ~$5.5B (Delta Jan 2023 release)
  - 2023: $6.8B, +22% (Delta Jan 2024 release)
  - 2024: $7.4B (Delta Jan 2025 release; TheStreet)
  - 2025: $8.2B, +11% (Delta Jan 13, 2026 release; FY2025 10-K)
  - Long-term target: $10B a year ("expects to grow to $10 billion over the next few years", FY2025 10-K)
- Q2 2026 (Apr-Jun): Amex remuneration $2.4B, +16% y/y; seventh straight quarter of double-digit cardholder-spend growth; loyalty revenue +19% (Delta Jul 10, 2026 release).
- 2025: more than 1 million card acquisitions for the fourth consecutive year (Delta FY2025 release).
- Delta 2025: operating revenue $63.4B; pre-tax income $6.2B (9.8% margin); operating income $5.8B; net income $5,005M (Delta Jan 13, 2026 release; aviationmetric).
- Delta net income series (GAAP): 2021 $280M; 2022 $1,318M; 2023 $4,609M; 2024 $3,457M; 2025 $5,005M (Delta 8-K earnings releases). Amex remuneration exceeded Delta's net income every year 2021-2025.
- Fortune (Apr 3, 2026): card deal ~ "$8 billion, or about 10%, of Delta's revenue in 2025" (Fortune's framing). Our own ratio: $8.2B / $63.4B = 12.9% (remuneration is cash; not all booked as revenue in the same year).
- Amex FY2025 10-K: Delta cobrand portfolio ~13% of Amex worldwide billed business and ~21% of worldwide Card Member loans (as of Dec 31, 2025).
- Amex 2025 worldwide billed business ~$1.67T (View from the Wing, citing Amex) -> Delta card spend est. 13% x $1.67T = ~$217B. $8.2B / $217B = ~3.8 cents per dollar (View from the Wing estimate). Treat as estimate.

## How miles are sold (Delta 10-K accounting, Revenue Recognition note)
- Loyalty program has two kinds of revenue arrangements: (1) tickets that earn miles, (2) sale of miles to participating companies (credit card, hotels, car rental, etc.).
- For the Amex marketing agreements, Delta allocates the cash to products delivered based on relative selling prices: award travel (miles), priority boarding, baggage fee waivers, lounge access, and use of the Delta brand.
- Travel portion is deferred and recognized as passenger revenue when miles are redeemed and travel occurs; brand/marketing elements recognized as delivered.
- Miles valued by equivalent ticket value (ETV), adjusted for miles not likely to be redeemed ("mileage breakage").
- Breakage definition: miles that are never redeemed. (Older data point: United expected 24% of miles to expire unused in 2012 - single-source, NOT used in script.)

## Loyalty programs worth more than airlines (2020-2021 financings)
- United, June 15, 2020 8-K / investor presentation: MileagePlus as collateral; valued at ~$21.9B = 12x MileagePlus Holdings 2019 EBITDA. 2019: $5.3B cash from sales of miles (~12% of United revenue), $1.8B EBITDA (~26% of adjusted EBITDAR), ~34% margin; 71% of 2019 cash from sales came from third-party partners, mainly Chase; 100M+ members (United 8-K; Skift; View from the Wing; Cranky Flier).
- Financing: initially $5B, United ultimately raised $6.8B secured by MileagePlus (mid-2020) (Skift/ substack summary; CNN). 
- United market value mid-2020: ~$9.2B-$10.1B (LoyaltyLobby, Skift, July 2020 share count). One blog says ~$13B. Either way, below the $21.9B program value. [Moderately sourced; script says "roughly $10 billion".]
- American, March 2021: raised $10B backed by AAdvantage (upsized from $7.5B); third-party appraisal valued AAdvantage at $19.5B-$31.5B; used part to repay Treasury loan (CNBC Mar 8, 2021; FlyerTalk; AA Q1 2021 release).

## American Airlines and Citi
- Dec 2024: Citi to become exclusive issuer of AAdvantage co-branded cards in the US in 2026 under a new 10-year agreement; Citi agreed to acquire Barclays' AA card portfolio and transition cardmembers in 2026 (AA/Citi releases Dec 2024; OMAAT).
- AA cash remuneration from co-brand card and other partners: ~$5.6B (12 months to Sep 30, 2024); $6.1B in 2024 (+17% vs 2023, incl. one-time payment tied to new deal); $6.2B in 2025 (AA FY2025 10-K).
- AA expects ~10% annual growth; as remuneration approaches $10B per year (by end of decade), annual pre-tax income projected ~$1.5B higher vs 2024 (AA release Dec 2024 / Q4 2024 8-K). Company projection, not guarantee.

- IATA Dec 9, 2025: 2025 net profit $39.5B, net margin 3.9%; 2026 forecast $41B, 3.9%.
- IATA June 2025: 2025 forecast $36.0B, 3.7% margin, $7.20 per passenger (earlier forecast, superseded).

## Regulators (neutral)
- May 9, 2024: CFPB and DOT joint hearing on airline and credit card rewards, moderated by CFPB Director Rohit Chopra and Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg (CFPB event page; ABA Banking Journal; Consumer Finance Monitor).
- CFPB report (May 2024): 1,200+ complaints about credit card rewards in 2023, more than 70% above pre-pandemic levels; themes: marketing vs terms mismatch, devaluation, technical redemption issues, revoked points.
- Sept 5, 2024: DOT inquiry; letters to American, Delta, Southwest, United on devaluation, hidden/dynamic pricing, extra fees, reduced competition; reports due in 90 days (DOT release; Fortune; Dallas News).
- Buttigieg (DOT release, Sept 2024): rewards programs "bring real value" ... "unlike a traditional savings account, these rewards are controlled by a company that can unilaterally change their value." (quote as given in search-result excerpt of DOT release; verify wording.)

## Credit Card Competition Act (neutral)
- Durbin (D-IL) / Marshall (R-KS). First introduced 2022, reintroduced 2023 (expired without action). Durbin-Marshall tried to attach it to the GENIUS Act (2025); airlines including American and United opposed (Payments Dive).
- Jan 13, 2026: reintroduced in the Senate the same day President Trump endorsed it on Truth Social (Marshall release; Payments Dive; Consumer Finance Monitor Jan 16, 2026).
- What it does: requires card-issuing banks with more than $100B in assets to enable at least two unaffiliated networks on their cards, so merchants get routing choices beyond Visa/Mastercard (Bloomberg Law; Durbin/Marshall releases).
- Supporters: merchants/retailers (lower swipe fees). Opponents: banks, card networks, airlines (Airlines for America "Protect Our Points") say it could cut rewards funding. Forbes (Jul 23, 2026): card industry spending ~$200M opposing; big-box retailers support. Status: not law as of the latest info found (July 2026).
