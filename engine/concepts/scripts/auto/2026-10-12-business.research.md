# Research brief: How Netflix makes money, and why it added an ad plan
Slot 2026-10-12-business (posts Mon Oct 12, 2026). Researched Sept 30, 2026.

## Core business (FY2025, Form 10-K for year ended Dec 31, 2025; Q4'25 shareholder letter Jan 20, 2026)
- FY2025 revenue: $45,183,036 thousand = $45.2B, +16% YoY (+17% FX-neutral).
- FY2025 operating income: $13.33B; operating margin 29.5% (vs 26.7% in 2024).
- FY2025 net income: $10.98B.
- Paid memberships: crossed 325 million in Q4 2025 (Netflix stopped reporting quarterly membership counts starting 2025).
- Revenue is overwhelmingly membership (subscription) fees; the 10-K attributes 2025 growth to membership growth, price increases and advertising.

## Ads
- Q4'25 letter (Jan 20, 2026): "Ad revenue rose more than 2.5x to over $1.5B" in 2025.
- Share of revenue (worked): $1.5B / $45.2B = 3.3%, so ads were about 3% of 2025 revenue; everything else (~97%) is mainly subscription fees.
- 2026 outlook (company guidance, not our prediction): ad revenue expected to roughly double, to about $3B (Q4'25 and Q2'26 letters). FY2026 revenue guidance narrowed in July 2026 to $51.0B-$51.4B.
- May 2026 upfront (Variety, Deadline, May 2026): ad tier reaches more than 250 million monthly active viewers (up from 190M cited Nov 2025); over 60% of sign-ups in ad markets choose the ads plan; 4,000+ active advertisers (Q1 2026).

## Why it added ads
- Q1 2022 (reported Apr 19, 2022): Netflix lost 200,000 subscribers, its first decline in paid users in more than a decade (CNBC, NBC News, CBS). Shares fell ~25% after hours. It said it would look at an ad-supported lower price and tackle password sharing.
- Reed Hastings (then co-CEO), April 2022 earnings interview: "Those who have followed Netflix know I've been against the complexity of advertising and in favor of the simplicity of a subscription. But as much as I'm a fan of that, I'm a bigger fan of consumer choice." (wording varies slightly across outlets; the fragment "I'm a bigger fan of consumer choice" is consistent across all).
- Ad plan ("Basic with Ads") launched November 2022 at $6.99 in the US.

## US prices (effective March 26, 2026; CNBC, Deadline, Hollywood Reporter)
- Standard with ads: $7.99 -> $8.99/month
- Standard (no ads): $17.99 -> $19.99/month
- Premium: $24.99 -> $26.99/month

## Latest quarter (Q2 2026, reported July 16, 2026)
- Revenue $12.6B (+13% YoY), operating income $4.2B, operating margin 33.4%.

## Worked example (US list prices, March 2026)
- Price gap per member: $19.99 - $8.99 = $11.00 a month; x 12 = $132.00 a year.
- That is the per-member revenue the ads plan gives up on price versus Standard, which ads have to make up for that member to bring in as much (simple illustration; ignores taxes, other countries, and members who would not have paid $19.99 at all).
- Ads share: $1.5B / $45.2B = 3.3%.

## Surprising fact
- Despite all the attention on the ad plan, ads were only about 3% of Netflix's 2025 revenue, while over 60% of new sign-ups (in ad markets) now pick the ads plan.
