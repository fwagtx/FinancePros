# Research brief: Bull vs bear markets, with acorns (Explainer, Acorn Economics, posts Oct 23, 2026)

- Definitions (common convention): a bear market is a decline of 20% or more from a recent peak. A bull market is a sustained rise, often measured as +20% from a low.
- Hartford Funds, "10 Things You Should Know About Bear Markets" (S&P 500 data since 1928):
  - 27 bear markets and 28 bull markets.
  - The average bear lasts 289 days (about 9.6 months); the average bull lasts 988 days (2.7 years).
  - On average, (current edition) stocks lose 35% in a bear and gain 112% in a bull; an older edition said 36% and 114%.
- Recent near-miss: the S&P 500 fell 18.9% from its Feb 19, 2025 record to its Apr 8, 2025 close, just short of a bear. It set a new record on June 27, 2025 (CNBC, CNN, April 2025).
- Last official bear: 2022. The S&P 500 fell about 25% from its Jan 3, 2022 peak to its Oct 12, 2022 low.
- Worked example (the recovery math): 100 acorns fall 35% to 65. Getting back needs +35, and 35 / 65 = +53.8%.
- Chartable: average move, bear -35% vs bull +112% (Hartford Funds).
- Surprising fact: a 35% loss needs a 54% gain to get back to even.
