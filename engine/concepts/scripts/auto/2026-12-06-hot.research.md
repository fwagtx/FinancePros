# 2026-12-06-hot research brief: What happens if you stop paying rent? (evergreen, posts Dec 6 2026)

Framing: US-wide. Rules vary by state and city; describe the common sequence only, label state examples as examples. Education, not legal advice. Point neutrally to local legal aid / 211.

## Common sequence (varies by state)
1. Rent late -> late fee, if the lease and state law allow (amounts/caps vary by state; no single national rule).
2. Written notice to pay or leave ("pay or quit" / rent demand). Examples:
   - California: 3-day notice to pay rent or quit; count skips Saturdays, Sundays and court holidays (CA courts self-help / CA AG "Know Your Rights: Eviction Process").
   - New York: 14-day rent demand (NY CourtHelp, nycourts.gov); written late-rent notice once rent is 5+ days late.
3. Eviction lawsuit (California: "unlawful detainer"; tenant generally has 10 court days to answer after personal service, CA courts).
4. Court judgment for possession (and possibly money judgment for back rent/fees/costs).
5. Removal carried out by sheriff/marshal with a court order (writ). CA: sheriff posts notice, ~5 days. Legal aid sources (NY, CT, OH) say landlords can't change locks or shut off utilities without a court case; note Texas has limited statutory lockout rules, so do NOT say "illegal everywhere".

## Records, credit, screening
- Credit reports: since the 2017 National Consumer Assistance Plan, the three credit bureaus generally do not show eviction cases/civil judgments; Experian: its credit report does not include eviction information (Experian Ask Experian).
- BUT unpaid rent/fees can be sent to a collection agency; if it reports, a collection account can appear on credit reports (Experian). Collections can be reported up to 7 years (FCRA, from original delinquency).
- Tenant screening reports (separate from credit reports): CFPB: eviction court cases could be on a tenant screening record for up to seven years; FCRA generally allows lawsuits/judgments for 7 years or until the statute of limitations runs, whichever is longer. Some states allow sealing/expungement or bar use of eviction records (CFPB Ask CFPB).
- SURPRISING FACT: Even dismissed cases can show up. Oct 2023: FTC and CFPB announced TransUnion and TransUnion Rental Screening Solutions would pay $15 million ($11M consumer redress + $4M CFPB civil penalty) to settle allegations its tenant screening reports included inaccurate/incomplete eviction records, including failing to accurately report case dispositions (e.g. dismissals) and including sealed cases. (Settlement of allegations.)

## Scale (Eviction Lab, Princeton, "Preliminary Analysis: Eviction Filing Patterns in 2025", Apr 16 2026)
- Landlords filed 1.23 million eviction cases in 2025 across the 48 sites tracked (38 city areas + 10 states, about one-third of US renter households; not nationally representative). 2024: 1.26 million. Second straight yearly decline; 2025 was 3.2% below the post-pandemic (2023-24) average.
- Average filing rate 8.0% = roughly 1 filing per 13 renter households (some outlets quoted 7.9%). Atlanta area: about 1 per 4 renters. NYC: 5%.
- Historical (Eviction Lab): ~3.6 million eviction cases filed nationwide in 2018 against 2.7 million households, similar to 2000-2018 annual average.
- Harvard JCHS (2026): record 49% of renters rent-burdened.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
Rent $1,500/month, two months unpaid = 2 x $1,500 = $3,000. With an example $75 late fee each month: $3,000 + 2 x $75 = $3,150, before any court costs. (Late fee amount is an assumption; legal limits vary by state.)

## Help (neutral)
Local legal aid and 211 can connect renters with legal services and, where available, rental assistance (CFPB eviction resources; 211). Funds limited, not guaranteed.
