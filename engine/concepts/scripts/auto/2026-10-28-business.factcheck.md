# Fact-check: 2026-10-28-business ("Why timeshares are so hard to resell")

Checked Oct 1, 2026 for an Oct 28, 2026 post. Only the .raw.json was read. Most official sites (consumer.ftc.gov, arda.org, sec.gov, MVW IR) block direct fetching, so the checks below rely on search-result excerpts of those sources.

## Verified claims
- **FTC quote (L2):** The FTC consumer alert "If you have a timeshare, scammers might target you" was published March 29, 2024. The full sentence is: "Timeshares are hard to sell and only a scammer will tell you they already have a buyer or can find one fast." The on-screen fragment matches word for word, including the final period. The title and date (March 2024) are correct. https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/03/if-you-have-timeshare-scammers-might-target-you
- **Hook (L1):** "Even the FTC says timeshares are hard to sell" is a fair paraphrase, because the alert literally says "Timeshares are hard to sell". Note that the alert is about resale scams that target owners. It is not an analysis of the market.
- **$24,740 (L3, cover):** This is the 2025 average transaction price from ARDA's State of the Vacation Timeshare Industry, 2026 edition (prepared by EY). It was $23,160 in 2024 (2025 edition). The label "ARDA, 2026 edition" is correct.
- **Maintenance fees (L6):** $1,120 (2021), $1,170 (2022), $1,260 (2023), $1,480 (2024) and $1,550 (2025) all match the ARDA figures as reported. Each year comes from the following year's edition (2022 to 2026 editions), which matches the post's "2022-2026 editions". The $1,550 figure for 2025 is confirmed as coming from the 2026 edition.
- **MVW (L4):** Marketing and sales expense for FY2025 was $943M, up from $919M in 2024 (10-K). Consolidated contract sales were "$1.8 billion", down 3%, in the company's Feb 2026 release. One search excerpt gives the exact figure as $1,762M, which I could not confirm.
- **"About half" (L5):** $943M / $1.8B = 52.4%. Using $1,762M, it is 53.5%. "About half" is accurate either way, and "more than half" would also be defensible.
- **Worked example (L7):** $1,550 x 10 = $15,500. This is correct, labeled "example" and qualified with "before any increases".
- **Sub-labels:** "ARDA, 2026 edition" is 18 characters and "FY2025 results" is 14 characters. Both are within 28.
- There is no advice or price prediction, no political content and no stale time words. The CTA is fine.

## Issues and fixes
1. **L2 quote is cut off and leaves out the clause the hook relies on (minor/moderate).** The caption starts mid-sentence at "only a scammer..." and skips "Timeshares are hard to sell and". The narration also drops "or can find one fast." Fix: caption and scene text should read "Timeshares are hard to sell and only a scammer will tell you they already have a buyer or can find one fast." Narration: "Its March twenty twenty-four alert says timeshares are hard to sell, and only a scammer will tell you they already have a buyer." Optionally add context: "in a warning about resale scams".
2. **L4/L5 should label contract sales as company-reported (minor).** Contract sales is an operating metric MVW reports itself, not GAAP revenue. Fix: change the left sub from "FY2025 results" to "company-reported" (16 characters). Optionally use "more than half" in the caption, narration and board, since the ratio is about 52 to 54%.
3. **L5/L8 slip from one company to the whole industry (minor).** "A resale buyer isn't paying for the pitch" and the split scene "New buyer pays... the sales pitch" generalize from Marriott's numbers. Fix: "At a developer like Marriott, much of a new price covers selling; a resale buyer isn't paying for the pitch." Also, "not on the resort" on the L5 board overstates what the data shows (the rest isn't all "the resort"). Drop it or change it to "not the week itself".
4. **Hook strength (minor).** The hook is true, specific and short (under 3 seconds), but "Even the FTC" relies on the viewer knowing what the FTC is. A stronger version: "Timeshares cost $24,740 new. Even the FTC says they're hard to sell." Either version is acceptable.
5. **L6 "the bill that never stops" (nit).** The fees last as long as you own the timeshare. "the bill that keeps coming" is safer.

None of these is a wrong fact or a mismatched number. Every on-screen value matches the spoken one.

VERDICT: PASS
