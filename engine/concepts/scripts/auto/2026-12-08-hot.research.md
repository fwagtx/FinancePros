# Research brief: 2026-12-08-hot — Giving your kid money for a house down payment

Format: Money Myth / explainer ("Gift a down payment? Here's the paperwork"). Evergreen; all figures valid for calendar/tax year 2026 (posting Dec 8, 2026).

## Federal gift tax (IRS)
- Annual gift tax exclusion, tax year 2026: **$19,000 per recipient**, unchanged from 2025. Per giver, per recipient: each parent has their own $19,000 for each child. (IRS newsroom, "IRS releases tax inflation adjustments for tax year 2026"; Rev. Proc. 2025-32 sec. 4.42(1); IRS Gift Tax FAQ: "if you give each of your children $19,000 in 2026, the annual exclusion applies to each gift").
- Married couple: two exclusions = **$38,000** to one child in 2026. (If all the money comes from one spouse, they can elect gift splitting, which itself requires Form 709 with spouse consent.)
- Unused exclusion does not carry over to the next year.
- Gifts above the exclusion: giver files **Form 709** (U.S. Gift (and GST) Tax Return), generally due April 15 of the following year. Filing usually means no tax due; the excess reduces the giver's lifetime exemption. Gift tax is owed only once cumulative taxable gifts exceed the lifetime amount.
- Lifetime basic exclusion amount (gift + estate), 2026: **$15,000,000 per person**, up from $13,990,000 in 2025, set by Public Law 119-21 (signed July 4, 2025; IRS calls it the Working Families Tax Cuts legislation, widely called the One Big Beautiful Bill Act). Indexed for inflation after 2026. (IRS newsroom release; Instructions for Form 706 (07/2026); Rev. Proc. 2025-32 sec. 3.14.)
- The gift tax is paid by the giver, not the recipient. The recipient generally does not owe income tax on a gift (IRS Gifts & Inheritances FAQ).

## Worked example (exact math)
Two parents give their child $50,000 for a down payment in 2026, $25,000 from each.
- Each parent's exclusion: $19,000. Combined: 2 x $19,000 = $38,000.
- Over the exclusion: $50,000 - $38,000 = $12,000 ($6,000 per parent).
- Each parent files Form 709 for 2026 reporting a $6,000 taxable gift; $6,000 comes off each parent's $15,000,000 lifetime exemption. Tax owed: $0.
- If they give $38,000 instead ($19,000 each): no Form 709 needed.

## Mortgage lender rules (gift letters, documented funds)
- Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-4.3-04 Personal Gifts (version 02/04/2026): gift funds may fund all or part of the down payment, closing costs or reserves (subject to minimum borrower contribution rules); not allowed on investment properties. Acceptable donors include relatives (blood, marriage, adoption, legal guardianship) and others with a documented close relationship.
- Gift must be evidenced by a **gift letter signed by the donor** stating the actual or maximum dollar amount and that **no repayment is expected**; lenders typically also ask for donor name, address, phone and relationship.
- Lender must verify the transfer: e.g., wire confirmation, copy of donor's check, donor's withdrawal + borrower's deposit, or settlement statement showing closing agent received it.
- "Seasoning"/sourcing: lenders review roughly the last two months (about 60 days) of bank statements; under Fannie Mae a "large deposit" (single deposit over 50% of total monthly qualifying income) must be sourced. Gift money that just landed is documented with the gift letter + transfer proof. (Fannie Mae B3-4.2-02; lender guides: legalclarity.org, morty.com.)
- FHA (HUD Handbook 4000.1): gift = cash with no expectation of repayment; gift letter (FHA versions are signed by donor and borrower and state the source of funds); lender must trace funds donor to borrower.

## Gift vs loan
- If the money is a loan, IRS Sec. 7872: a family loan charging less than the Applicable Federal Rate (AFR, published monthly by IRS) is a "below-market loan"; the forgone interest can be treated as a gift from lender to borrower (and as interest income to the lender). Exceptions: loans totaling $10,000 or less (not used for income-producing assets); special limits under $100,000.
- AFR example: October 2026 short-term AFR (annual) 4.25% (Rev. Rul. 2026-19). Rates change monthly, so script names the AFR without a number.
- A gift letter must say no repayment expected; a real loan is a debt, not a gift.

- 2025 report (transactions July 2024–June 2025): first-time buyers used personal savings 59%, financial assets 26%; "more than 1 in 5" got help from relatives/friends via gift or loan (NewsNation/KXAN coverage; Walnut Invest cites 22%). An MGIC slide reproducing NAR's chart shows 19% for gift-only (repeat buyers 5%). Gift-only vs gift-or-loan is ambiguous across secondary summaries; script uses "more than 1 in 5 ... gift or loan" which all sources support.
- First-time buyer share 21% (record low), median first-time down payment 10%.

## Surprising fact
Going over $19,000 doesn't mean paying tax: you file a form and it trims a $15 million lifetime exemption. Meanwhile the lender often wants more paperwork than the IRS.
