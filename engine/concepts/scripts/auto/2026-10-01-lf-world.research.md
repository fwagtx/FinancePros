# Research brief: Why the dollar is so strong (or weak) right now

## Why the dollar moved in 2026 (drivers)
1. Interest rates: higher US rates (and expectations of more) attract global savers into dollar assets. AEI says the most recent rise in the dollar was due to rising expectations for US interest rates.
2. Safe haven: war with Iran began Feb 28, 2026; dollar rose about 3% soon after (AEI, "The Dollar, Safe-Haven Demand, and the Iran War"). Euro and yen fell on energy exposure (CNBC Mar 2, 2026).
3. Oil: US has been a net petroleum exporter every year since 2020; in 2025 exported 2.8 million barrels/day more than imported (EIA). Higher oil hurts energy importers (Europe, Japan) more -> their currencies weaken vs the dollar. Surprising fact.
   - Brent: closed $105.28 on Sept 28, 2026 after hitting $108.83 intraday; hit ~$109 in early Sept 2026 (CNBC Sept 28, 2026; Al Jazeera).
4. Weak side (2025): DXY fell about 9.4% in 2025 (Barchart 9.37%), worst year since 2017 (~10% drop). Drivers: three Fed cuts in 2025 (Sept, Oct, Dec) to 3.50-3.75%, tariffs and policy uncertainty (Bloomberg Dec 31, 2025; NPR Jan 4, 2026). Euro rose about 13.4% vs dollar in 2025, ending ~1.1733.

## How the "strength" is measured
- DXY: dollar vs basket of 6 currencies; base 100 = March 1973. Euro weight 57.6% (largest), plus yen, pound, Canadian dollar, Swedish krona, Swiss franc.
- Record high 164.72 (Feb 1985); record low ~70.70 (March 2008).
- Strong dollar = one dollar buys more foreign currency. Not "good" or "bad", it shifts winners and losers.

## History (chapter)
- 1944 Bretton Woods: currencies pegged to dollar, dollar convertible to gold (Federal Reserve History).
- Aug 15, 1971: Nixon closed the gold window, ending dollar-gold convertibility for foreign governments (Federal Reserve History).
- DXY launched with 1973 = 100.
- Feb 1985 peak 164.72. Sept 22, 1985 Plaza Accord: US, France, West Germany, Japan, UK agreed "some further orderly appreciation of the non-dollar currencies is desirable." Dollar fell 4% the next trading day and about 40% over 1985-87 (Plaza Accord communique; Wikipedia/Frankel).
- March 2008 low ~70.70.

## Dollar's global role (scale)
- USD on one side of 89% of all FX trades; FX turnover $9.6 trillion per day in April 2025, up 28% from 2022 (BIS Triennial Survey, released Sept 30, 2025).
- Dollar share of global allocated FX reserves: 57.13% in Q1 2026, up from 56.42% in Q4 2025 (IMF COFER).

## WORKED EXAMPLE: a 2,000-euro trip
- Early Jan 2025: EUR/USD ~1.03 (low of $1.0306, Bloomberg Jan 2, 2025): 2,000 x 1.03 = $2,060
- End of 2025: ~1.17 (1.1733): 2,000 x 1.17 = $2,340 -> $280 more than Jan 2025
- Late Sept 2026: ~1.13 (1.1339): 2,000 x 1.13 = $2,260 -> $80 less than end 2025
Takeaway: same trip, $280 swing in one year with no change in the hotel's price.

## Wallet effects (education)
- Strong dollar: cheaper foreign trips, imports cheaper (can ease prices), US exporters' goods pricier abroad, US multinationals' foreign earnings translate into fewer dollars, emerging economies with dollar debt pay more.
- Weak dollar: pricier trips and imports, helps exporters and foreign earnings.
- Nobody reliably predicts currencies: no forecasts in script.
