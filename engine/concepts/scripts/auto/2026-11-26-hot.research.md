# 2026-11-26-hot research brief: Face-off: active funds vs passive index funds

Posts Thursday, November 26, 2026 (evergreen; every number dated). Education only, no buy/sell language.

## Definitions
- Active fund: a manager picks stocks trying to beat a benchmark; charges a higher fee for that work.
- Passive index fund: holds everything in an index (for example the S&P 500) to match it, minus a small fee.
- SPIVA (S&P Indices Versus Active), from S&P Dow Jones Indices, compares active funds to their benchmark after fees, and counts funds that closed or merged as underperformers.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. 2025 (calendar year): 79% of all active US large-cap equity funds underperformed the S&P 500, up from 65% in 2024; the fourth-worst year in the 25-year SPIVA history. Source: SPIVA U.S. Scorecard Year-End 2025 (S&P DJI, 2026); InvestmentNews, "Active managers stumble again in 2025 as large caps dominate".
2. First half of 2026 (to June 30, 2026): about 67% (67.2%) of large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500. Source: SPIVA U.S. Mid-Year 2026 (published September 2026), reported by multiple outlets (Sustainable Investing chart of the week Sep 28, 2026; TKer).
3. Longer horizons: roughly 9 in 10 US large-cap funds trailed over 15 years (SPIVA mid-year 2025: about 88-89% over 15 yrs; year-end 2025 coverage says ~90%). WEAKLY SOURCED exact figure; fetch of S&P PDFs blocked and search summaries conflicted. NOT used in the script.
4. Fees, 2025 (asset-weighted averages): actively managed equity mutual funds 0.64%; index equity mutual funds 0.05%; index equity ETFs 0.14%; all equity mutual funds 0.40% (unchanged from 2024). Source: ICI, "Trends in the Expenses and Fees of Funds, 2025" (2026) and ICI news release "Mutual Fund and ETF Fees Remained Near Historic Lows in 2025". The 2024 figures (ICI 2025 report) were the same 0.64% / 0.05%.
5. Passive overtook active: as of Dec 31, 2023, US passive funds held $13.252 trillion vs $13.244 trillion in active funds, the first time passive passed active across all asset classes. Source: Morningstar data via CNBC (Jan 18, 2024), Hargreaves Lansdown. As of Oct 2025 passive ~$19.1T vs active ~$16.2T (Morningstar, per search summary; secondary).

## Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
$10,000 invested once, 7% a year return before fees, 30 years, fee simply subtracted from the yearly return.
- Index-fund fee 0.05%: 10,000 x (1.0695)^30 = $75,063 (75,062.61)
- Active-fund fee 0.64%: 10,000 x (1.0636)^30 = $63,584 (63,584.29)
- Gap: $75,063 - $63,584 = $11,478 (11,478.32), about 15% of the low-fee result.
The two fees are ICI's 2025 averages; the 7% return is an assumption, not a prediction.

## Surprising fact
Even in 2022, active large-cap's best year since 2009, a majority (51%) still trailed the index. And passive funds now hold more US fund assets than active (since end of 2023).

## Fair caveats (for neutrality)
- Some active funds do beat the index in a given year; SPIVA measures averages and percentages, not every fund.
- Index funds by design never beat their index; they match it minus fees.
