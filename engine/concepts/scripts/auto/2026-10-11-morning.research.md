## Context / why it matters
- Inflation jumped in spring 2026 as the Iran conflict pushed energy prices up; it cooled from May's 4.2% peak but has been stuck at 3.4%, well above the Fed's 2% goal.
- The Fed has already hiked once and signaled more. The September CPI is among the last major data points before the Oct 28 decision.
- Normal-person money: credit card APRs and HELOCs are usually tied to the prime rate, which moves with the Fed's rate; savings account and CD yields tend to follow too. A hotter inflation print raises the odds of another hike; a cooler one lowers them. (Explain, don't predict.)
