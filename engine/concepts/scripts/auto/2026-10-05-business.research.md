## Core economics: pour cost vs food cost (industry benchmarks; trade sources, not official)
- Pour cost / beverage cost % = ingredient cost of the drink divided by its menu price. Food cost % is the same idea for food.
- Typical targets: bars aim for an 18-24% pour cost overall; kitchens aim for a 28-35% food cost. By type: spirits ~15-22%, draft beer ~20%, bottled beer ~22-28%, wine ~30-40%. (Backbar Academy "Average Restaurant Costs"; Horizon Beverage "Targeting beverage costs"; Meritage Technologies "Rules of thumb for beverage costs"; consistent across several trade guides.) WEAKLY SOURCED: these are rules of thumb from trade guides, not official statistics. Label as "typical targets, per industry guides".

## Worked example (hypothetical, assumptions stated)
- Both items priced at $12 (example prices).
- Cocktail at a 20% pour cost (inside the 18-24% bar range): ingredients = 0.20 x $12 = $2.40; left after ingredients = $12 - $2.40 = $9.60.
- Burger at a 30% food cost (inside the 28-35% kitchen range): ingredients = 0.30 x $12 = $3.60; left after ingredients = $12 - $3.60 = $8.40.
- Difference: $9.60 - $8.40 = $1.20 more per $12 sale from the drink, before labor and rent.

## Company filing: Flanigan's Enterprises (NYSE American: BDL), Form 10-K for fiscal 2025 (fiscal year ended around early October 2025; filed Dec 2025)
- Gross profit margin, restaurant food and bar sales (combined): 66.61% in fiscal 2025 (65.57% in fiscal 2024).
- Gross profit margin, package (liquor) store sales: 25.12% in fiscal 2025 (26.60% in fiscal 2024).
- Restaurant revenue from food and non-alcoholic beverages: $124,501,000 FY2025 ($114,795,000 FY2024). Restaurant revenue from alcoholic beverages: $31,764,000 FY2025 ($30,010,000 FY2024).
- Math: alcohol share of restaurant revenue FY2025 = 31,764 / (124,501 + 31,764) = 31,764 / 156,265 = 20.3%.
- Share of total company sales FY2025: restaurant food 61.25%, restaurant bar 15.63%, package stores 23.12%.
- Surprising fact: the same company selling alcohol by the bottle in its liquor stores keeps about 25 cents of each sales dollar after product costs; its restaurants (food and bar together) keep about 67 cents. Caveat: the 10-K does not split food vs bar margins; gross margin excludes labor and rent.
