# Research brief: Scam alert - sextortion threats demanding payment (Hot Take, posts Fri Jan 1, 2027)
Researched Nov 2, 2026. Evergreen: every number dated. No graphic content; focus on the money scam.

## Timely angle
No single news event in the week of research. Most recent official data: NCMEC sextortion data release (June 25, 2026)
covering 2025. Use that as the "latest numbers" angle (dated, not "this week").

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. NCMEC (June 25, 2026 release): in 2025 NCMEC received "more than 50,000 reports of financially motivated sextortion,
   which averaged 137 reports per day. This is up significantly from more than 36,000 reports received in 2024." [S1]
2. NCMEC (2024 release): "In 2023 alone, NCMEC received 26,718 reports of financial sextortion, up from 10,731 reports in 2022." [S2][S3]
3. NCMEC (June 2026): "Cooperating or paying rarely stops blackmail." Victims should get help before deciding whether to pay;
   "the blackmailer is to blame, not the child." Resources: CyberTipline (cybertipline.org) and Take It Down (free removal help). [S1]
   94% of public sextortion reports (when relationship known) came from the child victim or their parent. [S1]
4. FBI (bulletin; FBI San Francisco release Jan 2024): Oct 2021 - Mar 2023, FBI and HSI received over 13,000 reports of online
   financial sextortion of minors, at least 12,600 victims, mostly boys, at least 20 suicides. Typical victims: males 14 to 17,
   though any child can be a victim. Payment "is often requested in gift cards, mobile payment services, wire transfers, or
   cryptocurrency." Offenders usually outside the US (West Africa, e.g. Nigeria, Ivory Coast; Southeast Asia, e.g. Philippines). [S4][S5]
5. FBI advice to teens (2022-2024 coverage of FBI alerts): stop messaging, tell a trusted adult, report (1-800-CALL-FBI / tips.fbi.gov),
   don't delete profile or messages (evidence). [S6]
6. FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report (released April 2026): 1,008,597 complaints; about $20.9B total losses (+26% vs 2024).
   Extortion was #2 by complaints: 89,129 complaints; extortion losses $122.5 million (ransomware separate, $32.3M). [S7][S8][S9]
7. FBI IC3 2024 Internet Crime Report (April 2025): extortion 86,415 complaints, $143.2 million losses. [S10][S11]
   (CyberScoop cites a narrower "sextortion and other extortion" figure of 54,936 complaints / $33.5M for 2024 - categories differ; not used.)
8. Email "bluff" version (targets adults): mass emails quote a real but old password from a data breach as "proof," claim a webcam
   video, demand bitcoin. Usually there is no video. FBI IC3 PSA (June 2016, PSA160601): don't pay; file at ic3.gov with the email
   and bitcoin address. [S12][S13]
9. Help: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988). NCMEC Take It Down: free, hash-based, for images taken before age 18. [S1][S14]

## Worked example (our math)
- Growth 2022 -> 2025: 50,000 / 10,731 = 4.66 -> "nearly 5x in three years" (our math; 2025 is "more than 50,000", so at least 4.66x).
- Per day check: 50,000 / 365 = 137.0 -> matches NCMEC's 137 per day.
- IC3 extortion losses 2024 -> 2025: $143.2M -> $122.5M = -$20.7M (-14.5%, our math), while complaints rose 86,415 -> 89,129 (+3.1%, our math).

## The surprising fact
Paying usually doesn't end it: NCMEC says "cooperating or paying rarely stops blackmail," and many adult-targeted threat emails are
bluffs built on an old leaked password, with no video at all.

## Uncertain / caveats
- 2024 and 2025 NCMEC figures are "more than" rounded figures.
- IC3 2025 extortion complaint count (89,129) seen in secondary coverage (WaterISAC/Medium summaries); losses $122.5M in several outlets. ic3.gov not reachable from this session.
- FBI 13,000 / 20 suicides figures cover Oct 2021-Mar 2023 only.
