# Research brief: Why is a hearing aid so expensive? (posts Mon Jan 25, 2027, 6 PM Central; evergreen)

Format: "Why is X so expensive". Education only, neutral, no brand recommendations.
No timely news angle needed (evergreen).

## Key facts (dated)
1. PRICE: The average retail price for a PAIR of hearing aids in 2013 was $4,700 (range $3,300-$6,000), a bundled price that includes professional services. Source: National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM), "Hearing Health Care for Adults" (2016 report, citing Strom 2014), and NASEM press release June 2016. Repeated in later NIH/PMC literature (PMC9200460).
   - Cross-check (newer survey, not used on screen): HearingTracker reader survey: $4,672 average per pair in 2018; $2,694 per pair overall in its 2026 survey, $3,432 for prescription pairs (private pay). Prices vary widely by channel.
2. BUNDLING: Most hearing aid transactions use a bundled price: the evaluation/hearing test, device selection and fitting, follow-up care, and programming adjustments are rolled into one package price (NASEM 2016; ASHA Practice Portal "Unbundling Hearing Aid Sales"). Unbundled/online sales typically provide the device without the services, making devices substantially cheaper (NASEM 2014 workshop summary).
3. MARKET CONCENTRATION: Five manufacturers (WS Audiology, Demant, GN, Starkey, Sonova) control an estimated ~90% of the global market and ~84% of the US market (AELP estimate cited by The Capitol Forum, June 2026). Earlier: CNBC (Nov 2022) "five largest manufacturers ... more than 90% of the market"; ATscale (2019) "five suppliers hold more than 90%". Two+ sources agree on ~90% for the big five.
4. MEDICARE: Original Medicare doesn't cover hearing aids or exams for fitting them; you pay 100%. It can cover doctor-ordered diagnostic hearing exams. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer hearing benefits. Source: Medicare.gov "Hearing aids" coverage page.
5. OTC RULE: FDA final rule (Aug 2022) created an over-the-counter category for air-conduction hearing aids for adults 18+ with perceived mild-to-moderate hearing loss; effective October 17, 2022. Sources: FDA; AARP (2022); White House fact sheet Oct 17, 2022.
6. OTC PRICES: White House fact sheet (Oct 17, 2022): OTC products range from $199 to $999 per pair; administration estimated savings of about $2,800 per pair. NCOA guide also gives most models $199-$999 per pair. AARP (2022) projected $250 to $1,000+ per pair.
7. OTC LIMIT: OTC is only for perceived mild-to-moderate loss in adults; severe loss or under 18 needs prescription devices (FDA rule).

## Surprising fact
The price tag on a prescription pair usually isn't just the device: the hearing test, fitting, follow-ups and adjustments are bundled in (NASEM 2016).

## Worked example (our math)
Bundled prescription pair, 2013 average: $4,700 (NASEM)
Top of OTC launch range, 2022: $999 per pair (White House)
Difference: $4,700 - $999 = $3,701 (our math). Note: different years; the OTC price does not include professional fitting services.

## Chartable data series
No reliable evenly spaced yearly price series found (HearingTracker has only 2018 and 2026 survey points; NASEM is a single 2013 figure). Script uses stat/compare/list/board/news instead of a chart.
