## The story: Black Friday online spending on track to top $13 billion
Facts (Adobe Analytics, reported by Reuters Nov 27, 2026, late evening ET):
- US online spending on Black Friday (Fri Nov 27, 2026) reached $9.7 billion through 6:30 p.m. ET, up 12.8% from the same point last year.
- Adobe projects the full-day total at $13.0 billion to $13.4 billion. (Final tally not yet published at time of research - treat as projection; say "on track to top $13 billion".)
- Adobe's Sept 28, 2026 forecast had called for $12.9 billion on Black Friday, up 9.2% YoY. Adobe credited deeper discounts (TVs, apparel, appliances).
- Salesforce, which measures differently, projects full-day US online Black Friday spending of $18.7 billion, up 4%; it counted $5.9B US / $30.7B global by midday.
- Cyber Monday (Nov 30, 2026) forecast: $15.1 billion, up 6.2% (Adobe) - expected biggest online day of the season.
- Cyber Week (Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday) forecast: $47.5 billion, up 7.4% (Adobe).
- Full season (Nov 1 - Dec 31) Adobe forecast: $275.1 billion, up 6.7%. Discounts up to 30% off list during Cyber Week.

## Context
Black Friday online sales have risen every year since 2021 (2021 dipped slightly from 2020's $9.0B). Dollar totals are nominal; a record in dollars is not automatically a record in items bought. Different trackers (Adobe vs Salesforce) use different methods and are not directly comparable.

## What it means for a normal person
Shows consumers are still spending, with growth running ahead of forecast. A big total says where money went, not whether any individual deal was good. Neutral, no advice.
