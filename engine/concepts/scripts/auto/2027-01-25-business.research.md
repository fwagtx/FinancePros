# Research brief: How online retailers make money on free shipping
Show: How They Make Money (posts Mon Jan 25, 2027, 2 PM Central). Evergreen; every number dated.
Prior coverage to avoid: Amazon ads (2026-12-21). Angle here: who pays for "free" shipping.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. Amazon shipping costs (Form 10-K; "shipping costs" include sortation and delivery centers and transportation costs):
   - 2021: $76.7B (FY2022 10-K / annual report)
   - 2022: $83.5B (FY2022 and FY2023 10-K)
   - 2023: $89.5B (FY2023 10-K)
   - 2024: $95.8B (FY2025 10-K / 2025 Annual Report)
   - 2025: $102.7B (FY2025 10-K / 2025 Annual Report: "Shipping costs were $95.8 billion and $102.7 billion in 2024 and 2025."); Statista matches.
   - Amazon 10-K: cost of sales includes inbound and outbound shipping costs; "We expect our cost of shipping to continue to increase."
   - Trade coverage (2026): shipping costs were 17.5% of North America + International sales in 2025, down from a 19.2% peak in 2022.
   - Context: Amazon total net sales 2025 $716.9B (Q4 2025 release, Feb 5, 2026).
2. Free-shipping minimums (non-members):
   - Target: free shipping on orders of $35+ (or with a Target Circle Card); under $35 without the card: $5.99 flat fee (Target help page; 2026 guides agree on $35).
   - Walmart: free shipping on eligible orders of $35+ for non-members (threshold set in Jan 2017 when cut from $50, Walmart corporate release; March 2026 guide confirms $35). Walmart+ members: no minimum (since Dec 2020, Walmart corporate).
3. Memberships (as of 2026, multiple outlets: CNBC Select, AARP, Kiplinger, ShopBack 2026):
   - Amazon Prime: $139/yr or $14.99/mo (raised from $119 in Feb 2022, NPR/KUOW).
   - Walmart+: $98/yr or $12.95/mo.
4. Price built in: Amazon's 10-K books shipping inside cost of sales, i.e., it is paid out of the money customers spend on items and fees, not a separate free lunch.

## Chartable data series
Amazon shipping costs, $B, fiscal years (10-K): 2021 76.7 | 2022 83.5 | 2023 89.5 | 2024 95.8 | 2025 102.7 (+34% over four years; our math: 102.7/76.7 = 1.339).

## Worked example (our math)
A $28 cart topped up to the $35 minimum: 35 - 28 = $7 extra; 7 / 28 = 0.25 = 25% more spending.

## Surprising fact
Amazon spent about $102.7B on shipping in 2025, roughly one in every seven dollars of its $716.9B net sales (our math: 102.7/716.9 = 14.3%).

## Neutral framing
Education only; no stock tips; no predictions.
