# Research brief: How hair salons make money, booth rent vs commission (posts Nov 5, 2026)

## Official data
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook: hairdressers, hairstylists and cosmetologists held about 575,200 jobs in 2024; 48% were self-employed (barbers 76%). Median wage May 2024 $16.95/hr.
- BLS OEWS median hourly wage (SOC 39-5012, wage-and-salary workers): May 2019 $12.54 | May 2021 $14.26 | May 2023 $16.81 | May 2024 $16.95 | May 2025 $17.21.
- Census 2023: ~838,264 nonemployer beauty-salon businesses (NAICS 812112; booth renters, solo stylists), avg receipts ~$30,374; County Business Patterns: 84,176 beauty salons with paid employees (391,064 employees). About 10 nonemployer businesses per employer salon.
- CPI haircuts and other personal care services: +3.6% in the 12 months to August 2026 (BLS).
- Self-employment tax rate 15.3% (IRS).

## Models (industry sources: Vagaro, SalonRenter, trade guides)
- Commission: stylist is an employee (or paid a cut); common splits 40%-60% of service sales, 50/50 most common; salon supplies products, space, front desk, marketing.
- Booth rent: stylist rents a chair, typically $150-$400 a week in many US markets (higher in big cities); keeps service revenue; pays own supplies, taxes, insurance; no employer benefits.

## Worked example (hypothetical)
$1,000 a week of services.
- 50% commission: stylist $500, salon $500.
- $300/week booth rent: stylist $700 before supplies and taxes; salon $300.
- Break-even revenue: $300 / 50% = $600 a week. Below $600, commission pays the stylist more (before costs); above, booth rent does.
