# Research brief: How banks create money when they lend (Explainer, posts Oct 24, 2026)

- Fed H.6 (Sept 22, 2026 release): M2 for August 2026 was $23,342.8B seasonally adjusted (about $23.3T), up 5.7% y/y. Currency in circulation in August 2026 was $2,389.7B. Currency / M2 = 2,389.7 / 23,342.8 = 10.2%.
- M2 = M1 + small time deposits + retail money market funds (minus IRA/Keogh balances). Most of it is bank deposits.
- Bank of England, "Money creation in the modern economy" (McLeay, Radia, Thomas, Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1):
  - The majority of money is created by commercial banks making loans.
  - When a bank lends, it credits the borrower's account with a new deposit.
  - Repaying loans destroys money.
  - Banks are not simply intermediaries lending out savers' deposits, and they do not "multiply up" central bank money.
  - Limits: market forces (profitability, borrower demand), regulatory policy (capital) and monetary policy (interest rates).
- Fed: reserve requirement ratios were cut to 0% effective March 26, 2020.
- Worked example (illustrative): a $20,000 car loan adds a $20,000 deposit to the borrower's account, so the money supply rises by $20,000. Repaying it shrinks the money supply again.
- Chartable: Currency $2.39T vs M2 $23.34T (Aug 2026).
- Surprising fact: only about 10% of the M2 money supply is paper money and coins.
