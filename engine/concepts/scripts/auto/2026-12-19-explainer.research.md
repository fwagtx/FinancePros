# Research brief: The lease clauses to read before you sign (residential apartment lease)
Slot 2026-12-19-explainer. Researched Oct 20, 2026; posts Dec 19, 2026. All figures phrased with dates so they hold on the posting date.

## Hook / worked example (clearly labeled example)
- Many lease-break clauses charge a flat early-termination fee; RentCafe (rental listing site) says it is "often equal to one to two months' rent", usually with 30-60 days' written notice; other guides say 2-4 months. (Secondary sources; amounts vary by lease.)
- US median gross rent, 2024 ACS 1-year (Census table B25064): $1,487/month (reported by secondary summaries of the Census release; up from $1,448 in 2023 in inflation-adjusted terms).
- WORKED EXAMPLE (assumption: a two-month lease-break fee at the 2024 US median rent): $1,487 x 2 = $2,974, about $3,000. Before any unpaid rent, utilities or damage the lease also allows.
- Some leases instead keep the tenant liable for rent until a new tenant is found; many states require landlords to mitigate (re-rent). Federal SCRA lets qualifying service members end leases without penalty.

## Automatic renewal / notice periods
- Some leases renew automatically unless notice is given by a deadline. Wisconsin Stat. 704.15: an automatic renewal clause is not enforceable against a residential tenant unless the landlord gives written notice 15-30 days before the tenant's notice deadline pointing out the clause.
- If a lease ends with no notice, many tenancies roll to month-to-month on the same terms (secondary source). Wisconsin month-to-month notice: at least 28 days (Wis. Stat. 704.19).

## Security deposits (state rules vary)
- California AB 12 (Civil Code 1950.5), effective July 1, 2024: deposit capped at one month's rent, furnished or unfurnished (previously 2 months unfurnished / 3 furnished). Exception: small landlords (natural persons/LLCs of natural persons owning no more than 2 properties with no more than 4 units total) may collect up to 2 months; service members always 1 month. Pet deposits count toward the cap. (Brownstein Hyatt law-firm alert, multiple secondary sources.)
- Return deadlines vary by state: California 21 days after move-out (Civ. Code 1950.5); Texas 30 days (Tex. Prop. Code 92.103); New York 14 days with itemization (NY AG guidance, GOL 7-108). Range across states roughly 14-60 days.
- Bad-faith withholding penalties: California up to 2x the deposit; Texas $100 plus 3x the wrongfully withheld amount plus attorney's fees.
- New York caps deposits at one month's rent (NY AG, 2019 HSTPA).

## Late fees
- New York (RPL 238-a, HSTPA 2019): late fee max is $50 or 5% of monthly rent, whichever is LESS, and only after rent is more than 5 days late. Example: $1,000 rent -> $50 cap; $900 rent -> $45. (NY AG "Changes in NYS rent law"; secondary summaries.)
- Other states set different or no caps.

## Maintenance, subletting, renters insurance (described only)
- Lease clauses assign repair duties; most states impose an implied warranty of habitability that leases cannot waive (general; not used with numbers).
- Subletting clauses often require written landlord consent. Renters-insurance clauses may require a minimum liability policy. (Not used in script; no numbers verified.)

## Junk fees (federal)
- Dec 2, 2025: FTC and Colorado AG announced a $24 million settlement with Greystar, the largest US apartment manager, over allegations it advertised base rents leaving out mandatory monthly fees (pest control, valet trash, package concierge, utility admin). Order requires the total monthly price upfront. ($23M FTC / $1M Colorado per Multifamily Dive.) Settlement; allegations, not an admission (standard settlement).
- Dec 2024: FTC's final Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees covered only live-event tickets and short-term lodging; it excluded long-term rental housing.
- Mar 12, 2026: FTC issued an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on unfair or deceptive rental housing fees (Fed. Reg. doc 2026-04907); comments due Apr 13, 2026. As of Oct 2026 no proposed or final rule found. Phrase: "as of October 2026, still at the early comment stage / no final rule."
- Jan 2026 FTC warning letters to 13 property-management software firms about fee disclosure (secondary).

## Chartable data series: US median gross rent (ACS 1-year, Census B25064)
- 2021: $1,191 (confirmed, Hawaii DBEDT databook citing ACS)
- 2023: $1,448 in 2024 dollars per Census comparison (nominal ~$1,406, unconfirmed)
- 2024: $1,487 (confirmed via two secondary summaries of ACS)
- 2020: no standard 1-year release (COVID). 2025 vintage not confirmed released. Not charted in script due to gaps.

## Surprising fact
- California, the largest rental market, cut its deposit cap to one month only in July 2024; before that, a furnished unit could require three months.
- The FTC's 2024 junk-fee rule did not cover apartment rentals at all.
