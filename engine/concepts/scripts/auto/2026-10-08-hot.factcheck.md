## Issues

1. **Stale and omitted material news (FAIL).** Sleep Number filed Chapter 11 on June 12, 2026 in SDNY with about $672M of debt. It sold substantially all of its assets to Sleep Country Canada for $415M, and the sale closed July 31, 2026 (CNBC, Bloomberg, Spokesman-Review). The script relies entirely on "one mattress maker's annual report" and never says that the maker went bankrupt and was sold. This matters because it is the context for the 47.1% selling cost and the loss. Leaving it out makes a bankrupt outlier look like a normal industry example, and viewers will call it out.
   - Fix: add a line after L7, for example: "It filed for bankruptcy in June 2026 and was sold to Sleep Country Canada." Alternatively, say plainly in L2 or L7 that this is the bankrupt Sleep Number.

2. **Over-generalizing one company (FAIL-level framing).** The header, cover, post title and L10 speak about "mattresses" in general. Sleep Number is a vertically integrated smart-bed maker with about 570 of its own stores, so its cost structure is not typical. Also, Sleep Number's own data does not show that its discounts are fake. The "always on sale" part rests only on the Mattress Firm allegations, which concern a different company.
   - Fix: in L10, say "at Sleep Number" or "at one big bed maker". In L2, say "one mattress maker's annual report shows where the money goes". Consider a title like "Where your mattress money goes".

3. **L10 "the price mostly pays for the selling" is inaccurate.** 47.1% is not a majority. Selling costs more than making, but it is still less than half of the price.
   - Fix: "At Sleep Number, more of the price paid for selling the bed than for making it."

4. **L4 "between 57% and 62%" is slightly off.** The 2022 low was 56.9%.
   - Fix: "roughly 57 to 62 percent" (on screen: "~57%-62%").

5. **L8 "must be real".** The FTC Guides are guidance, and the text says a former price "should" be bona fide. This is minor.
   - Fix: "FTC guides say a 'was' price should be a real former price."

6. **L9 precision.** The benefit was $40 per qualifying order as a store voucher or cash, only for California MattressFirm.com orders from 2020 to 2024, and Mattress Firm denied wrongdoing. This is optional but safer.
   - Fix: on screen, add "no admission of wrongdoing". The news card could read "Mattress Firm settles suit alleging fake discounts (denies wrongdoing)".

7. **Hook (L1) is weak.** It is generic and not specific, and "never cheap" is an unsourced opinion. It takes about 3 s to say, which is fine.
   - Fix: lead with the surprising number, for example: "One bed maker spent 47 cents of every dollar just selling you the bed." Another option: "This mattress company spent more selling beds than making them, then went bankrupt."

8. **Cover and title payoff.** The cover shows "47.1¢" with "SALE" and "Always On Sale, Never Cheap?" The video explains cost structure but never shows why sales are constant or that the sale prices are fake, except for the separate Mattress Firm allegation. The payoff is only partial. See fixes 2 and 7.

9. **Time words.** "In 2025" and "10-K 2025" are fine for Oct 8, 2026, and there is no "this year" or "last year". The Mattress Firm settlement is about a year old. That is acceptable because it is dated, but it is not news.

10. **Advice, price predictions, politics.** None are present. The bankruptcy coverage cites tariffs, so if you add the bankruptcy line, do not add political commentary.

11. **Read-aloud.** L6 "eight twenty to make it and nine forty-two to sell it" reads fine. L8 "a was price" is awkward when heard, so say "a 'was' price, the crossed-out one," or "a former price". In L2, "it's not the foam" is fine.
