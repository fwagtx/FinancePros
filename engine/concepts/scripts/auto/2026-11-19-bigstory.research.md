## Facts: Walmart fiscal Q3 2027 (quarter ended Oct 2026), released Thu Nov 19, 2026
- Revenue: $188.8 billion ($188.84B) vs $187.27B expected; up 5.2% from $179.5B a year earlier. (CNBC, AP, Talk Business)
- Adjusted EPS: $0.65 vs $0.64 expected (FactSet via AP; CNBC).
- Walmart U.S. net sales up 3.6% to $125.0B; U.S. comp sales ex-fuel +2.9% (in line, StreetAccount). Last quarter +2.6%; year-ago quarter +4.5% (AP).
- Global eCommerce +17%; U.S. eCommerce +23%; advertising +37%; global membership fee revenue +17%.
- Operating income +16.0% to $7.8B (reported). Net income down 19.5% (Talk Business) -- not used.
- Raised full-year outlook, the 3rd raise this year (AP headline: "Walmart raises its outlook for the year for the 3rd time"):
  net sales growth now 4.9%-5.2% (was 4%-5%); adjusted op income growth 8%-8.6% (was 7%-8.5%); adjusted EPS $2.84-$2.89 (was $2.80-$2.87).
- CFO John David Rainey on the call: "between diesel and transportation, it will be over $2 billion of incremental costs that we have had to navigate this year" (Motley Fool transcript; Yahoo Finance; Talk Business; AOL "Sky-high diesel prices take a $2 billion bite out of Walmart"). Same >$2B fuel figure was first flagged in the Q2 call (Aug 2026).
- CEO John Furner: pricing "really depends on transportation costs and input costs as we move forward." (Globe and Mail). Also: hoping transportation and fuel costs come down "as soon as possible, we'll keep prices as low as we can" (Newsquawk headline paraphrase; CNBC interview).
- Walmart rolled back prices on nearly 8,000 products (Finimize/coverage of the release/call).
- Partly offset by nearly US$3B in tariff refunds (Globe and Mail) -- NOT used (avoid repeating Target tariff-refund topic).
- Sam's Club: ~2 million new members after new membership launch in late October (CNBC) -- not used.

## Diesel (context)
- EIA weekly U.S. retail diesel average, week of Nov 16, 2026: $5.937/gal, down 19 cents from $6.127 (Nov 9); third straight weekly decline; peak $6.352 on Oct 26; 2026 high $6.529 (wk of Sep 21). (Blue Book, OilPriceAPI, Logistics Management, weeklydiesel.com)
- Year ago, EIA week of Nov 17, 2025: $3.868/gal (FreightWaves; Truck Driver News). Our math: +$2.07, about +53%.
- High prices tied largely to the Iran conflict restricting energy supply (coverage).

## What it means for a normal person
Diesel moves the trucks that stock every store, so it ends up inside the price of groceries and goods. The biggest US retailer says that cost was over $2B extra this year, it is absorbing some via rollbacks, but its CEO says prices depend on transport and input costs from here. Takeaway: watch diesel, not just shelf prices, heading into the holidays.
