# 2026-11-30-business research brief: How Supreme makes money with limited drops, and why VF sold it

Researched Friday, October 2, 2026. Posts Monday, November 30, 2026. No fresh news angle found for late 2026; the story is evergreen (buy in 2020, sell in 2024). Note: sec.gov and most news sites were blocked from direct fetch; figures below are confirmed through search results quoting the filings / two outlets.

## Key facts (dated)

1. **VF bought Supreme for $2.1 billion.** Merger agreement signed Nov 8, 2020, announced Nov 9, 2020; closed Dec 28, 2020, "aggregate base purchase price of $2.1 billion" (VF 8-K Nov 2020; VF 10-Q for quarter ended Dec 26, 2020; Forbes Nov 9, 2020; Hypebeast; FashionNetwork). Sellers: Carlyle Group and Goode Partners.
2. **VF sold Supreme to EssilorLuxottica for $1.5 billion in cash.** Agreement signed July 16, 2024, announced July 17, 2024; closed Oct 1, 2024 (VF 8-K; WWD Oct 2024; Fashion Dive; Bloomberg July 17, 2024; Robb Report). VF used net proceeds to prepay debt.
3. **Supreme revenue under VF (VF 10-K filings, VF fiscal years end around March 31):**
   - FY2021 (Dec 28, 2020 to Apr 3, 2021, about 3 months only): $142.0M revenue, $21.5M net income (VF FY2021 10-K)
   - FY2022 (year ended Apr 2, 2022): $561.5M revenue, $82.4M net income (VF FY2022 10-K; Complex; Fashion Dive)
   - FY2023 (year ended Apr 1, 2023): $523.1M ($523.116M) revenue, down about 7% (VF FY2023 10-K; Fashion Dive; Complex; Highsnobiety)
   - FY2024 (year ended Mar 30, 2024): $538M revenue (VF FY2024 10-K / 8-K pro forma; WWD and Fashion Dive coverage of the sale: "Supreme generated $538 million in revenue during VF's latest fiscal year")
   - VF had projected about $600M for FY2022 (Hypebeast, Highsnobiety, May 2021). Actual $561.5M fell short.
4. **Write-down:** In FY2023 VF recorded about $735M of noncash impairment charges on Supreme goodwill and trademark (Q2 FY2023: $229.0M goodwill + $192.9M trademark; Q4 FY2023: $165.1M goodwill + $148.0M trademark; sum $735.0M). Sources: VF FY2023 10-K; VF Q4 FY2023 press release; Modern Retail.
5. **VF's stated reason (July 2024, CEO Bracken Darrell):** "Given the brand's distinct business model and VF's integrated model, our strategic portfolio review concluded there are limited synergies between Supreme and VF, making a sale a natural next step." Also said it "gives us increased balance sheet flexibility" and supports "more normalized debt levels." (VF/EssilorLuxottica release July 17, 2024, as quoted by WWD and Fashion Dive.) Part of Darrell's "Reinvent" program to cut debt.
6. **EssilorLuxottica's rationale (July 17, 2024, Francesco Milleri and Paul du Saillant):** "We see an incredible opportunity in bringing an iconic brand like Supreme® into our Company. It perfectly aligns with our innovation and development journey, offering us a direct connection to new audiences, languages and creativity." EssilorLuxottica owns Ray-Ban and Oakley. Founder James Jebbia stayed to run Supreme.
7. **The drop model:** Two seasonal collections a year (Spring/Summer, Fall/Winter), released in weekly drops every Thursday at 11 a.m. ET online and in stores, in limited quantities; popular items sell out fast (SupremeCommunity drop-time help page; multiple drop-list sites). At the 2020 deal, Supreme was almost entirely direct-to-consumer with 12 stores worldwide and more than 60% of revenue digital (VF 2020 announcement as reported by Glossy / Inverse / Boardsport Source).
8. **Resale / hype data:**
   - StockX "State of Resale" report (Aug 2024): Supreme trades down 3% year over year (Jan-Jul 2024 vs 2023); Supreme No. 2 in StockX apparel since Fear of God took the top spot in 2022 (StockX press release; FashionUnited Aug 2024).
   - Modern Retail (2024, citing StockX): Supreme 16% of StockX apparel market, down from 36% in 2020 and 19% in 2023. (Single outlet - weaker.)
   - StockX News: FW16 white Box Logo hoodie traded above $1,000 on average vs. a later identical one under $400 (undated article - weak; not used in script).
   - Example retail vs resale: Box Logo hoodie FW24 retail $168 (StockX listing; resale fluctuates - not used).

## Worked example (exact math)
- Paid: $2.1B (2020). Sold: $1.5B (2024).
- $2.1B - $1.5B = $0.6B = $600M less.
- $0.6B / $2.1B = 0.2857 = 28.6%, about 29% lower.
- Bonus: $1.5B / $538M (FY2024 revenue) = 2.79, about 2.8 times one year of sales.

## Surprising fact
VF paid $2.1B, wrote down about $735M of Supreme's value in FY2023, and sold it for $600M less than it paid, even though Supreme stayed profitable (FY2022 net income $82.4M) and revenue barely moved ($561.5M -> $538M). Scarcity drives the hype, but it also caps growth.
