## History / context
- The Fed sets one short-term overnight rate; mortgages track long-term yields (10-year), which move on expectations. A largely priced-in hike + yields easing from the Oct 7 peak = mortgage rates easing.
- 2024: Fed cut, mortgage rates rose. 2026: Fed may hike, mortgage rates fell.
- Strong dollar: money flows toward higher rates; winners = US travelers abroad, importers/import buyers; losers = US exporters, US firms' overseas sales; Japan pays more for imported food/fuel.

## What it means for a normal person
- Variable-rate debt (cards, HELOCs) reprices within a month or two of a Fed move; fixed loans don't change.
- New mortgage pricing follows bond markets; relief vs October but still ~0.9 pt above a year ago.
- Savers: rates follow the Fed; huge gap between average and top accounts.
- Paycheck: pay +3.1% vs prices +3.7% (latest yearly) -> real pay still behind; hikes aim to slow prices but can cool hiring.
