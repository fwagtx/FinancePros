# Fact-check: 2027-01-23-hot (shareholders in bankruptcy)
Checked Nov 24, 2026, for posting Sat Jan 23, 2027, 6 PM CT. Each claim was checked independently with web search. The brief was used only as leads.

## Issues found (all fixed in 2027-01-23-hot.raw.json)
1. **Line 0, weak or misleading hook.** "If your company goes bankrupt..." could mean the viewer's employer. Changed to "Own a stock that goes bankrupt? Shareholders get paid dead last." (11 words). It is true, specific and quick to say. The header had the same problem and now reads "Own a stock that goes / bankrupt? Now what?".
2. **Line 1, unclear 2022 figure.** S&P's Jan 2023 report counted 391 filings for 2022. S&P's Jan 2024 coverage says 2023's 642 filings were up 72.6%, which implies a revised 2022 base of about 372. Because the two disagree, the spoken "up from 391 in 2022" was dropped. The line now uses the confirmed fact: 785 in 2025, the most since 2010 (828).
3. **Line 1, chart points.** S&P's Jan 2025 report revised 2023 to 635, not 636 or 642. S&P's Dec 2025 report gives 687 for 2024; a Jan 2026 S&P PE article gives 688. The chart now shows only verified, latest-reported points: 2023 635, 2024 687, 2025 785. The 2022 point was removed. "Large" was added because S&P only counts companies with public debt of at least $2M or with assets or liabilities of at least $2M/$10M.
4. **Line 2, priority order.** The SEC bulletin's order is secured creditors, then unsecured creditors (such as bondholders), then stockholders. Bondholders can be secured, so "then bondholders" was loose. Narration now says "unsecured creditors like bondholders". The list item changed from "Bondholders" to "Unsecured debt".
5. **Line 3, overstated wording.** "Usually cancels" was changed to "often cancels" to match the SEC bulletin wording. The clause "even if the company survives" was cut to save words.
6. **Line 5, Lehman timeline error.** The card said "2008: Lehman wiped out", but LBHI shares were cancelled when the plan took effect on March 6, 2012. The card now reads 2008 "Lehman files", 2009 "Old GM: nothing", 2012 "Lehman cancelled", 2021 "Hertz: rare win". Narration now says "its shares were cancelled in 2012". The reveal counts were changed to 3 and then 4.
7. **Line 7, unclear SIPC point.** "SIPC won't help either. It covers up to $500,000 when a broker fails" did not say that stock losses are excluded. It now reads "SIPC covers up to $500,000 if your broker fails, not if your stock does." The stat sub-label was changed to "if broker fails" and the label to "SIPC limit".
8. **Line 4, awkward narration.** "Cancelled, that's zero" was changed to "If they're cancelled, that's zero." The math is unchanged and correct: 100 × $20 = $2,000, then $0. On-screen values match the spoken ones.
9. **Post captions.** IG, TikTok and YouTube captions were updated to match: new hook, "often", "unsecured creditors like bondholders", "785 large ... most since 2010", and SIPC as broker failure only. The IG caption also notes the $250,000 cash limit within SIPC's $500,000.

## Verified, no change needed
- **Old GM.** Old shareholders got nothing in the 2009 bankruptcy. Bondholders got about 10% of New GM, and the old shares stayed with Motors Liquidation Co. and were later cancelled.
- **Hertz.** Filed May 2020. The plan was confirmed June 10, 2021 and took effect June 30, 2021. Creditors (about $19B) were paid in full, and shareholders received more than $1B in value (cash, stock and warrants, about $7-8 per share). "Rare win" and "shareholders got value" are accurate.
- **Cover.** "Paid Dead Last" with "$0" is paid off by the hook and the math line.
- **Other checks.** No advice, no price predictions and no political opinion. There are no time words that will be wrong on Jan 23, 2027. 2025 stays the latest full year until S&P's 2026 tally, usually out in early January. That release could make "the most since 2010" stale on Jan 23, 2027, because mid-2026 S&P reports showed 2026 running at a 16-year-high pace. The spoken claim says "in 2025", so it stays true regardless.
- **Limits.** check.py reports OK: 9 lines, 138 spoken words, an 11-word hook, and the last line unchanged.
