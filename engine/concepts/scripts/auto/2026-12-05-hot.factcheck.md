## Issues

1. **Line 2: the "$1 million a year" wording is ambiguous and can read as wrong (must fix).** Text "Above $1 million a year, it's 37%" and say "Above a million a year, it's thirty-seven percent" sound like the 37% applies to anyone earning over $1M a year. The rule is narrower: withholding is 37% on the part of *supplemental wages* (bonuses and similar pay) over $1M paid in a calendar year, and that rate is mandatory. Someone with a $1.2M salary and a $50K bonus still gets 22% on the bonus. The list item "37% over $1M" has the same problem.
   - Fix text: "On bonus pay over $1 million in a year, it's 37%. Or it's lumped into a regular paycheck."
   - Fix say: "On bonus pay over a million in a year, it's thirty-seven percent. Or it's lumped into a regular paycheck."
   - Fix list item: "37% on bonus >$1M" (or "37% past $1M bonus").
   - Post: `ig` is already precise ("37% on the part over $1M"). In `yt_desc`, change "(37% over $1 million)" to "(37% on supplemental pay over $1 million in a year)".

2. **Line 7: "FICA is 7.65% on both" leaves out the cap (minor).** 7.65% applies only up to the $184,500 Social Security wage base. Above it, only 1.45% Medicare applies, plus the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax withheld on wages over $200,000. The point that both kinds of pay are treated the same still holds.
   - Suggested text: "FICA is the same 7.65% on both, up to the Social Security cap. It's not taxed more, just withheld differently."

3. **Line 1: awkward phrasing (style).** "withhold a flat 22% federal" / "a flat twenty-two percent federal" ends on an adjective with no noun after it.
   - Fix: "Employers often withhold a flat 22% for federal tax." / say "...a flat twenty-two percent for federal tax."

4. **Line 5: "the bonus owes $600" (style).** A bonus doesn't owe anything.
   - Fix: "so the bonus adds $600 in tax, not the $1,100 withheld."

## Overclaim check

The script doesn't claim "you always get money back." Line 6 makes the refund conditional on other withholding being on target, and line 4 labels the example "still 12% bracket." If a bonus crosses into a higher bracket, only the dollars above the threshold are taxed at the higher rate, the same as a raise would be, so "same brackets as salary" (line 3) is still accurate. Optional: a few words such as "even if it nudges you into a higher bracket, only the dollars above it pay more" would head off the obvious follow-up question, but it isn't required.
