## Facts
- Open enrollment for 2027 Marketplace coverage starts **November 1, 2026** (a Sunday) in most states. (HealthCare.gov; AARP; KFF; Sinclair/KFF-based story "ACA premiums jump for second straight year as subsidy loss squeezes shoppers")
- Enroll by **December 15, 2026** for coverage starting **January 1, 2027** on HealthCare.gov (multiple guides). Final end date is disputed across sources (Jan 15 per CMS confirmation in Aug 2026 per several guides; Dec 15 per older articles) -> NOT stated in script.
- **KFF: average increase in finalized 2027 benchmark (second-lowest-cost silver) premiums = 13%**; insurers had proposed a 15% median increase (KFF Aug 3, 2026 update of 276 insurers). (KFF "9 Things to Watch for the 2027 ACA Open Enrollment Period"; KFF/Peterson "How much and why ACA Marketplace premiums are going up in 2027"; Sinclair stations wcti12/fox56/bakersfieldnow citing KFF; Washington Post interactive Oct 29, 2026 "ACA premiums are going up again")
- **HealthCare.gov states: +11%; states with their own platforms: +16%** (KFF).
- Second straight double-digit year: 2026 median finalized rate change was 20% (KFF).
- Drivers per insurers (KFF): rising health care prices (medical trend ~10% for 2027), expiration of enhanced premium tax credits at end of 2025 and a sicker risk pool (~4 percentage points).
- **2026 average benchmark premium for a 40-year-old: $625/month, up from $497 in 2025** (KFF state indicator; Becker's: +26% U.S. average 2026). 2024: $477 (AOL citing KFF).
- **Our math:** $625 x 1.13 = about $706/month (not a published figure; applying KFF's national average increase to KFF's 2026 average).
- Subsidies: premium tax credits are pegged to the benchmark plan, so when the benchmark rises, subsidies also rise; many subsidized enrollees are partly or fully insulated. People without subsidies (including buying off-exchange) bear the full increase (KFF, via search snippets of "9 Things to Watch").
- Enhanced credits expired after 2025, so the 400% of federal poverty level cutoff is back: above it, no premium tax credit (KFF "subsidy cliff" quick take; CNBC/NBC "subsidy cliff" piece; IRS 36B rules). Single-person 2027 dollar threshold not confirmed -> not stated.
- Enrollment: CMS effectuated enrollment fell from about 22.1 million (Feb 2025) to 19.2 million (Feb 2026), about 2.9 million or 13% (CMS report via KFF "ACA Marketplace Enrollment Is Down By 3 Million..."; CBPP; Becker's). Sign-ups for 2026 were 23.1 million, down over 1 million. Causes disputed (administration cites fraud removal; CBPP disagrees) -> script states only the drop.
- Insurer exits in 20+ states (healthinsurance.org only) and Molina shrinking to 6 states (weak source) -> NOT used.

## Chartable data series
- KFF avg benchmark premium, 40-year-old: 2024 $477, 2025 $497, 2026 $625 (2022/2023 values not confirmed; 2027 dollar figure not published). Only 3 confirmed points -> no chart; money/compare/stat scenes used.

## What it means for a normal person
- Anyone buying their own plan (self-employed, gig workers, early retirees, people between jobs) faces new prices for 2027. Subsidized buyers' aid typically rises with the benchmark; unsubsidized buyers pay the whole increase. Auto-renewal can leave someone in a plan whose price changed; comparing plans and updating income during open enrollment is how subsidies get set.

## What to watch
- Final deadline ruling (appeal); CMS sign-up snapshots during open enrollment; any congressional action on premium tax credits. No predictions.
