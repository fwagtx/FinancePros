# Research brief: What Adidas lost when Yeezy ended (How They Make Money, posts Tue Jan 26, 2027, 2 PM CT)

Evergreen. No news peg needed; every number is dated by fiscal year (FY = calendar year for Adidas). All company figures in euros, continuing operations.

## The split (state plainly, no editorializing)
- Oct 25, 2022: Adidas terminated the Yeezy partnership "immediately" after the designer's antisemitic remarks; stopped production and all payments (Adidas ad-hoc release, Oct 25, 2022; Fortune Oct 25, 2022; CNBC Nov 9, 2022).
- At the time Adidas guided to a hit of up to ~€250M to FY2022 net income (Adidas ad hoc; Fortune/CNBC).

## Size of Yeezy before the split
- Adidas never disclosed Yeezy sales before the split. Analyst estimates: Morningstar ~€1.5-2B / "close to $2B" a year; Evercore ISI ~$1-2B (CNBC Nov 9, 2022).
- Adidas Annual Report 2023 (official, after the fact): Yeezy revenues were "more than €1,200 million" in FY2022.
- Feb 2023 (Adidas): not selling the leftover stock would mean losing ~€1.2B of revenue and ~€500M of operating profit in FY2023; worst case writing off all stock -> operating loss of up to €700M (incl. ~€500M Yeezy write-off + ~€200M one-off costs) (CNBC Feb 10 2023, PBS/AP, Irish Times/Bloomberg). Note: the €1.2B is potential REVENUE, not inventory at cost. The €500M write-off was a worst-case forecast that did NOT happen.

## What happened to the stock
- Adidas sold leftover Yeezy pairs in drops starting May 31, 2023. First drop ~€400M ($437M) (AP/PBS, NPR, Rolling Stone).
- FY2023: Yeezy drops added ~€750M of revenue and ~€300M of operating profit (Adidas AR2023 / FY2024 release).
- FY2024: remaining stock sold off "at least at cost"; ~€650M revenue, ~€200M operating profit; last pairs sold Q4 2024 (Adidas FY2024 press release Mar 5, 2025; AR2024; Retail Dive).
- FY2025: zero Yeezy revenue (Adidas FY2025 release, Mar 4, 2026).
- Donations: Adidas donated part of proceeds to groups fighting discrimination and hate, incl. Anti-Defamation League and Philonise & Keeta Floyd Institute (AP/PBS, NPR). Per Adidas spokesperson to JTA: >€250M donated or set aside by end of 2023 (€200M to adidas Foundation, €50M to organizations) - single source, do not put the number on screen.

- CHART (operating profit, € billions): 2021 1.99, 2022 0.67, 2023 0.27, 2024 1.34, 2025 2.06. Evenly spaced years, big V shape.
- FY2022 op profit also hit by ~€312M one-off costs (incl. Russia exit) and weak China - Yeezy is not the only cause.

## FY2023 net loss
- Net loss from continuing operations €58M in FY2023, first annual net loss in about three decades (first since 1992) (Adidas AR2023; Fortune/Reuters Mar 13 2024; CNN; Retail Dive). Some outlets cite €75M (total incl. discontinued ops). Adidas attributed it partly to an extraordinarily high tax rate. Use "helped push".

## Worked examples (our math)
1. Concentration: €1.2B / €22.511B = 5.33% -> "about 5%" of FY2022 revenue.
2. Margin: Adidas' Feb 2023 framing €500M op profit / €1.2B revenue = 41.7% -> "about 42%" vs whole-company FY2022 op margin 3.0%.
3. Leftover sales: €750M (2023) + €650M (2024) = €1.4B revenue; €300M + €200M = €500M op profit.

## Surprising fact
Yeezy was only ~5% of sales, but its high margin meant losing it (plus other issues) pushed Adidas to its first loss in ~30 years - and the "doomed" stock ended up selling for ~€1.4B instead of being burned.

## Takeaway (brand concentration risk)
A small slice of revenue can be a big slice of profit; when one partner controls a high-margin line, the company carries key-person risk. Education only, no stock view.
