# Research brief: Why insurance premiums keep rising (2026-10-15-explainer)
Prepared September 30 - October 1, 2026. Posts Thursday, October 15, 2026. Evergreen wording; every number dated.
Focus: car insurance (health insurance plans are covered by the Oct 6 and Oct 16 explainers). Home insurance mentioned only as a driver line.

## Timely angle (latest data: August 2026 CPI, released September 11, 2026)
- BLS CPI, August 2026: motor vehicle insurance index DOWN 5.1% over the 12 months ending August 2026 (index 848.231 vs 894.075 in August 2025); fell 0.8% in August (seasonally adjusted) after -0.3% in July. All items CPI +3.4% over the same 12 months. (BLS CPI news release, Aug 2026 results, USDL-26-1496; reported by The Money Overview / NewsBreak)
- Same report: motor vehicle maintenance and repair index UP 5.2% over the 12 months ending August 2026. (BLS CPI Aug 2026, via search results quoting the release)
- So the twist: after a historic run-up, car insurance prices are now falling year over year, while repair prices are still rising.

## Worked example (math)
- Cumulative rise, 2021 annual average to 2025 annual average: 893.5 / 565.3 = 1.5806 -> about +58% in four years.
- Check via compounding: 1.079 x 1.174 x 1.178 x 1.060 = 1.582 -> +58%.
- Example (hypothetical, for illustration): a policy that cost $1,000 in 2021 and rose exactly with the index would cost about $1,000 x 1.58 = $1,580 in 2025.

## The surprising fact: insurers were losing money
- AM Best: US personal auto combined ratio was 112.2 in 2022 (from 101.4 in 2021), worst in at least 30 years / above 109.4 in 2000. A 112.2 combined ratio = insurers paid about $1.12 in claims and expenses for every $1 of premium (underwriting loss of $12 per $100). (AM Best Market Segment Report 2023; CAS Actuarial Review; Insurance Journal May 2023)
- 2023: still above 100 (AM Best projected ~106.5; S&P ~104.9). 2024: 95.3, best post-pandemic result (Triple-I, June 2025 press release). Net written premiums +14.4% in 2023, +12.8% in 2024 (Triple-I).
- Meaning: premiums rose to catch up with claim costs; once profits returned, price increases slowed and then reversed (2026 CPI).

## Why: claim costs
- CCC Intelligent Solutions, Crash Course 2026: preliminary average total cost of repair (repairable vehicles) $4,818 in 2025, +1.7% vs 2024 (smallest increase since 2017). Average repairable appraisal was $3,421 in 2020 (about +40.8% to 2025). Vehicles 6 years old or newer averaged $5,721; 7+ years $3,682. (CCC; Repairer Driven News; Collision Repair Magazine)
- Sensors/ADAS: 28.3% of repairable estimates in Q4 2025 included at least one sensor calibration, up from 21.8% in Q4 2024. Average calibration fee $485.56; average scan fee $149.10. (CCC Crash Course 2026)
- Total losses: record 23.1% of auto claims were total losses in 2025 (CCC Crash Course 2026, via InsureMojo / CCC release "Higher Severity and Record Total Loss Frequency").
- Home insurance: premiums rose again in 2025 (Rate Insurance: +9.16% to $2,205 average; Insurify: +12% to $2,948 - sources disagree on level). Drivers cited: severe weather losses and high rebuilding costs (Carrier Management/Reuters, March 2026). Used only as "storms and rebuilding costs" in script.

## How a premium is built (definition)
- Premium roughly = expected claims + insurer expenses + profit margin. Combined ratio = (losses + expenses) / premiums; above 100 means an underwriting loss.

## Script-safe numbers
- +58% (2021 to 2025 avg CPI motor vehicle insurance)
- $1.12 paid per $1 premium (2022, combined ratio 112.2)
- 7.9% / 17.4% / 17.8% / 6.0% annual changes 2022-2025
- $4,818 avg repair 2025; about $486 avg calibration ($485.56)
- -5.1% insurance vs +5.2% repair, 12 months to August 2026
