## The story: Home Depot sold more, but to slightly fewer shopping trips; each receipt got bigger
Source: The Home Depot Q3 fiscal 2026 earnings release, Nov 17, 2026 (quarter ended Nov 1, 2026), via CNW/newswire.ca and SEC 8-K Exhibit 99.1. Confirmed by CNBC (Nov 17) and Panabee summary.

Key numbers (Q3 FY2026 vs Q3 FY2025):
- Net sales: $43.4B ($43,398M), up $2.0B or 4.9% (from $41,352M).
- Comparable sales: +2.2% (US +2.2%). Year-ago quarter: +0.2%. StreetAccount estimate: +1.8% (CNBC).
- Comparable customer transactions: -0.5% (year-ago -1.6%).
- Comparable average ticket: +3.0% (year-ago +1.8%).
- Total customer transactions: 392.7M vs 393.5M (-0.2%).
- Average ticket: $93.09 vs $90.39 (+3.0%). Difference $2.70 (our math).
- Net earnings: $3.7B ($3,713M) vs $3.6B, +3.1%. Diluted EPS $3.72 vs $3.62; adjusted EPS $3.86 vs $3.74 (consensus ~$3.86, in line).
- Online comp sales: ~+13% (CNBC/Panabee; not in release table - not used on screen).
- FY2026 guidance updated: total sales growth ~4.5% (was 2.5%-4.5%), comp sales growth ~1.4% (was flat to 2%), diluted EPS growth ~1.5% from $14.23.
- Leadership: CFO Richard McPhail named CEO effective Nov 23, 2026; Ted Decker steps down as CEO, stays chair (company 8-K, Nov 17).
- Quote (release): "Results in the third quarter exceeded our expectations." - Richard McPhail, incoming CEO.

## Context
- "Average ticket" = average amount spent per transaction (receipt). "Comparable" = stores open over a year, excluding the extra week effects.
- For at least five quarters, Home Depot's growth has come from bigger receipts, not more trips; transactions have been negative each quarter, though the decline is shrinking.
- On the Feb 24, 2026 Q4 call, management attributed ticket growth to "some price increases," a mix shift to higher-ticket items and customers trading up (MarketBeat call highlights). No Q3 attribution confirmed yet (call is 9 AM ET), so only cite the February explanation, with its date.
- In Q1 FY26, coverage said shoppers paused large discretionary projects, focusing on smaller maintenance (Nasdaq/MarketBeat) - background only.

## What it means for a normal person
- If home-improvement receipts feel bigger, the data matches: the average Home Depot receipt is up about $2.70 in a year.
- Bigger receipts can mean higher prices, pricier items, or both; the receipt total alone doesn't say which.
- Takeaway: watch the per-trip total, not just how often you shop.
