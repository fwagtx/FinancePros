## The story (all from Berkshire's Q3 2026 report, released Sat Nov 7, 2026, per AP, CNBC, Bloomberg)
- Berkshire repurchased about $9.6 billion of its own stock in Q3 2026 (July-Sept), the most so far this year; CNBC: "more than doubles buybacks in third quarter".
- Q2 2026 buybacks: about $4.5 billion (AP, Aug 2026). Q1 2026: about $234 million. Year-to-date total about $14.4 billion (reported).
- Operating earnings: $12.705 billion ($12.7B), down 5.8% from a year earlier; $8,907 per Class A share vs FactSet analyst estimate $8,821.84 (AP).
- Net profit: $22.112 billion ($15,502/Class A share), down 28% from $30.796 billion a year earlier (includes investment swings; Berkshire says to focus on operating earnings).
- Insurance underwriting profit: $1.35 billion vs $2.37 billion a year ago (Bloomberg: -43%). Geico underwriting $982 million vs $1.77 billion (AP: Geico spending more on ads, rising losses).
- Cash pile: $377.4 billion at end of September 2026, up from $365.5 billion at end of June 2026 (it was nearly $400B at end of March 2026).
- Greg Abel became CEO in January 2026 (Warren Buffett remains chairman). Under Berkshire's policy, buybacks happen only when the CEO and chairman believe the stock is below its intrinsic value; no set amount.

## What a buyback is
A company uses its cash to buy its own shares. Fewer shares exist, so each remaining share owns a slightly bigger slice of the company. Berkshire pays no dividend; buybacks are how it returns cash.

## Why it matters to a normal person
- Berkshire is one of the biggest companies in the S&P 500, so most S&P 500 index funds and many 401(k)s own a piece.
- Buybacks are a signal management thinks the shares are worth more than the price (per its own policy) - not a guarantee or prediction.
- Geico is the second-biggest US auto insurer; its underwriting profit falling is a sign of higher claims costs/ad spending.
