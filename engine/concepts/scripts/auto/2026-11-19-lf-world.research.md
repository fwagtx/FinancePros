# Research brief: How global supply chains work and why they break
Slot: 2026-11-19-lf-world (World Money, long-form). Research date: Oct 1-2, 2026. Posts Nov 19, 2026.

## News angle (dated, use carefully and neutrally)
- From Feb 28, 2026, commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz was largely halted after the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran; a brief reopening under a June 2026 US-Iran agreement broke down in early July 2026 (Wikipedia "2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis", citing Reuters/Lloyd's List). Major container lines (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd) suspended Gulf transits.
- Roughly 20% of the world's daily oil supply passes through Hormuz (EIA estimate, widely cited).
- NY Fed GSCPI rose to 1.82 in April 2026 from 0.68 in March 2026, highest since 2022 (NY Fed, reported by Reuters/PYMNTS/Insurance Journal, May 2026). June 2026 reading 1.25, May revised to 1.81 (NY Fed release July 6, 2026, via Reuters/PYMNTS).
- Status of the strait can change before Nov 19: say only dated facts (Feb-July 2026), no present-tense status.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL; assumption stated)
Assume one 40ft container holds 5,000 pairs of sneakers (hypothetical round number for illustration).
- 2019 avg: $1,420 / 5,000 = $0.284 -> about 28 cents a pair
- Sept 2021 peak: $10,377 / 5,000 = $2.0754 -> about $2.08 a pair
- Sept 24, 2026: $4,468 / 5,000 = $0.8936 -> about 89 cents a pair
Point: per-item freight stays small even at the peak; the bigger damage of a broken chain is missing parts, delays and empty shelves (see chip shortage).

## How it works
- About 80% of the volume of international goods trade is carried by sea (UNCTAD, Review of Maritime Transport).
- US used car and truck CPI rose 37.3% in 2021, Dec 2020 to Dec 2021 (BLS, 'Consumer prices up 7.0 percent over year ended December 2021', TED Jan 2022). Confirmed: BLS TED 'CPI: 2021 in review'.
- Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco Economic Letter, June 2023, 'Global Supply Chain Pressures and U.S. Inflation': links GSCPI pressure to higher US inflation.
- Drewry WCI average YTD: $1,756 through Sept 2023; $4,113 through late Sept 2024 (Drewry weekly releases via AJOT).
- US goods imports 2023: Mexico ~$475B, China ~$427B, Canada ~$419B (Census via USAFacts/Forbes).
- Just-in-time (JIT): parts arrive right when needed, little inventory; pioneered by Toyota (Toyota Production System). Cheap when things flow, fragile when one link fails.
- Just-in-case: keep buffer stock. Toyota after the March 11, 2011 Japan earthquake required suppliers to stockpile 2 to 6 months of chips depending on lead time (Reuters, March 2021); it handled the early 2021 chip shortage better than rivals.

## Breaks
- 2021 chip shortage: automakers cut about 10.5 million vehicles from 2021 production (AutoForecast Solutions, per industry reporting); AlixPartners estimated $210B of 2021 auto industry revenue lost (AlixPartners, Sept 2021). AFS: ~4M more lost in 2022, ~2M in 2023.
- Ever Given: grounded in the Suez Canal on March 23, 2021, refloated March 29, 2021 (six days). Lloyd's List estimated ~$9.6B of trade held up per day (~$400M an hour); westbound ~$5.1B, eastbound ~$4.5B; Lloyd's List called them rough calculations (Al Jazeera/Lloyd's List, Mar 25, 2021).
- Suez normally handles about 15% of global maritime trade volume; Panama about 5% (IMF blog, Mar 7, 2024 / PortWatch).
- Red Sea: Houthi attacks on shipping from late 2023. Suez trade volume down 50% year over year in Jan-Feb 2024 (IMF PortWatch); Cape of Good Hope volume up an estimated 74%. Rerouting adds 10+ days (UNCTAD, 2024). Suez Canal Authority: 13,213 ships in 2024 vs 26,000+ in 2023, about half (Reuters, 2025). Maersk: Suez traffic down 66% (Sept 2024).
- 2026: Gemini (Maersk/Hapag-Lloyd) moved some services back to Suez in mid-2026; container net tonnage through Suez Jan-Aug 2026 up 54.2% vs a year earlier (Seatrade Maritime / gCaptain, 2026). Carriers say routing depends on Red Sea stability.
- Panama Canal 2023 drought: Gatun Lake at record lows; daily transits cut from a normal ~36 to as low as 22 in late 2023; Neopanamax draft limited to 48.5 ft (Aug 15, 2023). Panama Canal transits fell 29% in fiscal 2024 (Seatrade Maritime).

## Rewiring
- Nearshoring: In 2023 Mexico became the top US goods trading partner, about $798B in two-way goods trade, passing China (US Census Bureau, Feb 2024; Forbes/Axios). Mexico also led in 2019 briefly in some months/years.
- Tariffs: neutral mention only (a tax on imports that can shift where firms source); no numbers.

## Surprising fact
- At the September 2021 peak, moving one container across the ocean cost over 7x the 2019 average, yet per pair of sneakers it is about $2 in the hypothetical; the costliest breaks are about missing parts, not freight per item (10.5M cars not built in 2021 for want of chips).
