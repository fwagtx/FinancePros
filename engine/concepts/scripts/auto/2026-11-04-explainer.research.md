# Research brief: Refinancing federal student loans into private, what you give up (posts Nov 4, 2026)

## Rules
- Refinancing federal loans with a private lender pays off the federal loans with a new private loan. It is permanent: you can't convert back to federal loans (CFPB; lender disclosures).
- You lose: income-driven repayment (since July 1, 2026 the Repayment Assistance Plan, RAP, is the IDR plan for new loans; RAP payments count toward PSLF); Public Service Loan Forgiveness; federal deferment and forbearance; federal death and total-and-permanent-disability discharge; any future federal relief.
- Federal rates are fixed for the life of each loan; set each July 1 from the May 10-year Treasury auction (undergrad = yield + 2.05 pts).

## PSLF
- As of January 2026: over 1.2 million borrowers, $90.6 billion forgiven, average ~$75,000 (FSA data via Student Loan Planner). Sept 2025: $87.6B, 1,183,600 borrowers, avg $74,100.

## Worked example (hypothetical refi rate)
$40,000, 10 years (120 payments).
- At 6.53% federal: payment = 40000 x r / (1 - (1+r)^-120), r = 0.0653/12 -> $454.80/mo; total $54,576; interest $14,576.
- At 5.00% private (example): $424.26/mo; total $50,911; interest $10,911.
- Difference: about $30.54/mo, about $3,665 over 10 years.
Surprising fact: the savings can be smaller than one federal benefit you give up (average PSLF forgiveness ~ $75,000).
