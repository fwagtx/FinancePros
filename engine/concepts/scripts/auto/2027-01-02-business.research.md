# Research brief: How travel insurance makes money (How They Make Money, posts Sat Jan 2, 2027)

Evergreen. No timely news hook needed; every number below carries its date.

## Core business model (plain words)
- Travel insurers collect many small premiums before trips; most trips go fine, a few get cancelled or interrupted and are paid back. The insurer keeps what is left after claims, seller commissions and operating costs.
- Trip cancellation/interruption packages were about 94.7% of US consumer spend on travel protection in 2024 (UStiA 2022-2024 study, released June 16, 2025; also cited by NAIC).
- Sellers get paid: online travel agencies and airlines "remain the leading distribution channel" in the US (UStiA, June 2025). Travel agent channel grew 22.8% from 2022 to 2024 to $762.8 million (UStiA).
- Policies are often bundled with non-insurance products (cancellation fee waivers, travel assistance services); only the insurance part is overseen by state insurance departments (NAIC topic page, last updated 4/01/2026).

## Key numbers (with dates and sources)
- Price: travel insurance usually costs 5% to 10% of the total trip price (NAIC topic page, last updated April 1, 2026).
- US spend: $5.56 billion in 2024; 86.97 million people covered by 54.87 million plans; total trip value protected $68.04 billion in 2024 (vs $49 billion in 2019). 2019 spend: $3.81 billion; 2024 is +46% vs 2019. Since 2002 spend rose from $810 million. Study by Willis Towers Watson for UStiA, members >90% of market. (UStiA press release June 16, 2025)
- Our math: $5.56B / 54.87M plans = about $101 per plan (2024). $5.56B / $68.04B = about 8.2% of trip value (2024).
- Europe (EIOPA thematic review, October 2019, questionnaire to 201 insurers in 29 countries): average commission 24% of gross written premium; average claims ratio 40% of gross written premium, similar across channels; some insurers below 20% claims ratio; max commission 94%; 24 insurers paid commissions above 50% with claims ratios below 35%. EIOPA issued a warning to the industry about high commissions.
- UK (FCA general insurance value measures data 2025, first published July 21, 2026, covering Jan-Dec 2025): claims costs as a proportion of premium for the 3 travel products were 44-48% in 2025 vs 31-37% in 2024. Premiums rose 12% while claims paid rose 47%. Travel claims acceptance 83-86%; claims complaints 5-6% of claims registered.
- US: NAIC added a travel insurance loss ratio to its Market Conduct Annual Statement (MCAS) ratios (travel data starts 2022); UStiA objected that publishing it could mislead consumers. A published US national figure could not be retrieved, so the script uses the European (EIOPA) and UK (FCA) regulator figures and says so.

## Surprising fact
- In regulator data, insurers paid out well under half of travel premiums as claims: about 40% on average in Europe (EIOPA, 2019) and 31-37% in the UK in 2024 (FCA). The seller's commission alone averaged 24% in Europe (2019).

## Worked example (our math)
- $3,000 trip x 8% (inside NAIC's 5-10% range; UStiA 2024 implies ~8.2%) = $240 premium.
- At Europe's 2019 averages (EIOPA): claims 40% x $240 = $96; commission 24% x $240 = $57.60; left for insurer costs, assistance and profit = $240 - $96 - $57.60 = $86.40.
- These are averages across all buyers, not what any one buyer gets back: most get $0, a few get far more.

## Neutral framing
- No advice on whether to buy. Explain the pooling model and who gets paid.
