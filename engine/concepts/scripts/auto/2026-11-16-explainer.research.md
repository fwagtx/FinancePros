# Research: Why some banks pay way more interest on savings than others

## Key numbers (dated)
- FDIC national average savings rate: 0.37% APY as of Sept 21, 2026 (FDIC National Rates and Rate Caps; via Motley Fool).
- FDIC national savings rate, January of each year: Jan 2022 0.06%; Jan 2023 0.33%; Jan 2024 0.47%; Jan 2025 0.41%; Jan 2026 0.39% (FDIC National Rates and Rate Caps; FRED SNDR). National rate cap Jan 20, 2026: 4.39%.
- Big-bank standard savings, Sept 2026: Chase Savings 0.01% APY; Wells Fargo Way2Save 0.01% (US News; CNBC Select). BofA Advantage Savings cited 0.01%-0.04% depending on source/tier.
- Top high-yield savings accounts, Sept 2026: up to about 4.20% APY (Bankrate; NerdWallet up to 4.21%).

## Why the gap
- Branch networks, ATMs and staff cost money; online-only banks skip much of that overhead and use higher rates to attract deposits. (Bankrate, NerdWallet, CNBC Select)
- Big banks have large existing deposit bases and many customers who don't shop around, so they don't need to compete on rate. (Bankrate; US News)
- Online banks compete almost entirely on rate to win deposits.
- FDIC insurance: $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category, applies at any FDIC-insured bank, online or not. (FDIC)

## Worked example: $10,000 for one year (simple, ignoring compounding within the year; APY already includes it)
- 0.01% APY: 10,000 x 0.0001 = $1
- 0.37% national average: 10,000 x 0.0037 = $37
- 4.00% example high-yield: 10,000 x 0.04 = $400  (400x the 0.01% account)

## Surprising fact
- The same $10,000 can earn $1 or about $400 a year depending only on where it sits.
