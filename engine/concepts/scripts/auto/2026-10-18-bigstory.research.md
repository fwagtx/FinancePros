## Picked story: Wall Street's bet on an October Fed hike collapsed; Fed now in quiet period
Facts:
- Fed raised the federal funds target range by 0.25 pt to 3.75%-4.00% on September 16, 2026 (12-0 vote), its first hike since July 2023 and first rate change under Chair Kevin Warsh. Prior range 3.50%-3.75% since Dec 10, 2025. Median projection showed one more hike in 2026. (Federal Reserve implementation note Sept 16, 2026; CNBC Sept 16, 2026; U.S. Bank)
- Next FOMC meeting: October 27-28, 2026 (decision Oct 28). Following meeting Dec 8-9 (decision Dec 9).
- Fed blackout ("quiet period") starts the second Saturday before a meeting -> Saturday October 17, 2026 (FOMC external communications policy; previews note the quiet period covers every trading day before the meeting).
- Odds of an October hike (CME FedWatch, as reported by Benzinga/Yahoo Finance): 22% before the Wednesday Oct 14 hotter-than-expected September CPI (3.7% vs 3.6% forecast) -> 50% after it -> 28% Thursday afternoon Oct 15 -> 14.9% after Warsh's remarks Oct 16. CNBC: ~15% futures-implied probability Friday morning Oct 16. FT: from ~27% to 15%. Polymarket: 14% hike / 87% no change.
- Warsh, Oct 16, 2026, at IMF meetings in Bangkok (discussion with Kristalina Georgieva): "I don't prejudge meetings." Gave no signal on rate path. (Reuters via Investing.com, CNBC, Yahoo Finance)
- FedWatch Oct 16 (one source): 87.3% no change, 12.7% hike to 4.00-4.25%.
- Goldman Sachs: "Warsh's comments indicate that the FOMC is set to pause in October," still expects a second and final hike in December. December hike priced high (one source ~89% FedWatch; Polymarket 79%) - WEAKLY SOURCED, avoid specific Dec number.

## Context / what it means
- The federal funds rate feeds into credit card APRs, HELOC rates, savings/CD yields and some auto loans; a hike (or pause) changes what borrowers pay and savers earn.
- Odds swung 28 points in one day on one inflation report; they are market bets, not Fed decisions.
