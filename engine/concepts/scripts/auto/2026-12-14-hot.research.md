# 2026-12-14-hot research brief: Why is a used car so expensive?

Format: "Why is X so expensive". Evergreen; every number dated. Education only, no advice, no predictions.
Avoid overlap: Texas $32,000 tax/title/fees (Dec 14 explainer), AAA $64,315 5-year new-car cost, ~$9,000 loan interest, lemon laws, monthly-payment trick.

## Key numbers (with dates)
1. Average used-vehicle LISTING price: $27,239 in August 2026 (Cox Automotive / vAuto, "Used vehicle inventory August 2026"; also CBT News). About 7% above a year earlier; highest monthly average since December 2022; up 0.9% from revised $26,999 in July 2026. Dealer supply 2.13 million units.
   - For context, September 2025: $25,393 (Cox). Note: one AOL piece attributed $25,393 to Sept 2026; that number is the Sept 2025 Cox figure, so it is NOT used.
2. 3-year-old used vehicles: average TRANSACTION price $32,461 in Q2 2026 (Edmunds, via CBT News, The Drive, Stacker syndication), a Q2 record, up 4% year over year and up 15.5% from Q2 2021. 38 days on lot in Q2 2025 and Q2 2026.
3. Surprise fact: used vehicles sold under $20,000 were 55.2% of the used market in Q2 2019 and 31.8% in Q2 2026 (Edmunds, via Stacker/WFTV/CBT News).
4. Supply story (why):
   - New-vehicle sales were about 13.9 million in 2022, the lowest since 2011 (12.7 million), because a parts (chip) shortage limited factory output (Kelley Blue Book/Cox estimate; AP via Ratchet+Wrench). Pre-pandemic benchmark ~17 million/yr (single-source, not used on screen).
   - Leasing collapsed: leases were 27.82% of new vehicles delivered in Q2 2021 and 19.65% in Q2 2022 (Experian State of the Automotive Finance Market Q2 2022; Experian blog "Leasing Continues to Decline in Q2 2022 Amid Inventory Shortages"; Auto Remarketing).
   - Edmunds' Ivan Drury: leasing penetration in 2022-2023 was the lowest in about 10 years, which shrank the pipeline of 3-year-old cars returning to the market (CBT News interview).
   - S&P Global Mobility forecast first-half 2025 lease maturities down 41% year over year, tied to scarce 2022 leasing.
   - Leases typically run about 3 years, so 2022's missing leases = missing nearly-new used cars around 2025-2026.
5. Wholesale (what dealers pay): Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index (Jan 1997 = 100, mix/mileage/seasonally adjusted), September readings as first reported by Cox Automotive:
   - Sept 2020: 161.2 (Cox, Oct 2020)
   - Sept 2021: 204.8 (Cox, Oct 2021; record at the time)
   - Sept 2022: 204.5 (Cox, Oct 7, 2022)
   - Sept 2023: 214.3 (Cox, Oct 2023)
   - Sept 2024: 203.0 (Cox via Calculated Risk, Oct 7, 2024)
   - Sept 2025: 207.0 (Cox, Oct 7, 2025)
   - Sept 2026: 205.9 (Cox, Oct 7, 2026; down 0.6% y/y, down 1.1% from Aug 2026)
   Caveat: Cox revises past values (e.g., Sept 2023's -3.9% y/y implies a revised Sept 2022 near 223). Values above are as first published each year.
6. Financing: average used-vehicle loan rate 11.19% in Q2 2026, down from 11.57% in Q2 2025 (Experian State of the Automotive Finance Market Q2 2026, via Auto Remarketing and The Shop Mag). Average used loan $27,852; average used payment $542.

## Worked example (exact math)
Manheim Sept 2026 vs Sept 2020: 205.9 / 161.2 = 1.2773 -> wholesale values about 27.7% higher than September 2020.
Sub-$20K share: 55.2% - 31.8% = 23.4 percentage points smaller (Q2 2019 to Q2 2026).
Lease share drop: 27.82% - 19.65% = 8.17 points (Q2 2021 to Q2 2022).

## Surprising fact
Under-$20,000 used cars went from more than half of used sales (55.2%, Q2 2019) to under a third (31.8%, Q2 2026).
