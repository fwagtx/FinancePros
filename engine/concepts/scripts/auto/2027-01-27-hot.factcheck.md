# Fact-check: 2027-01-27-hot (Fake IRS calls, texts and letters)

Posting date: January 27, 2027. Checked November 28, 2026. Firecrawl had no credits left, so I used WebSearch results that quote ftc.gov and irs.gov, plus news coverage.

## Claims verified (no change needed)
- **FTC fraud losses, each year's figure as first announced:** 2022 "nearly $8.8B" (Feb 2023 release); 2023 "more than $10B" (Feb 2024); 2024 "more than $12.5B" (Mar 2025); 2025 $15.9B. The 2025 figure comes from FTC testimony to the Joint Economic Committee on Mar 25, 2026, and AP/Spokesman/AOL reported it as "a record $15.9 billion". The FTC press release rounds it to about $16B. The chart points [8.8, 10.0, 12.5, 15.9] and the end label $15.9B match the narration.
- **+81% math:** 15.9 / 8.8 = 1.807, so +81% is correct. The note "+81%" is 4 characters.
- **Freshness:** 2025 is still the latest full year on the posting date. The FTC usually releases a new year's data in Feb or Mar, so the 2026 data will not be out by Jan 27, 2027. The same goes for the 2027 Dirty Dozen, which usually comes out in March, so "2026" is still correct.
- **IRS contacts you first by mail:** confirmed by IRS guidance and the 2026 Dirty Dozen ("generally contacts people by mail first").
- **Dirty Dozen 2026:** IR-2026-30, released March 5, 2026. The list includes email/text impersonation (phishing/smishing) and AI-enabled phone impersonation (voice cloning robocalls). Line 6 is correct.
- **Reporting:** IRS.gov/phishing says to email phishing@irs.gov and to forward the text to 7726 (SPAM).
- **Threatening arrest and demanding gift cards or wires:** IRS guidance says the IRS does not threaten to have police arrest you and does not demand a specific payment method such as a gift card, prepaid debit card or wire transfer.
- **Cover and title:** "Fake IRS Call?" and "The IRS Never Asks for Gift Cards" are paid off by lines 4-8.
- No political opinion and no price predictions. The "hang up, look it up" line is safety guidance, not financial advice.

## Issues found and fixes applied
1. **Line 0, weak hook.** The old hook ("That IRS call demanding gift cards? It's a scam. Every time.") was true but gave the answer away and left no reason to keep watching. **Fix:** text and say are now "IRS call demanding gift cards? It's fake. Here's the tell." That is 10 words, under 3 seconds, and line 4 ("The tell: ...") pays it off. The same change was made to the first line of the IG post.
2. **Line 3, wording.** The FTC says imposter losses were "more than $3.5 billion", not "about". **Fix:** "took more than $3.5 billion of that in 2025" in text and say. The IG post now says "more than $3.5 billion".
3. **Line 5, overbroad claims.** "text you first" is too broad. The IRS says it doesn't start contact by text, email or social media *to request personal or financial information*. "Crypto" is not in the IRS's standard list of demanded payment methods (gift card, prepaid debit card, wire). **Fix:** "It won't demand gift cards or wires, threaten arrest, or text asking for your info." List items are now: Demand gift cards / Demand a wire / Threaten arrest / Text for your info. The IG and YT descriptions no longer mention crypto.
4. **Line 7, unsafe and awkward to say.** "your notice's number" is hard to say aloud, and it sends viewers to a number printed on a letter that could be fake. IRS guidance is to verify through your IRS.gov account or a number listed on IRS.gov. **Fix:** text is now "Verify on IRS.gov. Email fakes to phishing@irs.gov; forward texts to 7726." and the say line matches. Split left items: "IRS.gov account", "Number on IRS.gov". The IG and YT descriptions were updated the same way. This also brought the total spoken words down from 147 to 137.
