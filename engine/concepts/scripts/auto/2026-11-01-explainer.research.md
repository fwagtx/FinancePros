# Research brief: Buying a home with a partner before marriage (posts Nov 1, 2026)

## Key facts
- NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers (transactions July 2024-June 2025): unmarried couples were 6% of all buyers and 11% of first-time buyers. Married couples 61% of all buyers.
- Unmarried couples' share of first-time buyers: 18% (2022 Profile), 16% (2023 Profile), 11% (2025 Profile).
- How title can be held (US, varies by state):
  - Joint tenancy with right of survivorship: equal ownership; if one owner dies, the other automatically owns the whole thing, even if a will says otherwise.
  - Tenants in common: shares can be unequal (e.g., 75/25); each share passes to that owner's heirs, not automatically to the partner.
- Unmarried partners generally don't get divorce-court property division; a written co-ownership (cohabitation/property) agreement sets who gets what if they split (Nolo, FindLaw).
- If both sign the mortgage, each is legally responsible for the whole loan, not half.

## Worked example
- $80,000 down payment: Partner A puts in $60,000 (75%), Partner B $20,000 (25%).
- Years later they sell and walk away with $100,000 of equity.
- Joint tenants (equal shares): $50,000 each. A gets $10,000 less than they put in; B gets $30,000 more.
- Tenants in common 75/25: A $75,000, B $25,000.
- Gap for Partner A: $25,000.
