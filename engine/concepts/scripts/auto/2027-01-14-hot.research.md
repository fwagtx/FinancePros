# Research brief: What happens if your landlord keeps your security deposit?
Slot: 2027-01-14-hot (Hot Take, posts Thu Jan 14, 2027, 6 PM CT). Researched Nov 15, 2026. Evergreen: every rule is a statute with its date; no "this week" angle used.
Education only, not legal advice. Rules vary by state; the four states below are examples.

## Angle / format
Money Myth-style: many renters assume a landlord can just keep a deposit and nothing happens. Fact: in several states, a landlord who wrongly keeps it can owe the tenant a multiple of the deposit (up to 2x in CA, 3x in MA, $100 + 3x in TX, bad-faith/willful standards vary).

## Confirmed rules (state statutes; checked via official statute text snippets, Nov 2026)

### California (Civil Code 1950.5)
- Deadline: within 21 days after the tenant vacates, landlord must return the deposit or send an itemized statement of deductions. (Civ. Code 1950.5(g); San Mateo County tenant page; CA Courts self-help guide.)
- Penalty: bad-faith retention "may subject the landlord ... to statutory damages of up to twice the amount of the security, in addition to actual damages." (1950.5(l); San Mateo Superior Court small-claims page.)
- Deductions allowed for unpaid rent, cleaning to move-in level, repair of damage "exclusive of ordinary wear and tear" (1950.5(b)).
- Wear and tear (CA DRE "California Tenants" guide, 2025 and 2026 editions): normal wear and tear includes "simple wearing down of carpet and drapes because of normal use or aging and includes moderate dirt or spotting"; "large rips or indelible stains" are damage.
- Professional carpet/other professional cleaning can't be charged unless reasonably necessary to return the unit to move-in condition, exclusive of ordinary wear and tear (1950.5, as amended by AB 2801).
- Deposit cap: one month's rent, furnished or unfurnished, effective July 1, 2024 (AB 12); exception: certain small landlords may take two months. (LA County DCBA page; CA DRE 2025/2026 guide; 1950.5(c).)
- AB 2801 (approved Sept 19, 2024) photo rules, now in 1950.5:
  - Beginning April 1, 2025: landlord must photograph the unit within a reasonable time after the tenant returns possession, before repairs/cleaning that will be deducted, and again after repairs/cleaning.
  - For tenancies beginning on or after July 1, 2025: landlord must photograph the unit immediately before, or at the start of, the tenancy.
  (AB 2801 bill text and 1950.5 on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, via gov-index snippets.)
- Small claims (CA Courts SC-100 form and Self-Help Guide, current as of 2024-2026): individuals may claim up to $12,500; businesses up to $6,250. Tenants can sue for deposit return there.

### New York (General Obligations Law 7-108, amended by HSTPA 2019)
- Cap: deposit limited to one month's rent (HSTPA, effective June 14, 2019; NY HCR fact sheet confirms "limits the amount of a security deposit for any apartment to one month's rent").
- Deadline: within 14 days after the tenant vacates, landlord must give an itemized statement of what is kept and return the rest. If the landlord misses it, the landlord "shall forfeit any right to retain any portion of the deposit." (GOL 7-108(1-a)(e); NY AG publications; NY AG 2026 court petition quoting it.)
- Landlord bears the burden of proving the amount kept is reasonable (7-108(1-a)(f)).
- Willful violation: "punitive damages of up to twice the amount of the deposit" (7-108(1-a)(g); Masseroli v Gatfield, 2024 NY Slip Op 24168).
- Excludes ordinary wear and tear.

### Texas (Property Code ch. 92)
- Deadline: refund or written itemized description of deductions on or before the 30th day after the tenant surrenders the premises (92.103; 92.109(d) presumption).
- Forwarding address: landlord is not obligated to return the deposit or itemize until the tenant gives a written statement of a forwarding address (92.107).
- Normal wear and tear can't be deducted (92.104); defined as "deterioration that results from the intended use of a dwelling" (92.001(4)) (Texas State Law Library guide).
- Penalty: a landlord who in bad faith retains a deposit "is liable for an amount equal to the sum of $100, three times the portion of the deposit wrongfully withheld, and the tenant's reasonable attorney's fees" (92.109(a)). Missing the 30-day deadline creates a presumption of bad faith (92.109(d)). Bad faith failure to itemize forfeits the right to withhold any portion (92.109(b)).
- Small claims: justice court (small claims) cases up to $20,000 (Gov't Code 27.031; Texas State Law Library FAQ).

### Massachusetts (G.L. c.186 s.15B)
- Deadline: return deposit (with interest) within 30 days after the tenancy ends.
- Damage deductions require an itemized list of damages sworn under pains and penalties of perjury, with written evidence of repair costs, within those 30 days; "reasonable wear and tear excluded."
- Penalty: for violations of key clauses, tenant "shall be awarded damages in an amount equal to three times the amount of such security deposit or balance thereof ... plus interest at the rate of five per cent ... together with court costs and reasonable attorney's fees" (s.15B(7)). (malegislature.gov; mass.gov.)
- Deposit cannot exceed the first month's rent (s.15B(1)(b), mass.gov guidance).

## Worked example (our math, Texas 92.109(a))
Landlord keeps $1,000 of a deposit in bad faith.
$100 + (3 x $1,000) = $100 + $3,000 = $3,100, plus the tenant's reasonable attorney's fees.
(Courts must find bad faith; missing the 30-day deadline creates a presumption of it, which the landlord can rebut.)

## The surprising fact
Missing the deadline itself costs the landlord: in New York, a landlord who misses the 14-day itemized statement forfeits the right to keep any of the deposit; in Texas, missing 30 days triggers a presumption of bad faith; and in Massachusetts the penalty is triple plus 5% interest and attorney's fees.

## Practical takeaways (education, not legal advice)
- Take dated photos/video at move-in and move-out.
- Give the landlord a forwarding address in writing (required in Texas before the clock matters).
- Ask for the itemized statement; check deductions against wear and tear.
- Small claims court is the usual route: up to $12,500 (CA individuals), $20,000 (TX).
