# 2026-10-14-hot research brief: What happens if your landlord sells the building?
Format: Money Myth (myth: "new owner, new lease / you have to move"; fact: the lease generally transfers with the building).
No single timely news hook; evergreen.

## Key facts (with dates/sources)
1. A lease survives a sale. General rule in US states: the buyer takes the property subject to existing leases; a fixed-term lease stays valid until it ends, same rent and same end date; the new owner cannot change those terms just because of the sale. (Lemonade renters guide; Outerbridge Law; BatesonLaw; multiple legal explainers, 2025-2026.) Rules vary by state; described as "in most states"/"generally".
2. Security deposit follows the tenant. California Civil Code 1950.5(h): when the landlord's interest ends (sale, assignment, etc.), the landlord must, within a reasonable time, either transfer the remaining deposit to the successor or return it to the tenant, and notify the tenant (by personal delivery or first-class mail) of the transfer, claims against it, the amount, and the successor's name, address and phone number. If the landlord does not comply, the successor is jointly and severally liable with the landlord for repaying the deposit. (Cal. Civ. Code 1950.5, via Justia/FindLaw/Santa Clara court PDF.)
   SURPRISING FACT: in California, the new owner can owe you the full deposit even if the old owner never handed it over (joint and several liability when 1950.5(h) is not followed).
3. Foreclosure: federal Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act (PTFA, enacted 2009, expired end of 2014, restored permanently effective June 23, 2018 by the Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act). The immediate successor after foreclosure must give bona fide tenants 90 days' notice before requiring them to vacate, and tenants with leases may stay to the end of the lease term; exception: a buyer who will occupy the unit as a primary residence can end the lease with 90 days' notice. (NLIHC PTFA memo; UNC School of Government; National Homelessness Law Center fact sheet.)
4. Month-to-month tenants: no fixed term, so a new owner can generally end the tenancy with the notice required by state law (e.g., California: 30 days if under 1 year, 60 days if 1 year or more; local just-cause/rent rules can add limits). Described in script only as "timing set by state law".
5. Scale: US homeownership rate Q2 2026 = 65.0% (same as Q2 2025, 65.0%), so about 35% of households rent. Total households Q2 2026: 133.8 million. Rental vacancy rate Q2 2026: 7.3%. (Census Bureau HVS release July 28, 2026; NAHB Eye on Housing Aug 2026.)

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
Assumptions: rent $1,800/month, fixed lease with 8 months left when the building sells, $2,000 deposit.
- Rent the new owner must honor: 8 x $1,800 = $14,400 at the same price through the end of the lease.
- Deposit: $2,000 moves to the new owner (or is refunded); in California the new owner can owe the $2,000 even if never transferred.
