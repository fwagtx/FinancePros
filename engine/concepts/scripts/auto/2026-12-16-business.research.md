# Research brief: How office landlords cope when workers stay home
Slot: 2026-12-16-business (How They Make Money, vertical short). Researched Saturday, October 17, 2026; posts Wednesday, December 16, 2026.
All figures are dated by month/quarter. Note: Trepp publishes monthly; by Dec 16 the October and November 2026 readings will be out, so the script always says "in September 2026".

## Timely angle
- Trepp, September 2026 (released early October 2026): overall US CMBS delinquency rate rose to 8.02%, highest since late 2020 (7.85% in August 2026; 7.55% a year earlier). Office delinquency rose 16 bps to **12.16%**, just below the all-time high of **12.34% (January 2026)**, and about a point above 11.13% in September 2025. Five large single-asset, single-borrower loans drove much of the increase; four became nonperforming or moved to foreclosure after missing maturity payoff dates. (Scotsman Guide citing Trepp. WEAK: only one outlet found for the September 2026 numbers.)
- Cross-check of the trend: Trepp August 2026 office rate 12.00%, overall 7.85% (CRE Daily). January 2026 office 12.34% all-time high, up 103 bps (MBA NewsLink, Feb 10, 2026; ConnectCRE).
- 12.16% is roughly 1 in 8 (1/8 = 12.5%; 12.16% = 1 in 8.2). "Roughly one in eight" is a fair rounding.

## Why: empty desks
- Cushman & Wakefield Q3 2026 US Office MarketBeat (October 2026): national office vacancy **19.8%**, down 25 bps year over year, third straight quarter of year-over-year improvement; recent high 20.1% in Q3 2025. Net absorption +50,000 sq ft in Q3; +15.5 million sq ft over the past year. Sublease space 89 million sq ft, lowest since Q4 2020. Only 7.1 million sq ft delivered in first three quarters of 2026, about one-fifth of the historical average.
- Flight to quality (same report): vacancy in top-tier properties **down 215 bps over the past two years**, while Class B and C vacancy has kept rising.
- Methodology varies: CoStar Q2 2026 national vacancy slightly below 14%; Yardi Matrix 17.6% as of May 2026. Always name the source.

## Values
- Green Street Commercial Property Price Index, June 2026: office values **34% below their 2022 peak**, up roughly 5% over the past year; all-property index about 14% below peak (CRE Daily). February 2026: office down 35% from peak (GlobeSt). Illustration: $100 of 2022 office value is about $66 at June 2026 (100 x (1 - 0.34) = 66).

## How landlords cope
1. Concessions: landlords are using bigger tenant-improvement (build-out) allowances and free-rent packages to lock in renewals (CoRadvisors, Aug 2026 leasing benchmarks). CompStak/Savills via Bisnow: TI allowances up 112% since 2016, peaking at $212/sq ft in gateway markets (article date unclear; WEAK, not used on screen).
2. Conversions: RentCafe 2026 report (March 2026): **90,300** apartment units planned from office conversions nationwide, **up 28%** from 70,600 a year earlier and nearly 4x 2022; offices are 47% of future adaptive-reuse projects. Leaders: New York 16,358, Washington DC 8,479, Chicago 4,360. RentCafe cites $213 billion in office loans coming due.
3. Loans that can't refinance: Trepp says much of the September 2026 rise came from big loans that missed maturity payoff dates and went nonperforming or to foreclosure.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled as example)
Assumptions: 10-year lease, $50 per sq ft per year asking rent, 12 months free.
- Rent paid: 9 paying years x $50 = $450 over 10 years.
- Effective rent: $450 / 10 = **$45 per sq ft per year**, a **10% discount** ($5 / $50), while the headline rent on the lease stays $50.

## Surprising fact
Even as national vacancy edges down (19.8% in Q3 2026, per Cushman), office loan delinquency sat near a record in September 2026 (12.16%, Trepp), because loans signed at old values are coming due on buildings worth about a third less (Green Street).
