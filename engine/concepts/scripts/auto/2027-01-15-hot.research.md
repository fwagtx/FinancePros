# Research brief: Money myth: "commission-free trading apps make nothing off your trades"
Slot: 2027-01-15-hot (Hot Take, posts Fri Jan 15, 2027, 6 PM CT). Researched Mon Nov 16, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its year or date; no "this week" angle used (latest hard data is full-year 2025, plus Q3 2026 context).
Education only: no app or stock recommendations, neutral on regulation. Robinhood is used as the example because it is the best-known commission-free app and publishes the numbers; single-company figures are attributed to Robinhood's filings.

## Angle / format
Money Myth. Myth: if a trading app charges $0 commission, it earns nothing when you trade. Fact: Robinhood reported $2.63 billion of "transaction-based revenues" in 2025 (mostly payment for order flow, PFOF, from market makers on options and stock orders, plus crypto trading revenue), and another $1.51 billion of net interest revenue (interest on customer cash, margin loans and securities lending).

## Key definitions
- Payment for order flow (PFOF): a market maker (wholesaler, for example Citadel Securities or Virtu) pays the broker to route customer orders to it; the market maker earns the bid-ask spread. Robinhood's 10-K describes transaction-based revenues as consisting primarily of amounts earned from routing user orders to market makers (options, equities), plus crypto transaction revenue.
- SEC Rule 606(a) (Regulation NMS): brokers must publish quarterly reports on where they route customer orders and the net payments they receive for that order flow (amended 2018, more granular reporting from 2020). Rule 607: brokers must disclose their PFOF policies to customers at account opening and annually. (SEC Rule 606/607; tastytrade and Robinhood 606 disclosure pages; SEC Federal Register notice on Rule 607 collection extension, April 2026.)
- Robinhood's own 606 reports (cdn.robinhood.com, FINRA 606 archive): list Citadel Securities and Virtu among the market makers paying for its order flow (2019 through 2026 reports). Q3 2019 report: options PFOF averaged $0.50 per contract (Citadel). Secondary (ChartRow, Q2 2026 606 data): about $356.6M in disclosed options payments in that quarter; not used on screen.

## The surprising fact: SEC 2020 settlement
- Dec 17, 2020 (SEC press release 2020-321): Robinhood Financial LLC agreed to pay a $65 million civil penalty, without admitting or denying the findings, to settle charges that it misled customers about its revenue sources (payment for order flow) from 2015 to late 2018 and failed to satisfy its duty of best execution.
- SEC found Robinhood's unusually high PFOF rates were a main driver of inferior execution prices, which deprived customers of $34.1 million in total, even after accounting for the commission savings. Also: cease-and-desist, censure, independent consultant. Robinhood said the settlement concerned historical practices. (SEC; ThinkAdvisor, Engadget, CoinDesk, Advisor.ca, Dec 17, 2020.)

## Regulation, 2022 to 2026
- Dec 2022: SEC proposed Regulation Best Execution and the Order Competition Rule (would have required many retail orders to go to auctions before a wholesaler could fill them).
- June 12, 2025: SEC formally withdrew 14 pending proposals, including Regulation Best Execution and the Order Competition Rule (Proskauer, Paul Hastings, Stinson, Carlton Fields, National Law Review). VERIFIED. PFOF remains legal in the US with Rule 606/607 disclosure and FINRA Rule 5310 best-execution duty.
- 2026: no new US final rule on PFOF found (SEC comment activity on options market structure, Rule 607 paperwork extension April 2026; FINRA best-execution comments Sept 2026).
- EU: MiFIR Article 39a (Regulation (EU) 2024/791) bans PFOF; member-state transition ended June 30, 2026; Germany's exemption expired July 1, 2026 (PwC Legal, BaFin statements via Regulation Tomorrow, A&O Shearman).

## Worked example (our math)
- Robinhood 2025: trading revenue + net interest = $2,628M + $1,514M = $4,142M, about 92% of ~$4.5B total net revenue ("our math"; 4,142 / 4,500 = 0.92).
- Growth: 2,628 / 785 = 3.35, so 2025 transaction-based revenue was more than triple 2023's.
- 2020 case: commission savings did not cover worse prices; customers still came out $34.1M behind in aggregate (SEC finding).
