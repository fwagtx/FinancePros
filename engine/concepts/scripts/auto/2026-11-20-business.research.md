# 2026-11-20-business research: How Black Friday really works for retailers (doorbusters, margins, returns)

Researched Oct 2, 2026. Posts Fri Nov 20, 2026 (Black Friday 2026 is Nov 27, 2026). Evergreen: every number dated.
Angle: RETAILER economics (not the "is Black Friday cheapest" consumer angle, which the Nov 17 video covered with Which?/Adobe).
Note: sec.gov, nrf.com, macysinc.com, bestbuy.com, nasdaq.com are blocked for direct fetch here; figures confirmed via search results quoting the filings/releases, cross-checked across 2+ results.

## The key company: Macy's, Inc. fiscal 2025 (year ended Jan 31, 2026; Q4 = Nov 2025-Jan 2026, includes Black Friday)
Source: Macy's Q4/FY2025 earnings release (Mar 18, 2026, 8-K Ex. 99.1) and FY2025 10-K; quarterly releases (8-Ks May 28, Sep 3, Dec 3, 2025).
- FY2025 net sales: $21.8 billion (down 2.4%); comparable sales up 1.5%.
- Q4 2025 net sales: $7.6 billion (down 1.7%), comparable sales up 1.8%.
- Q4 share of the year: 7.6 / 21.8 = 34.9% ("nearly 35%") in one of four quarters (an even split would be 25%).
- FY2025 gross margin rate: 38.0% (down 30 bp per the annual report; one outlet said 40 bp; not used).
- 10-K seasonality language: business is seasonal, with a significant/disproportionate portion of annual sales and earnings in the fourth quarter, which includes the holiday selling period.

### Chartable series: Macy's, Inc. gross margin rate by quarter, fiscal 2025
| Quarter (FY2025) | Net sales | Gross margin rate |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 (Feb-Apr 2025) | $4.6B | 39.2% (flat y/y) |
| Q2 (May-Jul 2025) | $4.8B | 39.7% (down 80 bp) |
| Q3 (Aug-Oct 2025) | $4.7B | 39.4% (down 20 bp) |
| Q4 (Nov 2025-Jan 2026) | $7.6B | 35.2% (down 50 bp; ~60 bp tariff impact per Macy's) |
Plain words: gross margin = what's left of each sales dollar after the cost of the goods. Macy's kept about 39 cents per dollar in Q1-Q3 and about 35 cents in the holiday quarter.
Caveat: Macy's attributes the Q4 y/y decline mainly to tariffs; the lower Q4 level vs other quarters is a seasonal pattern (holiday promotions/clearance). Do not claim doorbusters alone caused it.

## Doorbusters / loss leaders (definition)
- A loss leader/doorbuster is an item priced at or below cost (or very thin margin), often in limited quantities, to draw shoppers who then buy other, higher-margin items (Wikipedia "Loss leader"; HowStuffWorks "Is there a dark side to doorbuster deals?"; PolitiFact 2019 on doorbuster deals). Used only as a general definition, not a claim about a named retailer's pricing.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, stated assumptions)
- Store sells a TV that cost it $300 for $280: 280 - 300 = -$20 loss.
- Same shopper adds $100 of accessories (cables, mount) at an assumed 40% gross margin: 100 x 0.40 = +$40.
- Basket gross profit: -20 + 40 = +$20.
- If the TV is later returned (17% expected holiday return rate is an industry average, not per item), the store also has return handling costs; not quantified on screen.

## The surprising fact
Macy's busiest quarter is its thinnest: about 35% of fiscal 2025 sales came in the holiday quarter, yet that quarter's gross margin (35.2%) was the lowest of the year (39.2-39.7% in Q1-Q3).
