## Facts (all from ISM October 2026 Manufacturing PMI Report, released Monday Nov 2, 2026, 10:00 AM ET, unless noted)
- Manufacturing PMI: 55.5% in October 2026, up 1.0 point from 54.5% in September.
- Above 50 = expansion. October = 10th consecutive month of expansion (Jan-Oct 2026), following a 10-month contraction in 2025 (Mar-Dec 2025).
- Consensus forecast: about 54.5 (Reuters poll / investinglive) or 54.6 (FXStreet). Beat expectations.
- Production index: 59.0, highest since November 2021.
- New Orders: 55.5 (up 0.2). Employment: 52.4 (down 0.3, still growing). Inventories: 51.4 (back to growth). New export orders 51.2 (from 50.9).
- Prices index: 75.2, down from 77.9 in September, still elevated. 53.1% of respondents reported paying higher prices (58.6% in Sept). Higher prices cited for aluminum, copper, diesel fuel, electronic components.
- Supplier deliveries slowed for 11th straight month.
- Susan Spence (chair, ISM Manufacturing Business Survey Committee): four of five subindexes grew faster than in September; employment growth slowed (IndustryWeek).
- December 2025 context: PMI 47.9 (lowest reading of 2025, 10th straight month of contraction); prices index 58.5 (ISM / IndustryWeek).
- Separate survey: S&P Global final US manufacturing PMI October 54.9 (not ISM).

## Context / what it means for a normal person
- PMI is a monthly survey of purchasing managers at US factories; over 50 means activity is growing, under 50 shrinking.
- Busier factories: more orders and output, factory hiring still growing (employment 52.4), a sign the economy is not slowing.
- But the cost side: factories still report paying more for inputs (metals, diesel, electronics). Higher input costs can pass through to prices on goods people buy. Prices index eased from September but remains far above December 2025's 58.5.
- Strong growth + sticky prices is the mix the Fed watches when deciding rates (do not predict).
