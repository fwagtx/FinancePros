## Facts (IRS, announced Thursday Nov 5, 2026; IR-2026-133 / Rev. Proc. 2026-35)
- Adjustments cover almost 70 tax provisions for tax year 2027; they apply to 2027 income, on returns generally filed in 2028.
- Standard deduction 2027: single / married filing separately $16,600 (2026: $16,100, +$500); married filing jointly $33,200 (2026: $32,200, +$1,000); head of household $24,925 (2026: $24,150).
- Bracket income thresholds rise on average about 3.2% (chained CPI). Rates unchanged (10/12/22/24/32/35/37%).
- 2027 single thresholds (secondary reports citing Rev. Proc. 2026-35): 10% up to $12,800; 12% $12,801-$52,025; 22% $52,026-$109,125.
- 2026 single thresholds (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, Oct 2025): 10% up to $12,400; 12% to $50,400; 22% to $105,700.
- Child tax credit 2027: $2,300 (+$100) (NBC). EITC max (3+ kids) $8,496.

## Our math (single filer, standard deduction only, $60,000 wages, federal income tax only)
- 2026: taxable 60,000-16,100 = 43,900 -> 1,240 + 12% x 31,500 (=3,780) = $5,020
- 2027: taxable 60,000-16,600 = 43,400 -> 1,280 + 12% x 30,600 (=3,672) = $4,952
- Difference: $68 less federal income tax on the same pay. (Payroll taxes unaffected.)

## Chart: single-filer standard deduction by tax year (IRS)
2018 $12,000; 2019 $12,200; 2020 $12,400; 2021 $12,550; 2022 $12,950; 2023 $13,850; 2024 $14,600; 2025 $15,750 (raised from $15,000 by July 2025 law); 2026 $16,100; 2027 $16,600.

## Context / meaning
- Standard deduction = income not subject to federal income tax for people who don't itemize (most filers). Indexing to inflation keeps "bracket creep" from raising taxes when pay only keeps pace with prices.
- Does not affect Social Security/Medicare payroll taxes or state taxes.
