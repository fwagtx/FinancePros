# Research brief: How recruiters get paid for a hire (posts Jan 27, 2027)

No timely news hook needed; evergreen business-model explainer. All numbers dated.

## Key facts
- Contingency recruiters "usually earn a fee in the area of 20 percent to 25 percent of the role's first-year cash compensation (salary, bonus and signing bonus), and only if they succeed in filling the job." (SHRM, "How to Work Effectively with an Executive Search Firm", July 18, 2016)
- Retained executive search: industry standard fee "about 33 percent of the role's annual salary, bonus and signing bonus." (same SHRM article, July 18, 2016). Many firms bill retained fees in thirds (industry guides, 2026; not used on screen).
- Who pays: Robert Half FY2025 10-K: "Fees to clients are generally calculated as a percentage of the new employee's annual compensation. No fees for permanent placement talent solutions services are charged to employment candidates."
- Guarantee: Robert Half FY2025 10-K: estimates "the financial impact of permanent placement candidates who do not remain with its clients through the 90-day guarantee period... recorded as liabilities." Revenue recognized when candidates accept offers.
- Robert Half FY2025 (year ended Dec 31, 2025): total service revenues $5.379B (down 7% from $5.796B in 2024). Permanent placement talent solutions revenues $440M (down 9.8% from $487M in 2024): placements -13.6%, average fee per placement +3.8%. Protiviti revenues $1.95B. Contract talent (remainder) driven by hours worked and bill rates.
- Surprise: permanent placement fees were only about 8% of Robert Half's FY2025 revenue (our math: 440 / 5,379 = 8.2%). Most revenue comes from contract talent billed by the hour and Protiviti consulting.

## Worked example (our math)
20% x $80,000 first-year salary = $16,000 fee. At 25%: $20,000. Retained at 33% of $80,000 = $26,400 (not used).
