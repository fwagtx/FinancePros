# Fact-check: 2027-01-22-hot (deferred interest vs 0% intro APR)
Checked 2026-11-23 for posting Fri 2027-01-22, 6 PM CT. The research brief was not used.

## Claims verified
- **Deferred interest definition (lines 0, 1, 8; captions).** VERIFIED. The CFPB says "No interest if paid in full in 12 months" is a deferred-interest offer, and the "if" is the signal. If any balance is left when the promo ends, you can owe the interest that built up from the purchase date. A 0% intro APR does not charge interest back to the start. Sources: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/how-understand-special-promotional-financing-offers-credit-cards ; https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-encourages-retail-credit-card-companies-consider-more-transparent-promotions ; https://www.nerdwallet.com/credit-cards/learn/deferred-interest-promos-huge-interest-charges
- **Worked example (lines 2-5).** MATH OK. The starting balances 1,200, 1,100 ... 100 are 12 terms that add up to $7,800. $7,800 x 0.2999/12 = $194.94, which rounds to about $195. $195 against $100 left is 1.95x, so "almost double" is right. Spoken and on-screen values match ($1,200, 29.99%, $100/mo x 11, $100, $0, $195).
- **Store cards averaged 31.3% APR in 2024, general-purpose cards 25.2% (line 6).** VERIFIED. Source: CFPB, *The Consumer Credit Card Market* report to Congress, December 2025: https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_consumer-credit-card-market-report_2025-12.pdf (also covered by Orrick, Jan 2026: https://www.orrick.com/zh-TW/Insights/2026/01/Trends-and-Takeaways-from-the-2025-Credit-CARD-Act-Report). This is a separate figure from the December 2024 CFPB retail-card spotlight, which found 32.66% on new cards from the top 100 retailers.
- **"A 2015 CFPB report found over 40% of subprime borrowers got hit with back interest" (old line 6).** NOT VERIFIABLE AS WRITTEN. Removed.
  - I could not reach the report's own text. The secondary sources disagree on both the number and the year:
    - NCLC says 40% of subprime consumers could not pay off in time (citing the CFPB).
    - Another NCLC filing gives 43%, citing the CFPB's **October 2013** CARD Act report: https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/first-set-card-act-comments-2015.pdf
    - A law-firm summary of the 2015 report says subprime and deep-subprime consumers pay in full only about 50% of the time: https://ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2015/12/card-act-report-signals-continued-scrutiny-of-credit-card-practices
    - Other summaries of the 2015 report: https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2015/12/card-act-report-summary
    - The 2015 report itself: https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201512_cfpb_report-the-consumer-credit-card-market.pdf
  - The line also said "subprime borrowers", which is broader than what the sources describe: subprime consumers who had a deferred-interest promo.
