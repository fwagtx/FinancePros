# Research brief: Passive income myths, explained with acorns (posts Dec 16, 2026)

Angle: evergreen (no breaking-news hook). Core idea: "passive" income needs a big pile of capital first, real yields can be low, and "passive" rentals and side hustles still involve work and taxes.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. S&P 500 dividend yield (12-month dividends / price), year-end values (multpl.com by-year table; same figures on us500.com; YCharts Dec 2025 = 1.15%):
   2016 2.03% | 2017 1.84% | 2018 2.09% | 2019 1.83% | 2020 1.58% | 2021 1.29% | 2022 1.71% | 2023 1.50% | 2024 1.24% | 2025 1.15%
   (2026 estimate ~1.04% per chartrow.com, based on dividends through Dec 2025 - not used on screen; it is an estimate and will move.)
2. FDIC national rate, savings accounts: 0.38% (July 2026, FRED series SNDR from FDIC); 0.39% in March 2026; 0.38% reported for August 2026 (Walnut Invest). National rate = weighted average across insured institutions, not best offers. Phrase with date ("July 2026").
3. IRS Topic 425: rental activities, including rental real estate, are passive activities even if you materially participate (exceptions: real estate professionals; limited active-participation allowance). "Passive" is a tax category, not "no work."
4. IRS Topic 554: self-employment tax is 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare), generally on 92.35% of net self-employment earnings; applies at $400+ net earnings. Side hustle profit is self-employment income, not passive.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, assumptions stated)
Goal: $1,000/month = $12,000/year of income, before taxes, principal untouched.
Capital needed = annual income / yield.
- At an assumed 4% yield: 12,000 / 0.04 = $300,000
- At the S&P 500's year-end 2025 dividend yield of 1.15%: 12,000 / 0.0115 = $1,043,478 (about $1.04M)
- At the FDIC national average savings rate, 0.38% (July 2026): 12,000 / 0.0038 = $3,157,895 (about $3.16M)

## Surprising fact
At the average US savings-account rate (0.38%, July 2026), $1,000 a month of interest takes about $3.16 million. And the IRS calls rentals "passive" even when the owner works on them.
