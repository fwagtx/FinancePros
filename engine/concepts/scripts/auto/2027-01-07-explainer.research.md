# Research brief: Rent-to-own stores: the real price of weekly payments
Prepared Nov 8, 2026 for Jan 7, 2027 Explainer. Evergreen; every number dated.

## How rent-to-own (lease-to-own / "rental-purchase") works
- You rent an item (TV, couch, appliance) for weekly, biweekly or monthly payments. You own it only after you make every
  scheduled payment, or pay an "early purchase option" price. Usually no hard credit check; you can return the item and stop paying
  (you keep nothing you paid). Rent-A-Center ad disclosure: "you will not own the merchandise until the total amount necessary to
  acquire ownership is paid in full or you exercise your early purchase option."
- California law (Karnette Rental-Purchase Act, Civ. Code 1812.620+) defines "cost of rental" = total of all payments minus cash price.
- Because they are leases, not loans, federal law does not require an APR disclosure (NerdWallet/Raycom, Oct 2017).
- APRO (industry trade group) says rent-to-own is regulated in 47 states (rtohq.org, 2026).

## Cost vs retail (real, dated)
- NerdWallet + Raycom Media investigation, Oct 2017: Rent-A-Center charged on average about TWICE what comparable retailers
  charged in 40 states and DC; implied APRs 43% to 468% (except New Jersey, where state law caps at 30%).
- FTC rent-to-own survey report (2000): total cost to own "usually significantly higher than retail store prices."
- UK FCA (CP18/35, 2018): typical rent-to-own total cost about 3 times median mainstream retail price.
- Best Buy + Progressive Leasing (reported 2020, Bob Sullivan / Money Talks News): $1,000 TV costs $2,169 if leased the full term.
- Rent-A-Center ad listing (undated email, via milled.com): Hisense 65" TV, $19.99/week x 65 weeks, total $1,299.35, cash price
  $584.71 (about 2.2x). Could not open the dated page -> NOT used on screen; used only to shape the "for example" inputs.
- CFPB v. Acima (filed July 26, 2024): CFPB alleged customers paid more than twice the retail price; CFPB voluntarily dismissed the
  case with prejudice in March 2025 (Upbound release, Mar 6, 2025). So: allegation only, no finding. (No CFPB 2022 lease-to-own
  report found.)

## State laws (rules vary by state)
- APRO 2024 chart: 6 states cap both cash price and total cost: California, Hawaii, Maine, New York, Vermont, West Virginia;
  multipliers roughly 2.0x to 2.4x cash price by product.
- California: total of payments may not exceed 2.25 times the max cash price (Civ. Code 1812.644(c)).
- Vermont: total cost may not exceed 2.00 times the max cash price (APRO Vermont statute page).
- New Jersey: rate cap of 30% (NerdWallet/Raycom 2017).
- California AG settlement with Rent-A-Center, 2022: $15.5 million over allegedly inflated cash prices.

## Worked example (our math, hypothetical "for example" inputs modeled on the RAC listing above)
- TV with $600 cash price. Rent-to-own: $20/week x 65 weeks = $1,300. 1,300 / 600 = 2.17 (about 2.2x).
  Cost of rental = 1,300 - 600 = $700.
- Same $600 on a credit card at 22.36% APR (Fed G.19, accounts assessed interest, Q3 2026 = Aug 2026, released Oct 7, 2026),
  paid in 15 equal monthly payments (about the same 65 weeks): payment $46.22, total $693.29 (interest $93.29). About $693.
- Difference: about $607 more for rent-to-own (our math).
Caveat: the card comparison assumes the person qualifies for a card; many rent-to-own customers do not.

## Surprising fact
You can pay more than twice the price and still own nothing if you return it in week 64.
