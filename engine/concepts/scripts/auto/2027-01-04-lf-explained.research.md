# Research brief: How the 401(k) changed retirement in America
Explained, long-form. Posts Mon Jan 4, 2027. Researched Nov 5, 2026. Evergreen: every number dated.

## Timely angle
- No single news hook needed (made ahead). Soft tie-ins for Jan 2027: (a) SECURE 2.0 Saver's Match starts with 2027 contributions; (b) new year = new contribution limits. The IRS had NOT announced 2027 401(k) limits as of Nov 5, 2026 (Milliman projects $25,500, a projection only). Use 2026 limits, labelled "for 2026".

## The surprising fact
- Section 401(k) was added to the tax code by the Revenue Act of 1978, aimed at clarifying tax treatment of cash-or-deferred pay arrangements (mostly for executives / profit-sharing bonuses). It was not designed as a mass retirement plan. A benefits consultant, Ted Benna (The Johnson Companies), read it in 1980 as a way for ordinary workers to save pre-tax from their paychecks, with an employer match. The law took effect Jan 1, 1980. IRS issued rules (proposed regulations, Nov 10, 1981 per ASPPA) that allowed pre-tax salary-reduction contributions. Sources: CNBC (Jan 4, 2017), ASPPA (Nov 2018), Workforce interview with Benna, Gusto/Guideline histories. (1980 vs 1981 for "first plan" varies across sources: say Benna designed it in 1980 and the IRS blessed paycheck contributions in 1981.)
- Today: 401(k) plans held about $10.8 trillion as of June 30, 2026 (ICI quarterly retirement market data, Q2 2026; via PlanAdviser/PlanSponsor/401k Specialist). Year-end 2025: $10.1 trillion; year-end 2024: $8.9 trillion (ICI Fact Book 2025). Total US retirement assets: $51.2 trillion (June 30, 2026, ICI).

## How each works (definitions)
- Defined benefit (DB) pension: employer promises a set monthly check for life, usually by formula (years of service x % x final/average pay). Employer invests, bears market risk and longevity risk. Private DB pensions are insured (up to limits) by the federal PBGC.
- Defined contribution (DC) e.g. 401(k): worker (and maybe employer) puts money into an individual account; the benefit is whatever the account grows to. Worker decides whether to join, how much, how to invest, and how fast to spend it. Worker bears market, longevity and decision risk ("risk shift").
- 401(k) traditional contributions are pre-tax (lower taxable income now, taxed on withdrawal); Roth 401(k) is after-tax with qualified withdrawals tax-free. Payroll (FICA) taxes still apply to deferrals.

## Worked example (our math, illustrative)
- Pension-style formula, for example: 1.5% x 30 years x $60,000 final pay = $27,000 a year for life (0.015*30 = 0.45; 0.45*60,000 = 27,000).
- To pull $27,000 a year from a 401(k) using the classic "4% rule" rule of thumb (William Bengen, 1994; a guideline, not a guarantee): $27,000 / 0.04 = $675,000 needed at retirement.
- Tax example: $3,000 pre-tax contribution for someone in the 12% federal bracket lowers federal income tax by $360 (3,000 x 0.12), so take-home falls about $2,640 not $3,000 (ignores state tax; FICA still applies).
- Match example: Vanguard calls a 50% match on the first 6% of pay a typical design. On $50,000 pay: 6% = $3,000 in, employer adds $1,500. Contributing only 3% ($1,500) gets $750, leaving $750 a year of match unclaimed.

## Limits (IRS Notice 2025-67, IR-2025-111, Nov 13, 2025) — FOR 2026
- Employee deferral limit: $24,500 (up from $23,500 in 2025).
- Catch-up age 50+: $8,000. Ages 60-63 (SECURE 2.0 "super catch-up"): $11,250 instead.
- Total annual additions (employee + employer): $72,000.
- Roth catch-up rule wage threshold: $150,000.
- 2027 limits: not announced as of Nov 5, 2026 (projection $25,500 by Milliman; do not use as fact).

## Nudges: auto-enrollment
- Pension Protection Act of 2006, signed Aug 17, 2006: safe harbor for automatic enrollment (initial default 3-10%, starting 2008) and for default investments (QDIA). Sources: law-firm summaries (Holland & Hart, Littler), DOL.
- Vanguard How America Saves 2026 (data for 2025): 61% of Vanguard plans use automatic enrollment (vs 10% in 2006). Participation: 94% in auto-enroll plans vs 64% in voluntary plans (participant-weighted). Plan-weighted participation 86%.
- SECURE 2.0 Act (signed Dec 29, 2022), Section 101: new 401(k)/403(b) plans set up after Dec 29, 2022 must automatically enroll workers starting with plan years beginning after Dec 31, 2024 (i.e. 2025), at a default of at least 3% (statute: 3-10%, rising 1 point a year to at least 10%, max 15%). Exempt: businesses under 3 years old, small employers (10 or fewer employees), church and government plans, SIMPLE plans. Workers can opt out. Proposed regs Jan 10, 2025. Sources: Groom Law, Milliman, Ascensus.

## Saver's Match (SECURE 2.0), starts with 2027
- Federal matching contribution of 50% on up to $2,000 of retirement contributions = up to $1,000 a year, for eligible lower- and moderate-income savers; phases out by income. Based on contributions for tax year 2027; Treasury deposits start in 2028 into the person's retirement account (not Roth). Treasury/IRS Notice 2026-48 (Aug 2026) began rulemaking. Sources: PSCA (Aug 2026), 401k Specialist, ADP (Aug 2026), Pew (2024).

## Scorecard numbers
- Balances (Vanguard How America Saves 2026, year-end 2025): average $167,970; median $44,115 (median up from $38,176 in 2024). Average is pulled up by big accounts.
- Savings rates (Vanguard 2025): average employee contribution 7.6%; with employer, average total 12.1%.
- Hardship withdrawals: 6% of participants in 2025, a record (Vanguard), up from 1.7% in 2020.
- Access gap (BLS, Employee Benefits in the US, March 2025, released Sept 25, 2025): 72% of private-industry workers had access to a retirement plan; 53% participated; take-up rate 73%. 70% had access to DC plans; 14% to DB plans.

## Added rules used in the script (well-established; cite)
- Vesting (IRS "Retirement Topics - Vesting"): employee's own 401(k) deferrals are always 100% vested; employer matching contributions may vest on a schedule of up to 3-year cliff or 6-year graded.
- PBGC (Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation, pbgc.gov): federal agency that insures private-sector defined benefit pensions up to legal limits. No equivalent insurance for 401(k) investment losses.
- Roth 401(k) (IRS): after-tax contributions; qualified withdrawals tax-free. Plans may offer it (not required).
- 401(k) balances can be rolled over to a new plan or IRA when changing jobs (IRS rollover rules).
- Script uses Vanguard's "10% of plans auto-enrolled in 2006" vs 61% at year-end 2025 (Vanguard HAS 2026 via InvestmentNews/PlanSponsor).
- Median Vanguard balance 2024 $38,176 (not used in final script).
