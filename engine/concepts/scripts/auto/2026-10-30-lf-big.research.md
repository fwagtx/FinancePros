## The Fed (Oct 28, 2026)
- FOMC statement (federalreserve.gov, monetary20261028a, fetched): "approved ... by a 10 – 2 vote"; "maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 3-3/4 to 4 percent".
- Exact statement sentences: "Economic activity is expanding at a strong pace." "Productivity growth is strong, and capital investment is robust." "Job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little." "Inflation remains elevated." "The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal." "The Committee will deliver price stability."
- Dissents: "Voting against the monetary policy action were Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack, who preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point at this meeting." Cook's first dissent since joining the Board in 2022 (NBC News; search summaries). (Some secondary sites said "three dissents" -- wrong for this meeting; official statement says 10-2.)
- Warsh press conference transcript (Fed PDF, preliminary): "We will deliver price stability. No ifs, ands, or buts." "...we wouldn't be suffering from nearly six years of inflation above target." "If we had a firm conclusion about the conduct of monetary policy, we'd announce it to much fanfare today."
- Via CNBC (Oct 29 analysis): Warsh: "While overall financial conditions are tighter, I'm unwilling and unable to call them tight." and "If you think that we're tolerating some inflation target somewhere in the twos, let us disabuse you of it."
- History: Sept 16, 2026 quarter-point hike to 3.75%-4.00% (12-0), first hike since July 2023 (CNBC; Oct 16 brief). Dec 10, 2025: cut to 3.50%-3.75%, third cut of 2025 (multiple outlets). Held from Dec 2025 until Sept 2026 (June/July holds per CNBC).
- Next FOMC: Dec 8-9, 2026; decision Dec 9 with new projections.
- Market pricing (attributed, not a forecast): CME FedWatch odds of a December quarter-point hike rose from ~78% (78.3%) Tuesday Oct 27 to ~85% (85.0%) after the Oct 28 decision (CNN live blog Oct 28; noradarealestate summary of CME data). A later reading Oct 30 cited 79.6% (KuCoin/BlockBeats -- weak; not used).
- Prime rate 7.00% (Bankrate, Oct 14, 2026); prime is typically the top of the Fed range + 3 points (CNBC Oct 28).

## Your money (CNBC Oct 28 consumer piece unless noted)
- Credit cards: variable; follow prime within 1-2 billing cycles. Bankrate average 19.67% (Oct 14, 2026). LendingTree's Matt Schulz: "Credit card rates have risen for three straight months and are now at their highest levels since December 2025."
- Savings: some top online savings accounts pay more than 4% (Bankrate, via CNBC).
- Mortgages track long-term Treasury yields. Freddie Mac PMMS Oct 29, 2026: 30-year 7.24% (Oct 22: 7.32%; year ago 6.17%); 15-year 6.60%. Second straight weekly decline; lowest of the month (Khater).
- Auto: Edmunds' Joseph Yoon: "The headline new-car APR has essentially flatlined at the 7% threshold all year"; used-car rates above 10%.
- Federal student loans fixed; new loans 6.52%-9.07%.
- 401(k): when rates rise, existing bond prices fall (mechanics). 2022 history: S&P 500 -19.4% (price) as the Fed hiked (AP/FactSet; Oct 16 brief).

## Worked examples (our math, hypothetical)
- Card: $5,000 balance; each 0.25-point change = $12.50/yr. At 19.67%: $983.50/yr simple interest (~$984).
- Savings: $10,000 at 4% = $400/yr; 3.6% inflation = $360 of buying power; ~$40 ahead before taxes.
- Mortgage: $300,000, 30-year, principal + interest: 6.17% -> $1,831.57; 7.24% -> $2,044.49; difference $212.92/mo (~$213).
- Real rate: 4.00% (top of range) - 3.6% (PCE) = ~0.4 point.
