# Research brief: How Affirm makes money from merchants (2027-01-24-business)

Researched Nov 25, 2026. Posts Jan 24, 2027 (evergreen: every number dated by fiscal year).
Angle: the MERCHANT side of Affirm. Not repeating 2026-11-13-business (48% interest, revenue mix, $1,000 at 15% APR) or the 2027-01-11 long-form on pay-in-four.
Note: sec.gov, last10k.com and other filing hosts could not be fetched from this session (DNS blocked); figures below come from web-search results quoting the 10-Ks and earnings tables, cross-checked by arithmetic and against the 2026-11-13 brief.

## Timely angle
- No breaking news needed. Freshest full-year data: Affirm Form 10-K for fiscal 2026 (year ended June 30, 2026), filed late Aug 2026; Q4 FY2026 results Aug 27, 2026. Next 10-K will not arrive before the posting date.

## Key facts (Affirm, fiscal 2026 = year ended June 30, 2026, Form 10-K)
- Merchant network revenue: $1,149,932 thousand (~$1.15B), up from $882,658 thousand in FY2025 (+$267.3M, +30%). Confirmed: FY2026 10-K table (search), 2026-11-13 brief (10-K MD&A). Arithmetic: 882.7 + 267.3 = 1,150.0.
- This is the fee merchants pay Affirm; the 10-K says it is generally set as a share of GMV, and merchant incentives (fee reductions/rebates) reduce it (FY2023, FY2024 10-Ks).
- GMV FY2026: $50.2B, up 37% (Zacks; Affirm Q4 FY26 materials; 2026-11-13 brief). GMV FY2025: $36.7B (Affirm FY2025 annual report).
- Average merchant take, OUR MATH: 1,149.9 / 50,200 = 2.29% -> about 2.3%. The FY2026 10-K itself says merchant network revenue as a percentage of GMV decreased to 2.3% from 2.4% (FY2025 check: 882.7 / 36,700 = 2.41%).
- Bigger fees on 0% APR: 10-K language (FY2021, FY2023 and later): "While merchant fees depend on the individual arrangement between us and each merchant and vary based on the terms of the product offering, we generally earn larger merchant fees on 0% APR financing products." FY2021 10-K adds "and financial products with longer term lengths."
- Affirm FQ3 FY2026 shareholder letter: about 95% of GMV from 0% APR monthly installments at integrated merchants is funded by the merchant (quarter figure; context only).
- Product mix: not used on screen. June 2026 investor deck shows Pay in X 16%, 0% APR monthly 13%, interest-bearing 71% (labeled illustrative); the 14/70 split in the earlier brief could not be re-confirmed. Avoid.

## Why merchants agree to pay (attributed claims only; self-reported marketing, not audited)
- Affirm business page: its programs remove price as a barrier, "boosting cart conversions, increasing average order value, and expanding your customer base."
- Stripe case study page: businesses adding Affirm through Stripe see a 14% increase in revenue on Affirm-eligible sessions and 21% higher conversion on purchases of $250+ (Stripe/Affirm claim).
- Merchant case studies on investors.affirm.com (Alternative Airlines, Friendly Diamonds, SeatGeek) report higher average order values; these compare Affirm orders with other orders, not controlled tests.
- Script uses only the generic claim: "Affirm says" -> bigger orders, more checkouts, new shoppers.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, uses the 2.3% FY2026 average; real fees vary by merchant and product)
- $2,000 laptop bought with Affirm.
- Fee to Affirm = $2,000 x 2.3% = $46.00.
- Store receives = $2,000 - $46 = $1,954.
- On a 0% APR offer the fee is generally larger than average (10-K), so the store would keep less than $1,954 (no specific rate claimed).

## The surprise
- "Interest-free" is not free: on 0% APR plans the store generally pays Affirm a bigger fee. Store fees totaled about $1.15B in fiscal 2026, about 2.3 cents of every dollar spent through Affirm.
