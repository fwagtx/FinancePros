# Research brief: Why game consoles are often sold at a loss (How They Make Money, posts Dec 29, 2026)

Evergreen. No timely news hook needed; the hook is the 2021 Microsoft sworn testimony.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)

1. **Microsoft, May 2021 (Epic v. Apple trial, sworn testimony).** Lori Wright, Xbox VP of business development, asked about console margins: "We don't. We sell the consoles at a loss." Asked "does Microsoft ever earn a profit on the sale of an Xbox console?" she answered "No." She said the losses are made up through games and subscriptions. Apple later asked the court to find her not credible because she produced no documents backing the claim; Microsoft defended her testimony. (GeekWire, VGC, TechSpot, Axios, May 2021.)
   - Exact quote usable on screen: "We sell the consoles at a loss." (Lori Wright, Microsoft, May 2021)

2. **Sony PS5 launch, 2020-21.** Sony sold PS5 consoles at a loss at launch (Tom's Hardware: loss on each of ~4.5M PS5s sold in 2020, per Sony's Feb 2021 results). In August 2021 (Q1 FY2021 earnings call), Sony CFO Hiroki Totoki told Bloomberg the $499 disc-drive PS5 was no longer sold at a loss; the digital model still was at that time. (Bloomberg via GamingBolt, TechRadar, Aug 2021.) Do not state current prices.

   - Sony FY2025 (ended Mar 2026) results announced May 2026: PS5 shipments 16.0M in the year (vs 18.5M prior); 93.7M lifetime as of Mar 31, 2026 (secondary sources); G&NS posted record operating income (Sony, May 2026, via Inven Global / Simulation Daily).

4. **Platform cut.** A UK class action (Alex Neill v Sony, Competition Appeal Tribunal; certified Nov 2023; trial began March 2026; no judgment found as of Oct 2026) alleges Sony charges publishers a 30% commission on PlayStation Store sales. The CAT's Nov 2023 judgment describes the commission as largely set at 30% of the price paid by the consumer. Sony has said its model is justified. Xbox and Nintendo stores also have historically charged 30% (secondary list). Keep as "a UK lawsuit says".

5. **Nintendo counterexample.** Nintendo has historically aimed not to sell hardware at a loss (Goldman Sachs analyst; investor question in Feb 2026 Q&A). In its Feb 2026 earnings Q&A, president Shuntaro Furukawa said Nintendo focuses on per-unit hardware profitability and tries to avoid selling hardware at a loss, while not being "overly influenced" by short-term memory-cost spikes (Nintendo IR Q&A 260204e.pdf; Nintendo Wire, Feb 4, 2026). Nintendo said Switch 2 earns less per unit than Switch 1. Analyst estimates of Switch 2 per-unit losses from memory costs are unconfirmed; do not state.

## Worked examples (our own math)
- Network services growth, FY ended Mar 2022 to Mar 2026: 763.1 / 409.4 = 1.864 -> up about 86%.
- FY ended Mar 2026 vs prior year: hardware & others 1,391.6 / 1,583.2 = 0.879 -> down about 12%; digital software & add-on 2,415.3 / 2,290.5 = 1.054 -> up about 5%.
- Share of FY ended Mar 2026 external G&NS sales not from hardware & others: (2,415.3 + 763.1) / 4,570.1 = 69.5% -> about 70%.
- Example: a $70 digital game (example price, not a quoted price) x 30% platform commission = $21 to the platform; $49 to the publisher.

## The surprising fact
Microsoft testified under oath (May 2021) that it has never made a profit on the sale of an Xbox console; the business is games, subscriptions and the store cut.
