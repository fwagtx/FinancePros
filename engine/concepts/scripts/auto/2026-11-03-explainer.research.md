# Research brief: Why so many budgets fall apart in the second month (posts Nov 3, 2026)

## Data
- NerdWallet / Harris Poll (Mar 31-Apr 4, 2023, 2,000+ US adults): 84% of Americans with a monthly budget say they've exceeded it; 44% of those usually use a credit card for the extra.
- Debt.com 2026 Budgeting Survey: 85% of Americans say they budget; ~83% of budgeters cite rising costs as the biggest challenge.
- BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey, average annual expenditures per consumer unit: 2019 $63,036; 2020 $61,332; 2021 $66,928; 2022 $72,967; 2023 $77,280; 2024 $78,535 (chartable).
- Amazon Prime annual membership: $139 (or $14.99/month), 2026.

## Why month two breaks (explanation)
- Month one is usually built from the bills just paid (rent, utilities, phone).
- Irregular, non-monthly costs (annual renewals, gifts, car repairs, insurance premiums billed every 6-12 months) don't show up until later, so they look like "surprises".
- Fix commonly taught by planners: list yearly irregular costs and divide by 12 (a "sinking fund").

## Worked example (illustrative amounts except Prime)
- Gifts $1,000 + insurance renewal $800 + car repairs $461 + Prime $139 = $2,400 a year.
- $2,400 / 12 = $200 a month.
