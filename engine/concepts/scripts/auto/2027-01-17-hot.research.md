# Research brief: 2027-01-17-hot — Money Myth: bankruptcy erases every kind of debt
Researched Nov 18, 2026. Posts Jan 17, 2027 (evergreen; every number dated).

## The myth -> the fact
- MYTH: Filing bankruptcy wipes out every debt.
- FACT: A bankruptcy discharge releases the debtor from personal liability for *certain specified types* of debts. Section 523(a) of the Bankruptcy Code (11 U.S.C. § 523) specifically excepts various categories of debts from the discharge granted to individuals, so the debtor must still repay those debts after bankruptcy. (US Courts, Bankruptcy Basics: "Discharge in Bankruptcy")
- A valid lien not avoided in the case survives; a secured creditor can still enforce it against the collateral (e.g., a car or house). (US Courts, Discharge in Bankruptcy)
- Chapter 13 discharge is slightly broader than Chapter 7. (US Courts)

## Debts that generally survive (11 U.S.C. § 523(a))
- Domestic support obligations: child support and alimony — § 523(a)(5).
- Certain taxes — § 523(a)(1): e.g., income taxes for returns due within about 3 years before filing (via § 507(a)(8)), unfiled/fraudulent returns, trust-fund taxes (withholding, sales taxes). Older income taxes can sometimes be discharged, so "most recent taxes" not "all taxes".
- Fines, penalties and forfeitures payable to a government that are punitive (e.g., criminal fines) — § 523(a)(7).
- Student loans (government and qualified education loans), unless repayment would impose an "undue hardship" — § 523(a)(8). Proven in a separate lawsuit (adversary proceeding).
- Death or personal injury caused by operating a vehicle/vessel/aircraft while intoxicated (DUI injury debts) — § 523(a)(9).
- Debts from fraud / false pretenses (a)(2), embezzlement/larceny (a)(4), willful and malicious injury (a)(6). Note: fraud-type debts are excepted only if the creditor timely files a complaint in the bankruptcy court.
- Debts not listed on the schedules can also survive (court FAQ pages).
- Debts usually discharged (general info from district court FAQs): credit cards, medical bills, most unsecured personal loans.

## Student loans: Nov 2022 attestation process
- On Nov 17, 2022, the Department of Justice, in coordination with the Department of Education, issued guidance to DOJ attorneys on student loan undue-hardship cases. Borrowers file an adversary proceeding, then complete an Attestation Form; DOJ reviews it and can agree (stipulate) to discharge if the criteria are met. The court still decides. Applies to federal loans held by ED; private loans not covered. Legal standard (undue hardship / Brunner test) unchanged; guidance adds presumptions.
- Status 2026: secondary sources say the process is still in use (form updated May 2025; DOJ page updated March 2026). UNCERTAIN — not confirmed directly from a DOJ page in this research; script only says it started in Nov 2022.

## Worked example (our math)
- Increase FY2025 -> FY2026: 621,111 − 557,376 = 63,735 more cases; 63,735 / 557,376 = 11.4%.
- FY2022 -> FY2026: 621,111 − 383,810 = 237,301 more; 621,111 / 383,810 = 1.618 -> up about 62%.

## Surprising fact
Even after a successful bankruptcy, child support, recent taxes, criminal fines, DUI-injury debts and (without proving undue hardship) student loans are still owed. And a car or house loan's lien can survive, so the lender can still take the collateral.
