## Issues found (none block posting)

1. **Line 6 (Minnesota data has no date).** The MN Lottery figures come from a blog post dated April 2023, but the video says "pay" in the present tense. The numbers are still MN's published guidance, and the chart is correctly labeled as Minnesota's. For fairness, add a date. Fix the caption or sub to read "MN Lottery, 2023" and change the narration to "Minnesota's lottery says one-dollar games pay about sixty-three percent...". Also, "Pricier tickets pay more back, by design" comes from one state's data. Suggest "In Minnesota, pricier tickets pay more back, by design." This keeps one lottery's numbers from standing in for all lotteries.
2. **Line 1 / cover (small nuance in the hook).** "$15 on average" is the planned payout across every ticket printed, assuming every prize is claimed. The hook is true and specific, and it is not misleading because it says "on average". The cover "This $20 Ticket Pays $15" leaves out "on average", so a single ticket could be read as paying $15. Optional fix: make the cover "$20 Ticket, $15 Back (Avg.)". The hook takes about 3.3 seconds to say, slightly over the 3-second target. It is still acceptable, and the "on average" qualifier needs to stay.
3. **Line 7 (freshness).** FY2025 (ended Aug 31, 2025) is the latest complete year with an official release. TDLR has only given preliminary FY2026 figures: sales about flat, and transfers of about $1.64B through July. Last year's final release came out Oct 27, so FY2025 should still be current on Oct 10. Keep the "fiscal 2025" label, which the video already has. Optional: the Texas Lottery is now run by TDLR, since the Lottery Commission was abolished on Sept 1, 2025. "Texas Lottery" is still the correct brand name.
4. **Line 5 (awkward narration).** "return your twenty, so winning more than twenty is one in five point four three" sounds repetitive. Suggest: "But thirty-nine percent of those wins just give your twenty back. Odds of winning more than you paid: one in five point four three."
5. **Line 2 (style).** "Texas prints the plan" is a vague metaphor. Consider "Texas publishes the prize table: this game has about 25.7 million tickets."
