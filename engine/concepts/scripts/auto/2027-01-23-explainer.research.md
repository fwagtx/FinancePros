# Research brief: Secured credit cards: how a deposit turns into a credit history
Prepared Nov 24, 2026 for an evergreen explainer posting Sat Jan 23, 2027. No timely news hook needed; evergreen.

Research note: Firecrawl was out of credits and direct page fetches to consumerfinance.gov and myfico.com were blocked by the session network, so facts below were confirmed via web search result summaries of the official pages (and cross-checked against a second reputable source where possible). The fact-checker should open the primary pages.

## Core facts (with dates and sources)

1. **What a secured card is.** A credit card with a low limit that requires a cash deposit, usually equal to the credit limit; payment history is reported to credit bureaus.
   - Federal Reserve FEDS Notes, "An Overview of Credit-Building Products" (Dec 6, 2024): "credit cards with low credit limits that require a cash deposit usually equal to the credit limit."
   - Also: TransUnion, Experian explainers. Some issuers allow a smaller deposit for a given limit (SoFi), so say "usually."
   - Note: I could not open a CFPB page stating "deposit usually equals limit" directly; attribute to the Federal Reserve. CFPB "Building credit from scratch" checklist (Dec 2016) covers secured cards as a credit-building option.

2. **Reported like a regular card, but check the bureaus.** Not every issuer reports to all three major bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). Experian advises picking a card whose issuer reports to all three. Check before you apply.

3. **Deposit comes back.** If the account is in good standing, the deposit is returned when you close and pay the balance in full, or when the issuer upgrades ("graduates") you to an unsecured card (often as a statement credit). Refund timing on closing commonly 30-90 days, varies by issuer (Bankrate). Sources: issuer help pages, Bankrate, Experian.

4. **Graduation timing is issuer-specific.** Common reviews start around 6 months; guides cite roughly 6 to 18 months (MoneyGeek, NerdWallet, Experian). Some issuers do not offer graduation at all. Do not name issuers; say "timing varies by issuer."

5. **FICO weights** (myFICO "What's in your credit score"; repeated by Chase's FICO explainer): payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of credit history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%. myFICO notes the importance varies by person/model. (Search could only confirm the % via Chase's page quoting FICO; myFICO page is the canonical source.)

6. **Utilization.** The percentage of available revolving credit you are using (balance / limit), a key part of "amounts owed." Lower is generally better; FICO-sourced coverage says people with top scores use about 7% on average, and under 10% is often cited (myFICO amounts-owed article; Time, Bankrate).

7. **Minimum to get a FICO Score** (myFICO FAQ "FICO Score requirements"; myFICO support article on Resilience Index uses same criteria): at least one account opened for six months or more, and at least one account reported to a credit bureau within the past six months (also no "deceased" indication).

8. **Credit invisible (the surprising fact).**
   - CFPB, 2015 Data Point: about 26 million US adults (about 1 in 10) were "credit invisible" (no credit record) based on 2010 data.
   - CFPB, "Technical correction and update to the CFPB's credit invisibles estimate" (June 23, 2025): one bureau left out some file categories, so the 2010 count was overstated. Corrected: 5.8% of adults (13.5 million) credit invisible in Dec 2010 (was 11% / 25.9 million). Newest estimate: **2.7% of adults, about 7.0 million, in December 2020.** Unscored records in Dec 2010 corrected to 12.7% (29.7 million). Scored share rose from 81.6% (2010) to 87.5% (2020).
   - Corroborated by Auto Finance News and Orrick InfoBytes (June 2025). ACA International dated the 2.7% to 2010, which conflicts with the CFPB; CFPB says Dec 2020.

9. **Market size (context).** Philadelphia Fed "Secured Card Market Update": about 3.7 million secured cards held at the largest banks as of Sept 2023 ($817M balances), up from about 2.1 million ten years earlier. Secured cards were 5% of general-purpose cards originated in 2016 (CFPB 2017, cited by Philly Fed).

## Worked example ("our math")
- Deposit $300 -> credit limit $300 (assuming deposit equals limit).
- Balance $90 reported: 90 / 300 = 0.30 = 30% utilization.
- Balance $30 reported: 30 / 300 = 0.10 = 10% utilization.
- Lower utilization is generally better for the amounts-owed part of a FICO score.

## The surprise
Your own money, held as a deposit, becomes the limit, and paying that card on time builds the same payment history (35% of a FICO Score) a regular card does. And you can't get a FICO Score at all until an account has been open at least six months.
