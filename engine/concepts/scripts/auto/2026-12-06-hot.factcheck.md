## Verified claims
- **L0 hook, "An eviction case can follow you for seven years."** Accurate. CFPB (Ask CFPB 2104) says eviction court cases "could be on your tenant screening record for up to seven years". The FCRA allows lawsuits and judgments for 7 years or until the statute of limitations runs, whichever is longer, so "can" is not an overstatement. The court record itself may stay public longer, so the line slightly understates at worst. The hook is true, specific, about 3 seconds long, and makes the viewer want the answer. The cover ("Stop Paying Rent?", 7 YRS) is paid off by the video.
- **L1, 1.23M filings in 2025 across Eviction Lab tracked areas.** Confirmed by Eviction Lab "ets-report-2025" (Apr 16 2026): "Across all sites monitored, landlords filed 1.23 million eviction cases in 2025" (10 states and 38 cities). The scope wording "in areas Princeton's Eviction Lab tracks" is correct and does not claim a national figure. Note: the report gives 2024 as 1.25M (the brief says 1.26M). The script does not use the 2024 number.
- **L3, CA 3 days skipping weekends; NY 14 days.** Confirmed. AB 2343 (effective Sept 1 2019) excludes Saturdays, Sundays and judicial holidays from the 3-day pay-or-quit count (CCP 1161(2)). HSTPA 2019 set NY's rent demand at 14 days (NY CourtHelp). "Skipping weekends" leaves out court holidays, which is an acceptable simplification.
- **L2/L4, process steps.** A fair general sequence (late fee, notice, suit, judgment, removal by officer under a writ). The script makes no "illegal lockout" claim, which is good given Texas nuances.
- **L5, credit reports.** Correct. NCAP (July 2017) removed civil judgments, so eviction judgments generally do not appear on Experian, Equifax or TransUnion credit reports. Rent debt sent to collections can be reported for up to 7 years from the original delinquency (FCRA).
- **L6, screening reports up to 7 years per CFPB.** Matches CFPB wording.
- **L7, TransUnion $15M, 2023.** Confirmed: on Oct 12 2023 the FTC and CFPB announced that Trans Union LLC and TURSS would pay $15M ($11M consumer redress plus a $4M civil penalty). The allegations covered inaccurate or incomplete eviction records, including failing to accurately report case dispositions, duplicate entries and sealed cases. The script frames these as "claims", which is correct framing. (A separate CFPB-only $8M on credit freezes made $23M in total; the $15M figure is the correct one for tenant screening.)
