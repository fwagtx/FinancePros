# Research brief: Scam alert: fake IRS calls (Hot Take, posts Oct 23, 2026)

- TIGTA: from March 2013 to March 2025, 16,281 victims reported losing more than $114M to IRS impersonation scams. Average: $114M / 16,281 = about $7,002 per victim. Earlier: by Nov 2017, over 12,400 victims had lost more than $61.6M, and over 2.1M people had reported calls.
- FTC (2025 data, reported 2026): about $920M lost to government impersonators in 2025, with 375,000+ reports, up about 40%. Imposter scams overall: $3.5B lost in 2025, nearly triple 2020.
- IRS.gov ("Tips to help taxpayers recognize tax scams"; "How taxpayers can protect themselves from gift card scams"):
  - The IRS will never call to demand immediate payment.
  - It will never threaten to have local police arrest you.
  - It never requires a specific payment method like gift cards, prepaid debit cards or wire transfers.
  - It never accepts gift cards for a tax bill.
  - If you owe, you get written notice by mail.
  - Report suspicious calls to phishing@irs.gov.
- Scammers spoof caller ID and use fake names and badge numbers (IRS).
- TIGTA takes impersonation reports at tigta.gov.
- Chartable: TIGTA cumulative reported losses, $61.6M (Nov 2017) to $114M (Mar 2025). FTC imposter losses about $1.2B (2020) to $3.5B (2025).
- Worked example: $114M / 16,281 = about $7,000 per victim.
