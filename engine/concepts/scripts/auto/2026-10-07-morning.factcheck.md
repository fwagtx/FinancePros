## Verified claims
- Deficit $105.6B in August, up $12.7B from $92.8B in July (revised). The +13.7% matches BEA's wording exactly. The difference between the rounded figures is $12.8B, which is only a rounding effect.
- Imports $420.8B, up $17.2B (+4.3%). Exports $315.2B, up $4.5B (+1.4%). 420.8 - 315.2 = 105.6. "Rose, but more slowly" is correct.
- Record imports: The Globe and Mail/Reuters ("record imports") and CNBC/Analytics Insight ("climbed to a record USD 420.8 billion") both report it. The previous peak was March 2025 at $419.0B (as first published by BEA). The claim holds. Note that BEA's own release does not use the word "record" (see issue 1).
- Category jumps: BEA says imports of crude oil rose $3.3B, nonmonetary gold $3.1B and semiconductors $2.4B. (The $2.3B / $2.0B / $1.0B figures on the BEA page are the export changes, not imports.) Correct.
- "Widest since early 2025": CNBC says it was the steepest since the all-time gap in March 2025, and The Hill says it was the first time over $100B since March 2025. Both "early 2025" and "March 2025" are safe.
- Year to date: BEA says the deficit decreased $138.2B, or 19.9 percent, from the same period of 2025. Exports were up $267.7B (+11.8%) and imports up $129.5B (+4.4%). The 19.9% is correct.
- Compare values: "$92.8B" (6 characters) and "$105.6B" (7 characters) are both within the 7-character limit. No line has keep:true.
- The news is 1 day old, so it is not stale. "Out October 6" and "in August" will still be correct on Oct 7.
- There is no advice language and no price prediction. There is no political opinion: tariffs are mentioned only as a neutral cause.
- The cover and title (Record $420.8B of Imports) are paid off by lines 0-1.

## Issues

1. **Line 5, misleading cause (MUST FIX).** The text and narration imply the year-to-date gap is smaller because imports surged in 2025 and then fell back. In fact, Jan-Aug 2026 imports are up 4.4% from 2025, even counting the 2025 rush. The gap is smaller because exports rose 11.8% (+$267.7B). The pre-tariff surge was also mostly in Jan-Mar 2025, not in all eight months. The surge itself is well documented, but it does not explain the 19.9%.
   - text -> `The twist: January to August, the deficit is 19.9% smaller than a year earlier, because exports grew faster than imports.`
   - say -> `The twist? January through August, the gap is about twenty percent smaller than in twenty twenty-five, because exports grew faster than imports.`
   - Optional, if the tariff context should stay: `...smaller than in twenty twenty-five, a year that started with a rush of imports before tariffs. This year, exports grew faster.`

2. **Line 6, GDP wording misleads (MUST FIX).** "Though imports get subtracted when GDP is counted" suggests that imports directly lower GDP. Imports are subtracted only because they are already counted inside consumer, business and government spending, so buying imports does not by itself shrink GDP. "Isn't a debt" is also a simplification, because the deficit is financed by foreign money flowing into US assets. That said, "not a bill anyone pays back" is acceptable.
   - text -> `A trade deficit isn't a bill to pay back. It's buying versus selling, and GDP math subtracts imports only because they're already counted in spending.`
   - say -> `A trade deficit isn't a bill anyone pays back. It's buying versus selling. And GDP math subtracts imports only because they're already counted in what we spend.`
   - board lines -> `Not a bill to repay` / `It's buying vs selling` / `GDP nets out imports`

3. **Line 1, attribution (should fix).** "All-time high, per the government's trade report" puts the word "record" in the government's mouth, and BEA's release does not say it. The figure comes from BEA, but the record call comes from the press (Reuters), using BEA's own history.
   - text -> `That's an all-time high, based on the government's trade report out October 6.`
   - The news card can stay: its source line "BEA and Census, Oct 6, 2026" refers to the figure.

4. **Line 0, the hook is slightly long (minor).** "America imported a record four hundred twenty-one billion dollars in August" takes about 4 seconds. It is true and specific. To land it in under 3 seconds and match the screen better, use `America just imported a record four hundred twenty-one billion dollars.` (keep the caption as is). Saying "four hundred twenty-one billion" for an on-screen $420.8B is acceptable rounding; "nearly four hundred twenty-one billion" would be the strictest match.

5. **Spoken versus on-screen numbers (OK).** "About ninety-three" for $92.8B, "about one hundred six" for $105.6B, "about three hundred fifteen" for $315.2B and "about twenty percent" for 19.9% are all hedged with "about". These are fine.

6. **Line 3, "gold" (minor, optional).** BEA's category is nonmonetary gold, which excludes central bank gold. "Gold" is acceptable for a short video. "Computer chips" for semiconductors is fine.

7. **Line 5 scene tone (minor).** tone "up" and mood "up" frame a smaller deficit as good news. This is value-laden but small. Consider tone "neutral".
