# Research brief: Why is a cell phone plan so expensive? (Hot Take, posts Thu Dec 17, 2026)

Format: "Why is X so expensive". Researched October 18, 2026. Evergreen: every figure carries its year/quarter.

## The surprising fact (hook)
The price carriers charge per line has FALLEN for about two decades, but the share of the bill that goes to taxes, fees and government surcharges hit a record. "Phone service got cheaper. So why is your bill so big?"

## Key facts (with dates)
1. Tax Foundation, "Wireless Taxes and Fees" 2026 edition (latest; by Scott Mackey and co-authors):
   - Taxes, fees and government surcharges = 28.8% of a typical monthly wireless bill in 2026, the highest level ever recorded in the series.
   - Federal Universal Service Fund (USF) surcharge rate: 14.39% (up from 13.26% in the comparison year). State and local: 14.42% (from 14.25%). 14.39 + 14.42 = 28.81, i.e. 28.8%.
   - Over the past two decades the tax share rose from 16.2% to 28.8%, while monthly revenue per line fell from $50.64 to $33.14.
   - A typical household with four phones paying $100 a month pays about $345 a year in taxes, fees and surcharges in 2026 (up from about $330 in 2025).
   - State and local governments collect about $11.4 billion from wireless subscribers in 2026.
   - Driver of the record: mainly the federal USF surcharge increase.
2. Tax Foundation 2025 edition (for history): 27.60% in 2025 (from 26.77% in 2024); since 2012 the average charge from providers fell 29%, from $47.00 to $33.36 per line per month, while the tax share rose from 17.18% to 27.60%. Illinois highest in 2025 at 38.32%, Idaho lowest at 16.82%. (2026 state rankings not confirmed, so not used.)
3. FCC Auction 107 (C-band), concluded February 2021: net winning bids $81,114,481,921 (~$81.1 billion); gross $81.17 billion. Verizon (Cellco Partnership) gross winning bids $45,454,843,197 (~$45.5 billion, 3,511 licenses); AT&T $23,406,860,839 (~$23.4 billion). Source: FCC public notice of winning bidders, via Benton/Fierce/Light Reading coverage.
4. Verizon capital expenditures: $17.0 billion in full-year 2025 (Verizon 4Q25 earnings release, Jan 30, 2026). 2026 guidance $16.0-16.5B (secondary summary only; not used).
5. FCC Communications Marketplace Reports name AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon as the three facilities-based nationwide mobile providers. (No current FCC percent-of-connections figure confirmed; older pre-merger FCC figure: top four carriers 97.7%. Not used on screen.)
6. ARPA (company filings, Q2 2026, per ACCOUNT not per line, so not directly comparable):
   - T-Mobile postpaid ARPA $152.91 in Q2 2026, up 2% YoY (T-Mobile Q2 2026 earnings release, July 2026).
   - Verizon wireless retail postpaid ARPA $168.35 in Q2 2026 (vs $166.66), per Zacks-syndicated coverage of Verizon's 2Q26 results (weaker: secondary). AT&T postpaid phone ARPU for Q2 2026 not confirmed (only an analyst estimate of $56.51 found). Not used in script.
   - Note: Q3 2026 results (late October 2026) will be out before posting; these are labeled Q2 2026.
7. BLS CPI, wireless telephone services: long-term decline. Light Reading, citing BLS data: index fell about 28% from 2007 to 2025 and slipped 1.7% from 2020 to 2025 while all-items CPI rose about 24%. BLS changed the method/data for this index starting with July 2025 data (factsheet), so later figures are less comparable. Secondary-cited; exact BLS annual values not retrieved (bls.gov blocked). Used only as context for the "service got cheaper" tension, which the script states with the Tax Foundation's per-line figure instead.
8. Device "free phone" deals: Verizon and AT&T promos (e.g., up to $830 off iPhone 17) are typically paid as 36 monthly bill credits; T-Mobile uses 24-month installments (WhistleOut, mid-2026). Promotions change; weakly sourced; not used in script.

## Tension handled honestly
Government price data (BLS CPI) and the Tax Foundation both show the carrier's price per line has dropped long-term. The bill still feels expensive because (a) taxes/fees are a record 28.8% share, (b) people buy more lines and pricier plans, and (c) networks and spectrum cost tens of billions. Script says plainly: the plan got cheaper; what's stacked on top hit a record.

## Worked example (exact math)
Four-line plan at $100/month = $1,200/year. 28.8% x $1,200 = $345.60, about $345 a year in taxes and fees (matches Tax Foundation's ~$345 figure for 2026).
Per-line price change: $50.64 -> $33.14 = -$17.50, a 34.6% drop ($17.50 / $50.64).
