# Fact-check: 2026-10-05-hot ("Repay Money You Never Got?"), posts Oct 5, 2026

## Verified
- L3 math: $5,000 / 12 x 6 = $2,500. Correct, and labeled as an example.
- L4 math: $2,500 x (1 - 0.22 - 0.0765) = $1,758.75. Correct. 22% is still the 2026 flat supplemental withholding rate (IRS Pub 15 (2026); OBBBA / P.L. 119-21 made the TCJA rates permanent). FICA is 7.65%.
- L6: Under IRS Pub 525, a later-year repayment of $3,000 or less would be a miscellaneous itemized deduction, and those are suspended, so it can't be deducted. OBBBA (2025) amended IRC 67(g) and made the suspension permanent (the only new carve-out is educator expenses starting in 2026). The claim is correct.
- L7 math: 22% x $2,500 = $550. Correct. It is labeled "Example, 22% bracket".
- L5, same year vs. later year: In the same year, the employer can reduce wages and correct both income-tax and FICA withholding. In a later year, the employer can recover Social Security and Medicare (FICA) on Form 941-X (refunding the employee's share with written consent), or the employee can claim it on Form 843. Income tax withheld in a prior year can't be corrected through payroll. "FICA can be refunded" is correct.
- Every on-screen value matches the spoken one (L1-L9, cover). The cover ("$5,000", "-$550", "Repay Money You Never Got?") is paid off by the video.
- No political opinion, no price predictions, no quotes. The time words ("2025 law") are still correct on Oct 5, 2026.

## Issues
1. **L5 split card, right side: "income tax stays paid" is only true for $3,000 or less (factual overgeneralization).** If a later-year repayment is over $3,000, Pub 525 / IRC Section 1341 (claim of right) still allows an itemized deduction on Schedule A, or a tax credit for the prior-year tax, whichever is better. The script never mentions this, so viewers with bigger clawbacks are misled. Fix: change the item to "income tax: see $3,000 rule" (or "≤$3,000: no deduction"). Better still, add to L6 text and say: "Over $3,000, a special rule, Section 1341, can give some of it back." The post/yt_desc could add: "Repayments over $3,000 may qualify for a deduction or credit under IRC Section 1341."
2. **L5 left card, "withholding fixed": vague.** Fix: "income tax & FICA corrected".
3. **L4, $1,758.75 has no stated assumptions on screen.** It assumes 22% federal supplemental withholding plus 7.65% FICA, with no state tax. The IG post states this, but the video doesn't. Fix: change the stat sub to "after 22% federal + 7.65% FICA withholding (example)".
4. **L4 / L2, "Many agreements want that full $2,500" / "Many signing bonuses have a clawback": unsourced prevalence claims.** These are acceptable as hedged ("many"), but "Some agreements" is safer for L4. Note that the next-year gross-repayment demand is standard practice, because the employer can't recover prior-year income tax withholding.
5. **L7, wording: "$550 of tax on money you gave back."** This is accurate (the income tax paid on income that was repaid can't be deducted). It is a federal-only example and holds only if the FICA is actually refunded. Optional: put "federal" in the sub.
6. **Not legal/tax advice:** L8 is descriptive ("people check the fine print"), not an instruction. OK. The disclaimers say "not financial advice"; suggest "not financial, tax, or legal advice", since this is a tax and contract topic.
7. **Hook (L1):** "Quit early, and you may repay bonus money you never got." It's true and spoken in about 3 seconds, but it's generic. To make it stronger and more specific: "Quit early, and you could owe $2,500 on a bonus you only got $1,758 of."
8. **Read-aloud:** "seventeen hundred fifty-eight dollars and seventy-five cents" is long. Optional: say "about seventeen hundred sixty dollars" and keep $1,758.75 on screen. If you do, the spoken and on-screen numbers would differ slightly, so use "about $1,759" on screen too.
