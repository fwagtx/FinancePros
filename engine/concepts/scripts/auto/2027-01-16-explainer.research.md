# Research brief: GDP: what it measures and what it misses
Posts Sat Jan 16, 2027 (evergreen). Researched Nov 17, 2026.

## News angle
No single news hook needed (evergreen). Context: BEA's 2026 annual update of the national accounts was released Sept 30, 2026 and revised 2021-2025 figures; numbers below are the post-update values. BEA's annual 2026 GDP figure is not due until after the posting date, so we use full-year 2025.

## Definition
- BEA: GDP is the market value of the final goods and services produced in the United States in a period. It counts only market (paid) transactions plus a few imputations; it is not a welfare measure.

## Key numbers (all BEA, as revised Sept 30, 2026, via FRED)
- US nominal (current-dollar) GDP, 2025: $30,861.3 billion = about $30.86 trillion (FRED GDPA, last updated 2026-09-30).
- US nominal GDP, 2024: $29,381.0 billion.
- Real GDP growth, 2025: 2.3% (annual average over annual average). (Pre-revision BEA third estimate for Q4 2025, released spring 2026, had 2.1%; the Sept 30, 2026 annual update revised it to 2.3%.)

## Worked example (our math): nominal vs real
- Nominal growth 2025: 30,861.292 / 29,380.991 = 1.0504 -> about 5.0%.
- Real growth 2025 (BEA): 2.3%.
- So of about 5.0% nominal growth, only 2.3 points was more stuff; the rest was higher prices (roughly 2.7 points, our approximation; BEA's GDP price index is the exact measure).

Second worked example (illustration, not data): Pay a lawn service $50 -> GDP +$50. Mow it yourself -> GDP +$0, though the same lawn got mowed.

## What GDP misses (definitions)
- Unpaid household production (cooking, cleaning, childcare, mowing) is excluded from GDP.
- BEA study (Survey of Current Business, May 2012, Bridgman et al.): including nonmarket household production would raise nominal GDP about 39% in 1965 and about 26% in 2010 (25.7%). Share fell as more women entered paid work. Updated BEA satellite account (Feb 2022) covers 1965-2020.
- GDP counts spending to repair damage (e.g. car crash repairs, disaster rebuilding) as output; it does not subtract pollution or resource depletion; it does not measure leisure or how income is distributed.

## Surprising fact
Unpaid work at home would have added about a quarter (26%) to US GDP in 2010, per BEA, yet GDP counts it as zero. And GDP's creator, Simon Kuznets, warned in his 1934 report to Congress that national income is not a measure of a nation's welfare (exact wording varies across secondary sources; paraphrase only).
