# Fact-check: 2026-12-28-business (How Canva Makes Money)

Checked Oct 29, 2026, for posting Dec 28, 2026 (evergreen). The writer's brief was used only for leads. Every claim was checked separately with web search. fortune.com could not be fetched from this session because the host is not in the allowed network domains, so the Fortune claims rest on search excerpts from Fortune plus agreeing outlets (B&T, Startup Daily, SaaStr, Crypto Briefing, AI Weekly).

## Verified
- **$4B ARR by end of 2025** (lines[1], timeline lines[6]): TechCrunch, Feb 18, 2026, reporting Cliff Obrecht at Web Summit Qatar. Dealroom, Dataconomy and Longbridge agree. "Canva says" and the sub "Canva, Feb 2026" are correct.
- **265M+ monthly users and 31M+ paid users** (lines[2]): from the same TechCrunch report, with Dataconomy agreeing. TechCrunch dates these figures "in 2025", so "end of 2025" is acceptable.
- **25+ seat accounts doubled to $500M ARR** (lines[5]): TechCrunch says the B2B segment (companies with more than 25 seats) grew 100% to $500M ARR. Correct.
- **Math** (lines[4], lines[0]), recomputed in Python:
  - 4000 / 31 = 129.03, so "about $129" is right.
  - Non-payers: (265 − 31) / 265 = 88.3%, so "almost 9 in 10" is right.
  - Both inputs are "more than" figures, so "about" is the right word. The line is labeled "Our math". OK.
- **Launched 2013** (lines[6]): Wikipedia and several profiles say Canva launched in 2013. Some sources give 2012 as the founding year, so "launched" is the safe word, and the script uses it. OK.
- **Profitable since 2017, as Canva describes it** (lines[6]):
  - A Canva spokesperson told Startup Daily the company is profitable on an "operating free cash flow basis".
  - Perkins has said "profitable since 2017" (SmartCompany), and in 2023 said "profitable for the last six years" (CNBC). That matches 2017.
  - A 2026 report says Canva has been profitable "nine consecutive years".
  - The claim is attributed with "says". OK, with the caveat under Issue 3.
- **Aug 2026 growth forecast cut to 20% over AI costs** (lines[7]):
  - Fortune, Aug 12, 2026: expected revenue growth cut "by a third to 20%" because of the high cost of delivering AI features.
  - B&T, Startup Daily, SaaStr and Crypto Briefing all agree. The cut was 30% to 20% for 2026, in Canva's Q2 2026 shareholder update.
  - "In August 2026", "reportedly" and the source "Fortune, Aug 2026" are all fine.
- **Not a stock tip**: Canva is private, nothing tells viewers to buy anything, and all three post texts carry "not financial advice". OK.
- **Format rules**:
  - Compare values are all 7 characters or fewer: "265M+" and "31M+".
  - No line uses keep:true.

  - On-screen text matches the spoken narration on every line.

## Issues

### 1. lines[0] (hook): "It's still profitable" is not fair as an unqualified claim (fails the check)
Canva's filed Australian accounts show net losses:
- FY2022 showed a $222M loss, driven by share-based pay (Startup Daily).
- Mediaweek reported "sustained net losses" in the accounts covering 2024, even as revenue grew to US$2B.

Canva's profitability claim is on its own free-cash-flow basis, and that figure is not audited. The flat "profitable" in the hook misleads viewers, and the word "still" makes it sound like a current, accounting-level fact.
- text → `Almost 9 in 10 Canva users pay nothing. It says it's still cash-flow positive.`
- say → `Almost nine in ten Canva users pay nothing. It says it's still cash-flow positive.`
- scene.title → `Free. [Cash].`

### 2. cover.title and the post fields carry the same unqualified "profit"
- cover.title: `Free App Profit` → `Free App, $4B`
- post.title → `How Canva makes $4B when almost 9 in 10 users pay nothing`
- post.ig, first line → `Almost 9 in 10 Canva users pay nothing. It says it's still cash-flow positive.`
- post.tiktok → `Almost 9 in 10 Canva users pay nothing. So how does it make money? Not financial advice. #moneytok #personalfinance #canva #business #financeprostv`
  - The current text drops "almost" (the real figure is 88%) and asks "How is it profitable?", which has the same problem as Issue 1.

### 3. lines[6]: free-cash-flow profit needs the accounting-loss caveat
Attributing the claim with "says" is right. But the video never says that free cash flow profit is not accounting profit, and that difference is the whole reason the hook is open to challenge.

Older reporting also used a different measure for the 2017 start date. In 2019, Forbes, as reprinted by Top4, said "adjusted EBITDA since 2017".
- text → `Canva launched in 2013. It says it's been free cash flow positive every year since 2017, though its filed accounts show losses.`
- say → `Canva launched in twenty thirteen. It says it's been free cash flow positive every year since twenty seventeen, though its filed accounts show losses.`
- The scene timeline can stay as it is ("FCF profitable" is fine), or change to `FCF positive`.

### 4. post.ig and post.yt_desc: matching caveat (minor)
- post.ig: after `Canva says it has been free cash flow profitable every year since 2017.` add ` Its filed statutory accounts have shown losses, largely from stock-based pay.`
- post.yt_desc: after `every year since 2017,` add ` though its filed statutory accounts have shown losses,`

### 5. Minor and optional (would not fail the script)
- lines[2]: "monthly users" are monthly *active* users. Optional: `It had over 265 million monthly active users, and just over 31 million paid.` Say it the same way.
- lines[7]: the cut is to 2026 revenue growth. Optional: text `The catch: in August 2026, Canva reportedly cut its 2026 growth forecast to 20%, citing AI costs.` Say it the same way, with "twenty twenty-six" spoken.

## Summary
Every number checks out and the math is correct. The script fails on one point: it calls Canva "profitable" with no qualifier in the hook, cover, title and TikTok text. Canva's own claim is free-cash-flow profitability, and its filed accounts show net losses. The fixes are wording changes to text that already exists, plus the caveat in lines[6].

VERDICT: FAIL
