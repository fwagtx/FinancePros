# 2026-12-14-explainer research brief: Taxes, title and registration (costs after the sticker price)

Evergreen. No timely news hook needed. Worked example uses Texas because its state rules are published and simple.

## Key facts (with dates)
- Texas motor vehicle sales tax: 6.25% of the sales price minus any trade-in allowance (Texas Comptroller, motor vehicle tax guide 96-254; Comptroller release Sept 3, 2024; Comptroller ruling 201807013L, trade-in value excluded from taxable consideration, Tax Code 152.021). Current as of 2026.
- Texas title application fee: $33 in emissions counties ($28 in other counties) (Tarrant County Tax Office title fees page; Williamson County titles page). 2026.
- Texas base registration, passenger vehicle 6,000 lbs or less: $50.75 per year, plus county/local fees ($10 to $21.50) and small state fees ($7.50 inspection replacement fee, $4.75 TxDMV processing fee, $1 TexasSure) (county pages, 2026 fee calculators; not verified on txdmv.gov directly).
- Texas dealer documentary fee: OCCC rule 7 TAC 84.205, effective July 11, 2024: a doc fee of $225 or less is presumed reasonable and needs no filing; above $225 the dealer must file a notification and cost analysis with the OCCC, and cannot charge more than the filed amount. Not a hard cap. (OCCC adoption notice and filing instructions, 2024.)
- California doc fee cap: $85 (still in place as of 2026; Gov. Newsom vetoed SB 791 in Oct 2025, which would have raised it; CalMatters Oct 2025; Troutman Pepper July 2025; CA dealers association called $85 "by far the lowest in the country").
- Doc fees vary hugely: CarEdge dealer data puts Florida's average doc fee around $908 (Aug 2026) and Texas average about $228-$233 (July 2026). Sources disagree on whether Florida caps it (WEAK, third-party; not used in script).
- No-statewide-sales-tax states on vehicles: Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire, Oregon, but with catches (Delaware document fee of about 4.25-5.25% of value, Alaska local taxes, Montana/NH annual value-based fees, Oregon 0.5% privilege tax on new cars). Too many caveats; not used in script.
- Trade-in credit: most states tax only the difference; California, Hawaii, Virginia are commonly listed as not giving the credit (consumer guides; sources conflict on Hawaii). WEAK; script states only the Texas rule.

## Worked example (labeled EXAMPLE, Texas, 2026 rates)
Assumptions: $32,000 vehicle price, emissions county, passenger car under 6,000 lbs, dealer doc fee of $225 (the Texas no-filing threshold), sales tax computed on the vehicle price. County and small state fees excluded.
- Sales tax: $32,000 x 6.25% = $2,000.00
- Title: $33.00
- Base registration: $50.75
- Doc fee: $225.00
- Total extra: 2,000 + 33 + 50.75 + 225 = $2,308.75 -> out the door $34,308.75 before county fees.
Trade-in variant: $8,000 trade-in -> taxable $32,000 - $8,000 = $24,000 x 6.25% = $1,500.00; tax saved = $500.00 (= $8,000 x 6.25%).

## Surprising fact
Texas taxes the price minus the trade-in, so an $8,000 trade-in cuts the tax by $500 in the example. And doc fees vary by state rule: $85 cap in California versus $225 before any filing in Texas.
