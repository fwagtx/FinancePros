# Research: How Google makes money from free search

## Timely angle
- Latest filing: Alphabet Q2 2026 results (released July 22, 2026; 10-Q for quarter ended June 30, 2026). Google Search & other revenue grew 17% to $63.3 billion ($63.27B), up from about $54.2 billion in Q2 2025. Retail and finance were the largest contributors. Growth eased from 19% in Q1 2026. (Alphabet Q2 2026 earnings release / 8-K; Search Engine Journal; CNBC)
- Q2 2026: total Alphabet revenue $119.8B (+24%); TAC (traffic acquisition costs) $16.2B (+10%). (Alphabet Q2 2026 release)
- Next earnings (Q3 2026) expected late October 2026; do not reference results not yet released.

## Company numbers: Alphabet FY2025 Form 10-K (fiscal year ended Dec 31, 2025, filed Feb 2026)
- Total revenues 2025: $402.8B (2024: $350.0B), +15%. First year above $400B.
- Google Search & other 2025: $224,532M = $224.5B (2024: $198,084M = $198.1B), +$26.4B.
- Google advertising total 2025: $294.7B. Google Services: $342.7B. Google Cloud: $58.7B. Other Bets: $1.5B.
- Search & other share of total revenue 2025: 224.5 / 402.8 = about 55.7%.

## How it works (Alphabet 10-K definitions)
- Users search free; advertisers pay for ads shown alongside results. Google Ads sells placement through an auction where advertisers bid on searches/keywords.
- 10-K metrics: "paid clicks" = clicks on ads by end users on Google Search and other Google properties; "cost-per-click" = click-driven revenues divided by total paid clicks, "the average amount we charge advertisers for each engagement by users." So much of search revenue is charged per click.

## Worked example (exact math, FY2025)
- Per day: $224,532M / 365 = $615.15M -> about $615 million a day.
- Per second: $224,532,000,000 / (365 x 24 x 3,600 = 31,536,000 s) = $7,119.86 -> about $7,120 a second.

## Surprising fact
- Google paid Apple about $20 billion in 2022 to be the default search engine in Safari, a figure revealed in the US v. Google antitrust case (court documents, reported by CNN, Gulf News/Bloomberg, PhoneArena). Google spent $26.3B in 2021 on default-placement payments overall (trial testimony).
- Sept 2, 2025 remedies ruling (Judge Amit Mehta, US District Court, D.C.): Google may keep paying partners for distribution/default placement but cannot enter exclusive contracts; must share some search index and user-interaction data with qualified competitors; Google did not have to sell Chrome. (CNN Sept 3, 2025; AppleInsider; TidBITS)
