# Research brief: Why markets go up over the long run (Acorn Economics)
Evergreen, posts Wed Jan 6, 2027. No timely news hook needed; latest full-year data is calendar 2025.

## Core idea (the "why")
- Long-run stock returns come from: (1) earnings growth of companies, (2) dividends paid out (and reinvested), plus (3) changes in valuation (P/E), which tend to wash out over long periods. Framework: John C. Bogle / Keynes ("investment return" = dividend yield + earnings growth; "speculative return" = change in P/E). Bogle Center papers; Morningstar coverage.
- Earnings grow because the economy grows (more people, more output, productivity) and because inflation raises prices, revenues and profits in dollar terms. Long-run returns quoted below are nominal (not inflation-adjusted).

## Key numbers (NYU Stern, Aswath Damodaran, "Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills", histretSP, updated Jan 5, 2026)
- S&P 500 incl. dividends, value of $100 invested at start of 1928: $1,157,598.95 at end of 2025 (on screen "$1.16M").
- Compound annual return 1928-2025: our math from that series = (1,157,598.95/100)^(1/98) - 1 = 10.02% a year. (Fool/Shiller data: 9.92% CAGR 1928-2025, arithmetic avg 11.83%; arithmetic avg of Damodaran yearly returns = 11.86%, our math.)
- Up vs down years 1928-2025 (98 years, total return): 72 up, 26 down (our count from Damodaran series; matches Motley Fool/Shiller and Ben Carlson "72 up, 26 down").
- Worst years: 1931 -43.84%; 2008 -36.55%; 1937 -35.34%; 1974 -25.90%; 2002 -21.97%; 2022 -18.04%. Best: 1954 +52.56%, 1933 +49.98%.
- 2025: +17.78% total return (Damodaran); price index closed 2025 at 6,845.50, +16.4% price (Kiplinger, Reuters/MarketScreener).

## Caveat: long flat stretches
- S&P 500 price index closed 1999 at 1,469.25 and closed 2012 at 1,426.19 (below 1999). Also 2007 close 1,468.36. Price-only; with dividends 2000-2012 total was about +23% over 13 years (our math from Damodaran yearly returns). Sources: dividendgrowthinvestor.com; Wikipedia "Closing milestones of the S&P 500"; Calculated Risk.
- Past performance is not a guarantee of future results.

## Worked example (our math)
- $1,000 growing 7% a year for 30 years: 1,000 x 1.07^30 = $7,612.26. (7% chosen as a round illustrative rate, not a forecast; roughly the real return range: Shiller real CAGR 6.64% 1928-2025 per Motley Fool.)

## Surprising fact
- Even with 26 losing years, including -43.84% in 1931 and -36.55% in 2008, $100 became about $1.16 million (1928-2025, nominal, before fees/taxes).
