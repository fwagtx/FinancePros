# Research brief: Money myth - "Bonds are safe, so they can't lose value" (Hot Take, posts Mon Dec 28, 2026)

Evergreen. No news hook needed; the anchor is the 2022 bond-market loss.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. **Bloomberg US Aggregate Bond Index ("the Agg") returned -13.01% in calendar 2022**, its worst year since the index began in 1976. Prior worst: 1994 at -2.92%. (State Street SPDR factsheet table; IPE / Principal AM; Bloomberg Municipal Index Monthly Jan 2023; J.P. Morgan cited "slumped 13%... worst since it began in 1976".)
2. **Long-term Treasuries fell harder: iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF (TLT) total return -31.4% in calendar 2022** (benchmark -30.8%), per iShares/BlackRock fund page. Chartrow shows -31.2%. Use the issuer figure.
3. **Why: prices move opposite to rates.** FINRA: "for every 1 percentage-point change in interest rates, a bond will rise or fall in the opposite direction by an amount equal to its duration number." Example: duration 10, +1 point -> about -10%. It is an approximation, less precise for big moves (AllianceBernstein glossary).
   - Context: the Fed raised rates a cumulative 4.25 percentage points in 2022 (Yahoo Finance Morning Brief / AOL).
4. **Individual bond vs fund:** an individual bond held to maturity generally repays full face value unless the issuer defaults; a bond fund has no maturity date, so there is no point at which its price is guaranteed to come back (Bankrate; Morningstar; Vanguard notes the hold-to-maturity benefit is largely emotional, since both are priced by the same market).
5. **The surprise:** "safe" bonds (US Treasuries, high grade) had a worse 2022 than many expected: the broad index's loss was more than four times its previous worst year (-13.01% vs -2.92%).

## Worked example (OUR OWN MATH, hypothetical, FINRA rule of thumb)
Bond fund with duration 6 years, $10,000 invested, rates rise 2 percentage points.
Price change ~= -duration x rate change = -6 x 2 = -12%.
$10,000 x (1 - 0.12) = $8,800 (about $1,200 lower). Approximate; ignores interest paid and convexity.

## Takeaway (education, not advice)
"Safe" for high-quality bonds mainly means low default risk, not a price that never moves. Price risk rises with duration.
