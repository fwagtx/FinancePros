# Research brief: Pre-qualified vs pre-approved: what the words really mean
Slot: 2026-12-23-explainer (posts Wed Dec 23, 2026, 12 PM CT). Evergreen; no news hook needed. Researched Oct 24, 2026.
Note: direct page fetches (consumerfinance.gov, myfico.com, ftc.gov) failed with DNS errors in this environment; all facts confirmed via search-result excerpts of the official pages, cross-checked.

## Core definitions (CFPB, Ask CFPB "What's the difference between a prequalification letter and a preapproval letter?")
- CFPB (paraphrase, not a verbatim quote for the video): both letters state how much a lender is willing to lend, up to a certain amount and based on certain assumptions. They give useful information about your likelihood of getting a loan but "are not guaranteed loan offers" (wording as excerpted).
- Lenders use the terms "prequalification" and "preapproval" differently / inconsistently. CFPB says not to focus on the label.
- "Some lenders offer a prequalification letter based on unverified information that you report and will only issue a preapproval letter based on verified information." (as excerpted in search results)
- Some lenders may issue a written commitment letter valid for a certain period to lend up to a specified amount, subject to limited conditions.
- Takeaway framing (education): what matters is what the lender checked (self-reported vs verified income/assets/credit).

## Typical differences (industry framing, not CFPB rule)
- Prequalification: often self-reported numbers; often a soft credit inquiry (no score impact).
- Preapproval: often documented income, verified assets, full (hard) credit pull. Varies by lender. (Opendoor guide; National Mortgage News commentary: process matters more than label.)

## Hard inquiries and FICO (myFICO)
- "For most people, one additional credit inquiry will take less than five points off their FICO Scores." (myFICO blog / FICO spokesperson quoted in press)
- FICO Scores consider inquiries from the last 12 months; inquiries stay on the report 2 years.
- Soft inquiries are not considered by FICO Scores.
- Rate shopping: mortgage, auto and student loan inquiries within a window count as a single inquiry. Older FICO versions: 14 days; newer versions: 45 days (expanded in 2004). Lender chooses the version. Also: such inquiries in the 30 days before scoring are ignored.
- Does NOT apply to credit cards (Press Herald/FICO).

## Worked example (hypothetical)
- 3 mortgage lenders pull credit (hard inquiries) within 14 days -> inside even the shortest (14-day) window -> FICO counts 3 pulls as 1 inquiry. 3 inquiries -> 1 counted. Works under both 14-day and 45-day versions.
- Contrast: 3 credit card applications in 14 days = 3 separate inquiries (no rate-shopping dedupe).

## Prescreened "pre-approved" mail (FCRA / FTC)
- Prescreened offers (often called "preapproved") are unsolicited offers based on credit-report information showing you meet the issuer's criteria. FCRA requires each to include a notice of the right to opt out.
- Prescreen pulls show as soft inquiries; don't affect scores (Equifax). Applying later usually triggers a hard inquiry; the offer can still be declined if you don't meet conditions.
- Opt out: OptOutPrescreen.com or 1-888-5-OPT-OUT (1-888-567-8688), run by the major credit bureaus. Five years (online/phone) or permanently (sign and mail a Permanent Opt-Out Election form). FTC.

## Surprising fact
- FTC (Sept 2022): alleged Credit Karma's "pre-approved" card offers (Feb 2018 - Apr 2021) were false for many; nearly one third of consumers who applied for "pre-approved" offers were denied. Credit Karma paid $3 million under the order; it said it disagreed with the allegations. Commission vote 5-0. (Allegation; no admission.)
