## Issues

1. **Line 1: the on-screen value differs from the spoken one (must fix).** The caption says "$1.1161" and the narration says "about a dollar twelve." 1.1161 rounds to 1.12, but the rules count the caption and narration as mismatched numbers.
   **Fix:** change the caption to `It touched about $1.12, the weakest since May 2025, and that changes what your dollar buys.` and keep scene.value "$1.1161" with sub "Low since May 2025". Or change the narration to "It touched one dollar eleven sixty-one…". The first option reads better out loud.

2. **Line 6: "Imported goods" cheaper is overstated (must fix).** A weaker euro lowers the dollar cost of goods priced in euros, meaning imports from the eurozone, not imports in general. Pass-through to US shelf prices is also partial and slow, and tariffs affect it too. There is no source for this claim.
   **Fix:** replace "Imported goods" with "European imports" and add nothing about shelf prices. Or drop it and keep only "Trips to Europe". Matching caption fix is not needed because the caption does not mention imports.

3. **Lines 6 and 7: the claim about euro stock funds is directional and slightly overgeneralized (minor).** The caption says "European stock funds can shrink a bit". That is true only from currency translation, for unhedged funds, with share prices held equal. Hedged funds are not affected this way. Line 7's board "Weak euro = smaller fund" states it as a flat rule.
   **Fix:** line 6 caption/narration: "…so unhedged funds holding European shares can lose a bit in dollar terms." Line 7 board: "Weak euro = smaller fund (in $)" or "…= fund worth less in $".

4. **Line 3: viewers could confuse the chart's endpoint with today's rate (minor).** The chart ends at "$115" (September average) right after line 1 said "about a dollar twelve". The labels are accurate, but a viewer may wonder why it shows $115 when the euro is at $1.12.
   **Fix:** set `end` to "$115 Sept avg" if it fits, or add to the narration: "…about a hundred fifteen on average last month, and about a hundred twelve today." Also, "this September" is in the past by Oct 5; "last month" is clearer.

5. **Line 5: label the month and year (minor).** "February's average rate" / "€2,000, Feb avg" should say Feb 2026 to be unambiguous. The rate itself (1.1824) is never shown.
   **Fix:** from-label `€2,000, Feb '26 avg`. Caption: "…at Feb 2026's average rate ($1.18)…". The example is clearly labeled as a hypothetical ("Example", "before fees"). Good.

6. **Hook (line 0): acceptable, could be stronger.** "The euro hit a seventeen-month low overnight." It is true, specific, current, and under 3 seconds (8 words). But it does not tell the viewer why it matters to them, so many viewers will not feel they need the answer.
   **Stronger option (about 3 s):** "The euro just hit a seventeen-month low, and your Europe trip got cheaper." Or keep it as is and let line 1's "changes what your dollar buys" carry the stakes. Optional.
