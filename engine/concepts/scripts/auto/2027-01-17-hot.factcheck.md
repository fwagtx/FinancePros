# Fact-check: 2027-01-17-hot ("Money Myth: bankruptcy erases every kind of debt")
Checked Nov 18, 2026 for posting Sun Jan 17, 2027, 6 PM CT. I did not use the brief or research.md. Every figure was checked against web search results. uscourts.gov and the Firecrawl scraper were blocked, so the US Courts figures were confirmed from search snippets of US Courts pages and of outlets that cite them.

## Verified claims
- **FY filings (12 months ending Sept 30):** 2021 434,540; 2022 383,810; 2023 433,658; 2024 504,112; 2025 557,376; 2026 621,111. All are CONFIRMED.
  - FY2026: the US Courts release of Oct 22, 2026 says filings rose 11.4%, from 557,376 to 621,111. Business filings were 27,054 and nonbusiness filings were 594,057.
  - FY2022 and FY2021: 383,810 vs 434,540, a drop of 11.7%.
  - FY2024 and FY2023: 504,112 vs 433,658, a rise of 16.2%.
  - FY2025: 557,376, a rise of 10.6%.
- **Year-over-year math:** 621,111 / 557,376 = +11.43%, so "11.4%" is correct. Filings have risen four years in a row (FY23, FY24, FY25, FY26), so "fourth straight yearly rise" is correct.
- **The original "+62%":** the math was right (621,111 / 383,810 = +61.8%), but the framing was misleading. See issue 2.
- **11 U.S.C. § 523(a) items:**
  - domestic support obligations, including child support and alimony: (a)(5)
  - certain (priority/recent) taxes: (a)(1)
  - fines and penalties payable to a government: (a)(7), plus criminal restitution: (a)(13)
  - fraud debts: (a)(2). These survive only if the creditor proves the fraud under § 523(c), so "usually" is correct.
  - death or personal-injury debts from drunk or drugged driving: (a)(9)
  - student loans unless there is "undue hardship": (a)(8)
  - All CONFIRMED.
- **Credit cards, medical bills and personal loans:** these are generally dischargeable. One exception: under (a)(2)(C), luxury charges or cash advances made shortly before filing are presumed nondischargeable. Because of that exception, I softened the wording to "can wipe out".
- **DOJ/ED attestation process:** it was launched Nov 17, 2022. It is still in effect: the form was revised in May 2025, and the DOJ guidance page was updated in 2025-2026. No rescission was found. "Since November 2022" will still be accurate on Jan 17, 2027.
- **Hook:** "Bankruptcy wipes out all your debt? Not even close." is 9 words, under 3 seconds, true and specific. It asks a question the viewer wants answered and the video answers it. The value "MYTH" on the paper prop labeled "DEBT" matches. PASS.
- **Cover and title:** "Bankruptcy Myth", the header "Money Myth: Bankruptcy Erases All" and the post title are all answered by lines 3-8. PASS.
- **Other checks:** no political opinion, no price predictions and no personal advice. The "reset button" line is a general description, not a recommendation. I found no stale time words for Jan 17, 2027.

## Issues found (all fixed)
1. **Line 1: unsupported implication.**
   - Problem: "And lots of people are finding out." implies that filers are discovering the myth, which is not supported.
   - Fix (text): "And filings are climbing. US filings hit about 621,000 in the year ending September 2026."
   - Fix (say): "And filings are climbing. US filings hit about six hundred twenty-one thousand in the year ending September twenty twenty-six."
   - Also: the caption text now says "about 621,000" to match the spoken words and the "621K" on the chart. Before, it said 621,111 while the narration said "about six hundred twenty-one thousand".
2. **Line 2: cherry-picked base and awkward "By our math".**
   - Problem: measuring +62% from the FY2022 low (the bottom of the cycle) overstates the trend. "By our math" also reads awkwardly out loud.
   - Fix (text): "That's up 11.4% from a year earlier, the fourth straight yearly rise."
   - Fix (say): "That's up eleven point four percent from a year earlier, the fourth straight yearly rise."
   - Chart note changed from "+62%" to "+11.4%". The chart points are unchanged and identical in both chart scenes.
3. **Line 3 and the split card: overstated "Erased".**
   - Problem: credit card debt is not always discharged; the luxury-purchase and fraud exceptions apply.
   - Fix (text and say): "Here's the fact. A discharge can wipe out many debts, like credit cards and medical bills."
   - Board: "Can wipe out:" / "cards, med bills".
   - Left heading on the split card (line 4): "Erased" changed to "Can erase".
4. **Line 5: awkward and inaccurate phrasing.**
   - Problem: "so do injuries caused by drunk driving" says the injuries survive, when it is the debts that survive.
   - Fix (text and say): "Debts from fraud usually stick around too, and so do debts for injuring someone while driving drunk."
5. **Post captions:**
   - IG now adds "the fourth straight yearly rise", "(usually)" for fraud debts, and "drunk-driving injury debts".
   - The YT description now adds "up 11.4% from a year earlier".
   - The exact figure 621,111 stays in the captions, where it is sourced.
