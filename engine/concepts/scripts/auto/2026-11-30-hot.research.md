# 2026-11-30-hot research brief: Scam alert, fake deployed soldiers (military romance scams)

Researched October 2, 2026. Posts Monday, November 30, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its year/period.
No timely single-week news hook; the angle is the FBI 2025 Internet Crime Report (published April 2026) plus Army CID's long-running warnings.

## Key facts (dated)

1. FBI IC3, 2025 Internet Crime Report (released April 2026): confidence/romance fraud = 23,159 complaints, $929.3 million in losses in 2025, up from $672.0 million in 2024 (+38%; 929.3 / 672.0 = 1.383). Complaints up 29%.
   - People 60 and older: $584 million of those 2025 losses (about 63%).
   - More than $19 million of 2025 confidence/romance losses had a likely AI connection (fake profile images, real-time video).
   Sources: ic3.gov 2025 IC3 Annual Report PDF (via search excerpts); Techlicious; This Week in Worcester; Red Sift. Two+ outlets agree.

2. FTC on military cover stories: FTC Data Spotlight "Romance scammers' favorite lies exposed" (Feb 2023, covering 2022 reports): top lie was a friend/relative sick, hurt or in jail (nearly a quarter of reports); next: investment advice, "followed closely by the lie that the scammer was in the military", or needed help with a delivery. Claiming to be on a faraway military base is a popular excuse for never meeting; "offshore oil rig worker" is another.
   - Same spotlight: 2022: nearly 70,000 people reported a romance scam; reported losses $1.3 billion. 24% of people who reported losing money said they paid with gift cards. 40% said contact started on social media.
   Source: ftc.gov Data Spotlight Feb 2023; FTC press release Feb 2023 (search excerpts).

3. FTC median loss: romance median reported loss $2,218 in Q3 2025 (a bit more than 11,200 people reporting losses, $398 million that quarter). First nine months of 2025: 55,604 reports, losses over $1.16 billion (up 22% year over year).
   Sources: Central Oregon Daily (Nov 2025, citing FTC); Global Dating Insights (citing FTC).
   Earlier medians: $2,000 (2023, FTC "Love Stinks" blog, Feb 2024); $2,500 (2020, FTC spotlight Feb 2021).

4. Army CID warnings (army.mil articles, multiple years): impostors take the real name and rank of a serving soldier, add photos from the internet. Requests: money to "purchase leave papers" from the Army, medical expenses for combat wounds, a flight home from the war zone, special laptops, international telephones, transportation fees. CID officials say these are all lies. Scammers claim units have no phones or that the Army blocks access to bank accounts. Red flag per CID: within weeks of an online relationship, the "soldier" asks for money. CID has seen killed-in-action soldiers' identities used. Tips: cid.army.mil.
   Sources: army.mil/article/130861, /67457, /91823, /95075 (search excerpts; army.mil blocked from fetch).

5. Payment methods: FTC lists wire transfer, gift cards (give the numbers on the back), payment apps and cryptocurrency as the ways scammers ask to be paid; gift cards were 24% of romance-loss reports in 2022 (FTC spotlight Feb 2023). Gift card payments to romance scammers rose nearly 70% in 2020 (FTC spotlight Feb 2021).

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled as example)
A typical escalation following CID's list of asks:
- Leave papers: $500
- Flight home: $1,200
- Medical bills: $2,500
- Total: 500 + 1,200 + 2,500 = $4,200
- Versus FTC median romance loss in Q3 2025 of $2,218: 4,200 / 2,218 = 1.89, nearly double.

## The surprise
Army CID says the classic asks (buying "leave papers", paying for a flight home from a war zone, combat medical bills) are all lies; the identity is often a real soldier's name and photos.

## Warning signs (descriptive)
- Weeks into an online relationship, a "deployed soldier" asks for money.
- Claims of no phone access or blocked bank accounts; never video chats.
- Asks for gift cards, wire transfers or crypto.
