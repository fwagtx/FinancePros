# Research brief: Target-date funds, how the autopilot works (slot 2026-11-05-explainer)

Researched October 1, 2026. Posts November 5, 2026. Evergreen; no news hook needed. Latest editions used: Vanguard How America Saves 2026 (25th edition, June 2026, covers year-end 2025 data); Morningstar 2026 Target-Date Landscape (covers 2025).
Note: sec.gov, finra.org, dol.gov and vanguard.com pages are blocked from direct fetch in this environment; all facts below were confirmed through multiple search results quoting those sources.

## What a target-date fund is
- One fund holding a mix of stock and bond funds; the year in the name is the approximate year the investor expects to retire (SEC Investor Bulletin: Target Date Retirement Funds, SEC/DOL, 2010).
- The mix shifts from mostly stocks toward bonds over time on a set schedule called the glide path.
- Funds with the same target date can follow different glide paths and charge different fees (SEC bulletin; DOL "Target Date Retirement Funds - Tips for ERISA Plan Fiduciaries"; FINRA).
- Not guaranteed: SEC bulletin says they do not guarantee sufficient retirement income at the target date and investors can lose money (paraphrase; not used as a quote).

## "To" vs "through" (FINRA, "Save the Date: Target-Date Funds Explained")
- Word-for-word (confirmed in two separate search results): "A "to retirement" target-date fund will reach its most conservative asset allocation on the date of the fund's name."
- Also: "A target-date fund designed to take an investor "through retirement" continues to rebalance and generally will reach its most conservative asset allocation after the target date."

## A named glide path: Vanguard Target Retirement funds (a "through" design)
- About 90% stocks until 25 years before the target date (age 40 if retiring at 65; starts at age 25 in Vanguard's chart).
- 50% stocks at the target date (age 65).
- Lands at about 30% stocks / 70% bonds about seven years after the target date (age 72).
- Source: Vanguard glide path pages / Vanguard research "Choice of equity landing points can benefit target-date fund investors" (May 2025); Morningstar Vanguard Target Retirement series page; Bogleheads wiki cross-check.
- Chart points (age, % stocks, assuming retirement at 65): [25, 90], [40, 90], [65, 50], [72, 30].

## Default investment (QDIA)
- Pension Protection Act of 2006 authorized qualified default investment alternatives (QDIAs); DOL final QDIA regulation issued in 2007. Target-date funds are one of the allowed QDIA types and became the most popular.
- Vanguard How America Saves 2026 (year-end 2025 data): among plans designating a QDIA, 98% of designated QDIAs were target-date funds (2% balanced funds).
- HAS 2026: 61% of Vanguard plans had adopted automatic enrollment (year-end 2025).
- HAS 2026: nearly 2 of every 3 dollars contributed were invested in target-date funds in 2025.
- HAS 2026: 96% of plans offered target-date funds; 69% of participants were in a professionally managed allocation (all-time high).
- (Not used: single-TDF share is reported as 61% in some summaries and "62% single TDF or balanced fund" in others; ambiguous, left out.)

## Fees (Morningstar Target-Date Landscape reports)
- Asset-weighted average expense ratio of target-date mutual funds (chartable series):
  - 2021: 0.34% (implied by 2022 drop "to 32 from 34 basis points")
  - 2022: 0.32%
  - 2023: 0.30%
  - 2024: 0.29%
  - 2025: 0.27% (Morningstar 2026 Target-Date Landscape)
  - 2013: about 0.60% (context; "roughly half the level from a decade ago")
- Morningstar 2026 report also: target-date strategy assets $4.8 trillion at end of 2025, up 20.3%; CITs 54% of target-date assets; five firms control 80% of assets.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- Balance $10,000 x 0.27% (2025 asset-weighted average) = $10,000 x 0.0027 = $27 a year in fund expenses.

## The surprising fact
- Most people "pick" a target-date fund without picking it: 98% of designated default investments in Vanguard plans were target-date funds (HAS 2026), so auto-enrolled savers land there automatically.
