# Research brief: Scholarships vs grants vs work-study (2026-12-06-explainer, Ticker)

## Core definitions (confirmed Oct 7, 2026)
- Scholarships: gift aid usually awarded on merit, talent or other criteria (schools, states, private groups). Not repaid.
- Grants: gift aid usually based on financial need (federal Pell Grant, state and school grants). Generally not repaid. Exception: a student who withdraws early may have to return "unearned" federal grant aid (Return of Title IV funds); the student's grant overpayment is capped at half of the grant funds received/scheduled, and overpayments of $50 or less are not owed (school R2T4 policies, e.g. Miami Univ., Pitt State, citing ED rules).
- Federal Work-Study (FWS): need-based, determined through the FAFSA; a part-time job (often on campus) where the student earns at least the current federal minimum wage (currently $7.25/hr), paid directly to the student (typically every two weeks) for hours worked, not credited to the bill. Money is earned, not granted: no work, no money. (Texas Comptroller FWS summary; UGA OSFA; studentaid.gov.)
- FWS earnings are subject to federal/state income tax (W-2) but generally exempt from FICA if enrolled full time and working less than half time; FAFSA excludes prior-year work-study earnings from income (OSU SFA, Bates, Going Merry).

## Key numbers
- Max Federal Pell Grant 2026-27 award year (July 1, 2026 - June 30, 2027): $7,395; minimum $740 (ED Dear Colleague Letter GEN-26-01, Jan 30, 2026; Mapping Your Future "remain flat"). Unchanged since 2023-24.
- FWS appropriation for the 2026-27 award year: $1.23 billion (flat), Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, P.L. 119-75, signed Feb 3, 2026 (ED CB-26-05 / NASFAA / cu.edu). FY2027 budget proposals to cut FWS are pending/unsettled: NOT used in script (could change before Dec 6, politically charged).
- College Board, Trends in Student Aid 2025: in 2024-25 undergraduates received an average of $12,080 in grant aid per full-time-equivalent student; total aid averaged $16,810 per FTE undergrad; $173.7 billion in total grant aid to postsecondary students in 2024-25 (+5.4% inflation-adjusted). Note: "grant aid" in College Board includes federal, state, institutional and private grants AND scholarships.

## Taxes (the surprise)
- IRS Publication 970: a scholarship or fellowship grant (incl. Pell) is tax-free only to the extent used for qualified education expenses: tuition and required fees, plus books, supplies and equipment required for courses. Amounts used for room and board (and travel etc.) are taxable income. Worksheet 1-1 splits tax-free and taxable parts.

## Worked example (hypothetical)
- $10,000 scholarship. $7,000 goes to tuition, fees and required books; $3,000 goes to housing.
- Tax-free part = $7,000. Taxable part = 10,000 - 7,000 = $3,000 reported as income (whether it's actually taxed depends on the student's total income and standard deduction).

## Surprise fact
- "Free money" can be taxable (room and board portion of scholarships/grants), while "earned" work-study money doesn't count against you as income on next year's FAFSA.
