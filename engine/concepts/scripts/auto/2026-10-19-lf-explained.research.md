# Research brief: Social Security: how it works and will it run out (long-form Explained, posts Mon Oct 19, 2026)

Timely angle: the 2026 Social Security Trustees Report (released June 9, 2026) projects the retirement (OASI) trust fund is depleted in the fourth quarter of 2032, a quarter earlier than last year's 2033 projection. All future dates below are the Trustees' PROJECTIONS (intermediate assumptions), not predictions by us.
Note: the Oct 13, 2026 explainer already covers the COLA formula; only mention the COLA in one or two lines (2.8% for 2026).

## 2026 Trustees Report (SSA press release 2026-06-09; SSA OACT TR 2026 highlights / TRSUM; CRS IF13256 June 25, 2026; CNBC 2026-06-09; AARP; 401k Specialist; TheStreet)
- OASI (retirement + survivors) reserves projected depleted in Q4 2032; income then sufficient to pay 78% of scheduled benefits.
- 2025 report: OASI depletion projected in 2033 with 77% payable (ASPPA 2025-06; Wikipedia summary). CNBC: 2026 date is three months earlier.
- DI (disability) fund: able to pay 100% of scheduled benefits through at least 2100 (not depleted in the 75-year window).
- Hypothetical combined OASDI: depleted Q3 2034, then 83% payable. Same 2034 year as the 2025 report.
- 75-year actuarial deficit: 4.42% of taxable payroll (2026-2100), up from 3.82% in the 2025 report.
- Closing the gap immediately: payroll tax 12.40% -> 16.65%, OR cut scheduled benefits 25.2% for all current and future beneficiaries, OR 30.3% for only those newly eligible in 2026 and later (2026 TR via search snippets; CRR Boston College).
- Reasons for worsening: assumed ultimate fertility rate lowered from 1.90 to 1.75 children per woman; the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025) added a temporary extra standard deduction for people 65+, so less income tax on benefits flows to the trust funds (CRFB analysis June 9, 2026; SSA press release).
- Commissioner Bisignano quote exists but two sources differ by one word ("for now" vs "now"): DO NOT quote it.

## 2025 operations (2026 TR, "Trust Fund Financial Operations in 2025")
- Total income $1.449 trillion: payroll taxes $1,323B, income taxation of benefits $58B, interest $69B.
- Total cost $1.609 trillion, mostly benefits.
- Reserves fell $160.2B: from $2,721B (start of 2025) to $2,561B (end of 2025).
- December 2025: benefits paid to about 70 million people: 56M retired workers and dependents, 8M disabled workers and dependents, 6M survivors.
- About 185 million people had covered earnings and paid payroll taxes in 2025.
- Workers per beneficiary: about 2.6 in 2025 (TR 2026 table; 185/70 = 2.64). About 5.1 in 1960 (CRS/NCPSSM summaries). Projected about 1.9 by 2075 (CRS).

## How it works (2026 rules)
- Payroll tax 6.2% from worker + 6.2% from employer = 12.4%; self-employed pay the full 12.4%. Applies to earnings up to the 2026 taxable maximum of $184,500 (2025: $176,100). (CRS R46658/IF11747; SSA)
- Max employee tax 2026: $184,500 x 6.2% = $11,439.
- Example: $60,000 salary: 6.2% = $3,720 from the worker + $3,720 from the employer = $7,440 a year.
- Pay-as-you-go: today's taxes mostly pay today's beneficiaries; surplus goes into trust funds held in special-issue Treasury securities that earn interest.
- 40 credits (about 10 years of work) needed for retirement benefits (SSA EN-05-10035).
- Benefit formula (2026 eligibility): AIME = highest 35 years of wage-indexed earnings / 420 months. PIA = 90% of first $1,286 + 32% of AIME from $1,286 to $7,749 + 15% above $7,749; PIA truncated to the dime. (SSA OACT; CRS)
- Max PIA at full retirement age, eligible in 2026: $4,152 (CRS).
- Full retirement age 67 for born 1960+; claim at 62 = 70% of PIA (30% reduction); delayed credits 8%/year to 70 = 124%. (Schwab; NCOA; CRS R44670)
- COLA: 2.8% for 2026; average retired-worker benefit $2,015 -> $2,071 (SSA 2026 COLA fact sheet). Formula covered in Oct 13 video.

## WORKED EXAMPLE (hypothetical worker, AIME $5,000, eligible 2026)
- 90% x $1,286 = $1,157.40
- 32% x ($5,000 - $1,286) = 32% x $3,714 = $1,188.48
- 15% tier: $0 (AIME below $7,749)
- PIA = $2,345.88 -> truncated to $2,345.80 a month at 67.
- At 62: 70% x $2,345.80 = $1,642.06. At 70: 124% x $2,345.80 = $2,908.79 (before COLAs).
- If only 78% were payable (OASI projection after depletion): 78% x $2,345.80 = $1,829.72; 22% less = $516.08 less a month.
- Replacement: $2,345.80 / $5,000 = 46.9% of average indexed monthly earnings.

## History
- Aug 14, 1935: FDR signs the Social Security Act. Quote (exact excerpt of signing statement): "We can never insure one hundred percent of the population against one hundred percent of the hazards and vicissitudes of life" (FDR Library; American Rhetoric; SSA).
- Jan 31, 1940: first monthly check, $22.54, to Ida May Fuller of Ludlow, Vermont. She paid $24.75 in Social Security taxes over about 3 years and collected $22,888.92 before she died in 1975 at 100. (SSA history; Wikipedia; History.com)
- 1975: automatic COLAs begin.
- 1983: OASI fund was projected to be depleted in late 1982; interfund loans kept benefits flowing until the 1983 amendments (April 1983), based on the Greenspan Commission: up to half of benefits taxable for higher-income beneficiaries, retirement age raised gradually 65 -> 67, scheduled payroll tax increases moved earlier, new federal employees covered. (CRS R47040; SSA history)
- Law: trust funds have no borrowing authority; SSA can't pay more than the fund holds plus incoming revenue (CRS RL33514; CRFB).

## Surprising fact
"Running out" doesn't mean zero: even after OASI depletion in 2032, incoming taxes would still cover about 78% of scheduled benefits (Trustees' projection).
