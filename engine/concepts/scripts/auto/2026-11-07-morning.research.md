## Facts (Federal Reserve G.19 Consumer Credit, September 2026 data, released Friday Nov 6, 2026)
- Total consumer credit rose about $27.84 billion in September 2026 (August revised: +$8.19 billion). [GuruFocus, Investing.com, usmuslims/press coverage]
- Total grew at a 6.4% seasonally adjusted annual rate in September, up from 1.9% in August; total outstanding about $5.224 trillion. [PYMNTS, Nov 2026]
- Revolving credit (mostly credit cards): +14.5% annualized in September vs -4.4% annualized in August; balance $1.369 trillion. [PYMNTS; Investing.com/GuruFocus]
- Revolving is about a quarter of outstanding consumer credit but accounted for more than half of September's increase (approx. +$16.3B revolving, +$11.5B nonrevolving). [PYMNTS; other coverage]
- Nonrevolving: +3.6% annualized in Sept, from 4.1% Aug, 4.7% July. [PYMNTS]
- Q3 2026 (Fed G.19, Nov 6): average credit card interest rate on accounts assessed interest 22.36% (Q2: 22.15%); all accounts 21.19% (Q2: 20.94%). [Fed G.19 via LendingTree, WalletHub]
- Context: July 2026 revolving grew 2.5% annualized (ABA Banking Journal, Sept 2026). LendingTree notes the Q3 rate rise came before the Fed's September quarter-point hike.

## Our math
- $1,000 balance carried a full year at 22.36% simple interest = $223.60 (about $224). Ignores compounding and payments; illustration only.

## Chartable series

## What it means for a normal person
- Card balances are growing fast again, at a time when carrying a balance costs about 22% a year. Paying the statement balance in full avoids interest; carrying a balance does not.
