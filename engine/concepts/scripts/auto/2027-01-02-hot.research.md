# Research brief: 2027-01-02-hot (Hot Take, Money Myth)
Topic: "If your home went up in value, you made that much money."
Researched Tuesday, Nov 3, 2026. Posts Sat Jan 2, 2027. Evergreen: every number is dated.

## Angle
Myth: a higher home value = that much profit. Fact: a paper gain is not profit. Selling costs, mortgage interest, upkeep/taxes/insurance and inflation all come out of it. Neutral: no buy/sell advice, no price predictions. (Fair caveat: renters also pay for housing, so this is not "owning is bad"; it is "the price change is not your profit".)
No specific timely news hook needed; evergreen. Context: NAR says 2025 had record-high home prices.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. Home prices did rise a lot. S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller U.S. National Home Price Index (NSA, annual average, via FRED CSUSHPINSA):
   2016 180.890 | 2017 191.354 | 2018 202.425 | 2019 209.397 | 2020 222.058 | 2021 259.957 | 2022 298.308 | 2023 305.717 | 2024 321.325 | 2025 328.497
   2016 -> 2025: 328.497 / 180.890 = 1.816 -> up about 82% (81.6%). [FRED/S&P, retrieved Nov 3, 2026]
2. Inflation over the same span. CPI-U (CPIAUCNS, annual average): 2016 240.007 -> 2025 321.943 = up 34.1%.
   $300,000 in 2016 = 300,000 x 1.34139 = about $402,400 in 2025 dollars (our math). [BLS via FRED]
3. Selling costs: Redfin says sellers typically pay about 6% to 10% of the sale price in closing costs including agent commissions, transfer taxes and title fees. Bankrate/CoreLogic ClosingCorp: non-commission seller closing costs average 1.81% of sale price. [Redfin blog; Bankrate]
4. Commissions after the NAR settlement (rules took effect Aug 2024): Redfin says average buyer's agent commission was 2.42% in Q3 2025 (vs 2.36% a year earlier), 2.43% in Q2 2025; most sellers still pay the buyer's agent. Listing-side average about 2.88% (Clever survey, Feb 2026, third party). [Redfin press releases May 16, 2025 and Dec 8, 2025; HousingWire]
5. Mortgage rates: Freddie Mac 30-year fixed average (MORTGAGE30US, annual average): 2016 3.65%; 2022 5.34%; 2023 6.81%; 2024 6.72%; 2025 6.60%; 2026 YTD 6.52%. [Freddie Mac PMMS via FRED]
6. Maintenance: Fannie Mae rule of thumb: budget 1% to 4% of home value per year for maintenance, repairs and replacements (example: $3,500/yr on a $350,000 newer home). Angi data via NerdWallet: actual spending often 0.5%-1%. Rule of thumb, not a measured average. [Fannie Mae yourhome.fanniemae.com; NerdWallet]
7. Capital gains exclusion: IRS Publication 523: exclude up to $250,000 of gain on sale of a main home ($500,000 married filing jointly) if ownership and use tests are met (owned and lived in 2 of last 5 years) and no exclusion in prior 2 years. [IRS Pub 523]
8. NAR annual median existing-home price: 2023 $389,300; 2024 $407,600; 2025 $414,300 (NAR breakouts table, May 2026). Monthly: June 2026 $442,800; Sep 2026 $417,500 (FRED HOSMEDUSM052N, updated Oct 13, 2026). NAR's Yun called 2025 a year of "record-high home prices".

## Worked example (our math, hypothetical round numbers)
- Bought 2016 for $300,000 with 20% down ($60,000), loan $240,000, 30-year fixed at 3.65% (2016 average rate).
- Worth $450,000 in 2026. Paper gain: 450,000 - 300,000 = $150,000.
- Selling costs at the low end, 6%: 0.06 x 450,000 = $27,000.
- Monthly payment (P&I): 240,000 x r / (1 - (1+r)^-360), r = 0.0365/12 -> $1,097.90.
- Interest paid in first 120 months: $78,563 (about $78,600); remaining balance about $186,814.
- 150,000 - 27,000 - 78,600 = $44,400 left (our math) before property taxes, insurance and repairs.
- Upkeep at Fannie Mae's 1% low end on $300,000-$450,000 = $3,000-$4,500 a year, roughly $30,000+ over 10 years (illustrative).
- Inflation: $300,000 of 2016 money = about $402,000 in 2025 dollars (CPI +34%).

## The surprising fact
In our example, the $150,000 "gain" shrinks to about $44,400 after just two costs: selling at 6% and ten years of interest at a 3.65% rate. And that is before taxes, insurance, repairs or inflation.
