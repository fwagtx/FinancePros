## Facts (all released Nov 17, 2026 unless noted)
- NAHB/Wells Fargo HMI fell 4 points to 25 in November 2026 (October: 29). Lowest since April 2012 (NAHB; Reuters calls it a "more than 14-1/2-year low"; CNBC "14-year low").
- Economists (Econoday / Reuters poll) expected it unchanged at 29.
- Any reading below 50 means more builders view conditions as poor than good (NAHB definition).
- November 2025 reading: 38 (NAHB release Nov 2025).
- 46% of builders cut prices in November — highest share in the post-Covid era (NAHB). Average price reduction: 6%, unchanged for an 8th straight month.
- 68% of builders used sales incentives — also a post-Covid high; 20th straight month at or above 60%.
- Components: current sales conditions -6 to 28; sales expectations (next 6 months) -2 to 30; prospective buyer traffic -2 to 16 (lowest in 15 years excluding the 2020 pandemic onset, per Reuters/CNBC).
- Regions (3-month moving averages): Northeast 34 (-4), Midwest 35 (-5), South 27 (-3), West 23 (-3).
- NAHB chief economist Robert Dietz (per summaries of release): "Mortgage rates above 7%, softer consumer confidence and an uncertain economic outlook are holding buyers back." Also cited: tariff-driven material costs, construction labor shortages (immigration enforcement), regulation. Quote wording NOT verified against primary text (cnbc.com / nahb.org not fetchable from this session) — do not use as on-screen quote.
- Mortgage rate: 30-year fixed average 7.51% on Monday Nov 16, 2026 per Mortgage News Daily (cited by CNBC); 6.38% a year earlier.

## What it means for a normal person
- New-home buyers: builders are competing for few buyers — nearly half cutting prices (avg 6%) and about two in three offering incentives (e.g. rate buydowns, closing costs). Leverage for buyers of new homes is the highest since Covid.
- But financing is pricier: 30-year rates ~7.5% vs ~6.4% a year ago, which is why traffic is so thin.
- Watch next: housing starts/permits (Census), existing-home sales (NAR), mortgage rates, Fed path.
