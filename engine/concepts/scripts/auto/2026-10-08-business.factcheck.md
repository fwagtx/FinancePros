## Issues

1. **Line 5, misleading framing (must fix).** The line reads: "With no reserve requirement since 2020, banks can **also** lend that cash out."
   - "Also" suggests the same $5,000 can earn 3.9% at the Fed and be lent out at the same time. That double-counts: a dollar kept as reserves is not lent out.
   - It also suggests the 0% requirement is what lets banks lend deposits. Before 2020 the requirement was at most 10% on transaction accounts, so banks could already lend most deposits. Today's banks are limited by capital and liquidity rules, not reserve ratios.
   - Fix: "Or they can lend it out instead, and since 2020 there's no reserve requirement at all." A simpler option: "Or lend it out at even higher rates." Keep the board as "Reserve requirement: 0% since 2020."

2. **Lines 1, 3 and 4, and the cover: the 55x fairness caveat (should fix).**
   - The comparison is gross rate against gross rate. It is not the bank's profit. The bank pays FDIC assessments, branch and operating costs, and in practice does not park all deposits at the Fed.
   - The 0.07% is the average for *interest* checking. Many checking accounts pay 0%, so for them the multiple is effectively infinite.
   - The hedge "can earn" keeps line 1 true. Line 4 ("the bank gets $195") needs the same hedge.
   - Fix for line 4: "At the Fed, the bank could get $195, before its costs." Or add a short on-screen sub on line 4: "before bank costs".

3. **Hook (line 1): acceptable, could be stronger.** It is true, specific and about 3 seconds long (the spoken "fifty-five" pushes it close to the limit). It tells the viewer the answer up front but not the fresh news.
   - Stronger version: "The Fed just raised what it pays your bank to 3.9%. Your checking? 0.07%." This leads with the Sept 16 hike, which is timely and concrete.
   - Shorter version: "Your checking pays 0.07%. The Fed pays your bank 3.9%."

4. **Line 4 narration, awkward read-aloud (nit).** "You get three fifty" can be heard as $350. Fix: "you get three dollars and fifty cents" or "about three bucks".

5. **Line 6 narration (nit).** "Six hundred twenty point four billion dollars" is a mouthful. You could say "over six hundred twenty billion dollars" and keep $620.4B on screen. That is still consistent, but the spoken and on-screen values would differ slightly. If you want them to match exactly, keep the current wording.

6. **Line 9 (nit).** "One of the cheapest loans it can get" is a fair characterization. You could add "it can get" framing to make clear it is a metaphor. No change is required.

7. **Cover and title payoff: OK.** The cover "Your Bank Earns 55x Your Rate" is paid off by lines 1-4. It is slightly stronger than the hedged narration ("can earn"). Consider "Your Bank Can Earn 55x Your Rate".

## Verdict

All numbers and chart points are verified and the math is correct. Line 5's "also lend that cash out" is a misleading factual framing: it double-counts the same money and misstates what the 0% reserve requirement changed. Fix it, and add the "before costs" hedge on line 4, before posting.

VERDICT: FAIL
