# Research brief: Stocks vs bonds, explained with acorns (posts Oct 17, 2026)

## Definitions (SEC Investor.gov)
- Stock: a share of ownership in a company. Value rises and falls with the company and the market. No promised return.
- Bond: a loan to a company or government. The issuer pays interest and promises to repay the principal at maturity. Bond prices fall when interest rates rise.

## Crash-year comparison (Damodaran, NYU Stern, "Annual Returns on Stock, T.Bonds and T.Bills", Jan 2026 update)
- 2008: S&P 500 (with dividends) -36.55%; 10-year US Treasury bond +20.10%.
- 2022: S&P 500 -18.04%; 10-year US Treasury bond -17.83%. SURPRISING FACT: both fell almost the same amount, because rates rose fast (2022 was the worst bond year in decades; Motley Fool: worst since 1937 for Treasuries).

## Worked example (2008 returns, 100 acorns, 60/40 split)
- Stocks: 60 x (1 - 0.3655) = 60 x 0.6345 = 38.07
- Bonds: 40 x (1 + 0.2010) = 40 x 1.2010 = 48.04
- Total: 86.11 of 100 -> down about 14%, vs down about 37% for all stocks.
