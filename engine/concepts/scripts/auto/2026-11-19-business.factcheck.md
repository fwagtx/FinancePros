# Fact-check: 2026-11-19-business (How Duolingo makes money from a free app)

Checked Oct 2, 2026 for a Nov 19, 2026 post. Sources: Duolingo FY2025 Form 10-K and Q4/FY2025 shareholder letter (SEC EDGAR excerpts), Duolingo Q2 2026 shareholder letter, 8-K and 10-Q (quarter ended June 30, 2026), Duolingo FY2021 to FY2023 10-K financials, plus reputable coverage (Zacks/Yahoo Finance, Class Central). Math recomputed in python.

## Verified
- FY2025 revenue $1,037.6M, up 39% from $748.0M (10-K: "+$289.6 million, or 39%"). Recomputed: +38.7%.
- Subscription revenue $873.4M (+44%). Share: 84.2%, so "about 84%" is correct.
- Other revenue $164.1M, made up of advertising $79.725M, Duolingo English Test $42.006M, in-app purchases $40.479M and other $1.937M. Sum check: 873.4 + 79.725 + 42.006 + 40.479 + 1.937 = 1,037.55, which matches $1,037.6M. Ads share is 7.7%, so "about 8%" is correct.
- Revenue series: 2020 $161.7M, 2021 $250.8M, 2022 $369.5M, 2023 $531.1M, 2024 $748.0M, 2025 $1,037.6M. All match the filings.
- Q2 2026: 12.7M paid subscribers (+17%), 140.6M MAUs (+10%), 58.7M DAUs (+23%).
- Paid share: 12.7 / 140.6 = 9.03%. Duolingo counts paid subscribers as unique paying accounts at period end. The count **excludes non-paying family-plan members and free-trial users**. So "about 9% of its monthly users paid" is accurate, and arguably slightly conservative about who has premium access. Two caveats: subscribers are an end-of-period count, while MAUs are a quarterly average; and Duolingo itself describes subscribers as roughly 9% of MAUs. "About" covers both.
- Hypothetical: 1,000,000 x 9% = 90,000; x $60 = $5.4M; 1,000,000 - 90,000 = 910,000. It is clearly labeled hypothetical. The $60/yr figure is lower than US list price (Super annual is about $84 to $96, and FY2025 subscription revenue divided by subscribers implies roughly $80+). That makes it a conservative round number, which is acceptable with a label.
- Tiers: Free (ads, limited hearts/energy), Super (no ads, unlimited hearts), Max (Super plus AI features at a higher price). Accurate.
- Neutrality: no stock tip, no price prediction, no political content. "Not financial advice" is present.
- Spoken and on-screen numbers match, allowing for spoken rounding ($161.7M said as "about 162 million"; $1.04B said as "over a billion"; $79.7M said as "about 80 million").
- Label lengths: all within limits except the cover title (see fix 1). Hook title "A [free] app made $1B" = 21 chars (limit 22). Stat sub = 26. Compare subs = 15 and 14. Board lines: 24 or fewer. List items: 3 words each.
- "Free app" framing is fair, because the app is free to download and use. A small nuance: the Duolingo English Test is a separate paid product, not revenue from the free app itself. Not misleading enough to block the post, but see optional fix 5.
