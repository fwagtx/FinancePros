# 2026-10-13-hot research brief: What happens if your employer underpays your paycheck?

Format: Money Myth / Hot Take. Angle: most people think they just get the missing money back. Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), unpaid minimum wage or overtime can come back DOUBLED (back wages + an equal amount of "liquidated damages").

## Core rules (federal, FLSA; current as of September 2026)
- Remedy: unpaid minimum wages or unpaid overtime "and an additional equal amount as liquidated damages" (29 U.S.C. 216(b)). DOL: the Secretary of Labor may sue for back wages and an equal amount as liquidated damages (dol.gov/general/topic/wages/backpay).
- Employer avoids the doubling only by convincing the court it acted in good faith AND with reasonable grounds to believe it was legal (29 U.S.C. 260; LegalClarity, Patriot Software summaries).
- Court also awards the worker a reasonable attorney's fee and costs, paid by the defendant (29 U.S.C. 216(b)).
- Deadline (statute of limitations): 2 years; 3 years if the violation was willful (29 U.S.C. 255). "Willful" = employer knew, or showed reckless disregard for whether, its conduct violated the FLSA (Supreme Court, McLaughlin v. Richland Shoe, 1988).
- Overtime = 1.5x regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Federal minimum wage $7.25/hour (unchanged since July 2009).
- Retaliation: FLSA section 15(a)(3) makes it unlawful to fire or discriminate against an employee for filing a complaint (oral or written complaints count, Kasten 2011). Remedies include reinstatement, back pay, equal liquidated damages, attorney's fees. DOL WHD complaints are confidential (DOL retaliation fact sheet).
- State laws can add more (not used in script; varies by state).

## Worked example (hypothetical)
- Pay $20/hour. Overtime rate = 1.5 x $20 = $30/hour.
- 5 overtime hours a week unpaid: 5 x $30 = $150/week.
- 52 weeks: $150 x 52 = $7,800 unpaid.
- Plus equal liquidated damages: $7,800 x 2 = $15,600 total (before attorney fees).

## The surprising fact
Most people think a wage claim just gets you what you were owed. Federal law's default is DOUBLE (back pay + equal liquidated damages), and the employer pays the worker's attorney's fees.

## Other context
- EPI (May 2017, Cooper & Kroeger): minimum wage violations alone cost workers an estimated $15 billion a year; affected workers lost ~$64/week, nearly a quarter of weekly earnings. (Old estimate; not used.)
- DOL Workers Owed Wages database: >222,000 workers can claim >$161.4M in unclaimed back wages; DOL holds for 3 years then sends to Treasury (Capitol News Illinois; date of figure unclear - not used).
