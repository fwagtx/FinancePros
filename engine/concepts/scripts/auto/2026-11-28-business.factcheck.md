# Fact-check: 2026-11-28-business ("Who Pays for Your Rewards?")

Checked 2026-10-02 for posting on Sat 2026-11-28. Only the raw.json was read; the research.md was not. Web search was used for every claim, and the math was recomputed in python.

## Verified OK
- **2025 Nilson total, $198.25B.** US merchants paid $198.25B in credit and debit processing fees in 2025, a record and up 5.9% from 2024. Source: Merchants Payments Coalition citing the Nilson Report. 198.25/187.20 = +5.9%, which checks out.
- **2022 ($160.70B), 2023 ($172.05B) and 2024 ($187.20B).** All match the Nilson Report and its GlobeNewswire releases.
- **2.36% average Visa/Mastercard credit swipe fee, 2025.** Confirmed by the Merchants Payments Coalition (up from 2.02% in 2010). $100 x 2.36% = $2.36, and 2% of $100 = $2.00, so the math is correct and the example is labeled as an example.
- **Amex FY2025.** Discount revenue was $37,401M and card member rewards expense was $18,409M (FY2025 10-K / ARS), which round to $37.4B and $18.4B.
- **Boston Fed 2010 study (Schuh, Shy, Stavins, PPDP 10-3).** Each cash-using household pays $149 a year to card-using households, and each card-using household receives $1,133 a year. The $149 is correct and is framed as "estimated".
- **"Biggest slice goes to the issuing bank."** This is supportable. Interchange goes to the issuer and is typically 70-90% of the merchant discount rate, the largest component.
- **Settlement facts.** On June 9, 2026, Judge Brian Cogan (E.D.N.Y.) preliminarily approved the revised $38B Visa/Mastercard settlement. It would let merchants decline premium consumer and commercial cards. Reuters, via US News and WTAQ syndication, ran "US judge OKs Visa, Mastercard $38 billion swipe fee settlement".
- **Compare values are all 7 characters or fewer** ($2.36, $2.00, $37.4B, $18.4B). No keep:true flags. No advice, price predictions or political opinion. The "Not financial advice" disclaimer is present.

## Issues

### 1. WRONG DATA: line 2 chart 2021 point and the text "up from $126B in 2021" (FAIL)
Nilson's **2021 total was $137.83B**. $126.35B is Nilson's **2022 credit-card-only** figure (up 20.2% from 2021), not the 2021 all-card total. That makes the chart mix series and overstate growth.
- Fix the chart `points`: `[[2021, 137.83], [2022, 160.7], [2023, 172.05], [2024, 187.2], [2025, 198.25]]`
- Fix the text (see the full replacement in issue 3).

### 2. STALE / WILL-BE-WRONG NEWS: line 8 settlement (FAIL)
The **final approval and fairness hearing is set for November 16, 2026**, 12 days before posting. On top of that, 978 merchants and trade groups filed objections on Sep 10. By Nov 28 the judge may already have granted or rejected final approval.

The present-tense "letting stores refuse" also states a right that merchants do not have yet. The headline "Judge preliminarily OKs Visa, Mastercard swipe fee deal" is shown as a Reuters headline but is a paraphrase, not word for word.

Replacement for line 8, which stays true whatever happens on Nov 16 as long as there is no reversal:
- text: `A Visa/Mastercard settlement, preliminarily approved in June 2026, would let stores decline some premium cards.`
- say: `A Visa and Mastercard settlement, preliminarily approved in June twenty twenty-six, would let stores turn down some premium cards.`
- scene: `{"t": "news", "headline": "US judge OKs Visa, Mastercard $38 billion swipe fee settlement", "source": "Reuters, June 9, 2026", "mood": "think"}`

**Required before posting:** re-check the docket on Nov 27. If final approval was granted or denied after the Nov 16 hearing, rewrite the line to state that outcome with its date.

### 3. HOOK: too long, and the $198B is misframed (FAIL-contributing)
- **Length.** "Your card rewards aren't free. Stores paid a hundred ninety-eight billion dollars." is 12 words and about 20 syllables, roughly 4-5 s. That is over the 3 s limit.
- **Framing.** Paired with "rewards aren't free", it implies stores paid $198B for rewards. The $198B is all processing fees: credit plus debit (credit alone was $157.8B), including network and processor fees. Only part of it funds rewards.
- **Mismatch.** The caption says "US stores ... in card fees in 2025" but the voice omits the year and "card fees".

Replacement for line 1 (10 words, about 2.5 s; a question that makes the viewer need the answer):
- text: `Who really pays for your card rewards? Start with the stores.`
- say: `Who really pays for your card rewards? Start with the stores.`
- scene: `{"t": "hook", "title": "Who pays? [Stores]", "prop": "card", "label": "2025", "value": "$198B", "mood": "shock"}`

Line 2 then has to speak the number itself, because "That's what..." no longer has anything to refer to:
- text: `US merchants paid $198B in card processing fees in 2025, per the Nilson Report, up from $138B in 2021.`
- say: `In twenty twenty-five, US merchants paid a hundred ninety-eight billion dollars in card fees, says the Nilson Report.`
- scene: same as now, but with the corrected 2021 point (issue 1).

### 4. OVERCLAIM: line 9 "rewards are recycled swipe fees"

- say: `So swipe fees help fund your rewards, and stores build that cost into prices.`
