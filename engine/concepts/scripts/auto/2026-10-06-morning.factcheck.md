## Issues
1. **Nominal vs inflation-adjusted (lines 0, 2; header, cover, post). MUST FIX.** Every price is nominal and nothing in the script says so. CPI-U annual average went from 258.811 (2020) to 321.943 (2025), up 24.4%. In real terms, home electricity rose about 5.8% (1.316/1.244). Electricity still rose faster than general prices, but a bare "up 32%" suggests a much larger real jump than happened. Fix: add one spoken and captioned clause, e.g. after line 2: say "That's faster than overall inflation, which was about twenty-four percent over the same stretch." Caption: "Before inflation. Overall prices (CPI) rose about 24% from 2020 to 2025." Alternatively, put "(not adjusted for inflation)" in the line 2 caption and in the post ig/yt_desc.
2. **Line 5 implies data centers raised prices.** "Part of the story is demand" directly follows the price lines, which tells viewers that demand from data centers and factories is why prices rose. EIA's statement is about consumption, not prices. Fix: say "Separately, the EIA expects record power use this year, driven by data centers and factories." Keep the caption as is.
3. **Header says "Your power bill up 32%" (header field).** The 32% is the price per kWh, not the bill. Bills depend on usage, and line 6 makes that point itself. Fix: "Home power prices\nup 32% since 2020".
4. **Hook (line 0), judged.** It's true, specific, about 10 spoken words (~3 s), and works. "Since 2020" with a 2025 endpoint is fine; the 2026 forecast is higher (+38%), so the claim is if anything conservative. To make it stronger and more personal, change it to "Your electricity costs a third more per unit than in twenty twenty." Or keep it, but add "before inflation" in line 2 per issue 1, not in the hook. OK as is.
5. **Line 2 spoken rounding (minor).** Saying "about seventeen" while the screen shows 17.3 is acceptable. "Seventeen point three" would match the screen exactly and support the 32% math. Optional.
6. **Line 3 assumption (minor).** The example holds usage constant at 1,000 kWh and uses the all-in average price. Consider a caption tweak: "Example, same 1,000 kWh/month at the US average price: about $132 in 2020, $173 in 2025."
7. **Line 6 (minor).** Not advice, and not instruction-style. "Price: set for you" is broadly true, but customers in retail-choice states (e.g., Texas) do pick a plan or rate. Fine for a short. The "happy" mood is slightly odd for the topic. Nit.
8. **Time words.** "Today" (lines 1, post) is correct on Oct 6. Later viewers will see stale wording, which is acceptable for a dated news short; the post explicitly says Oct 6, 2026. Line 4 says "the September forecast". It's correct at posting, but the October STEO will replace it about an hour later. Acceptable, since it's labeled "Sep 2026".
9. No political opinion, no price prediction by the channel (the forecast is attributed to EIA), no advice. The cover "Power Prices Up 32%" is paid off.

## Summary
The facts and math all check out. The one material problem is the missing nominal/inflation context on a headline percentage (issue 1). Issue 2's implied causation and issue 3's header wording also need a small fix. All three fixes are one sentence each.
