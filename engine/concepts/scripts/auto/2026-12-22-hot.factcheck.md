- **lines[1] and [2], CFPB figures: correct.** The CFPB's June 2025 "Technical Correction and Update to the CFPB's Credit Invisibles Estimate" gives 2.7% (7.0M) credit-invisible adults in Dec 2020. It revises Dec 2010 to 5.8% (13.5M), down from the original 11.0% (25.9M). "No credit file" is accurate wording. The 2010 and 2020 figures are both corrected estimates, so comparing them is fair. Math: 5.8% to 2.7% is a drop. tone "up" on the 2.7% side is fine because fewer invisibles is good news.
- **lines[4], secured card: correct.** The deposit "usually" or "typically" equals the limit (Citi, Experian, TransUnion). Some issuers accept a smaller deposit, so "usually" is the right hedge. The board labels it "Example:", which counts as a labeled hypothetical.
- **lines[5], 30% utilization: correct as attributed.** Experian says "30% is the point at which it starts to have a more pronounced negative effect" and that "there's no specific point" where utilization goes from good to bad. The line is framed as an "Experian guideline", so the attribution is OK. Math: 0.30 x $200 = $60. The on-screen value matches the spoken one.
- **lines[6], myFICO: correct.** A score needs at least one account open 6+ months, at least one account reported within the past 6 months, and no deceased flag. The timeline "when" values (Day 1 / Mo 6 / Mo 6+) are 4-5 characters.
- **lines[7], CARD Act / Reg Z 1026.51(b)(1): legally correct but incomplete.** Applicants under 21 need an independent ability to pay (income **or assets**) or a cosigner/joint applicant who is 21 or older. Most major issuers no longer accept co-signers, so a viewer who hears "or a co-signer" may assume that route is generally open. Fix below.
