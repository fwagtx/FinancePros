# Research brief: Scam alert, fake rebate and cashback apps (posts Nov 7, 2026)

## How it works
- Fake "cashback/rebate/rewards" apps or sites (often pushed via social media ads or texts) promise big rewards; the app shows a growing balance, sometimes pays a small amount first, then demands a "processing", "activation", "verification" or "withdrawal" fee (or tax) before you can cash out. Some ask you to link a card or log in to your bank.
- BBB: you should never have to pay a fee to use a cashback app or to receive a payout; a legitimate reward doesn't require upfront payment. Similar BBB warnings about fake gas/grocery vouchers collecting "processing, activation or shipping fees".
- FTC: reward-points-expiring texts are often scams; if someone says you must pay via bank transfer, wire, crypto, payment app or gift card to get money, that's a scam. Report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
- FTC Data Spotlight (Dec 2024) on "paying to get paid" task scams: small early payouts build trust, then victims deposit money to "unlock" earnings; task-scam reports went from 0 (2020) to 5,000 (2023) to ~20,000 in H1 2024.

## Numbers
- FTC 2025: "website or app" was the top contact method among fraud reports with a loss (31%); social media 28%; social media losses $2.1B in 2025.
- FTC total reported fraud losses: 2022 $8.8B | 2023 $10.0B | 2024 $12.5B | 2025 $15.9B (chartable).

## Worked example (hypothetical)
App shows a $480 "cashback balance" on $500 of shopping = 96% back; it asks for a $49.99 "withdrawal fee", then a $99 "tax": you pay $148.99 and get $0. A real 2% cashback on $500 = $10.
