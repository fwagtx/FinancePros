# Fact-check: 2026-11-07-business (posts Nov 7, 2026; checked Oct 1, 2026)

Scope: script JSON only (lines, scenes, cover, post). Official pages (globenewswire, sec.gov, stocktitan) were blocked from direct fetch, so the checks rely on search excerpts of the company releases plus Investing.com, Hawaii Public Radio and Hoodline.

## Verified
- **L2 (Marks Garage sale)**: Mobile Infrastructure (BEEP) announced on Apr 24, 2026 that it completed the sale of Marks Garage, a 308-stall parking facility in Honolulu, for $16.5M. Proceeds paid down $8.1M of CMBS principal and $4.5M of its Preferred Line of Credit. Sources: GlobeNewswire/Investing.com/Sahm Capital, Apr 24, 2026. The script correctly calls it a "garage".
- **Hook math**: 16,500,000 / 308 = $53,571.43. "Over $53,000" and "$53,571" are correct.
- **L3 (~2% cap rate)**: the company's own figure, from the Apr 24 release and the Q1/Q2 2026 results: "weighted average implied capitalization rate on disposed assets is approximately 2% based on parking net operating income". It covers all assets sold so far under the 36-month, $100M asset rotation program (cumulative proceeds over $30M, about $33M by Q2), not Marks Garage alone. "Its sales" is attributed and mostly fair.
- **L4**: 2% cap rate = $2 of NOI a year per $100 of price. Correct.
- **L5 wording**: the company said the assets were "sold at prices reflecting their value to buyers for alternative uses including development, corporate campuses, and strategic land positions." The script gives an attributed paraphrase without quote marks, so this is acceptable as wording. See issue 1 for the framing.
- **L7 math**: $2M × 2% = $40,000. ($2M + $20M) × 2% = $440,000. The "$22M assessed" sub-label is consistent. Spoken and on-screen values match. The example is labeled.
- **L8 (22%)**: the Parking Reform Network Parking Lot Map (2023) found that on average about 22% of land in the central cities of US metros with more than 1 million people is dedicated to parking (Strong Towns, Sept 2023; The Hill; Scientific American).
- **L8 ("Most US property taxes hit buildings too")**: supported by the Lincoln Institute: the traditional US property tax applies the same rate to land and improvements, and Pennsylvania is the only state that enables split-rate taxation. The post credits the Lincoln Institute.
- **Sub-labels**: all are 28 characters or fewer. "Parking Reform Network, 2023" is exactly 28, so it fits with no margin.
- No advice, price predictions or political opinion. "Not financial advice" is in the post.

## Issues
1. **L5 + post IG/YT: misleading for the hook asset (substantive).** Marks Garage's buyer was Bank of Hawaii, which paid $16.5M to use the 308-space garage for **employee parking** (Hawaii Public Radio, May 22, 2026; Hoodline, May 2026). Coming straight after the Honolulu sale, "So what are buyers buying? Development sites, corporate campuses and strategic land" implies the Marks buyer was making a land bet, which is the opposite of what is reported. The YT description is worse: "priced at about a 2% cap rate on parking profit because buyers wanted the land."
   Fix L5: "Across its sales, the company says buyers valued sites for other uses: development, corporate campuses, strategic land." Optionally add: "Honolulu's buyer, Bank of Hawaii, wanted employee parking."
   Fix YT: drop "because buyers wanted the land" and say "...priced at about a 2% cap rate on parking profit across its sales so far."
   Fix the list title: "What buyers wanted" → "Uses buyers valued (co. says)".
2. **L3: the 2% could be read as Marks Garage's own cap rate.** Make the scope explicit. Text/say: "The company says its asset sales so far averaged about a 2% cap rate on parking profit." The stat label is fine.
3. **L6 ("taxpayer"): term misapplied.** In US real estate, a "taxpayer" is historically a small one- or two-story *building* put up to cover the land's property tax while the owner waits to redevelop. It proliferated in Depression-era NYC and was later displaced by strip malls (Wikipedia, "Taxpayer (building)"). It is not a standard name for a parking lot or garage, so "Real estate calls this a taxpayer" overstates.
   Fix: "Old real estate slang for a cheap placeholder building is a 'taxpayer': it covers the tax while the land waits. Parking can play the same role." Shorter option: "Parking can work like what real estate once called a 'taxpayer': a cheap use that covers the tax while the land waits."
4. **L7–L8: the lot-versus-garage logic doesn't match the hook.** The example and "a lot is a cheap way to wait" describe surface lots, and the header says "Why parking lots are land bets". Marks Garage is a multi-level structure that also has retail/arts space (The ARTS at Marks Garage). Its building is taxed, and its buyer is keeping it as parking. Viewers may think the Honolulu asset is a lot.
   Fix: change the header to "Why parking can be a land bet". In L8, say "a surface lot is a cheap way to wait", or add a bridge line: "Not every sale is a land play, but for surface lots..."
5. **Hook per-stall framing (minor).** $53,571 is the total price divided by stalls. That price also buys the land, the structure and the ground-floor retail space, so "pay over $53,000 for one parking spot" is a simplification. It's acceptable as a hook but should be labeled once. The post title already says "per space". Optional: the hook scene value "1 spot" → "per space". The hook itself is strong (true, specific, under 3 seconds).
6. **L7 example assumptions (minor).** 2% is a high effective rate for many US cities. Label the assumptions on screen, e.g. title "Example: 2% tax, full value" (21 chars), or in the say line "assuming a two percent tax on full value".
7. **L8: the 22% scope needs a qualifier.** The PRN figure is an *average* across central cities of metros over 1 million. Text/say: "...about 22% of downtown land in big US metros is parking, on average." The stat label is fine.
8. **Timing / staleness.** Mobile Infrastructure has historically reported Q3 results in early-to-mid November, and no date had been announced by Oct 1. Q3 results could come out on or just before Nov 7 with an updated cumulative cap rate. Fix: avoid present tense. "The company said in 2026 its sales so far priced around 2%." Keep "In April 2026", which stays correct.
9. **L2 (minor).** Hoodline says the buyer closed in May 2026, while the company's Apr 24 release says the sale was completed. "In April 2026, Mobile Infrastructure sold" follows the company's release and is acceptable. "Announced the sale" would be the safer verb.
10. **Spoken (minor).** L2 says "sixteen and a half million" with no "dollars". Consider "sixteen point five million dollars".
