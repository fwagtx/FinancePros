## Verified claims (all correct)
- **Social Security wage base:** $184,500 for 2026, up $8,400 from $176,100 (SSA, Oct 2025). The chart's 2020-2024 values (137,700 / 142,800 / 147,000 / 160,200 / 168,600) are correct. The yearly increases are 5,100, 4,200, 13,200, 8,400, 7,500 and 8,400, so 2023 (+$13,200) is the largest. The base has risen every year since 2020 and is indexed to the national average wage index.
- **Maximum Social Security tax:** 6.2% x 184,500 = $11,439. The employer pays a matching 6.2%.
- **Medicare and Additional Medicare Tax:** Medicare is 1.45% with no cap. The extra 0.9% must be withheld once wages from one employer pass $200,000, whatever the filing status. The liability thresholds are $200,000 for single filers and $250,000 for married filing jointly ($125,000 married filing separately). Employee FICA totals 7.65%.
- **2026 standard deduction and brackets:** the single standard deduction is $16,100 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32). The single brackets are 10% up to $12,400, 12% up to $50,400, then 22%.
- **W-4:** the 2020 redesign removed allowances and uses five steps (personal information, multiple jobs, dependents, other adjustments, signature). A W-4 filed before 2020 stays valid until the employee changes it.
- **Box 1 vs Boxes 3 and 5:** traditional 401(k) deferrals are left out of Box 1 but count in Boxes 3 and 5. Section 125 health premiums are left out of all three boxes. An HSA funded through a cafeteria plan (Code W) is left out of Boxes 1, 3 and 5. Code D covers 401(k) elective deferrals. Code AA covers Roth 401(k) money, which stays in Box 1. Code DD is the total cost of employer health coverage, both shares, and is not taxable. Employer matches are not reported.
- **New codes for tax year 2026:** TT (qualified overtime, meaning only the premium half of time-and-a-half), TP and TA (employer contributions to a Trump account, under section 128) are confirmed. The tips and overtime deductions apply to 2025-2028. See issue 1 for TP.
- **W-2 deadline:** Jan 31, 2027 is a Sunday, so 2026 W-2s are due to workers by Feb 1, 2027 (the 2026 W-2/W-3 instructions say the same).
- **Missing or wrong W-2:** the steps are to ask the employer for a W-2c, contact the IRS after the end of February (the IRS sends the employer a letter), and use Form 4852 if the filing deadline arrives first, then amend if needed. This matches the IRS and Nolo.
- **BLS pay frequency:** in February 2023, 43.0% of private establishments paid biweekly (BLS Beyond the Numbers / CES).
- **Texas:** has no state income tax.
- **Roth vs traditional:** described accurately and without a recommendation.

## Issues

### Required (factual)
1. **L68, Code TP is mislabeled.** The final 2026 W-2/W-3 instructions define Code TP as the *total amount of cash tips reported to the employer*, not "qualified tips." Whether tips qualify is decided on the return, together with the occupation code in Box 14b.
   - text: `W-2s for tax year 2026 add new codes: TT for qualified overtime, only the extra half of time-and-a-half, and TP for cash tips reported to the employer.`
   - say: `W-2s for tax year twenty twenty-six add new codes: T T for qualified overtime, only the extra half of time and a half, and T P for cash tips reported to the employer.`
   - scene: unchanged ("TP: tips" is fine).
2. **L87 implies Medicare stops at the cap.** "...of FICA wages, until the cap" reads as covering both taxes, but Medicare has no cap (as L29 and L82 say).
   - text: `Three: Social Security is 6.2% of FICA wages up to the cap, and Medicare is 1.45% with no cap.`
   - say: `Three: Social Security is six point two percent of FICA wages up to the cap, and Medicare is one point four five percent with no cap.`
   - scene: unchanged.

### Minor or optional (do not block)
3. **L3:** BLS counts "private establishments/businesses," not employers. Suggested text: `In February 2023, 43% of private businesses paid every two weeks. That's 26 pay stubs a year.` Suggested say: `In February twenty twenty-three, forty-three percent of private businesses paid every two weeks. That's twenty-six pay stubs a year.`
4. **Payoff of "three wages" (L59):** the example only ever shows two distinct values. Spell out when all three differ. Suggested text: `Boxes 5 and 6 are Medicare wages and tax. Box 5 usually matches Box 3. Above the Social Security cap, all three wages differ.` Suggested say: `Boxes five and six are Medicare wages and tax. Box five usually matches box three. Above the Social Security cap, all three wages differ.` This brings the total to about 1,795 words with issues 1 and 2.
5. **Rounded narration vs exact cents on screen (L14, L45, L50, L51):** the narration rounds with "about" while the screen shows cents, e.g. L45 shows $136.88 / $32.01 and the narration says "about 137 / 32 / 169." This is acceptable, but if exact parity is wanted at L45 with no added words, use say: `Social Security at six point two percent is one thirty-six eighty-eight. Medicare at one point four five percent is thirty-two oh one. Together, one sixty-eight eighty-nine of FICA per check.`
6. **L61:** the per-check rounding means real Box 4 withholding is $3,558.88. Suggested text: `Quick math check: Box 4 should be 6.2% of Box 3. Here, that's about $3,558.80.` Suggested say: `Quick math check: box four should be six point two percent of box three. Here, that's about three thousand five hundred fifty-nine dollars.` Alternatively, leave as is, since L62 already gives the rounding caveat.
7. **L69, naming:** "Trump account" is the statutory name (P.L. 119-21, section 530A), so it is neutral. A short descriptor would help viewers: `...contributions to a Trump account, a new savings account for children.` That adds 6 words, so check the cap.
