## History: the late-1990s telecom / dot-com boom
- After the Telecommunications Act of 1996, telecom companies invested more than $500B, mostly financed with debt, in fiber, switches and wireless networks; capacity outstripped demand (Wikipedia "Telecoms crash"; consistent with Dallas Fed 2002 and FDIC Bank Trends 2002 on heavy telecom debt). MODERATE: $500B is a widely cited estimate.
- Merrill Lynch estimate (cited by Florida Trend): by the time the bubble burst, about 39 million miles of fiber ran under the US, with only about 10% in use. (Other estimates differ: 2.6% to <5% lit -- not used.) WEAK-MODERATE; attribute to Merrill Lynch estimate.
- Global Crossing filed for bankruptcy in January 2002 with $12.4B of debt. WorldCom filed in July 2002, then the largest US bankruptcy, after an ~$11B accounting fraud.
- Afterward the fiber was not ripped out; creditors sold networks for a fraction of cost and buyers (e.g., Level 3) used them; that cheap capacity later carried the internet's growth (analysis, techfinitive / substacks -- framed as commonly made point).
- Andrew Odlyzko (1998) showed internet traffic was roughly doubling once a year, not every 3 months as widely claimed.

## Differences now vs then (analysis, attributed)
- Today's biggest spenders fund much of it from businesses that already make money (Meta Q3 revenue $64.6B, +26%; $82.45B in cash and securities) vs 1990s carriers financed mostly with debt. But borrowing is rising fast (see bond data) and free cash flow has turned negative at Meta (Q3) and Alphabet (Q2).
