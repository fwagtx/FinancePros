# Research brief: OPEC explained: who controls oil prices

## Timely angle (all dated)
- UAE left OPEC and OPEC+ effective May 1, 2026 (announced Apr 28, 2026; WAM, CNBC Apr 28, 2026). UAE was OPEC's 3rd-largest producer (behind Saudi Arabia, Iraq). UAE produced 3.4 million b/d of crude in 2025, with ~4.2 million b/d effective capacity (EIA Today in Energy #67804).
- EIA: OPEC (with UAE) produced ~28.0 million b/d of crude in 2025 = 35% of world crude; without UAE = 31%. OPEC+ = ~46% in 2025; without UAE ~42%. (EIA, Today in Energy, 2026)
- OPEC after UAE exit: 11 members: Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Iran, Kuwait, Venezuela (the five founders), Libya, Algeria, Nigeria, Equatorial Guinea, Congo, Gabon. (Angola left Jan 2024.)
- Iran war: US/Israeli strikes on Iran began late Feb 2026; tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz severely disrupted from early March 2026; US-Iran ceasefire Apr 7, 2026 but shipping disruption continued (CNBC Apr 21, 2026; World Bank blog; CRS R45281). Brent had its biggest monthly rise on record in March 2026 (World Bank).
- IEA Oil Market Report, Sept 2026: global oil production fell 1.6 mb/d m/m to 100.1 mb/d in August 2026; more than 10 mb/d of Gulf output remained shut in.
- EIA STEO, Sept 2026: Brent spot averaged $91/b in August 2026, $7 higher than July; crude shut-ins averaged 6.7 mb/d in August 2026.
- OPEC+ (8 then 7 core members) Aug 2, 2026: +188,000 b/d for September 2026, completing rollback of the 1.65 mb/d voluntary cut from April 2023 (CNBC Aug 2, 2026; OPEC press release Aug 2, 2026).
- OPEC+ Sept 6, 2026 (virtual): Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, Oman kept September 2026 required production for October 2026; next meeting Oct 4, 2026 (OPEC press release Sept 6, 2026; Energy Connects; TASS).
- October 2026 required production (TASS/Nation Thailand, Sept 2026): Saudi Arabia 10.478 mb/d; Russia 9.949; Iraq 4.431; Kuwait 2.676; Kazakhstan 1.628; Algeria 1.007; Oman 0.841.
- Saudi Arabia told OPEC (direct communication, OPEC MOMR Sept 2026) it produced 6.238 mb/d in August 2026, down 1.897 mb/d on the month, lowest since 1990 (Bloomberg Sept 10, 2026; Al-Monitor Sept 2026). Secondary-sources estimate for Saudi August: 7.276 mb/d (OPEC MOMR Sept 2026). Script uses the direct-communication figure and labels it.

## Worked example (exact math)
- Saudi quota vs output, August 2026: OPEC+ July 5, 2026 meeting set Saudi August 2026 required production at 10.416 mb/d (+62,000 b/d; Worldoil Jul 5, 2026; Interfax; Saudi Gazette). Sept/Oct 2026 target 10.478.
  Saudi told OPEC it produced 6.238 mb/d in August 2026.
  Gap: 10.416 - 6.238 = 4.178 mb/d below target. 4.178 / 10.416 = 40.1% below. 6.238 / 10.416 = 59.9% (about 60%) of target.
- Barrel math: 1 barrel = 42 gallons (EIA). A $10 move in crude per barrel = $10 / 42 = $0.238 = about 24 cents per gallon of crude cost (not the full pump price).
- 2020 cut: 9.7 mb/d out of roughly 100 mb/d world demand (pre-pandemic) ~ about a tenth (approximate; script says "roughly a tenth").

## History
- Founded Sept 14, 1960, Baghdad conference, by Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, Venezuela. Secretariat in Geneva, moved to Vienna in 1965 (OPEC Brief History).
- Oct 1973: Arab members of OPEC (OAPEC) imposed oil embargo on the US and others over support for Israel in the October war. By end of embargo in March 1974, oil price rose from about $3/barrel to nearly $12 (US State Dept Office of the Historian; History.com). Note: embargo was OAPEC (Arab producers), not every OPEC member.
- Quota system: OPEC formally adopted a production ceiling of 17.5 mb/d in March 1983 (London extraordinary meeting); earlier attempt March 1982 (Wikipedia chronology; CSMonitor 1982).
- 1985-86: Saudi Arabia, tired of cutting while others overproduced (Saudi output ~2.4 mb/d Aug 1985 from >10 mb/d in 1980), abandoned role as swing producer. Spot prices briefly fell below $10 in first week of April 1986, one-third of the ~$30 of Nov 1985 (FRBSF Weekly Letter, June 6, 1986).
- 2014-16: Brent peaked ~$115 June 2014. Nov 27, 2014 OPEC kept output ceiling unchanged (Washington Post, CNBC). Brent fell as low as ~$27 in Jan 2016. Naimi (Saudi oil minister) MEES interview Dec 2014: "it is not in the interest of OPEC producers to cut their production, whatever the price is." US crude production rose from 5.0 mb/d (2008) to 9.4 mb/d (2015) (EIA).
- Dec 10, 2016: Declaration of Cooperation, 13 OPEC + 11 non-OPEC producers led by Russia = OPEC+ (OPEC Bulletin 2017).
- March 2020: Saudi-Russia price war. Apr 12, 2020: OPEC+ agreed record 9.7 mb/d cut for May-June 2020 (CNBC Apr 12, 2020). Apr 20, 2020: NYMEX WTI May contract settled at -$37.63, first negative ever (CFTC interim report).
- 2023: April 2023 voluntary cut 1.65 mb/d; Nov 2023 further 2.2 mb/d voluntary. 2.2 unwound Apr-Sept 2025; 1.65 unwinding began Oct 2025, paused Q1 2026, completed with Sept 2026 increase.

## US shale
- US crude production record 13.6 mb/d in 2025; world's largest producer since 2018 (8th straight year); ~40% higher than Russia or Saudi Arabia; Permian ~48% of US output (EIA Today in Energy #67844).

## Spare capacity
- EIA definition: production that can be brought online within 30 days and sustained for at least 90 days. Historically held almost entirely by OPEC, mostly Saudi Arabia (EIA "What drives crude oil prices: Supply OPEC").

## Benchmarks & futures
- Brent: North Sea blend; ICE Brent futures (London). ICE says ~75% of world's physically traded crude prices off Brent directly or indirectly.
- WTI: US benchmark; NYMEX futures, delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma.
- OPEC sets production targets, not a price; prices set by trading in benchmarks/futures; producers like Saudi Aramco price cargoes relative to benchmarks.

## Surprising fact
- Oil once cost less than nothing: WTI futures settled at minus $37.63 on April 20, 2020 (CFTC).
- And: in Aug 2026 Saudi Arabia, OPEC's leader, pumped ~40% below its target (10.416 vs 6.238), because of the war, not a quota decision.
