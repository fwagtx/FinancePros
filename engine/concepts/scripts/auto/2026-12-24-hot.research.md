# 2026-12-24-hot research brief
Money myth: "Moving to a no-income-tax state always saves you money."
Researched Sunday, October 25, 2026. Posts Thursday, December 24, 2026, 6 PM Central. Evergreen: every number carries its year/source.

## News angle (context only, not a hook)
- Washington's governor signed SB 6346, a 9.9% tax on income above $1 million, on March 30, 2026. It takes effect January 1, 2028 (first payments 2029). A repeal measure (Initiative 645) was on the November 3, 2026 ballot; the outcome is not known at writing, so the script avoids saying anything about Washington after 2026. (Bloomberg Tax; CPA Practice Advisor, Mar 31 2026; Morgan Lewis, Mar 2026.)

## Which states have no income tax (be precise)
- In 2026, NINE states do not tax wage/salary income: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming. (Kiplinger, SoFi "9 states" 2026 lists.)
- New Hampshire: its interest-and-dividends tax was fully repealed effective January 1, 2025 (HB 2, June 2023, sped up the phase-out). (AARP NH tax guide; multiple 2026 lists.)
- Washington: no wage tax, but taxes long-term capital gains: 7% above the exemption (~$278,000 for 2025) plus an extra 2.9% on gains over $1 million (9.9% top rate) from 2025 (SB 5813, signed May 20, 2025). Upheld by the WA Supreme Court in March 2023. Because of that, some outlets (e.g. Motley Fool, Dec 2025) say EIGHT states have no income tax at all.
- Tennessee: Hall tax on interest/dividends ended 2021 (background; not used on screen).
- SCRIPT WORDING: "In 2026, nine states don't tax wages" (true under either count; WA's new millionaires tax doesn't start until 2028).

## Offsetting costs
- Sales tax (Tax Foundation, "State and Local Sales Tax Rates, 2026", as of January 1, 2026): highest average combined state+local rates: Louisiana 10.13%, Tennessee 9.61%, Washington 9.57%, Arkansas 9.48%, Alabama 9.46%. Population-weighted national average 7.53%. -> Tennessee is 2nd-highest, Washington 3rd (both no-wage-tax states).
- Property tax: Tax Foundation (2025 report, 2023 data, via Bankrate and Rocket Mortgage): Texas effective property tax rate on owner-occupied homes 1.36% of home value, 7th-highest. Tax Foundation notes NH and Texas "rely heavily on [property taxes] in lieu of other major tax categories." ## The surprising fact (ITEP "Who Pays?", 7th edition, January 2024; 2024 tax law at 2023 income levels)
- Florida has the most regressive state and local tax system in the US (ITEP Tax Inequality Index).
- Florida: lowest-income 20% pay 13.2% of income in state+local taxes; top 1% pay 2.7%. (Sales/excise: 7.4% vs 1.0%; property: 5.5% vs 1.6%.)
- Ten most regressive (most to least): Florida, Washington, Tennessee, Pennsylvania, Nevada, South Dakota, Texas, Illinois, Arkansas, Louisiana. -> 6 of the 10 (FL, WA, TN, NV, SD, TX) levy no wage income tax.
- National averages: lowest 20% pay 11.4%, middle 20% 10.5%, top 1% 7.2%. 41 states tax the top 1% less than every other group; 44 states' systems worsen inequality.
- ITEP ties regressivity largely to weak or no personal income taxes and heavier reliance on sales and excise taxes.
- No 8th edition found as of October 2026.

## Worked example (clearly labeled illustration, simple math)
- Assumption: a household spends $30,000 a year on taxable purchases.
- At Tennessee's average combined rate 9.61%: 30,000 x 0.0961 = $2,883.
- At the 7.53% national average: 30,000 x 0.0753 = $2,259. Difference: $624 a year.
- Takeaway: whether the move saves money depends on income, home value and spending; higher earners skip more income tax, lower earners feel sales tax more (ITEP).
