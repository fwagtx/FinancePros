# 2027-01-19-hot research brief: What happens if you split rent with a roommate who moves out?
Prepared Nov 20, 2026 for posting Jan 19, 2027. Evergreen; no timely news hook needed.
Format: Money Myth ("I only owe my half") -> fact (joint and several liability).

## Core legal concept (educational, general; laws vary by state and lease)
- Joint and several liability: roommates who sign the same lease are "jointly and severally liable" to the landlord.
  Nolo: "one for all and all for one"; the landlord can seek the full amount of rent from anyone who signed the lease.
  (Nolo, "Legal Risks and Downsides of Sharing an Apartment or House With Roommates"; Nolo, "Renting a House or Apartment With Roommates")
- If one co-tenant stops paying or moves out, the others must still pay the full rent for the rental period, or face
  eviction for nonpayment (Nolo Every Landlord's guide; Nolo "When a Roommate Moves Out"; RentCafe).
- A private roommate agreement does NOT change the tenants' joint and several liability to the landlord; it only
  governs what roommates owe each other (Nolo).
- The departing roommate is not automatically off the hook either: under the lease the landlord can still hold them
  responsible for unpaid rent and damage (FindLaw). Remaining roommate may try to recover the share from the
  departed roommate, e.g. in small claims court (Nolo small claims book; FindLaw "Can I sue a roommate for breaking a lease").
- Eviction for unpaid rent typically applies to all renters on the lease (RentCafe).
- Security deposit: on a joint lease the landlord typically holds one deposit for everyone and generally does not have
  to return any part until the lease ends and all original tenants move out, unless the lease says otherwise;
  roommates usually settle a departing person's share between themselves, often with the replacement roommate paying
  it (Nolo; Apartment Therapy; UNL Student Legal Services; RentecDirect).
- Deductions for damage come out of the shared deposit, whoever caused it (Nolo / roommate guides).
- General info only: check your lease, and your state attorney general or local tenant resources
  (e.g., Massachusetts AG "Guide to Landlord and Tenant Rights").

## The surprising fact
Splitting rent 50/50 between roommates does not split your legal obligation. On a joint lease, each signer can owe 100%.

## Worked example (OUR MATH, hypothetical)
- Rent $2,000/month, split two ways: $2,000 / 2 = $1,000 each.
- Roommate moves out, stops paying: landlord can ask you for the full $2,000. Extra cost to you: $2,000 - $1,000 = $1,000/month.
- 3 months left on the lease: 3 x $1,000 = $3,000 extra (our math).
- Deposit: $2,000 deposit paid $1,000 each; landlord usually holds the whole $2,000 until the lease ends.
