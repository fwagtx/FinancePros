# Research brief: Why Verizon gives away free phones (How They Make Money, posts Fri Dec 11, 2026)

Evergreen: every figure carries its fiscal year / quarter. Latest annual report = Verizon FY2025 Form 10-K (filed Feb 2026). Latest quarterly data checked: Q2 2026 (July 2026).

## Core idea
Verizon sells devices at or below cost (equipment revenue < cost of equipment), then earns it back through monthly wireless service on multi-year promos. "Free" phones are 36 monthly bill credits, typically tied to a pricier unlimited plan, and the credits stop if the customer leaves or pays off the phone early.

## Key numbers (with dates and sources)
- **Wireless equipment revenue, FY2025: about $25.5B ($25,470M)** vs **$23,217M in FY2024**. Cross-check: quarterly releases Q1 2025 $5.4B + Q2 $6.3B + Q3 $5.6B + Q4 $8.2B = $25.5B. (Stock-Analysis-on.net income statement from 10-K; Verizon quarterly earnings releases / 8-K exhibits.)
- **Cost of wireless equipment, FY2025: $28,976M** vs **$26,100M in FY2024** (+11.0%). (Verizon FY2025 10-K consolidated results; also Stock-Analysis-on.net.) Consumer segment cost of wireless equipment FY2025: $23,930M (vs $21,259M) - segment table in 10-K.
- **Gap FY2025: $28,976M - $25,470M = $3,506M (about $3.5B) more spent on devices than device revenue.** FY2024 gap: $26,100M - $23,217M = $2,883M (my calculation). Note equipment = phones and other wireless devices, and 10-K says promotions reduce equipment revenue; some promo cost is also amortized against service revenue, so the true subsidy is not a single line.
- **Total wireless service revenue (Consumer + Business), recast in FY2025 10-K: FY2023 $79.5B, FY2024 $82.1B, FY2025 $83.7B** (Consumer $69,382M + Business $14,321M in 2025; $67,951M + $14,122M in 2024; $65,820M + $13,714M in 2023). Up about 2.0% in 2025. Verizon reclassified device protection revenue into wireless service revenue in Q1 2025, so older pre-recast figures differ.
- **Total operating revenue FY2025: $138.2B** (vs $134.8B FY2024). Consumer segment revenue FY2025 $106.8B (~77% of total).
- **Consumer postpaid ARPA (avg revenue per account, monthly): $147.36 in Q4 2025**, +1.2% y/y; $147.50 in Q2 2025. (Verizon Q4/Q2 2025 earnings releases.)
- **Q4 2025 wireless service revenue $21.0B**, +1.1% y/y; postpaid phone net adds 616,000 (best since 2019).
- **Consumer retail postpaid phone churn (monthly %)**, Verizon 4Q25 Financial & Operating Information (official):
  - Q1 2024 0.83%, Q2 2024 0.79%, Q3 2024 0.83%, Q4 2024 0.88%, Q1 2025 0.90%, Q2 2025 0.90%, Q3 2025 0.91%, Q4 2025 0.95%.
  - Full year: 0.83% (2024), 0.92% (2025). Q1-Q3 2025 values confirmed in Verizon's quarterly press releases; Q4 0.95% confirmed by several outlets.
  - Later: Q2 2026 consumer postpaid phone churn 0.84% (Phonearena/Benton coverage of 2Q26 release). Q1 2026 is conflicting in snippets (0.90% vs 0.97%) - not used.
- Q2 2026 note: total revenue $34.3B, down 0.7% y/y with lower equipment sales; reports say Verizon cut back on phone subsidies in 2026 (secondary, not used on screen).

## Promo mechanics (confirmed by 2+ outlets + Verizon community moderator)
- Free phone = monthly bill credits over **36 months** (Droid-Life, Apr 29 2026: Verizon and AT&T lock in for 36 months; T-Mobile went back to 24 months for phones. WhistleOut: "up to $830 off iPhone 17 ... applied over 36 monthly bill credits").
- Usually needs a new line and/or a premium unlimited plan (WhistleOut: free iPhone 17 requires new line on Unlimited Ultimate; Tom's Guide and Droid-Life: deals push top-tier plans; Clark.com: 36-month commitment to an unlimited plan).
- Credits stop if you cancel service, pay off the device early, or move to an ineligible plan; remaining device balance becomes due (Verizon Community moderator; Money Crashers; SmartPlanet: ending early forfeits remaining credits).
- Plan prices (one line, autopay, mid-2026 listings; sources conflict): Unlimited Welcome $65, Unlimited Plus $80, Unlimited Ultimate $90-$95. So the gap between base and premium is roughly $25-$30/month. Script uses a labeled hypothetical $25/month gap.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- $830 phone credited $23.06/month x 36 = $830.16 (credits cover the phone).
- If the required plan costs $25/month more than the plan a customer would otherwise pick: $25 x 36 = $900.
- $900 > $830.16, so in this example the extra plan cost exceeds the phone credit by $69.84. If the customer would pay for the premium plan anyway, the phone is effectively free. (Money Crashers uses the same method: monthly gap x 36 vs phone price.)

## The surprising fact
Verizon spent about $3.5B more on devices than it collected for them in 2025 - on purpose - because it collected $83.7B in wireless service revenue, and fewer than 1 in 100 consumer phone customers leave in a given month.

## Neutrality
No stock talk, no plan recommendation. Takeaway framed as the math, not an instruction.
