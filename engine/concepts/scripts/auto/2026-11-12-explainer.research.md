# Research brief: How to budget when your income changes every month
Slot 2026-11-12-explainer (posts Thu Nov 12, 2026). Researched Oct 1, 2026.

## Key facts (dated)
- Fed SHED, "Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025" (released May 13, 2026; latest edition as of Nov 2026):
  - In 2025, 30% of adults had income that varied at least occasionally through the year (29% in 2024, 28% in 2023).
  - In 2025, 11% of adults struggled to pay bills in the prior 12 months because their income varied (similar to 2024).
  - Self-employed adults: 58% said income varied month to month; those who worked for someone else: 28% (10% struggled to pay bills).
  - Lower-income adults more likely to have income variability and related hardship.
- JPMorgan Chase Institute, "Weathering Volatility 2.0" (October 2019): families at the median level of income volatility saw a 36% month-to-month change in income (Chase checking data, 6M+ families, 2013-2018); volatility roughly constant 2013-2018; 65% of families lacked a cash buffer sufficient to weather a simultaneous income dip and expense spike.

## Common methods (described, not advised; widely described by banks/regulators, e.g., Nebraska Dept. of Banking & Finance, Capital One)
- Baseline / lowest-month budgeting: essentials planned around the lowest month of the past 6-12 months.
- Income buffer / holding account: income lands in one account; a steady amount is paid out; good months refill it.
- Percent-based allocation: fixed percentages of each deposit, so amounts scale with income.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, made-up freelancer)
Six months of income: $3,200, $4,100, $2,600, $3,800, $5,000, $2,900.
- Total = 21,600; average = 21,600 / 6 = $3,600; lowest = $2,600; highest = $5,000.
- Baseline method: plan on $2,600.
- Buffer: in the $5,000 month, 5,000 - 2,600 = $2,400 above baseline goes to a holding account.
- Percent method at 20%: 0.20 x 2,600 = $520; 0.20 x 5,000 = $1,000.

## Surprising fact
The typical (median) family's income moved 36% month to month in JPMorgan Chase Institute data (2019 report) -- volatility isn't just a gig-worker issue: 28% of people who work for an employer reported varying income in 2025 (Fed SHED).
