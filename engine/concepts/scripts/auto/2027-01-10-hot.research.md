# Research brief: What happens if you send a Zelle payment to the wrong person?
Slot: 2027-01-10-hot (Hot Take, posts Sun Jan 10 2027, 6 PM CT). Researched Nov 11 2026. Format: Money Myth ("my bank will just reverse it").

## Timely angle
None needed; evergreen. (Context only, not in script: NY AG v. Early Warning Services still pending; NY trial court denied dismissal July 20 2026; Zelle says it will appeal.)

## Core facts (with sources)
1. Cancel rule (Zelle FAQ, zelle.com/faq/how-to-use): if the recipient is already enrolled, money arrives "within minutes" and a completed payment cannot be canceled/reversed. If status is "pending" (recipient not enrolled with that email/number), the sender can cancel. Unclaimed payments expire after 14 days and funds return to the sender. Same wording repeated on many bank/CU FAQ pages (SoFi, Regions, Golden 1, Vision FCU).
2. What banks do: they tell you to contact them immediately and to ask the recipient to return the money; some say they cannot open a dispute for a wrong-recipient payment (Truity CU). Recovery is NOT guaranteed (Elliott Advocacy cases; Bank of America declined to reverse in one reported case).
3. Federal law (Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.2(m)): an "unauthorized EFT" is one "initiated by a person other than the consumer without actual authority". A payment you sent yourself to the wrong person is generally not "unauthorized". Nuance: CFPB supervisory work and FAQs (2021-2023) say P2P payments are EFTs covered by Reg E and some misdirected-payment cases can be "incorrect EFT" errors banks must investigate; outcome depends on facts. Script says "generally not unauthorized" and "not guaranteed".
4. CFPB lawsuit: CFPB sued Early Warning Services (Zelle operator) plus JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America and Wells Fargo in Dec 2024 (alleged >$870M customer losses over ~7 years). CFPB dismissed it with prejudice on March 4 2025 (D. Ariz.). Status as of Nov 2026: over. New York AG filed own suit Aug 2025; survived motion to dismiss July 2026.
5. Practical steps (bank guidance + consumer advocates): contact your bank right away; reach out to the recipient politely in writing; keep screenshots/records; small claims court (unjust enrichment) as a last resort; limits vary by state. Prevention: double-check the phone number/email and the name shown before sending; some suggest a small test payment first.

## Worked example (our math)
You mean to send $500 to your roommate but type one wrong digit.
- Recipient not enrolled -> pending -> cancel; or it expires after 14 days, $500 back.
- Recipient enrolled -> $500 lands in minutes; no cancel. You ask bank + stranger; not guaranteed.
- Scale: $1.2T / 365 = about $3.3B/day (our math); Zelle says avg $3.4B/day in 2025 (total was slightly above $1.2T).

## Surprising fact
The "Cancel" button only exists while the payment is pending, i.e., the stranger hasn't enrolled. If they're on Zelle, the money is gone in minutes, and federal "unauthorized transfer" protections generally don't apply because you authorized it.

## Uncertain
- Exact 2024/2025 totals are "over" figures ($1T+, $1.2T+); shown as $1T / $1.2T.
- Reg E treatment of user-typo misdirected payments is nuanced; we avoid claiming banks owe nothing, only "generally not unauthorized" and "not guaranteed".
