# Research brief: Face-off: 20% down vs 5% down on a house (2026-11-29-hot)
Researched Oct 2, 2026. Posts Nov 29, 2026. Every number carries its date.

## Key facts (dated)
1. **Median existing-home price, August 2026: $429,100**, up 1.6% from a year earlier; 38th straight month of year-over-year gains. Sales 3.98M SAAR (-2.0% m/m). Inventory 1.62M, 4.9 months' supply. (NAR release, Sept 10, 2026; confirmed by HousingWire and GlobeNewswire copy of the release.)
2. **Freddie Mac PMMS 30-year fixed, week of Oct 1, 2026: 7.28%** (prior week 7.03%; a year earlier 6.34%; 15-year 6.60%). Highest since November 2023. (Freddie Mac release "Mortgage Rates Average 7.28%", Oct 1, 2026; Fox Business, Yahoo Finance.)
3. **NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers (Nov 2025; buyers July 2024-June 2025):** first-time buyers' median down payment **10%** (highest since 1989; NOT 9% - 9% was the 2024 Profile). Repeat buyers' median down payment **23%** (same as 2024). First-time buyer share 21% (record low); median first-time buyer age 40. (NAR news; Virginia REALTORS; National Mortgage Professional.)
4. **PMI cost:** Freddie Mac (My Home) says borrowers typically pay **$30 to $70 a month per $100,000 borrowed** (= 0.36%-0.84% of the loan a year). Wider industry ranges (about 0.3%/0.46% to 1.5% a year) depend on credit score and down payment. (Freddie Mac My Home "Breaking down PMI" / "The Math Behind Putting Down Less Than 20%", quoted by Wells Fargo, NerdWallet, Yahoo Finance search results; Freddie page itself blocked to direct fetch.)
5. **PMI removal rule (Homeowners Protection Act of 1998, CFPB):** borrower can request cancellation when the balance reaches **80%** of the home's original value; servicer must **automatically terminate** PMI when the balance is scheduled to reach **78%** of original value (if current on payments). Final termination at loan midpoint. Applies to conventional loans with PMI (not FHA MIP). (CFPB "When can I remove PMI from my loan?"; Federal Reserve HPA summary; NCUA.)

## Worked example (exact math, hypothetical)
Assumptions: price = Aug 2026 NAR median $429,100; 30-year fixed at 7.28% (Freddie Mac, Oct 1, 2026); principal & interest only (no taxes/insurance); same rate for both (in reality, rates/PMI vary by credit and down payment).
Formula: M = L * r / (1 - (1 + r)^-360), r = 0.0728/12.

| | 20% down | 5% down |
|---|---|---|
| Down payment | $85,820 | $21,455 |
| Loan | $343,280 | $407,645 |
| Monthly P&I | $2,348.76 | $2,789.16 |
| Total interest, 30 yrs | $502,275.01 | $596,451.58 |
| PMI (Freddie $30-$70/mo per $100K) | $0 | $122.29-$285.35/mo |

- Cash up front difference: $85,820 - $21,455 = **$64,365** less with 5% down.
- Monthly P&I difference: **$440.40** more with 5% down (before PMI).
- Extra interest over 30 years: $596,451.58 - $502,275.01 = **$94,176.57** (before PMI).
- PMI at 78% scheduled LTV: 78% x $429,100 = $334,698; on the 5%-down schedule the balance reaches that at **month 145 (~12 years)** with no extra payments.
- Surprising fact: on the median home at 7.28%, the 5%-down borrower pays roughly $94K more interest, yet most first-time buyers (median 10%) do not put 20% down; PMI ends automatically by law.

## Trade-offs (neutral)
- 5% down: far less cash up front, earlier entry possible, cash left for reserves; but bigger loan, higher payment, PMI until ~78% LTV, more total interest.
- 20% down: no PMI, smaller payment and less interest; but takes longer to save, ties up more cash.
No advice, no price/rate predictions.
