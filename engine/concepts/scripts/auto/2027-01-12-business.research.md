# Research brief: How gyms make money from New Year's resolutions (and the people who never come back)
Show: How They Make Money | Posts Tuesday, January 12, 2027 | Researched November 13, 2026
Lens company: Planet Fitness (largest US gym chain by members). All company numbers from its fiscal 2025 Form 10-K
(fiscal year ended Dec 31, 2025, filed Feb 25, 2026) and official quarterly results releases.

## Timely angle
- No single news event needed: the video posts in peak "resolution season". Planet Fitness's own FY2025 10-K says
  "member joins are typically higher in January as compared to other months of the year."
- The FY2025 10-K also says it has sponsored "Dick Clark's New Year's Rockin' Eve" for the past 11 years and is the sole
  presenting sponsor of the Times Square New Year's Eve celebration.
- Most recent official data (Q3 2026 release, Nov 5, 2026): approximately 21.5 million members and 2,962 clubs as of Sept 30, 2026.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
Planet Fitness, fiscal 2025 (year ended Dec 31, 2025) - Q4/FY2025 results release (Feb 24, 2026) and FY2025 10-K:
- Members: approximately 20.8 million as of Dec 31, 2025; net growth of about 1.1 million in 2025.
- Clubs: 2,896 system-wide as of Dec 31, 2025; about 90% owned by franchisees.
- Total revenue FY2025: $1,324.1 million ($1.3 billion), up 12.1%.
  - Franchise segment: $468.0M; Corporate-owned clubs: $546.1M; Equipment (sold to franchisees): $310.1M.
- Net income FY2025: $220.3 million. System-wide sales: $5.3 billion (vs $4.8B in 2024).
- PF Black Card penetration: 62.6% (Dec 31, 2021) -> 66.5% (Dec 31, 2025) (10-K).
- Average monthly dues per member: $17.63 (2021) -> $19.51 (as of Dec 31, 2025) (10-K).
- Current standard annual fee: $49; enrollment fees $0 to $59 (FY2025 10-K).
- Both memberships priced well below the 2024 industry average of $69/month (Health & Fitness Association, cited in the 10-K).
- Seasonality: "member joins are typically higher in January as compared to other months of the year" (FY2025 10-K).
- Pricing: Classic Card for new members raised from $10 to $15 in 2024 (Fortune, May 10, 2024; company); CEO called 2025 "the
  first full-year of our 50 percent price increase for new Classic Card members." Planned national Black Card increase was
  PAUSED in Q1 2026 pending a pricing review (Q1 2026 release, CEO quote). Do not quote a Black Card list price.

2026 quarterly members (official releases):
- Mar 31, 2026: approximately 21.5 million members, 2,909 clubs (Q1 2026 release, May 2026). Trade coverage/call: more than
  700,000 net new members in Q1 2026.
- Sept 30, 2026: approximately 21.5 million members, 2,962 clubs (Q3 2026 release, Nov 5, 2026).
- So: the January-quarter jump (+~0.7M, our math 21.5 - 20.8) was followed by no net growth in the total through Sept 30.
  Caution: that is NET; it shows joins roughly matched cancellations, not how many individual people quit.

The "never come back" research (peer-reviewed):
- DellaVigna & Malmendier, "Paying Not to Go to the Gym," American Economic Review 96(3), 2006: data on 7,752 members of three
  US health clubs. Members choosing a monthly fee over $70 attended on average 4.3 times a month, paying a price per expected
  visit of more than $17, when a 10-visit pass would have cost $10 per visit. They forgo about $600 over a membership.
  Authors attribute it mainly to overconfidence about future self-control.

## Worked example (our math)
- Members per club, end of 2025: 20.8 million / 2,896 clubs = 7,182 (about 7,200) members per club on average (system-wide,
  approximate because 20.8M is rounded).
- Study price gap: $17 per visit vs $10 per visit = 1.7x, i.e. 70% more per visit.
- Personal check: monthly fee / visits actually made = real price per visit (e.g. $15 / 1 visit = $15; $15 / 10 visits = $1.50).

## The surprising fact
A gym chain with about 7,200 members per club (our math, end of 2025) can only work because members do not all show up;
a peer-reviewed 2006 study found monthly members paid over $17 per visit when a pay-per-visit pass cost $10.

## Uncertainties
- No official Planet Fitness disclosure of attendance, visit frequency, or club capacity. Do not claim "most members never go"
  as a Planet Fitness fact; capacity figures (e.g. ~250-300 people) are third-party estimates only - not used.
- 20.8M and 21.5M are "approximately" in company releases; per-club figure is our math.
- The 2006 study is older and about three clubs with $70+ monthly fees, not Planet Fitness.
- "More than 700,000 net new members in Q1 2026" is from earnings coverage/call; release gives only the 21.5M total.
