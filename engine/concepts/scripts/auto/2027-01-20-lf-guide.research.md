## Worked examples (our math)
1. Reports per second, 2024: 2024 had 366 days x 86,400 s = 31,622,400 seconds. 31,622,400 / 1,135,291 = 27.85 seconds -> about one report every 28 seconds. Per day: 1,135,291 / 366 = 3,101.9 -> about 3,100 reports a day.
2. Average reported fraud loss per fraud report, 2025: $15.9 billion / about 3 million reports = about $5,300 per report (rough: FTC rounded totals; not every report includes a loss).
3. Per day, 2024: 1,135,291 / 366 = about 3,100 reports a day.
4. Freeze cost: 3 bureaus x $0 = $0 (federal law since Sept 21, 2018).

## Rules and limits (official)
- Credit freeze: free at Equifax, Experian and TransUnion by federal law since Sept 21, 2018 (Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act; FTC "Free credit freezes are here," Sept 2018). Must contact each of the three bureaus separately. Free freezes for children under 16 too (parent requests).
- Timing: online/phone freeze request placed within 1 business day; lift ("thaw") within 1 hour of online/phone request; mail requests 3 business days (USA.gov, updated Nov 13, 2025; FTC).
- Freeze does not affect your credit score; existing creditors can still see your file; you can still use your cards. You need your PIN/password (or account login) to lift it. Lift before applying for credit, then refreeze.
- Credit "locks" are bureau products that may carry monthly fees; only freezes have the federal free guarantee (FTC).
- Fraud alerts: free. Initial fraud alert lasts 1 year (was 90 days before Sept 2018), renewable; contact ONE bureau and it must tell the other two. Extended fraud alert lasts 7 years, for identity theft victims with an FTC Identity Theft Report or police report (FTC).
- Free credit reports: weekly, from all three bureaus, permanently, at AnnualCreditReport.com (FTC consumer alert, Oct 2023). Only authorized site. Reports do not include scores. Phone: 1-877-322-8228.
- IRS Identity Protection PIN (IP PIN): 6-digit number; anyone with an SSN or ITIN who can verify their identity can request one; free; new number each calendar year; fastest via IRS Online Account; prevents someone else filing a return with your SSN. Online tool generally unavailable mid-Nov to mid-Jan. IRS guidance: "Phone calls, emails or texts asking for your IP PIN are scams." (IRS Tax Tip 2026-56, July 16, 2026; IRS.gov/ippin) [CHECK exact quote wording]
- Recovery: IdentityTheft.gov (FTC) or 1-877-438-4338 -> Identity Theft Report + personalized recovery plan. FTC steps: 1) call the companies where fraud occurred (fraud department), close/freeze accounts, change logins; 2) place a fraud alert and get your credit reports; 3) report to FTC at IdentityTheft.gov; then dispute fraudulent items, consider extended alert/freeze.

## The surprising fact
- The strongest single defense, a credit freeze at all three bureaus, has been free by federal law since Sept 21, 2018, takes minutes online, can be lifted in about an hour, and does not change your credit score.

## Chapter plan
1. Freeze your credit (3 bureaus, free, timing, lift/refreeze)
2. Watch your reports (weekly free reports, what to look for, fraud alerts)
3. Lock down accounts and mail/phone habits (phishing, imposters; FTC guidance on not sharing codes; strong passwords and multifactor)
4. Protect your taxes (IP PIN)
5. If it happens (IdentityTheft.gov steps in order; extended alert)
6. Recap checklist

## Common mistakes
- Freezing at only one or two bureaus. Confusing fraud alert with freeze. Losing the freeze PIN/login. Forgetting to thaw before a loan application. Paying for a "lock" thinking it is required. Using fake "free credit report" sites. Giving codes or PINs to callers.
