# Fact-check: 2026-10-21-business ("How nail salons make money")
Checked 2026-10-01 for posting 2026-10-21. Script JSON only.

## Verified
- Census SAS via FRED, employer nail salons (NAICS 812113), establishments subject to federal income tax: revenue 2019 $8,200M, 2020 $6,408M, 2021 $10,889M, 2022 $12,853M (REVEF812113TAXABL). Expenses 2022 $10,073M (NSEESTFITEF32812113). Chart points and the $12.9B / $10.1B / $6.4B figures match.
- 10,073 / 12,853 = 0.784, so 78¢ is correct.
- Hypothetical math: 30 - 15 - 3 - 8 = 4; 4/30 = 13.3%, so "about 13%" is correct. Extra client 30 - 15 - 3 = 12, correct. Assumptions are labeled "(assumed)" and the example is labeled hypothetical.
- 50% commission "common rule of thumb": supported. Industry sources (Zenoti, Vagaro, others) put tech commission at roughly 40-60% of service revenue, with 45-50% the most common. Chair or booth rent is a real model, typically $150-$600+/week; the script gives no rent numbers.
- BLS median manicurist/pedicurist pay of $16.66/hr in May 2024 is correct for that year, but newer data is out (see issue 1).
- On-screen text matches the spoken numbers on every line.
- Every sub-label and board line is 28 characters or fewer. The longest is "Tech, 50% commission: -$15" at 26. The two subs are "Census, 2022" (12) and "BLS, May 2024" (13).
- No advice, price predictions, politics, quotes or allegations.

## Issues
1. **Line 6, stale BLS figure (must fix).** The OEWS May 2025 estimates are out: the median for manicurists and pedicurists is $17.19/hr ($35,760/yr). Fix: "median pay was $17.19 an hour in May 2025" / say "seventeen dollars and nineteen cents ... May twenty twenty-five"; sub "BLS, May 2025"; also update the IG and YT text.
2. **Lines 1, 2 and 7, "thin margins" framing not supported (must fix).** The Census figures show about 22¢ left per dollar, a roughly 22% aggregate pre-tax margin for employer salons. Owner pay for unincorporated firms is not on payroll, so that 22% also covers owners' pay. That is not thin. The hook's "shock" mood, the "techs aren't getting rich *either*" wording (which implies salons aren't either), and "That's why salons lean on..." present the margin as thin. The hypothetical 13% is also well below the Census aggregate, and the script never says so. Fixes: reframe the hook as "keep about 22 cents", or say the hypothetical salon is a tighter case than the industry average. Change "either" to "And for techs, median pay was...". Soften "That's why" to "Ways salons boost that margin:".
3. **Line 1, weak and dated hook.** "Spent 78¢ of every dollar" is not surprising (most businesses spend most of their revenue), it opens with a 2022 date, and it runs about 3.5-4 s spoken. A stronger version: "A $30 manicure can leave the salon about $4." The hypothetical needs to be flagged on screen, e.g. "Example: a $30 manicure might leave the salon $4." Or use a Census-true line: "Nail salons keep about 22 cents of every dollar."
4. **Lines 1-3, data recency caveat.** The SAS series ends at 2022 because the SAS was folded into the Census Annual Integrated Economic Survey (AIES). AIES 2023 data came out Feb 2026 and AIES 2024 data on Sept 3, 2026, with detail published down to 6-digit NAICS "where available." I could not confirm from search (data.census.gov is blocked) that 812113 nail salons appear in the AIES. Before posting, check data.census.gov AIES tables for 812113. If they exist, update to the newer year or say "latest in the Census Service Annual Survey series". AIES and SAS are not strictly comparable, so do not splice the two into one chart.
5. **Post title, payoff gap.** "Nail salons spend 78 cents of every dollar. Here's where." The video never breaks down where the Census 78¢ goes; it only shows a hypothetical. Fix: "Where the money from a $30 manicure goes (example)", or add a real Census expense breakdown such as payroll share.
6. **Line 8, minor.** "Rent is already paid" is applied to the $8 "rent and bills", but some bills such as utilities can vary with clients. Fine as a labeled example; optionally say "rent is fixed".
7. **Minor.** "Salons with employees" is fine; the series is employer firms subject to federal income tax, effectively all employer salons.

VERDICT: FAIL
