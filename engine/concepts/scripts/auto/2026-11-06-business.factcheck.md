## Verified

Sources: Live Nation FY2025 release and 10-K (search excerpts), Music Business Worldwide, Billboard, CelebrityAccess.
- Math (rechecked in python): 3,081.2 / 25,200 = 12.2%, so "12%" is OK. 1,134.4 / about 2,366–2,400 = 47–48%, so "nearly half" is OK. 1,134.4 / 3,081.2 = 36.8%, so "~37%" is OK. 1,134.4 / 346 = $3.279, so "$3.28" is OK.

- Litigation:
  - DOJ and states sued in May 2024.
  - DOJ announced a settlement on March 9, 2026. The proposed terms: a $280M fund for states, a 15% cap on primary fees, 4-year limits on exclusive contracts, and divestiture/booking changes at 13 amphitheaters. Live Nation keeps Ticketmaster.
  - The settlement still needs Tunney Act approval by Judge Arun Subramanian (SDNY). The comment period ran from July 13 to Sept 4, 2026. No approval had been reported as of late September 2026.
  - On April 15, 2026, a jury found for the litigating states on all remaining claims, including monopolization and tying, with a $1.72 per-ticket overcharge finding.
  - The companies filed motions for JMOL and, in the alternative, a new trial. Hearings were held around July 29–31, 2026, and the ruling was still pending as of Sept 2026. No remedy or final judgment has been entered.
  - Sources: NPR, NBC, Billboard, Paul Weiss, Crowell, TicketNews (Jul/Aug/Sep 2026), MBW, Digital Music News, TechTimes.
