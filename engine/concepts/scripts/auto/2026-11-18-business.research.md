# Research brief: How shoe companies make money on limited drops (2026-11-18-business)

Researched Oct 2, 2026. Posts Nov 18, 2026. Note: sec.gov, about.nike.com, stockx.com, prnewswire and most news sites were blocked for direct fetch in this session; every figure below is confirmed through search results quoting the official filing/release (several independent results each) and checked by arithmetic.

## Timely angle
- StockX "Big Facts" midyear report (released Aug 12, 2026; compares global StockX sales Jan-Jun 2026 vs Jan-Jun 2025): Vans' average resale price premium rose from +12% to +69%, the biggest premium expansion of any sneaker brand; Vans trades up 238%. Jordan sales +6%, Jordan average prices +5%. Palace average premium +9% -> +81% on Nike soccer collabs. Supreme apparel premium 11% -> 41%. (StockX / PR Newswire Aug 12, 2026; WWD "3 Biggest Surprises From StockX's Midyear Resale Report"; SGB, FashionUnited, Fashionista, Aug 2026)
- "Price premium" = how far the average resale price sits above original retail. Meaning: per $100 of retail, Vans pairs resold for about $169 on average in H1 2026 (100 x 1.69).
- Key point: the brand is paid once, at retail. Resale premiums go to resellers and to StockX (fees), not to the brand.
- StockX 2025 Big Facts / Current Culture Index (Dec 2025): ASICS Gel-1130 was 2025's best-selling sneaker on StockX; Mizuno +148%, Brooks +1,508% sales growth.

## Nike, fiscal 2026 (year ended May 31, 2026) - 10-K filed July 2026; Q4 release June 30, 2026
- Revenue: $46.4 billion, flat reported, down 2% currency-neutral.
- NIKE Brand revenue: $45.2 billion.
- NIKE Direct (Nike-owned stores, Nike app, SNKRS app, nike.com): $17.7 billion vs $18.8 billion in FY2025, "primarily driven by a decrease in traffic". Share of NIKE Brand: 17.7 / 45.2 = 39.2% (about 39%).
- NIKE Brand wholesale: $27.5 billion vs $25.9 billion in FY2025. Share: 27.5 / 45.2 = 60.8% (about 61%).
- Gross margin: 42.9%, up 20 basis points from 42.7%. Includes a $986 million benefit in cost of sales from expected recovery of IEEPA tariffs (after the Feb 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling). Q4 gross margin 49.2% included ~900 bps from that recovery.
- Net income $3.1B; diluted EPS $2.10 ($1.58 excluding the tariff benefit).

## Nike FY2025 (year ended May 31, 2025) 10-K: what discounts do
- Gross margin fell 190 bps to 42.7% from 44.6%, "primarily due to lower NIKE Brand ASP ... primarily due to higher discounts and changes in channel mix".
- Nike "took actions to reduce supply of certain footwear products in the marketplace through increased markdowns across NIKE Direct and discounts and higher sales returns with wholesale partners," which hurt revenue and gross margin. NIKE Direct fell 13%, $21.5B -> $18.8B.
- Surprising fact: the scarcity model breaks when too much of a hyped shoe is made; Nike's own 10-K blames discounts for the margin drop.

## SNKRS (company-stated)
- Nike statement to Sneaker News, May 2026: Nike App and SNKRS engineering teams combined into one team; "We remain deeply committed to SNKRS"; SNKRS "remains a critical tool" and will deliver "curated launches alongside always-on assortments". Reports (Complex, May 2026) said up to ~90% of SNKRS staff were laid off; Nike did not confirm a percentage. Not used on screen except as "SNKRS drop app" description.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, stated assumptions)
- A $200 sneaker that costs the brand $90 to make and ship (assumption).
- Full price: 200 - 90 = $110 gross profit; 110 / 200 = 55% margin.
- 30% markdown: price 200 x 0.70 = $140; 140 - 90 = $50 gross profit; 50 / 140 = 35.7% margin.
- Same shoe, same cost: profit falls from $110 to $50, down 54.5% ((110-50)/110).
- Resale: at StockX's H1 2026 Vans average premium (+69%), $200 retail -> $338 resale; the extra $138 goes to the reseller/platform, not the brand.

## Caveats
- No company publishes what share of revenue comes from limited drops; do not claim one.
- StockX data covers only StockX sales (self-reported by StockX).
