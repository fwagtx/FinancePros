# Research: Scam alert - fake charities after natural disasters (posts Nov 27, 2026)

Note: ftc.gov, justice.gov, ic3.gov and most news sites were blocked for direct fetch by the proxy. Figures below are confirmed from search results quoting the official source, cross-checked across two or more results where noted.

## Timely angle (dated)
- Hurricane Lowell damaged Kaua'i, Hawaii, in September 2026. On Sept 16, 2026 the Hawaii Department of the Attorney General (AG Anne Lopez) warned the public to be alert for scams, including fraudulent charities and crowdfunding, and home repair scams. Says government agencies and legitimate relief organizations will not pressure people. Reports go to the AG's Tax & Charities Division (808-586-1480). (Hawaii Governor newsroom release 2026-55; Maui Now, Sept 16, 2026; KHON2)
- FTC consumer alert "When donating, support those in need, not a scammer" (Aug 27, 2026) cites flooding in Nepal and Tibet and earthquakes in Venezuela, Japan and Colombia; says scammers create fake charities that seem official, or impersonate well-known charities. (consumer.ftc.gov, 2026/08; confirmed in two searches)
- Other 2026 warnings: Texas AG consumer alert after Hill Country flooding (July 2026); West Virginia AG after flooding.

## Key numbers
- FTC Consumer Sentinel: consumers reported losing $15.9B to fraud in 2025 (about 3 million fraud reports). Up from $12.5B in 2024. (FTC JEC testimony, Mar 2026; Crowdfund Insider, Spokesman-Review Apr 3, 2026, Finextra)
- Imposter scams: top reported category for the fifth straight year in 2025; 1M+ reports, $3.5B+ losses; ~20% of reporters lost money. (FTC press release June 2026; CNBC June 26, 2026; BleepingComputer)
- FBI IC3 2025: 1,008,597 complaints, $20.877B losses (+26% vs 2024). Investment fraud $8.6B. No separate charity/disaster figure found in search results. (IC3 2025 Annual Report; CyberScoop; AARP)
- Giving USA 2026: US charitable giving was an estimated $617.2B in 2025, first time above $600B; individuals $394.2B. (IU Lilly Family School of Philanthropy; NonProfit PRO)
- National Center for Disaster Fraud (DOJ, founded 2005 after Hurricane Katrina): DOJ announced its closure effective March 31, 2026; DOJ says it processed more than a million complaints since founding (older DOJ page said "over 220,000"; numbers conflict, so not used on screen). Reason given: partner agencies run their own hotlines. (justice.gov OPA "Closing the National Center for Disaster Fraud"; justice.gov/disaster-fraud). So the NCDF hotline is NOT presented as current.
- FTC does not publicly break out charity-scam losses in the headline Sentinel numbers found; no charity-specific dollar figure is used.

## Warning signs (FTC, IRS, Hawaii AG), descriptive
- Pressure to donate right away, no time to research (FTC charity fraud page; Hawaii AG: legit relief orgs don't pressure).
- Payment only by cash, gift card, wire transfer or cryptocurrency (FTC Aug 27, 2026 alert; FTC: "safest way to donate is by credit card or check").
- Names that sound like large well-known charities; fake websites (FTC; IRS "Keep an eye out for common charitable contributions scams").
- Spoofed caller ID; claims you already pledged; refusal to send information or explain how money is used (FTC).
- Text-message donation requests (FTC Aug 2026).

## Checking a charity
- IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search (TEOS): shows whether an organization is eligible to receive tax-deductible contributions; legitimate charities give their EIN on request. (irs.gov)
- BBB Wise Giving Alliance (Give.org): 20 Standards for Charity Accountability; Standard 8: at least 65% of total expenses on program activities; Standard 9: fundraising no more than 35% of related contributions. (give.org; aele.org copy of standards)
- FTC also points to Charity Watch and Candid; Charity Navigator is another rating site.

## Tax rule used in worked example
- Starting tax year 2026 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act), non-itemizers can deduct cash gifts to qualified charities (e.g., 501(c)(3)) up to $1,000 single / $2,000 married filing jointly; not for donor-advised funds; not indexed. (Fidelity, Vanguard Charitable, Fidelity Charitable, Ameriprise)
- Gifts to an organization that is not a qualified charity are not deductible.

## Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
Assumptions: single filer, takes the standard deduction, 22% federal bracket, gives $500 cash in 2026.
- Qualified charity: $500 is under the $1,000 cap, so the full $500 is deductible. Tax saved = $500 x 0.22 = $110.
- Fake charity: deduction $0, tax saved $0, and $0 reaches disaster victims. The $500 is gone.
Growth math for chart: 15.9 / 3.3 = 4.82, "nearly 5 times" 2020's reported losses.

## The surprising fact
Reported US fraud losses nearly quintupled from 2020 to 2025 (as first reported), and the DOJ's dedicated disaster-fraud center, opened after Katrina, closed on March 31, 2026.
