# Research brief: Tax deduction vs tax credit - why credits are worth more (Explainer, Ticker) - posts Tue Jan 26, 2027, 12 PM Central (evergreen)

All figures are for TAX YEAR 2026 (returns filed in early 2027) unless a year is given. Confirmed Nov 27, 2026 with web search.
Note: www.irs.gov could not be fetched directly in this session (network allowlist); IRS figures come from search snippets of the IRS newsroom release (irs.gov/ko and /ht mirrors of the English release) and matching reputable outlets (Tax Foundation, Kiplinger, TaxAct, NerdWallet).

## Definitions (stable law)
- Tax deduction: lowers TAXABLE INCOME. Value = deduction x your marginal tax rate (the rate on your last dollars).
- Tax credit: lowers the TAX BILL itself, dollar for dollar.
- Nonrefundable credit: can cut your tax to $0 but no further; any leftover is lost.
- Refundable credit: if the credit is bigger than your tax, the IRS pays the difference as part of your refund.

## Key 2026 numbers (tax year 2026)
- Standard deduction 2026: $16,100 single / married filing separately; $32,200 married filing jointly; $24,150 head of household. (IRS newsroom, "IRS releases tax inflation adjustments for tax year 2026 including amendments from the One Big Beautiful Bill", Oct 2025; Rev. Proc. 2025-32; Tax Foundation 2026 tax brackets)
- Child Tax Credit 2026: up to $2,200 per qualifying child (same as 2025; indexed to inflation going forward under the July 2025 law). (IRS release via search; Kiplinger; TaxAct)
- Refundable portion (Additional Child Tax Credit) 2026: up to $1,700 per child, unchanged from 2025. (Kiplinger; TaxAct; IRS release)
- EITC 2026 maximum (fully refundable): $8,231 with 3+ qualifying children (up from $8,046 in 2025); $664 no children, $4,427 one, $7,316 two. (Tax Policy Center; Kiplinger; Motley Fool) -- two+ reputable outlets agree; not used on screen.
- 2026 single brackets: 12% on $12,401-$50,400; 22% on $50,401-$105,700; 24% on $105,701-$201,775. (Tax Foundation; NerdWallet; Kiplinger)
- Context only, NOT tax year 2026: tax year 2027 figures already announced (Nov 2026): CTC $2,300, refundable part $1,800 (azfamily / Florida Realtors). Don't use; avoid confusion.

## Worked example (our math)
- $1,000 deduction, 22% bracket: $1,000 x 0.22 = $220 less tax.
- $1,000 deduction, 12% bracket: $1,000 x 0.12 = $120.
- $1,000 credit (any bracket): $1,000 less tax.
- Gap at 22%: $1,000 - $220 = $780; credit is worth about 4.5x the deduction (1,000 / 220 = 4.55).
- Refundable vs not (our math): owe $500 in tax, get a $1,000 credit. Nonrefundable: tax goes to $0, other $500 is lost. Refundable: tax goes to $0 and $500 comes back in the refund.
- Brackets reference (previous episode 2027-01-19 "In the 22% bracket? Why you don't pay 22%"): marginal rate only applies to income in that band; mention briefly.

## Surprising fact
- A $1,000 deduction is usually worth only a few hundred dollars ($220 at 22%), while a $1,000 credit is worth the full $1,000 -- and a refundable credit can pay you even if you owe $0.
