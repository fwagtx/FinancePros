# Research brief: How solar companies make money on leases and loans
Slot 2026-12-18-business (posts Fri Dec 18, 2026). Written Oct 19, 2026. Show: How They Make Money.

## Timely angle
- The homeowner tax credit (Section 25D, 30%) ended for expenditures after Dec 31, 2025 (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025). By Dec 2026 it has been gone for a full year, while leases/PPAs (company-owned systems) can still use the business credit (48E). This is why $0-down leases are the industry's main pitch in 2026.

## Three business models
1. Cash: homeowner pays the installer; installer earns the sale margin. Homeowner's 25D credit ended after 2025.
2. Lease / PPA ("subscription"): the company (e.g. Sunrun) owns the panels on the roof. Customer pays a fixed monthly amount (lease) or a fixed price per kWh (PPA), terms up to 25 years, often $0 down (Sunrun.com: "as little as $0 down"; monitoring, maintenance, insurance "for up to 25 years"). The company keeps the tax credit and the decades of payments, which it finances with tax-equity and securitized debt.
3. Loan: homeowner owns the system; a lender (often a fintech partnered with installers) earns interest; dealer fees paid by the installer to the lender are built into the loan principal.

## Tax credit status (verify-critical)
- 25D Residential Clean Energy Credit: "You can't claim residential clean energy credits for expenditures made after December 31, 2025." (IRS Instructions for Form 5695; IRS FAQ Fact Sheet 2025-05 at IRS.gov/EnergyCreditFAQs). Expenditure counts as made when installation is completed (25D(e)(8)(A)); a 2026 install doesn't qualify even if paid in 2025. Carryforward of unused credits unchanged (CRS Insight IN12611).
- 48E (business Clean Electricity ITC) for third-party-owned residential solar: still available if construction began by July 4, 2026, or placed in service by Dec 31, 2027 (SEIA OBBB explainer, July 2025; pv magazine USA, July 8, 2025). Foreign-entity (FEOC) restrictions apply from 2026.
- Sunrun's average ITC: 44.0% in Q2 2026 (up from 42.6% a year earlier); 44.2% in Q1 2026 (base 30% plus adders). Source: Sunrun Q2 2026 / Q1 2026 earnings releases (8-K Ex. 99.1). NOTE: seen via search summaries of the release; sec.gov blocked direct fetch.

## Dealer fees on solar loans (CFPB)
- CFPB "Solar Financing" Issue Spotlight, Aug 7, 2024: program/lending/platform/dealer fees "typically range between 10 to 30 percent of the cash price of the solar project", not disclosed to consumers as part of the APR, and included in the loan principal (quoted by Goodwin, Cooley, Mayer Brown summaries; PDF itself blocked). Also flagged "net cost" pitches that subtract the expected but not guaranteed 30% tax credit from loan principal, and loans that re-amortize unless ~30% of principal is prepaid.
- Same day: CFPB, Treasury and FTC consumer advisory on solar sales.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- Cash price $30,000; dealer fee 20% of cash price (inside the CFPB's 10-30% range) = $6,000.
- Loan principal = $30,000 + $6,000 = $36,000. Borrower pays interest on $36,000, not $30,000.

## Surprising fact
- Sunrun's average tax credit (44.0% in Q2 2026) is bigger than the 30% homeowners used to get, and homeowners who buy now get 0% federal credit, while the leasing company still collects one.
