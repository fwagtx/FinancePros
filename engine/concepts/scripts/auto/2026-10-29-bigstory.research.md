## Context
GDP measures the value of everything the US economy produces; "real" means adjusted for inflation; quarterly rates are annualized. Consumer spending is about two-thirds of GDP. A strong GDP number is about the whole economy, not any one household's paycheck; prices up 3.6% over the year (September PCE) is what households feel at the register.

## What it means for a normal person
Growth was strong in Q3, driven by shoppers and AI investment, but prices were still up 3.6% from a year earlier in September, so a growing economy and a tight budget can both be true.

## What to watch next
BEA's second estimate of Q3 GDP (late November) and third estimate; revisions can be large (Q2 went from 1.5% to 2.2%).
