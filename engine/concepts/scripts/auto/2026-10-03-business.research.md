# Research brief: How investment banks make money on IPOs (2026-10-03-business, short)

Note: many sites were blocked for direct fetch in this environment; every number below was confirmed through web search results quoting the named source, and cross-checked across at least two results where possible.

## News angle (timely, still the defining IPO story of 2026)
- SpaceX IPO: priced at $135 per share on Thursday, June 11, 2026; began trading on Nasdaq (ticker SPCX) on Friday, June 12, 2026.
- Base deal: 555.6 million shares at $135 = $75 billion, the largest IPO ever. With the greenshoe (15% overallotment) the full raise could reach ~639 million shares, just over $86 billion. Previous US-listed record: Alibaba, $25 billion in 2014. (Fortune, June 11, 2026; CBS News; CNBC)
- Underwriters: Goldman Sachs as lead-left bookrunner, plus Morgan Stanley, BofA Securities, Citigroup and J.P. Morgan as joint book-running managers. (Fortune citing the S-1)
- Fees: about $500 million in underwriting fees, near 0.7% of the $75 billion deal; SpaceX had negotiated fees under 0.75% (Bloomberg News, via Yahoo Finance). Alibaba's banks earned 1.2% in 2014, long the mega-deal benchmark.
- Split: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley about 20% of the pool each, about $100 million apiece; BofA, Citi and JPMorgan about $75 million each (Yahoo Finance / Bloomberg; KuCoin and Futu repeat it).
- Greenshoe: SpaceX negotiated a rare zero-fee greenshoe on the roughly $11 billion overallotment, so banks forgo about $75 million (Outlook Business; Yahoo Finance).
- First day (June 12, 2026): opened at $150, closed at $160.95, up 19% from $135; intraday high $176.52 (up ~31%). Over 500 million shares traded. Company valued around $2.1 trillion at the close. (CNBC June 12, 2026; CBS News)
- Fortune (Shawn Tully, June 11, 2026) argues banks win another way: investors given underpriced shares send trading commissions back to the underwriters ("soft dollars"). This is an argument/estimate, not a disclosed figure; do not state the 30% figure as fact.

## Worked example (exact math)
- Fee: 0.7% x $75,000,000,000 = $525,000,000, reported as "about $500 million" (the exact rate is "under 0.75%"; reported total ~ $500M).
- Same size at the typical 7%: 7% x $75B = $5.25 billion. SpaceX's fee is one-tenth of the standard rate.
- Typical mid-size IPO: a $100 million IPO at the 7% gross spread: bank buys at $93 million, sells at $100 million, keeps $7 million.
- First-day pop on base shares: ($160.95 - $135.00) = $25.95 per share x 555.6 million shares = $14.42 billion ("money left on the table", base deal only, before any greenshoe shares).
- $14.42B / ~$0.5B fees = about 29 times the fees.

## How the business works (definitions)
- Gross spread (underwriting discount): underwriters buy the shares from the issuer below the IPO price and resell at the IPO price; the difference is their fee. Usually split into management fee, underwriting fee, and selling concession.
- Greenshoe / overallotment option: lets underwriters sell up to 15% more shares; used to stabilize the price after the IPO.
- Money left on the table (Ritter): (first-day close - offer price) x shares offered.

## The "7% solution" (the surprising pattern)
- Chen & Ritter (2000), "The Seven Percent Solution", Journal of Finance: gross spreads cluster at exactly 7% for mid-size IPOs.
- Ritter, "Underwriting Statistics Through 2025": of 2,522 US operating-company IPOs 2001-2025 raising at least $30 million, 93.3% of those raising $30-160 million (inflation-adjusted) paid exactly 7%; only 45.1% of those above $160 million did.

## Company numbers: Goldman Sachs, fiscal year 2025 (10-K / Q4 2025 earnings release)
- Investment banking fees: $9.34 billion (up 21% vs 2024), made of Advisory $4.73B + Equity underwriting $1.78B + Debt underwriting $2.83B (sums to $9.34B).
- Equity underwriting was higher in 2025 "reflecting significantly higher net revenues from initial public and convertible offerings".
- Total firm net revenues: $58.3 billion in 2025. Global Banking & Markets: $41.45 billion.
- Math: $1.78B / $58.3B = 3.05%, so all equity underwriting (IPOs plus other stock deals) was about 3% of Goldman's 2025 revenue.

## The fact that surprises people
- On SpaceX, the investors the banks chose made about $14.4 billion on paper in one day, roughly 29 times the ~$500 million the banks were paid. The banks accepted a tenth of the normal fee because the prize is the relationship: the trophy deal, the league-table credit, and the big investor clients who get the allocations.
