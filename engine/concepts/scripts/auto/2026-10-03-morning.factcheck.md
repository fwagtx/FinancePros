## Issues
1. **Line 1, wrong attribution (FACT).** "That's from Friday's jobs report" points back to both numbers in the hook. The 3.4% prices figure is August CPI from Sept 11, not the jobs report. The required Sept-pay-vs-Aug-CPI label is missing at the point where the comparison is first made.
   - text: `Pay is from Friday's jobs report, prices from August inflation. And hiring? Just 29,000 jobs in September.`
   - say: `Pay is from Friday's jobs report, prices from August inflation. And hiring? Just twenty-nine thousand jobs in September.` (18 words, same as before)
   - scene: unchanged.
2. **Line 6, the worked example is not labeled hypothetical, and "a 3% raise" reads like a claim about the viewer.** Average hourly earnings are an average, not anyone's raise. The math is correct.
   - text: `Hypothetical: $50,000 salary. A 3% raise is $1,500. Keeping pace with 3.4% inflation takes $1,700: $200 short.`
   - say: `Say you earn fifty thousand dollars. A three percent raise is fifteen hundred. Keeping pace with inflation takes seventeen hundred. That's two hundred short.` (24 words, same as before)
   - scene: unchanged. Board lines are fine.
3. **post.title and tiktok (minor): month mixing is unlabeled, and the title ends awkwardly on "September jobs".**
   - post.title: `September jobs: pay up 3%, August prices up 3.4%` (48)
   - tiktok: `Pay up 3.0% (Sept), prices up 3.4% (Aug CPI). Just 29,000 jobs in Sept. Not financial advice. #moneytok #jobsreport #inflation #financeprostv` (141)
4. **Line 0 hook (optional, not a fail item).** It is true, specific and creates tension. At 10 words it takes about 3 seconds, which is borderline. To tighten it:
   - text `Pay is up 3.0%. Prices are up 3.4%.`
   - say `Pay's up three percent. Prices? Three point four.` (8 words; the new total would be 133)
   - scene unchanged.
5. **Line 1 narration (fixed by #1).** "the hiring number was even weaker" compared hiring with a pay statistic, which is a non-sequitur when read aloud.
6. **Cover "Your Raise vs Prices" / header "raises lag prices" (minor, OK).** The worked example pays these off. "Raises" is a loose word for average hourly earnings. Acceptable once line 6 is labeled hypothetical.
