# Fact-check: 2027-01-27-business (How Recruiters Get Paid)

Note: Firecrawl was out of credits, and direct fetches of sec.gov were blocked by the network policy. Verification used web search results that quote the SEC filings and SHRM text.

## Verified claims
- Robert Half revenue: 2021 $6.461B, 2022 $7.24B (record), 2023 $6.39B, 2024 $5.796B, 2025 $5.379B. Chart points 6.46 / 7.24 / 6.39 / 5.8 / 5.38 match.
- FY2025 permanent placement talent solutions revenue: $440M, down 9.8% from $487M (FY2025 10-K). 440 / 5,379 = 8.2%, so "about 8%" is correct.
- FY2025 10-K: "Fees for successful placements are paid only by the employer and are generally a percentage of the new employee's annual compensation. No fee for placement services is charged to employment candidates." Confirmed. (The 10-K says "annual compensation". The video's "first-year pay" wording is used only for the SHRM claim, so it is correct.)
- 10-K revenue recognition: the company estimates the cost of placements that don't stay through a 90-day guarantee period and books that as contract liabilities. Confirmed. "Early quitters" overstated this, because hires can also be let go. Reworded.
- SHRM (Mark Feffer, July 18, 2016): contingency fees are "in the area of 20 percent to 25 percent" of first-year cash compensation, and only if the role is filled. Confirmed. Retained search at about 33% is attributed to the same article by secondary sources, and Heidrick & Struggles' 10-K ("approximately one-third of the estimated first year compensation") backs it up independently. OK.
- Math: 20% x $80,000 = $16,000. Correct.
- Contract talent plus Protiviti consulting make up most of revenue. Protiviti was about 34% of 2024 revenue, and permanent placement is about 8%. OK.
