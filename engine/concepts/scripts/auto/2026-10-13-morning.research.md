## Facts (IMF WEO, October 2026; released at the IMF/World Bank Annual Meetings in Bangkok; AP dateline Bangkok, Oct 12, 2026 US time; Japan Times/Axios/The Week coverage dated Oct 13)
- Global growth 2026: 3.1%, up from 3.0% in the July 2026 update (+0.1 pt). (AP; Reuters; CNBC)
- Global growth 2025: 3.5% (AP: "well below the 3.5% expansion in 2025").
- Global growth 2027: 3.4% (AP: "Growth in 2027 should rebound to 3.4%"; The Week/Greentech summary agree).
- Advanced economies: 2.0% (2025) -> 2.1% (2026) -> 1.9% (2027). (AP)
- Emerging & developing economies: 4.5% (2025) -> 3.7% (2026) -> 4.4% (2027). (AP)
- United States: 2.3% in 2026 and 2.3% in 2027 (2026 unchanged; 2027 raised 0.2 pt from April). Axios: fastest among G7. Supported by consumer spending, fiscal policy, tech investment. IMF puts ~30% probability on US growth below 1% in 2027 (down from 35% in April).
- China 4.5% (2026); India 7.0% (2026 and 2027); Japan raised to 0.7% (2026).
- Why: AI-driven investment boom offsets damage from surging oil and gas prices tied to the Middle East war. (Reuters: "IMF says AI investment propping up global growth, but war and debt threaten outlook"; CNBC: "AI boom offsets war hit")
- Oil assumption: average $96/barrel in 2026, a nearly 41% increase this year (AP); $87 in 2027 (Reuters summary). Assumes Strait of Hormuz reopens March 2027.
- Global headline inflation: 4.7% in 2026, easing to 4.1% in 2027. (AP; Reuters)
- Concentration: 32 countries account for 95% of global growth (AP).
- Risks "skewed to the downside": prolonged Middle East conflict, a reassessment of AI profitability, worsening fiscal/debt outlook, extreme weather; warning of a possible "disorderly" spike in sovereign bond yields.
- Quote (AP): IMF research director/chief economist Silvana Tenreyro: "The outlook is highly uneven." (Title varies by outlet: Reuters/CNBC "chief economist"; AP "research director".)
- Policy: Tenreyro said monetary policy should remain "sufficiently restrictive" to return inflation to target in a timely way.

## What it means for a normal person
- AI spending is a big reason growth is holding up; much of that spending is by a handful of big US tech firms, which are also large weights in typical index funds/401(k)s. IMF lists an AI profitability reassessment as a key risk.
- Higher oil feeds inflation (4.7% global forecast); IMF wants central banks to stay "sufficiently restrictive" - i.e., interest rates high enough to cool prices, which matters for loan and card rates.
- US is forecast as the fastest-growing G7 economy (2.3%).
