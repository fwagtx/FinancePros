## Definitions / mechanics
- Inflation = a broad, sustained rise in prices = falling purchasing power of each dollar.
- CPI-U: BLS index of prices paid by urban consumers for a basket of goods and services; reference base 1982-84 = 100. August 2026 NSA index 334.980. Shelter is the biggest category.
- Fed target: 2% over the longer run, measured by PCE price index; adopted January 25, 2012 (Statement on Longer-Run Goals and Monetary Policy Strategy, federalreserve.gov timeline).
- Fed tool: raise rates -> borrowing costs up -> spending cools -> price pressure eases (with a lag).

## Causes
1. Demand-pull: more spending than the economy can supply. 2020: M2 money supply grew about 25% (Gigafact; Atlanta Fed "M2 increased 25 percent over the past year", May 2021); St. Louis Fed: 26.9% y/y growth in February 2021, faster than the 1970s.
2. Cost-push: input costs rise (energy). June 2022 energy +41.6% y/y (BLS); Aug 2026 gasoline +27.4% y/y.
3. Money: Milton Friedman: "Inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon." (1963, Inflation: Causes and Consequences) -- one school of thought.
4. Expectations / wage-price spiral: if people expect prices to rise, they demand raises and firms raise prices; Fed's 2012 statement says the target helps keep expectations "firmly anchored".

## Purchasing power (BLS annual average CPI-U)
- 1970 avg 38.8; 2000 avg 172.2; 2025 avg 321.943.
- $1 in 1970 ~ $8.30 in 2025 prices (321.943/38.8 = 8.30); a 2025 dollar buys what about 12 cents did in 1970 (38.8/321.943 = 0.1205).
- Prices 2000 -> 2025: x1.87 (321.943/172.2).
- Rule of 72: years for prices to double = 72 / inflation rate. At 3% -> 24 years; at 2% -> 36 years.
- At 3.4% for 10 years: prices x1.397 ( ~40% higher); $100 buys ~$71.58 of today's goods.

## Worked example (hypothetical, exact math)
$10,000 in a savings account paying the FDIC national average 0.38% APY (FDIC national rate, August 2026 data; savingadvice.com Sep 9, 2026, Fortune Sep 3, 2026), while prices rise 3.4% over the year (the August 2026 12-month CPI rate):
- Year end balance: 10,000 x 1.0038 = $10,038
- Buying power in start-of-year dollars: 10,038 / 1.034 = $9,707.93 -> about $9,708; loss of about $292 of buying power.
- Same $10,000 at a hypothetical 4.0% rate: 10,400 / 1.034 = $10,058.03 -> about $10,058 (+$58).
- Real rate shortcut: nominal rate - inflation (0.38% - 3.4% ~ -3.0%).

## Tools people use (education, not advice)
- Know your real return/real raise: nominal minus inflation. Aug 2026 example: wages +3.1% vs prices +3.4% = real -0.3%.
- Savings rates: FDIC national average 0.38% APY (August 2026); rates vary widely by bank.
- I bonds (TreasuryDirect): composite rate = fixed + 2 x semiannual inflation + fixed x semiannual inflation. For bonds issued May 1 - Oct 31, 2026: fixed 0.90%, semiannual inflation 1.67% (3.34% annualized, CPI-U Sep 2025 324.8 -> Mar 2026 330.213), composite 4.26% (Treasury May 1, 2026 release). Rates reset each May 1 and Nov 1; a new rate for Nov 2026 - Apr 2027 issues was announced Nov 1, 2026 (unknown at research time; don't state). Fixed rate stays for 30-year life. Limit $10,000 per person per calendar year electronically. Can't redeem in first 12 months; cashing before 5 years costs the last 3 months of interest. (Paper-bond-by-tax-refund option discontinued Jan 1, 2025 per reports; don't mention.)
- TIPS (TreasuryDirect): 5, 10, 30-year terms; principal indexed to CPI-U; fixed interest rate paid every six months on the adjusted principal; at maturity you get the greater of adjusted or original principal. $100 increments. Hypothetical: $10,000, 2% rate, 3% inflation in a year -> principal $10,300, a year's interest at 2% = $206. (Inflation adjustments are generally taxable federally in the year they occur -- general knowledge, frame carefully or skip.)
- Social Security COLA: automatic yearly inflation adjustment (CPI-W, Q3 average vs prior Q3, rounded to 0.1%; can't go negative; first appears in January checks).
- Fixed-rate debt: a fixed-rate loan payment doesn't rise with inflation; variable-rate debt can rise when the Fed hikes.
- Long-term assets like stocks have historically outpaced inflation over long periods but fall in the short run; no guarantee. (General; not sourced with numbers -> keep vague or omit.)

## Surprising fact
- A dollar from 1970 buys only about 12 cents' worth of 2025 stuff (BLS CPI annual averages).
- Also: average paychecks rose 3.1% in the year to August 2026, but real hourly pay fell 0.3%.
