# 2026-11-28-hot research brief: Scam alert, fake charities on Giving Tuesday

Posts Saturday, Nov 28, 2026. Giving Tuesday 2026 is Tuesday, December 1, 2026 (after the posting date; first Tuesday after US Thanksgiving, Nov 26, 2026). Confirmed by Wikipedia/GivingTuesday and multiple nonprofit calendars (Neon One, Engaging Networks, days.to).

Distinct from the 11-27 short (disaster charities): no FTC $15.9B fraud-loss figure, no FTC red-flag list, no non-itemizer deduction, no IRS search tool.

## Angle / hook (timely)
GivingTuesday's own Data Commons estimates $4.0 billion was donated in the US on GivingTuesday 2025 (Dec 2, 2025), up 13% from 2024. That much money in one day attracts copycats. Hook: "Americans gave four billion dollars on Giving Tuesday 2025. Scammers noticed."

## Key facts (with dates)
- GivingTuesday 2025 (US): $4.0B donated, 38.1M participants (+6%), 19.1M gave money; cumulative since 2012: $22.5B. Source: GivingTuesday blog "2025 results" (Data Commons); NonProfit PRO; AP via Messenger-Inquirer/Barchart.
- GivingTuesday 2024 (US): $3.6B, up 16% from 2023; 36.1M participants. Source: GivingTuesday blog (Dec 2024); Philanthropy News Digest; NonProfit Times; Business Wire 2024-12-11.
- GivingTuesday 2023 (US): $3.1B, essentially flat vs 2022 (+0.06%); participants 34M, down 10%. Source: Impact Wealth; Fortune 2023-11-30; Neon One.
- 2021: $2.7B (+9% vs 2020); 2020: $2.47B (up >25% from $1.97B in 2019); 2019: initially reported $511M (online only), later revised to $1.97B (online+offline estimate). Sources: GivingTuesday blog (2020 results, Data Commons report); MinistryWatch; City & State NY; Wikipedia.

## How scammers piggyback (sourced)
- FTC consumer alert "Donate to charities (not scammers) on Giving Tuesday" (consumer.ftc.gov, November 2025): "Not everyone asking for money is a real charity"; year-end calls, texts and emails asking for donations; dishonest telemarketers use names that sound like well-known charities; safest ways to give are credit card or check; if the only way to pay is cryptocurrency, a payment app, gift card or wire, it's likely a scam; "Double-check links": pay attention to who's asking even if a friend posts it on social media; on a crowdfunding page "money will go directly to the organizer, who might not be closely tied to the cause." (Page blocked for direct fetch; text confirmed via two search results, incl. militaryconsumer.gov repost.) Earlier FTC Giving Tuesday alerts: Nov 2021, Nov 2023, Nov 2024 ("Give to a charity, not a scam").
- California AG Rob Bonta, Giving Tuesday charity consumer alert, Nov 27, 2023 (oag.ca.gov): don't trust unsolicited emails or texts; "anyone can make a QR code, buy a portable card reader and send a text message or make a graphic"; don't assume fundraising on social media is legitimate even if shared by someone you trust; crowdfunding pages formed overnight may lack experience/staff.
- North Carolina AG Jeff Jackson (ncdoj.gov) and Arizona AG Kris Mayes (azag.gov, Nov 2025), Illinois AG (Dec 1, 2025, Shaw Local): scammers impersonate legitimate charities with similar names; verify name, logo and email address because sham charities mimic legitimate ones. Texas AG also issued a Giving Tuesday alert.
- BBB (komonews/BBB, Give.org): fake charities use social media, crowdfunding sites and text messages; unauthorized campaigns impersonating real charities; text-to-give impostor scam example (BBB/News5 Cleveland). Give it directly through the charity's website (BBB wording).
- Lookalike pages: AGs' "verify name, logo and email" + Bonta "make a graphic" (sourced as above).

## Worked example (exact math)
GoFundMe (US) charges a 0% platform fee and a transaction fee of 2.9% + $0.30 per donation (GoFundMe pricing page/help center; confirmed by several fee guides 2025-2026).
- $50 gift on a real GoFundMe fundraiser: fee = 2.9% x $50 + $0.30 = $1.45 + $0.30 = $1.75. Amount to organizer = $50 - $1.75 = $48.25 (96.5%).
- $50 to a fake page: $50 lost, $0 reaches the cause (100% lost).
Note: FTC says crowdfunding money goes to the organizer, who may not be closely tied to the cause.

## Yardstick
BBB Wise Giving Alliance Standard 8: a charity should spend at least 65% of total expenses on program activities (program service expenses / total expenses). Source: give.org "BBB Standards for Charity Accountability".

## The surprising fact
Even on a legitimate crowdfunding platform, the money goes straight to the person who set up the page, not to a charity, per the FTC (Nov 2025).
