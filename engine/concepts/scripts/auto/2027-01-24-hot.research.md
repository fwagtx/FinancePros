# Research brief: Money myth - 0% credit utilization gives you the highest possible score
Slot: 2027-01-24-hot (Hot Take, Money Myth format, posts Sun Jan 24, 2027 6 PM Central). Researched Wed Nov 25, 2026. Evergreen; no news hook.
Note: myfico.com and fico.com could not be fetched from this session (network allowlist) and Firecrawl was out of credits. Facts below come from web-search excerpts of the official pages, each cross-checked against a second outlet. Angles NOT used (already covered): statement-date timing (2027-01-18 explainer), "paying in full builds credit" (2026-12-26 hot).

## Core facts
- **Amounts owed = 30% of a FICO Score.** myFICO "What's in my FICO Score / Amounts owed": amounts owed on accounts determines 30% of a FICO Score. Matches American Express Credit Intel, Bankrate.
- **Utilization definition.** myFICO: utilization is the amount of available revolving credit you are using at the time your score is calculated (reported balances / reported limits). Snapshot of what the issuer reported, not what you owe mid-month (myFICO; Nasdaq/Experian same point).
- **0% vs low utilization (the surprise).** myFICO FAQ (revolving accounts): FICO's research finds that people who report no revolving balances (0% utilization) are "slightly more risky" than people with a small reported balance / low utilization, so a score can drop slightly going from low to $0, all else equal. myFICO blog "Zero percent utilization vs low utilization": a $0 reported balance on every card may be interpreted as a lack of recent credit activity. myFICO press release (Business Wire, Jul 2, 2024): "a low utilization rate might be better for your FICO Score than 0% utilization"; a small purchase paid off in full can keep utilization low "without paying interest." Experian ("Is 0% utilization good for credit scores?") agrees: low single digits is ideal, 0% gives no added benefit.
- **The gap is small.** myFICO's wording is "slightly." No official point figure is published. Do not quote a points number.
- **High scorers use a little, not zero.** myFICO "Traits of the 800 FICO Score holder": on average, "exceptional" (800+) score holders use about 7% of their available credit (article undated). Experian 2026 data: consumers with 800+ FICO Scores average about 7% utilization in 2026, similar to prior years (Experian "How many Americans have an 800 credit score", 2026). Two sources agree: about 7%. (Caveat: an older FICO "high achiever" review cited by Kiplinger gave a 4% median for 800+; different stat/date. Script uses "about 7%" attributed to myFICO and Experian 2026.)
- **No memory in classic models, trended data in newer ones.** Classic FICO scores (e.g., FICO 8) score the balances on the report at the time the score is pulled; once a lower balance is reported, the utilization part reflects it (myFICO "at the time your score is calculated"; widely described as "utilization has no memory"). FICO Score 10T and VantageScore 4.0 use trended data looking back about 24 months, including average and highest utilization over the period (Experian "What is trended data in credit scores?"; Chase "FICO 10T vs VantageScore 4.0"; Milliman, May 18, 2026).
- **Interest never needed.** Paying the statement balance in full avoids interest (standard grace period); myFICO itself says a small purchase paid off can keep utilization low without paying interest. Takeaway: 0% vs low single digits is a small difference; carrying interest-bearing debt is never required.

## Worked example (our math)
- Total card limits $5,000; reported balance $150 -> 150 / 5,000 = 0.03 = **3%** utilization.
- Same cards all reporting $0 -> 0 / 5,000 = **0%**.
- If the $150 is paid in full by the due date, interest = $0.

## The surprise
Paying every card down to $0 before it reports can score slightly LOWER than letting a small balance report, per myFICO, and the 800+ crowd averages about 7% utilization, not 0%.
