## The story (all figures from China's National Bureau of Statistics release, Monday Oct 19, 2026, as reported by Reuters/AP/CNBC)
- Q3 2026 (July-September) real GDP: +4.5% year over year. Reuters poll forecast: 4.4%.
- Q2 2026: +4.3% y/y, described by Reuters as a more-than-three-year low (weakest since Q4 2022).
- Q1 2026: +5.0% y/y (NBS, April 16, 2026; China Daily/Xinhua). Q4 2025: +4.5% y/y.
- Quarter over quarter: +1.2% in Q3 (forecast 1.1%), vs 0.9% in Q2.
- First three quarters 2026: GDP 107.1 trillion yuan, +4.6% y/y. Beijing's 2026 target: 4.5% to 5%. (2025 full year: 5.0%.)
- September 2026 retail sales: +1.1% y/y (August: +0.4%; forecast ~0.5-0.6%); fastest since March.
- September 2026 industrial output: +5.5% y/y (August: +5.2%), in line with forecasts.
- Property (real estate development) investment, Jan-Sep 2026: -20.6% y/y (Jan-Aug: -19.9%).
- Fixed-asset investment, Jan-Sep 2026: -7.2% y/y.
- Analyst: ANZ's Raymond Yeung said risks remain tilted to the downside, citing weak domestic demand, deflationary pressure and the property downturn (paraphrase via Reuters coverage, not a verbatim quote).
- Coverage headlines (AP/CNBC): growth "picks up," "exports surge"; WSJ (via The Wire China): growth powered by exports while consumer confidence and property remain weak.

## The export engine (China customs data released Oct 14, 2026; AP, Bloomberg, SCMP, Fortune)
- September 2026 exports: +36.3% y/y to about $447.18 billion (Wind poll forecast ~25.7%). August: about +25%.
- September imports: +31% to about $313.65 billion.
- September trade surplus: about $133.53 billion (August: $119.1 billion).
- Jan-Sep 2026 trade surplus: about $938.1 billion; on track to beat 2025's full-year record of about $1.2 trillion.
- Customs official (Wang) attributed export growth to technology products such as chips and electronic components, on global demand for AI computing power and electricity infrastructure.
- AP: pressure mounting from trading partners (EU and others) for China to rebalance trade.

## Context / what it means
- China is the world's second-largest economy. Its Q3 rebound came mainly from selling abroad; home spending (retail +1.1%) and property (-20.6% investment) remain weak.
- When a big economy grows by exporting more than it imports, its trade surplus swells; trading partners (US, EU) push back over trade. For a normal person: much of what's on store shelves passes through this trade relationship; trade frictions affect tariffs, prices and jobs (general context; no prediction).
- Low-base caveat: September 2025 exports to the US had dropped sharply amid tariffs, so some 2026 y/y jumps are flattered by a weak base.

## What to watch
- Whether Beijing adds stimulus for consumers; Q4 GDP (mid-January 2027); trade talks with the US/EU; October trade data (early-mid November).
