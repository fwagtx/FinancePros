# Research brief: What happens if your employer goes bankrupt with your pension? (Hot Take, posts Oct 25, 2026)

- PBGC (Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation), a federal agency created by ERISA in 1974, insures private-sector defined benefit pensions.
- PBGC FY2025 Annual Report:
  - Protects about 30 million workers, retirees and beneficiaries; the single-employer program covers about 18.4M people in about 22,000 plans.
  - In FY2025, PBGC paid over $6.4B in benefits to nearly 926,000 retirees in PBGC-trusteed plans.
- 2026 maximum guarantee (single-employer plans terminating in 2026; PBGC tables): $7,789.77 a month ($93,477 a year) at age 65, straight-life annuity. It is lower if benefits start earlier and higher if later.
- Most retirees' vested benefits fall under the max, so they are paid in full (PBGC FAQs).
- 401(k)s: ERISA requires plan assets to be held in trust, separate from the employer's assets, so creditors can't take them in a bankruptcy (US Dept. of Labor).
- Worked example (illustrative): a pension of $9,000 a month at 65, minus the $7,789.77 cap = $1,210.23 a month not guaranteed.
- Chartable: PBGC max monthly guarantee at 65: 2025 $7,431.82; 2026 $7,789.77.
