## Facts (all dated)
- Brazil presidential runoff, Sunday Oct 25, 2026: Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva won 51.8% to 48.2% over Sen. Flavio Bolsonaro (Benzinga; BigGo Finance). Lula's fourth term (Yahoo Finance).
- Prediction markets Polymarket and Kalshi had priced Bolsonaro at roughly 80% before the vote (Yahoo Finance). Bolsonaro had unexpectedly finished first in the first round, and markets had rallied on expectations of a shift right (Globe and Mail).
- Analysts cited: traders unwinding bets on a Bolsonaro win and tighter fiscal policy; fiscal worries about Lula's campaign promises (Globe and Mail / wire; KuCoin flash).
- iShares MSCI Brazil ETF (EWZ), US-listed, Mon Oct 26, 2026: closed $34.15, down 19.82% (Motley Fool quote, 4:00 PM ET); "closed down 19.8%, the second-worst single-session loss in its 26-year history"; only worse: -23.1% on March 16, 2020 (Yahoo Finance). Intraday low -26.3%. Year-to-date gain shrank to 8.5% (Yahoo Finance). [A different Yahoo piece said ~28% -- intraday/premarket; closing 19.8% used.]
- Bovespa (Ibovespa), Mon Oct 26, 2026: closed down 13.88% at 176,608 points (Rio Times); "closed down 13.9 percent in its largest daily percentage drop since March 2020" (wire copy via Globe and Mail search snippet). Intraday low 169,516, about -17%.
- B3 circuit breaker: trading halted 30 minutes after the Bovespa fell 10% from its last close; first halt since the early-2020 Covid crash (Globe and Mail; Money Times; BigGo).
- Brazilian real, Oct 26 close: down 8.1% to 5.415 per US dollar (Globe and Mail wire); dollar +8.79% to 5.4154 reais (Rio Times) -- consistent. Intraday the real was down more than 11%.
- Brazil's Treasury cancelled debt auctions scheduled Oct 27-29 (Rio Times) [single source, not used].

## Context / what it means
- An election result that surprised markets repriced a whole country's assets in one day: stocks, the currency, and the US-listed fund that tracks them.
- For US investors, a single-country fund (EWZ) took the full hit, while a broad emerging-markets index holds Brazil as only about 4%, and the S&P 500 rose 0.7% the same day. This shows concentration vs diversification -- explained as math, not advice.
- Currency adds to it: a US investor in Brazilian stocks loses on both the stock fall and the real's fall in dollar terms.
