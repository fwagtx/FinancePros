## Lens company: Camden Property Trust (Houston apartment REIT)
- Size: "owns and operates 172 properties containing 58,759 apartment homes across the United States" (Q4 2025 release, Feb 5, 2026, SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1).
  FY2025 10-K: as of Dec 31, 2025, owned interests in, operated or was developing 175 properties / 59,921 apartment homes (incl. 1,162 under construction).
- What the "non-rent" money is: 10-K (FY2025, also FY2020/2023 wording identical): amounts for "other services considered non-lease components within a lease contract, primarily consisting of utility rebillings and other transactional fees." Camden combines them with rent on one income-statement line but discloses the split in each earnings release.
  CAVEAT: this is NOT all "junk fees" or profit; utility rebillings pass through some of Camden's own utility costs. Script must say "mostly utility rebills and fees".

### Worked example (OUR MATH)
- Share: 173.6 / 1,573.5 = 11.03% -> "about 11%" of Camden's 2025 property revenue came from non-lease services.
- Per home: $173.6M / 58,759 homes = $2,954 a year; / 12 = $246.2 a month -> "roughly $246 a month per apartment."
  Caveat: rough average. Unit count is the year-end operating count; includes utility pass-throughs; actual fees vary widely by property.

## The surprising fact
Roughly one dollar in nine that Camden collected from its apartments in 2025 (about $174M) was not rent, and it has stayed at about 11% every year since 2019.

## Regulation / enforcement (FTC, official releases, read directly)
- Jan 16, 2025: FTC and Colorado sued Greystar, "the nation's largest multi-family rental property manager" (manages 800,000+ units, owns interest in 100,000+). Complaint: advertised rents excluded mandatory fees; hidden fees "cost consumers ... hundreds of millions of dollars since at least 2019"; fees "allegedly range from tens to hundreds of dollars a month". Named fees: "valet trash" fees, package handling fees, utility fees, fees to distribute utility bills, "verification fees" for outside renters' insurance, media/smart home packages. Consumers "cannot opt out". Some fees revealed only after an application fee/holding deposit, deep in a 40- to 60-page lease. Consumer quoted: lease "quoted just over $1,000 ... I pay about $1,400 NOT INCLUDING UTILITIES". Greystar keeps a percentage of rent and fees it collects. Greystar denied wrongdoing (its statement: no resident pays a fee "they have not seen and agreed to in their lease").
- Dec 2, 2025: Greystar agreed to pay $24M ($23M to FTC, mostly for refunds; $1M to Colorado) and must clearly show total monthly price incl. mandatory fees, and disclose all fees before taking any payment such as an application fee. Stipulated order needs court approval (status of approval not confirmed).
- Dec 2025: FTC Chairman Ferguson directed staff to begin the process of proposing a rule on unfair/deceptive rental-housing fees (Real Deal, Dec 9, 2025; Frankfurt Kurnit). FTC's existing junk-fee rule covers only live-event tickets and short-term lodging. Status of any rental fee rule as of Nov 2026: not confirmed -> NOT used in script.
- Secondary-source fee ranges (NOT verified against the complaint text, NOT used on screen): valet trash often $25-35/mo, package concierge $15-20/mo, media/smart-home $75-175/mo.

## Other context (not in script)
- California SB 611 (Chapter 287, Statutes of 2024, signed Sept 2024): bans fees for serving notices and for paying rent by check; limits military-tenant deposits; effective dates reported as April 1 / July 1, 2025 (sources differ). The original advertised-rent-includes-fees provision appears to have been dropped from the enacted version -> uncertain, NOT used.
- 2024 NMHC/Grace Hill Renter Preferences Survey (fielded June-July 2023, 172,703 renters): 34% called no fees on debit/credit card rent payments "absolutely essential." Not used (time budget).
- CFPB: no standalone 2024 "junk fees in rental housing" report found; CFPB 2024 tenant-screening reports and Sept 2024 debt-collection report touch on rental fees. Not used.
- Invitation Homes $48M FTC settlement (Sept 2024) is single-family rentals, not apartments; not used.
