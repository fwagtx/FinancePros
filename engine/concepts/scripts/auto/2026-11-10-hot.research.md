# 2026-11-10-hot research: What happens if someone steals your credit card number?

## Rules (federal law)
- Credit cards (Truth in Lending Act / Fair Credit Billing Act, Regulation Z 12 CFR 1026.12(b)): maximum liability for unauthorized use is $50. If you report the loss before the card is used, $0. If the loss involves your credit card NUMBER but not the card itself, you have no liability for unauthorized use (FTC consumer advice "Lost or Stolen Credit, ATM, and Debit Cards").
- Disputes: to use the Fair Credit Billing Act billing-error process, send written notice so the issuer receives it within 60 days after it sent the first statement showing the charge (phone alone does not trigger FCBA rights; online if issuer accepts).
- Debit/ATM cards (Electronic Fund Transfer Act / Reg E): report before use = $0; within 2 business days of learning of loss = max $50; after 2 business days = up to $500; not reporting unauthorized transfer within 60 days after the statement is sent = possibly unlimited loss (FTC).
- Big card networks also have voluntary zero-liability policies (not needed for the script).

- 2024: 1.1 million identity theft reports total; credit card fraud was the top identity theft type. 449,032 / 1,135,270 = 39.6%.

## 2025 update (FTC full-year 2025 data, cited by Experian, 2026)
- 597,770 credit card fraud (identity theft) reports in 2025, up 33% from 449,094 in 2024 (revised); 1,358,253 identity theft reports in 2025. Note prior-year values differ slightly between Data Book editions.

## Worked example
Thief runs up $1,200 on your stolen card NUMBER (card still in your wallet): credit card -> you owe $0 of the $1,200.
Same $1,200 drained from a lost DEBIT card reported after 2 business days (within 60 days of the statement): you could lose up to $500; 1,200 - 500 = $700 back at minimum.

## Surprise
If only your credit card number is stolen, federal law says you owe $0, but a debit card reported late can cost up to $500 or more.
