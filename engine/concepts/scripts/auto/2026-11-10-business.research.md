# 2026-11-10-business research: How grocery stores make money charging brands for shelf space

## Core facts
- Slotting fees (slotting allowances): one-time payments a manufacturer makes to a retailer to get a new product placed on the shelf. They are part of a wider set of "vendor allowances" (volume rebates, advertising/promo money, slotting fees).
- FTC staff report (November 2003), case studies of 5 categories (fresh bread, hot dogs, ice cream/frozen novelties, shelf-stable pasta, salad dressing): for products that paid, average slotting allowances per item, per retailer, per metropolitan area ranged from $2,313 to $21,768 by category. Refrigerated products more likely to pay and paid more; direct-store-delivery products paid less. Slotting fees can be a large fraction of a new product's first-year revenue. (Data is early-2000s; no newer official public survey.)
- Ingles Markets 10-K, fiscal 2025 (year ended Sept 27, 2025): vendor allowances, "primarily composed of volume or purchase based incentives, advertising allowances, slotting fees, and promotional discounts", applied as a reduction of merchandise costs totaled $151.9 million (fiscal 2024: $146.9 million).
- Ingles fiscal 2025: net sales $5.33 billion; net income $83.6 million (fiscal 2024: $5.64B sales, $105.5M net income). Gross profit $1.27B, 23.9% of sales.
  -> Fiscal 2025 vendor allowances ($151.9M) were about 1.8x net income ($83.6M). 151.9/83.6 = 1.817.
  -> Net margin: 83.6 / 5,330 = 1.57%, about 1.6 cents per dollar of sales.
- Kroger 10-K: "recognizes all vendor allowances as a reduction in merchandise costs when the related product is sold." Albertsons 10-K: records Vendor Allowances as a reduction of Cost of sales.
- FMI (The Food Retailing Industry Speaks): food retailer net profit 3% in 2020, 2.3% in 2022, 1.6% in 2023, 1% in 2019.

## Worked example
One new salad dressing at one chain, 10 metro areas, at the top FTC category average ($21,768): 10 x 21,768 = $217,680. At the bottom ($2,313): 10 x 2,313 = $23,130.

## Surprise
In fiscal 2025, Ingles got more from vendor allowances ($151.9M) than it kept as profit ($83.6M).
