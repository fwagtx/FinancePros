# Research brief: LLC vs sole proprietor: what changes and what doesn't
Slot: 2026-12-26-explainer (posts Sat Dec 26, 2026, 12 PM CT). Researched Oct 27, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its year/source.

## Angle / hook
No breaking news needed. Evergreen myth: "forming an LLC lowers my taxes." By default it changes federal income and self-employment tax by $0 for a single-member LLC. What it changes is legal (liability shield), paperwork and state fees. Year-end (late December) is when many side-hustlers think about forming one for the new year.

## What does NOT change (federal tax, by default)
- IRS Publication 3402 (Taxation of LLCs): an LLC with only one member is "treated as an entity disregarded as separate from its owner for income tax purposes" unless it elects otherwise. Individual owner reports on Schedule C (Form 1040), files Schedule SE.
- Pub 3402: the owner "isn't an employee of the LLC... the owner is subject to tax on the net earnings from self-employment of the LLC which is treated in the same manner as a sole proprietorship."
- IRS Topic 554: "sole proprietor" includes the member of a single-member LLC that is disregarded; SE tax generally applies at $400+ of net earnings.
- Self-employment tax rate: 15.3% = 12.4% Social Security (up to the wage base) + 2.9% Medicare (no cap). (IRS Topic 554.) Additional 0.9% Medicare above $200,000 (single) — not used in script.
- Exception: a disregarded SMLLC with employees is treated as a corporation for employment taxes (wages paid on/after Jan 1, 2009) and uses its own EIN (IRS IRM 5.1.21). Not used in script.

## Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
Assumption: $50,000 net profit, single owner, no S-corp election, under the wage base.
- SE earnings = $50,000 x 0.9235 = $46,175 (Schedule SE multiplies net profit by 92.35%)
- SE tax = $46,175 x 0.153 = $7,064.78, about $7,065
- Same figure whether the business is a sole proprietorship or a default single-member LLC.
(Income tax on the profit is also unchanged by the LLC by default; not computed here.)

## What DOES change
- Liability (state law): an LLC generally shields the owner's personal assets from the business's debts and obligations. It does NOT shield: debts the owner personally guaranteed (common for leases/loans), the owner's own negligence or misconduct (professional malpractice exposure depends on state law; some states limit professionals' use of LLCs). Courts can "pierce the veil" when owners commingle funds, ignore formalities, undercapitalize or commit fraud; rules vary by state. Sources: UpCounsel, CorpNet, Haynie & Co. CPAs (secondary/legal-services blogs; consistent; general legal principle).
- Separate bank account / separate books expected (commingling is a veil-piercing trigger).
- State fees vary. One example: California FTB: "Every LLC that is doing business or organized in California must pay an annual tax of $800" until cancelled, even if inactive (ftb.ca.gov, LLC business type page). First-year exemption (AB 85) applied to LLCs formed 2021-2023 only per most sources (conflicting; not used). Plus an additional LLC fee if CA gross receipts exceed $250,000 (not used).
- Optional: an LLC can elect to be taxed as a corporation (Form 8832) or as an S corporation (Form 2553, if eligible). An S election changes how owner pay is taxed (salary through payroll plus distributions). Mention neutrally only; no advice.

## BOI / Corporate Transparency Act (status as of Oct 2026)
- FinCEN interim final rule (announced March 2025) exempted domestic companies and US persons from BOI reporting.
- FinCEN final rule issued Aug 11, 2026 (effective Aug 14, 2026) made that permanent: only entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in the US remain "reporting companies." (Sidley, Steptoe, Troutman, Adams and Reese, ABA Washington Letter.) Not used in the script to save time; low risk if mentioned as "US LLCs don't file BOI reports with FinCEN as of 2026."
- New York LLC Transparency Act (effective Jan 1, 2026) applies to foreign LLCs registered in NY (state law, separate).

## The surprising fact
Forming a single-member LLC, by itself, changes your federal tax bill by $0. Same Schedule C, same 15.3% self-employment tax. And in some states (California) it adds a yearly $800 tax.

## Weakly sourced / caution
- Liability limits (guarantees, own negligence, veil piercing) are from legal-services/CPA blogs, not statute; they are standard, widely agreed general principles but vary by state. Phrase as "generally."
- Form 2553 vs 8832 sequencing: sources conflict; script only says "an S corp election (Form 2553)".
