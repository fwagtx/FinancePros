## Facts (October 2026 Employment Situation, BLS, released Fri Nov 6, 2026, 8:30 AM ET)
- Nonfarm payrolls: +165,000 (seasonally adjusted) in October 2026. (CNBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance, Nov 6)
- Forecasts: Dow Jones consensus 59,000 (CNBC); Bloomberg survey ~70,000 (Yahoo); Reuters/FactSet 65,000. Bloomberg headline: "above all forecasts".
- Unemployment rate: 4.1%, down from 4.2% in September; forecast was 4.2%. (CNBC, Bloomberg, Yahoo)
- Labor force participation rose slightly (Yahoo).
- Revisions: September revised up to 60,000 from 29,000 (+31,000) (CNBC, Yahoo); August revised to 186,000 (+53,000) (CNBC only).
- Average hourly earnings: +0.4% month over month, +3.1% year over year (estimates 0.3% and 2.9%). (CNBC, Yahoo)
- Inflation context: CPI up 3.7% year over year in September 2026 (released Oct 14) (Yahoo Finance preview / Globe and Mail-AP preview; cpiinflationcalculator). AP: inflation-adjusted average hourly earnings fell 0.7% y/y in September, the sixth straight year-over-year drop.
- Fed context: before the report, CME FedWatch priced about a 70% chance of a 25-bp rate HIKE at the Dec 9 Fed meeting (CNBC live blog, Nov 5-6). Fed held at 3.75%-4% after its October meeting (BeInCrypto). Yahoo notes "the Federal Reserve's recent rate hikes".
- Hiring context (AP via Boston Globe, Nov 6): 2026 average before October was 68,000/month; 2025 averaged 9,700/month (weakest outside a recession since 2002); 2023-24 averaged 166,000; break-even pace now just over 5,000/month (Yale Budget Lab estimate).
- Markets context (CNBC): Thursday Nov 5 Nasdaq -5.5%, S&P 500 -4.4%, Dow -3.4%; futures rebounding Friday premarket.

## What it means for a normal person
- More hiring + lower unemployment = jobs somewhat easier to find, especially after a "low-hire, low-fire" stretch.
- Pay is rising 3.1% a year but prices rose 3.7% (through Sept), so average pay still trails inflation.
- A strong report can keep the Fed on track to raise rates (markets priced ~70% hike odds before the report), which would push up variable rates like credit cards and HELOCs. Not a prediction; describe as market pricing.
