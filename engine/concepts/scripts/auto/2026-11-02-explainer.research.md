# Research brief: Why fees quietly eat your acorns (expense ratios). Posts Nov 2, 2026 (Acorn Economics)

## Definition
- Expense ratio: the annual fee a mutual fund or ETF charges, as a percent of assets; taken out of fund assets, so it lowers your return every year (SEC Investor.gov).

## Data (Investment Company Institute)
- Asset-weighted average expense ratio, equity mutual funds: 1996 1.04%; 2000 0.99%; 2020 0.50%; 2022 0.44%; 2023 0.42%; 2024 0.40%; 2025 0.40% (down 62% since 1996).
- Index equity ETFs: 0.14% average in 2025.
- Sources: ICI "Trends in the Expenses and Fees of Funds, 2025" (2026); ICI news release "Mutual Fund and ETF Fees Remained Near Historic Lows in 2025"; ICI Fact Book.

## Worked example
- $10,000, 7% a year before fees, 30 years, fee subtracted from the yearly return:
  - no fee: 10,000 x 1.07^30 = $76,123
  - 0.1% fee: 10,000 x 1.069^30 = $74,017
  - 0.4% fee: 10,000 x 1.066^30 = $68,032
  - 1% fee: 10,000 x 1.06^30 = $57,435
- 0.1% vs 1%: $74,017 - $57,435 = $16,582.
- Surprise: a 1% fee takes about 25% of the no-fee ending balance (1 - 57,435/76,123 = 24.5%).
- The 7% is an illustration, not a prediction.
