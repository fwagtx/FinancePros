# Research brief: 2026-12-05-hot (Money Myth: bonuses are taxed at a higher rate than your salary)

Researched Tuesday, October 6, 2026. Posts Saturday, December 5, 2026, 6 PM CT. Evergreen; no news hook needed (year-end bonus season is the natural angle, stated without time words).

## The myth vs the fact
- MYTH: a bonus is taxed at a higher rate than salary.
- FACT: there is no separate federal "bonus tax rate." Bonuses are wages. IRS Pub 525: cash bonuses or awards from an employer for good work "must generally be included in your income as wages" and are shown on Form W-2. At filing, bonus + salary are added together and taxed at the same ordinary-income brackets.
- Why it looks higher: bonuses are "supplemental wages" (IRS Pub 15 (2026), section 7: bonuses, commissions, overtime, severance, awards, back pay, etc.), and employers often WITHHOLD on them differently than on regular pay. Withholding is a prepayment, not the final tax. The difference is settled at filing as a refund or a balance due.

## Withholding rules (IRS Pub 15, For use in 2026)
- Optional flat rate method: 22% federal income tax withholding on supplemental wages (if paid separately or identified separately, and income tax was withheld from regular wages in the current or prior year).
- Mandatory: supplemental wages over $1 million paid to an employee in a calendar year: the excess is withheld at 37% (highest rate), regardless of Form W-4.
- Aggregate method: employer combines the bonus with regular wages for the period and withholds on the total as if it were one regular paycheck, using the W-4 and Pub 15-T tables. (This can withhold more or less than 22%.)
- 22%/37% made permanent by P.L. 119-21 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act), which extended the TCJA rates (P.L. 115-97). Stated neutrally.
- Consistency note: the 2026-12-05 severance short (noon) says "Employers can withhold a flat twenty-two percent federal ... Real tax is settled at filing." This script matches that.

## 2026 brackets and deduction (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, released Oct 9, 2025), single filer
- 10%: $0 to $12,400
- 12%: $12,400 to $50,400
- 22%: $50,400 to $105,700
- (24% to $201,775; higher brackets above; not needed)
- Standard deduction, single: $16,100

## FICA (2026)
- 7.65% employee share: 6.2% Social Security (on wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, SSA) + 1.45% Medicare (no cap). Bonuses are wages for FICA, so the same 7.65% applies to salary and bonus (below the wage base). Nothing extra on the bonus.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL; assumptions stated)
Single filer, 2026 brackets, standard deduction only, no other income, credits or adjustments; employer uses the 22% flat method on the bonus; salary withholding assumed accurate.
- Salary $60,000; bonus $5,000; total wages $65,000.
- Taxable income without bonus: $60,000 - $16,100 = $43,900 (12% bracket).
- Taxable income with bonus: $65,000 - $16,100 = $48,900. Still below $50,400, so all $5,000 of bonus is taxed at 12%.
- Tax on taxable income $48,900: 10% x $12,400 = $1,240; 12% x ($48,900 - $12,400 = $36,500) = $4,380; total $5,620.
- Tax on $43,900: $1,240 + 12% x $31,500 = $1,240 + $3,780 = $5,020.
- Extra federal income tax caused by the bonus: $5,620 - $5,020 = $600 (= 12% x $5,000).
- Withheld on bonus at 22%: 0.22 x $5,000 = $1,100.
- Over-withheld: $1,100 - $600 = $500, which comes back as part of the refund (or shrinks a balance due).
- FICA on bonus: 7.65% x $5,000 = $382.50 (same rate as on salary: 7.65% x $60,000 = $4,590).
- Contrast: if taxable income were already in the 22% bracket (above $50,400), the bonus's federal income tax would be 22%, roughly equal to the withholding. A bonus that crosses $50,400 is split: only the part above $50,400 is taxed at 22% (marginal brackets).

## Surprising fact
The 22% that comes out of a bonus check is just a withholding estimate; for someone in the 12% bracket, the IRS hands much of it back at filing. A bonus is taxed exactly like salary.
