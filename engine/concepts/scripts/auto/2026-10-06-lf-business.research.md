## House edge (math, stable facts)
- American roulette: 38 pockets (1-36, 0, 00); single number pays 35 to 1. House edge 5.26%. European single-zero: 2.70%. (Wizard of Odds)
- WORKED EXAMPLE: bet $1 on every one of the 38 numbers = $38. One wins: paid $35 + your $1 back = $36 returned. Lost $2 of $38 = 2/38 = 5.26%. On single-zero wheel: 37 numbers, $37 bet, $36 back, $1/37 = 2.70%.
- Blackjack: 6-to-5 payout instead of 3-to-2 raises house edge by about 1.39% (0.0465 x (1.5 - 1.2) = 1.39%); a player gets a blackjack about 4.75% of hands in a 6-deck game. Example: $10 blackjack pays $15 at 3:2, $12 at 6:5. (Wizard of Odds; BJ21)
- Baccarat: house edge 1.06% banker (casino charges 5% commission on banker wins), 1.24% player, 14.36% tie. (Wizard of Odds; PlaySmart)
- Nevada Regulation 14.040: every gaming device must theoretically pay out at least 75% of amounts wagered. (Nevada Gaming Commission Regulation 14)

## Slot hold
- UNLV Center for Gaming Research: statewide Nevada slot hold was 7.15% in 2025 (fact-check) vs 6.55% in 2004; hold up 26% over the past 10 years; Las Vegas Strip highest average hold, 7.5%; Reno lowest 5.21%. NGCB said any tightening is within legal limits.
- Exact quote (UNLV report, via Las Vegas Review-Journal): "Hold percentage — the portion of money gambled that the casino retains — has a huge impact on casino revenues"
- WORKED EXAMPLE (churn): $100 bankroll, $1 per spin, 1,000 spins = $1,000 wagered (re-betting winnings). At 7.15% hold, average loss = $1,000 x 0.0715 = $71.50; you walk out with $28.50 on average (if all 1,000 spins are played). The edge applies to every dollar wagered, not the dollars you walked in with.
- NOTE: sources conflict on whether 6.55% is the 2004 hold or the 2004-2025 average, and on the Strip figure (7.5% vs 7.57%); the script uses only the 2025 statewide 7.1% and "tighter over the past decade".

## Company numbers: MGM Resorts International, FY2025 Form 10-K (filed Feb 11, 2026)
- Consolidated net revenues $17.5 billion (2024: $17.2B). Net income attributable $206 million (2024: $747M). Adjusted EBITDA $2.4 billion.
- Las Vegas Strip Resorts net revenues ~$8.4 billion, down 4%; casino revenue up 3%, rooms revenue down 9%, F&B down 4%.
- Las Vegas Strip Resorts slot handle $24,565 million; slot win $2,306 million; slot win % 9.4% (2024 9.4%).
- Table games drop $6,127 million; table games win $1,541 million; table games win % 25.2% (2024: 24.4%). Note: "drop" is cash/markers exchanged for chips, not total bets; chips get re-bet many times, so win% of drop is far higher than house edge.
- Occupancy 92%, ADR $249, RevPAR $229.

## Surprising fact: gaming is the minority of Strip revenue
- Nevada Gaming Abstract, fiscal year ended June 30, 2025 (51 Strip casinos grossing $1M+): total revenue $21.1 billion (-3.7%); gaming revenue $5.51 billion = 26.1% of total; room revenue $7.1 billion (-5.1%). Net income $154.2 million, down 81%. Interest paid $2.2 billion (10.6% of revenue). Occupancy 89.06%, ADR $250.72. (NGCB abstract via CDC Gaming, Las Vegas Sun/Vegas Inc, Bettors Insider, June 2026)
- So on the Strip, rooms ($7.1B) out-earned gaming ($5.5B) in FY2025.

## History
- March 19, 1931: Gov. Fred Balzar signs Assembly Bill 98, legalizing wide-open gambling in Nevada during the Depression. (History.com; Mob Museum)
- 1955: Legislature creates the Nevada Gaming Control Board. (NGCB About Us)
- 1962: Edward Thorp publishes Beat the Dealer (card counting). Casinos responded with multiple decks, earlier shuffles, barring counters; the book also drew many new (mostly losing) blackjack players. (Wikipedia; Blackjack Apprenticeship)

## Customer takeaway (education)
- The house edge is the price of play; it is charged on every dollar wagered, so time and speed of play set the cost. Casino loyalty comps are funded from expected (theoretical) loss.
