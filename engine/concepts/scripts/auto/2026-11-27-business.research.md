# 2026-11-27-business — How freight brokers make money on the spread (How They Make Money)

Research date: October 2, 2026. Posts Friday, November 27, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its period.
Note: direct fetches of sec.gov, investor.chrobinson.com, q4cdn, dat.com, globenewswire, etc. were blocked by the network proxy; every figure below was taken from search-result excerpts of the cited filings/releases, cross-checked where possible.

## The model in one line
A freight broker sells a truck ride it doesn't own. The shipper pays the broker a rate; the broker hires a trucking company (carrier) for less; the gap is the broker's "spread". C.H. Robinson reports that gap as **adjusted gross profit (AGP)** = revenue minus purchased transportation (direct costs), excluding amortization of internally developed software (non-GAAP; defined in the FY2025 10-K).

## Company: C.H. Robinson (CHRW), the biggest US broker. Segment: NAST (North American Surface Transportation: truckload, LTL, other)

### FY2025 (10-K for the year ended Dec 31, 2025, filed Feb 2026)
- NAST total revenues: **$11,562,714 thousand (~$11.56B)**
- NAST adjusted gross profits: **$1,706,329 thousand (~$1.71B)**; up 4.0% from $1,641.2M in 2024 (Q4 2025 release / search excerpts)
- NAST AGP margin, FY2025 (computed): 1,706.329 / 11,562.714 = **14.76% ≈ 14.8%**
- Q4 2025 NAST AGP margin: 14.6% (up 20 bp); truckload rate per mile and cost per mile both +2.5% y/y in Q4 2025 -> flat AGP per mile (Q4 2025 earnings release)
- Company total revenues 2025: $16.2B (down 8.4%)
- Owns very little transportation equipment and doesn't employ the drivers; "more than 450,000" transportation providers on its platform in 2025, mostly contracted motor carriers (FY2025 10-K, via search excerpt)

### Pricing mechanism (C.H. Robinson 10-K, MD&A / business description)
- Customers sign contracts for an estimated number of shipments at pre-determined rates; most commitments are for one year or less.
- Robinson buys the majority of its truckload capacity on a spot-market or transactional basis, even for contract customers.
- So: sell price is mostly locked; buy price floats. When carrier costs jump faster than locked prices, the spread shrinks; when costs fall, it widens.
- Evidence, FY2021 10-K: purchased transportation rose 48.0% in 2021 vs revenue +45.5%; truckload linehaul cost per mile (ex fuel) rose ~26% in Q3 2021 -> margin compression.

## WORKED EXAMPLE (hypothetical, based on the real FY2025 NAST average)
- Shipper pays the broker: $1,000 for a load
- Broker pays the carrier: $852
- Spread: $1,000 - $852 = $148 -> $148 / $1,000 = **14.8%**, matching Robinson's 2025 NAST average AGP margin (14.76%).
- Real loads vary widely; this is an average, not a typical single load.

Spot linehaul still >30% above August 2025 for all three equipment types. (One excerpt says contract $2.42; most say $2.41 with a 22-cent gap.)

## THE SURPRISE
The biggest US freight broker owns almost no trucks, yet kept about $1.7 billion of the $11.6 billion shippers paid its North American unit in 2025. Its margin is lowest when trucking is booming (2021: 12.4%), because it promised shippers a price and then had to pay carriers market rates.

## Neutral framing
Education only. No forecast of rates, margins or the stock. Do not say the August 2026 gap will raise margins.
