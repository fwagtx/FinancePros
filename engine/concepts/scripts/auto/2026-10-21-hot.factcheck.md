# Fact-check: 2026-10-21-hot ("The Job That Charges You")

Checked Oct 1, 2026 for posting Oct 21, 2026. Only the script JSON was read. ftc.gov, consumer.ftc.gov and the news sites were blocked for direct fetch, so the checks rely on search-result excerpts of the FTC pages and reputable outlets.

## Verified
- **2020-2024 reported job-scam losses (FTC "job scams and employment agencies"):** $90M (2020), $131M (2021), $179M (2022), $286M (2023) and $501M (2024) match the FTC Data Spotlight (Dec 2024) and the FTC 2024 Consumer Sentinel release (Mar 2025): "jumped from $90 million to $501 million." The FTC also says 2020 to 2023 was "more than threefold"; 286/90 = 3.18, which is consistent.
- **Math:** 501/90 = 5.57x, so "more than five times bigger in four years" (line 4) and the "over 5x" note are correct.
- **Check example (line 6):** $2,000 check, $1,500 sent on, check bounces, you're out $1,500. The arithmetic is correct and the example is labeled ("For example", "Check (example)"). The on-screen values match the spoken ones.
- **Pay-first asks (line 5):** equipment, training, and deposits to unlock earnings (task scams) all match FTC guidance and the Dec 2024 Spotlight.
- **Sub-label lengths:** every label is 28 characters or less. The longest are 22 characters: "Real job: they pay you", "Scam job: you pay them" and the hook title. The money labels are "Check (example)" (15) and "You're out" (10). The split items are 15 or less and the list items are 21 or less.
- **Chart:** on-screen values match the narration. No advice, price predictions or political opinion.

## Issues

1. **Stale data: 2025 data now exists (FAIL).** The FTC released its 2025 Consumer Sentinel data in March 2026: a record $15.9B in fraud losses, up about 27% from $12.5B. The FTC data also shows $150.4M in business/job-opportunity losses in Q4 2025 alone (25,002 reports, median loss $2,000). The script still treats 2024 as the latest year in the hook (line 1), the cover, the chart (lines 3-4) and all post text, and it cites "FTC (March 2025)". Posting this on Oct 21, 2026 would present 19-month-old data as current.
   - **Fix:** pull the full-year 2025 "job scams and employment agencies" loss figure from the FTC 2025 Consumer Sentinel Data Book (March 2026). Add a 2025 point to the chart and rebase the hook, cover, end label, "over Nx", "in N years" and post text on 2025. Update the sources to "FTC Consumer Sentinel 2025 Data Book (March 2026)". I could not retrieve that exact subcategory number because the FTC pages were blocked. It must be pulled from the Data Book itself; do not estimate it from quarterly figures.

2. **The FTC quote is not word-for-word (line 7 text, say and quote scene; post ig).** The FTC job-scams page reads: "Honest employers, including the federal government, will never ask you to pay to get a job." The script drops ", including the federal government," without marking the cut.
   - **Fix:** use the full sentence. Alternatively, quote only the fragment: The FTC says honest employers "will never ask you to pay to get a job."

3. **Hook wording (line 1).** "Cost people $501 million" leaves out the word "reported". The spoken "five hundred one million dollars" is awkward and takes about 3.5 seconds.
   - **Fix:** after updating to 2025, say for example: "People reported losing $X million to fake jobs last year." Or open on the twist: "This job pays you negative fifteen hundred dollars." Say "five hundred and one million" (or the new figure) naturally.

4. **Unsupported generalization (line 8).** "People who spot it notice one thing" is an invented claim about behavior, and it also reads awkwardly.
   - **Fix:** "The giveaway: they ask you to pay before your first paycheck."

5. **Minor nits:**
   - The split scene's mood is "happy" on a scam topic; "think" would fit better.
   - The tags include "task scam", but the video only mentions task scams indirectly ("deposit to unlock pay"). That is acceptable.

## Time words
None of the script's own time words become wrong on the posting date. The only time problem is that the data is stale (issue 1).

VERDICT: FAIL
