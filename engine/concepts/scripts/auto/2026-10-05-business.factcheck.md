# Fact-check: 2026-10-05-business ("Why restaurants love selling you drinks")

Posting date: Oct 5, 2026. Checked Oct 1, 2026. Line numbers are 1-indexed positions in `lines`.

## Verified claims
- **L2, McDonald's Investor Day, Sept 23, 2026: specialty drinks "high margin".** Confirmed. The Investor Day material (8-K exhibit 99.1, transcript, Restaurant Dive, CNBC) calls the specialty beverages "high margin with low complexity". The paraphrase is fair and it is not presented as a direct quote. Supporting context: McDonald's targets 1.5 points of beverage market-share gains by 2030, the drinks over-index with Gen Z, and McDonald's said "Beverages are no longer just an add-on, but a reason to visit." That supports "betting big". The event is 12 days old on the posting date, so it is not stale.
- **L3, pour cost definition.** Correct: ingredient cost divided by the drink's price.
- **L4, benchmarks of 18-24% for bars and 28-35% for kitchens.** These match common industry guides (Vanta Insights, Purimax, restaurant inventory blogs, and others). They are labeled as industry guides in the caption ("Industry guides say"), in the narration and in the post. The chart sub-labels read "bar pour cost" and "kitchen food cost". The chart title "Typical cost targets" is acceptable.
- **L5-L6, worked example.** Labeled "Example"/"Say". Math is correct: 12 x 0.8 = 9.60, 12 x 0.7 = 8.40, difference 1.20. "Before staff or rent" is a good caveat.
- **L7, BLS ground beef series APU0000703112 (100% beef, U.S. city average).** August 2026 is $6.923, which matches. Chart points are August values for each year: Aug 2022 $4.94, Aug 2023 $5.08, Aug 2024 $5.577 and Aug 2025 $6.318 all match search-result excerpts of the BLS/FRED data. One excerpt gave Aug 2022 as $4.47, but others gave $4.94, which is consistent with the script. Year over year: +$0.605 (+9.6%). Since Aug 2022: +40%.
- **L8, Flanigan's FY2025 10-K (FY ended Sept 27, 2025).** Package store gross margin is 25.12% (26.60% in FY2024). Restaurant food and bar gross margin is 66.61% (65.57% in FY2024). Both match.

## Issues
1. **L8 to L9: the evidence does not support the conclusion (substantive).** The Flanigan's comparison is liquor *stores* against *restaurants with food and bar combined*. It does not separate drink margin from food margin. If anything, it shows that serving adds margin, not that drinks out-earn food. L9 "So drinks often carry the profit" is presented as following from it and generalizes one company's data. Fix: reword L8 to say what the data shows, for example "Same alcohol, different margin: Flanigan's liquor stores kept 25.1% after product costs, its restaurants (food and bar combined) kept 66.6%." Reword L9 to rest on the benchmarks, for example "Drinks usually cost less to make than food, so menus push them." Or drop the Flanigan's beat.
2. **Cover/hook payoff is partial.** The cover "Why McDonald's Bets on Drinks" and hook L1 promise a McDonald's answer. The video gives one line on McDonald's and then switches to *bar alcohol* pour-cost benchmarks and a bar-and-restaurant chain. McDonald's specialty drinks are non-alcoholic (refreshers, crafted sodas, energy drinks), so the 18-24% bar pour cost is not McDonald's economics. The header "Why restaurants love selling you drinks" fits the body better. Fix: either make the cover and hook the general one ("Why restaurants make more on drinks than food"), with McDonald's as the news peg, or pay off McDonald's specifically. For example, add a line: "McDonald's wants 1.5 points more beverage market share by 2030, and says the drinks are high margin and simple to make." Also add a bridge after L2 that labels the benchmarks as generic, for example "Across the industry, drinks are cheaper to make than food."
3. **L8: on-screen and spoken values differ.** The caption shows 25.1% and 66.6%; the narration says "about twenty-five percent" and "about sixty-seven". Fix: say "twenty-five point one percent" and "sixty-six point six percent".
4. **L7: chart labeling.** The chart points are August values, but the x-axis shows bare years, which reads as annual averages. Fix: retitle the chart "Ground beef, August price per lb (BLS)". Also, "Food keeps climbing, too" is a weak bridge to the drinks argument. Consider "Meanwhile, burger meat costs ~10% more than a year ago" (+9.6% YoY is verified).
5. **L1: opening is OK but could be stronger.** It is a true question, about 2 seconds long. A stronger version is specific and newsy: "McDonald's just told investors its new drinks are high margin." Or, to pay off the header: "Your $12 cocktail costs the bar about $2.40 to make." That is a hypothetical at the 20% target, so label it as an example.
6. **L5: label the example as hypothetical.** Make the caption "Hypothetical: a drink and a burger both cost $12."

No advice, price predictions or political opinion. No time words go wrong on Oct 5 ("In September 2026" and "August 2026" are both fine). The disclaimer is present. Post text is consistent with the script. The post's "Flanigan's FY2025 10-K: ... restaurants (food and bar) 66.6%" is fine.

Sources: McDonald's 2026 Investor Day press release / 8-K exhibit 99.1; stockanalysis.com MCD Investor Day transcript; Restaurant Dive; CNBC (Sept 23, 2026); BLS/FRED APU0000703112; Flanigan's FY2025 10-K (SEC); Vanta Insights, Purimax and other pour-cost guides.

VERDICT: FAIL

### Remaining issues
1. **L8, "Same alcohol, different margin" is misleading (substantive).** The 66.6% restaurant figure is food and bar combined, so it is not a margin on the same alcohol. The lead-in tells viewers the opposite of the qualifier that follows it in the same sentence, and the beat still does not show drinks beating food. Fix: replace the lead-in with "Selling it as a meal pays more:" or simply "In fiscal 2025, Flanigan's liquor stores kept 25.1% after product costs; its restaurants, food and bar combined, kept 66.6%." Alternatively, cut the Flanigan's beat. The post text has no "same alcohol" wording, so it is fine.
2. **L1 and cover, "make more on your drink than your food" (moderate).** This is true per dollar of sales (margin %), but not necessarily in dollars: a $3 soda does not out-earn a $12 burger. Fix the L1 caption and narration to "Restaurants often keep more of each dollar on your drink than on your food." The cover "Why Drinks Out-Earn Food" is acceptable as a short title if L1 is clarified. "Why Drinks Pay Better Than Food" is safer.
3. **L7, "hit" (minor).** "Hit $6.92" implies a peak. Use "averaged $6.92 a pound in August 2026", which matches the post.

The math is correct. There is no advice, prediction, political opinion or wrong time word, and the disclaimer is present.
