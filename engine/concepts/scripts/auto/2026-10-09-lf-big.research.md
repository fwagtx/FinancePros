## 2. Run-rate explained
- Run-rate / annualized revenue = recent period's revenue x 12 (or x 4). A snapshot, not cash collected over a year.
- Example (hypothetical): a lemonade stand sells $100 in its best month -> "run-rate" $1,200, even if it sold $400 all year.
- OpenAI's own forecast (FT via Reuters, Sept 19, 2026): revenue of $36B for 2026, rising to $350B in 2030 -- shows the gap between a year-end run-rate (~$50B) and full-year revenue ($36B).
- Gross vs net example (hypothetical): $100 sale through a cloud partner that keeps $20 -> gross counter books $100, net counter books $80. Same sale, different "revenue."

## 4. History: the 2000 dot-com bust
- Nasdaq Composite closed at a record 5,048.62 on March 10, 2000; bottomed at 1,114.11 on October 9, 2002 -> -77.9%, "about 78%". It did not regain the peak until April 2015. (Multiple sources: Financial History magazine, Wikipedia, encyclopedia)
- October 9, 2002 bottom = exactly 24 years before the posting date.
- Cisco: briefly world's most valuable public company on March 27, 2000 (record close $80.06). Shares didn't close above that until Dec 10, 2025 ($80.25) -- about 25 years later -- even though revenue nearly quintupled since 1999. Its P/E was 96.7 in late 1999 vs about 19 forward in Dec 2025. (Barchart, Sherwood News, Slashdot/Bloomberg)
- Lesson (not a prediction): a real, growing business can still be a stock that was priced too high; booms can be real technology AND have overbuilding.
