# Research brief: Employer match formulas decoded: "50% up to 6%" (Explainer, posts Nov 20, 2026)

Researched Oct 2, 2026. Evergreen; no news hook needed. Latest data: Vanguard How America Saves 2026 (released June 16, 2026; covers 2025 plan data), Fidelity Q2 2026 Retirement Analysis (published Aug/Sept 2026; data as of June 30, 2026), PSCA 68th Annual Survey (2024 plan year, published Nov-Dec 2025), IRS rules.
Note: many source pages (Vanguard, Fidelity, PSCA, plansponsor) were blocked for direct fetch from this environment; figures confirmed via search results quoting those sources, cross-checked across 2+ outlets where possible.

## Key facts
- Vanguard How America Saves 2026 (2025 data):
  - Most common single match formula: 50% of contributions on the first 6% of pay (max employer match = 3% of pay). It is the most frequently used single formula; reported in ~12% of plans offering a match (estimated 2025 data). Sources: Vanguard HAS 2026 via search summaries, calculatorflux/TheStreet citing Vanguard.
  - Average maximum promised match: 4.7% of pay, a record; median 4.0% (plans with single- or multi-tier match formulas). Sources: Vanguard press release June 2026 ("Matching contributions have risen over time to a record 4.7%"), TheStreet (June 2026). Prior year (HAS 2025, 2024 data): average 4.6%, median 4%.
  - Plan mix (as reported in search summaries; year mapping less certain): about half of plans offer match only, 36% match + nonmatching, 10% nonmatching only. Not used on screen.
- Fidelity Q2 2026 Retirement Analysis (data as of June 30, 2026):
  - Average employer contribution rate: 4.8% of pay. Employee contribution: record 9.6%. Total savings rate: 14.4% (record, held for second consecutive quarter).
  - 81.2% of 401(k) savers contributed enough to get their employer's full match.
  Sources: about.fidelity.com Q2 2026 Retirement analysis; NAPA-Net (Sept 2026); PSCA news (Sept 2026); TheStreet.
- PSCA 68th Annual 401(k) Survey (2024 plan year): 81.3% of plans have some match (52.5% match only + 28.5% match and nonmatching); average maximum available match 4.7%; employers spent 3.4% of eligible payroll on matching; 44.1% of plans with matching use immediate vesting for the match (up from 39.7% in 2023). Source: PSCA news Nov-Dec 2025.
- IRS rules:
  - Safe harbor basic match: 100% of the first 3% of pay deferred + 50% of the next 2% deferred = max 4% of pay. Non-QACA safe harbor matching contributions must be 100% vested immediately (QACA: within 2 years).
  - Employee's own deferrals are always 100% vested.
  - Other employer matching contributions must vest at least as fast as: 3-year cliff (100% after 3 years of service) or 6-year graded (20% per year from year 2, 100% after 6). Source: IRS "Issue Snapshot - Vesting schedules for matching contributions"; IRS 401(k) plan overview.

## The surprising fact
"50% up to 6%" sounds like a 50% match, but the most the employer adds is 3% of pay, and only if the worker puts in at least 6%.

## Worked examples (hypothetical, exact math)
Assume $60,000 salary, formula "50% up to 6%":
- Worker contributes 6%: 0.06 x 60,000 = $3,600. Match = 0.50 x 3,600 = $1,800 (= 3% of pay). Total going in: $5,400.
- Worker contributes 3%: 0.03 x 60,000 = $1,800. Match = 0.50 x 1,800 = $900 (1.5% of pay). Difference vs 6%: $900 a year of match not received.
- Worker contributes 10%: match still capped at 0.50 x (6% x 60,000) = $1,800.
Safe harbor "100% of first 3% + 50% of next 2%" at $60,000 with 5% contributed: 1.00 x 1,800 + 0.50 x 1,200 = 1,800 + 600 = $2,400 = 4% of pay (max).
