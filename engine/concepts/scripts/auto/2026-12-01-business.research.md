# How They Make Money: trash companies and the landfill (research brief)
Prepared Oct 2, 2026 for posting Dec 1, 2026. Evergreen: every figure carries its fiscal year/date. No timely news hook used.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. WM (Waste Management) FY2025 10-K, revenue by line of business (gross, BEFORE intercompany eliminations), year ended Dec 31, 2025:
   - Collection (commercial + industrial + residential + other): $17,569M (2024: $16,801M)
   - Landfill: $5,347M (2024: $4,958M); of the 2025 landfill figure, $1,566M was intercompany (WM's own trucks/transfer stations paying WM landfills) -> about 29% of landfill revenue is "paid by itself"
   - Transfer: $2,629M (2024: $2,448M)
   - Recycling processing and sales: $1,866M (2024: $1,890M)
   - Total company revenue (net of intercompany): $25,204M in 2025
   - Source: WM FY2025 10-K (filed Feb 2026); confirmed via two separate search queries quoting the 10-K. sec.gov and investors.wm.com could not be fetched directly.
2. WM landfills: owned or operated 253 solid waste landfills (plus 4 hazardous waste landfills) as of Dec 31, 2025. Weighted average remaining landfill life about 38 years on permitted airspace (about 39 years including expansion airspace). Seeking expansion permits at 15 landfills. Source: WM FY2025 10-K. 253 also cited in WM's June 2026 investor presentation (per search).
3. WM internalization of waste (based on disposal costs): 69.4% in full-year 2025 (68.8% in 2024); 70.1% in Q4 2025. Source: WM Q4/FY2025 earnings release, Jan 28, 2026. WM 10-K: internalization "generally allows us to realize higher consolidated margins and stronger operating cash flows."
   - WM landfill depletable tons: 128.8M in 2025 (125.0M in 2024).
4. Republic Services FY2025 10-K (filed Feb 18, 2026): 207 active landfills; about 67% of total solid waste volume collected in 2025 went to landfills it owns or operates (about 67% in 2024 too). Note: Republic measures by volume, WM by disposal cost, so they are not identical metrics.
5. Internalization (definition): the company's own trucks haul trash to its own landfill, so the disposal fee (the "tipping fee") is kept in-house instead of paid to a rival dump.
6. Tipping fee: what a hauler pays per ton to unload ("tip") at a landfill. EREF (Environmental Research & Education Foundation) 2025 analysis: U.S. MSW landfill average (unweighted) $63.48 per ton in 2025; $52.62 in 2018 (+20.6%). EREF 2024 analysis: $62.28/ton national average (a 10% jump); large landfills charge over $70. Private landfills charge ~34% more than public ones (EREF). Sources: Waste Today, Waste Advantage, Recycling Monster coverage of EREF reports.
7. Moat: Republic's 10-K: permits for new landfills or expansions "have become more difficult and expensive to obtain," "often take years," and face resistance from citizen groups and political pressure.

## Surprising fact
WM's trucks bill more ($17.6B gross in 2025), but every truck must dump somewhere. WM sends about 69% of its waste (by disposal cost) to its own 253 landfills, and about $1.6B of its $5.3B landfill revenue in 2025 came from its own operations.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
A 10-ton truckload at the 2025 EREF U.S. average of $63.48/ton: 10 x $63.48 = $634.80 in tipping fees.
If the truck and the landfill have the same owner, that $634.80 stays inside the company.

## Caveats / weakly sourced
- WM segment figures were read from search-engine extracts of the 10-K (sec.gov blocked); landfill $5,347M and intercompany $1,566M confirmed by two queries; collection $17,569M by two queries.
- EREF 2024 ($62.28) vs 2025 ($63.48) implies ~2%, while coverage says ~4% YoY for 2025; possibly different landfill samples/weighting. Script uses only the 2025 figure.
- WM does not disclose a landfill-only operating margin; internalization rate used instead.
