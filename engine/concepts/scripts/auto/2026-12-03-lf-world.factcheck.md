# Fact-check: 2026-12-03-lf-world (YouTube long-form, posts Dec 3, 2026)

Topic: Spanish silver, and how Potosí's mines created the first world currency. Checked October 4, 2026 using about 22 searches and fetches. Line numbers are 0-based indexes into `lines`.

## Issues that must be fixed

1. **Line 22 (with line 21's headline): the cost covers only the roof phase, not the five-year restoration.** The Art Newspaper piece (Aug 26, 2026, "Bolivia's most visited museum kicks off five-year restoration project") exists. In that report, about $513,000 from the IDB pays for the *roofs*: 3,178 sq m of roofing over about 150 days, with the museum staying open. The Central Bank of Bolivia pays for the rest. Only the full restoration is expected to take five years. Bolivian outlets give the IDB figure as Bs 5.9 million.
   - Fix (text/say): "The first phase, repairing its leaking roofs, costs about $513,000, financed by the Inter-American Development Bank. The full restoration is expected to take five years."
   - Fix (scene): label "Roof repairs, phase 1" and sub "IDB-funded".

2. **Line 21: "That old mint still stands" is wrong.** The building that still stands, the Casa Nacional de Moneda, is the second mint. It was built 1759-1773 to a design by Salvador de Villa. The 1572 mint from line 19 was a different building.
   - Fix: "Its 1700s successor, built to replace the original mint, still stands. In August 2026, The Art Newspaper called it Bolivia's most visited museum."

3. **Lines 78, 80 and 91: the legal-tender timeline starts too late.** The Act of Feb 9, 1793 ("An act regulating foreign coins, making them legal tender...") first made Spanish milled dollars legal tender, at 100 cents. The 1806 act mainly dealt with foreign gold coins. Spanish dollars were legal tender from 1793 until the Act of Feb 21, 1857 (which is correctly stated), so 64 years, not "half a century" from 1806.
   - Fix line 78: "A 1793 law made foreign coins like it legal tender." Timeline event: "1793 / Foreign coins OK".
   - Fix line 80: "So for more than 60 years, Spanish and Mexican silver was legal money..." Caption: "Legal tender, 1793-1857".

## Should fix

4. **Lines 33-34: the "hundreds of thousands" estimate has no source I could find.** Arzáns did claim 8 million deaths (History Today/Lane review), and Galeano made the figure famous. Kris Lane calls 8 million impossible given the size of Andean society then. I found no source for the specific rival estimate "hundreds of thousands" (also the right-hand sub on line 33).
   - Fix, unless the brief names a specific historian: "Historians such as Kris Lane say that number is impossible for the Andes' population at the time. But the real toll was still heavy." Right-side value "Lower", sub "Still heavy".

5. **Line 64: "a third" slightly overstates the estimate, and "sent" is the wrong verb.** Barrett (1990, cited by Flynn & Giráldez) estimates Japan *produced* about 30% of the world's silver in the 1500s. That figure is about production, not exports.
   - Fix: "Japan sent silver too. By one estimate it produced about 30 percent of the world's silver in the fifteen hundreds." Caption: "Japan: ~30% of world silver".

6. **Line 58: the ratios are quoted from another historian.** The 1:5.5-7 versus 1:12.5-14 ratios, and the line "the value of silver was twice as high in China as in Spain", are Flynn & Giráldez quoting Han-sheng Chuan (1969).
   - Fix: "Citing historian Han-sheng Chuan, Dennis Flynn and Arturo Giráldez conclude silver was worth about twice as much in China."

7. **yt_desc sources: the cited works don't contain the arbitrage example.** The 11 oz -> 2 oz gold -> 22 oz example is in Flynn & Giráldez, "Arbitrage, China, and World Trade in the Early Modern Period" (JESHO, 1995). It is not in the Journal of World History "Born with a Silver Spoon" article.
   - Fix: add that citation. Also, the "CHAPTERS" heading in yt_desc has no timestamps. Fill it in or remove it before posting.

## Minor / style (not FAIL reasons)

- **Line 31: who turned the wheels is uncertain.** "turned the mint's giant wooden wheels alongside mules": the sources say mules powered the rolling mills and enslaved men drove the mules. Safer: "...and worked the giant wooden rolling mills, driven by mules."
- **Line 12: the population figure varies by source.** 160,000 is a commonly cited figure, but the Encyclopedia of Latin American History says ">100,000 by 1600". The label "Historian estimate" hedges it acceptably. Optionally say "well over 100,000, perhaps 160,000".
- **Line 13: "one of the biggest anywhere on Earth" is a stretch,** since Ming-era Chinese cities and others were far larger. Suggest "one of the largest in the world at the time".
- **Line 69: "its specs" is vague.** The yuan was modeled on the *Mexican* silver dollar, and the yen was set to match it. Suggest "modeled on the Spanish and Mexican silver dollars".
- **Line 52 say: lowercase "china".** Cosmetic only; capitalize it.
- **Line 0 hook: true, specific and under 3 seconds.** "Copied" slightly overstates things, since the dollar was defined by the Spanish coin's value and silver content, not its design. Slightly stronger and more exact: "Your dollar was defined by a Spanish silver coin." This is acceptable as written.

## Verified OK

- **Coinage Act 1792:** the quote in line 1 is word-for-word ("each to be of the value of a Spanish milled dollar as the same is now current, and to contain three hundred and seventy-one grains and four sixteenth parts of a grain of pure..."). 371 4/16 = 371.25, and 371.25/480 = 0.7734 troy oz.
- **Hamilton based the dollar's silver on a Treasury assay of worn Spanish dollars:** OK.
- **Coinage Act of Feb 21, 1857 ended foreign coins' legal-tender status:** OK.
- **BEP on the $ sign:** "most widely accepted explanation" that $ evolved from "Ps" for pesos, with the S written over the P. OK.
- **Two bits:** 2 x 12.5 cents = 25 cents. OK.
- **NYSE:** became fully decimal on Jan 29, 2001. "Often traced to the Spanish dollar" is properly hedged.
- **Hamilton 1929 QJE, Table A** ("Average annual imports of treasure in pesos by ten-year periods", gold and silver, all of Spanish America, registered at Seville). All 16 chart points match: 286,932 / 437,750 / 234,521 / 1.118M / 2.093M / 3.573M / 5.070M / 5.832M / 10.641M / 13.923M / 11.162M / 10.928M / 10.393M / 6.685M / 5.107M / 2.131M.
  - "About 235,000" in the 1520s, "13.9M" in the 1590s and "2.1M" in the 1650s are correct.
  - 13.92 / 0.2345 = 59.4, so "roughly 60 times" is OK.
  - The chart title "American treasure into Spain" is accurate for gold plus silver.
- **Hamilton's silver total:** "16,632,648.20 kilograms of pure silver" registered 1503-1660, so 16.6M kg is OK. Hamilton put smuggling at roughly 10-50% of registered amounts, so "real totals were higher" is OK.
- **Potosí:** discovered 1545, with Diego Huallpa as the traditional discoverer.
  - The peak year was 1592, at about 7.1 million oz declared at the treasury office.
  - Ore was refined in about 80 mills along the Ribera.
  - The mint was founded 1572.
  - Mercury amalgamation was introduced from Mexico on Toledo's orders, and Huancavelica supplied the mercury.
  - "Vale un Potosí" is correct.
- **Mita:** Toledo, 1573, about 13,500 men a year from 16 provinces (some sources say 13,400). OK.
- **Enslaved Africans at the mint:** mostly men from Angola and Congo (Lane). OK.
- **Barrett:** 85% of the world's silver came from the Americas, 1493-1800. OK.
- **Flynn & Giráldez ratios:** Canton 1:5.5-7 and Spain 1:12.5-14, from 1592 to the early 1600s. Line 3 compares 12.5 / 5.5 = 2.27, which is "about twice". OK.
- **Arbitrage example:** 11 / 5.5 = 2 oz gold, and 2 x 11 = 22 oz of silver, so the silver doubles before costs. It matches the authors' own example.
- **China and the Pacific route:**
  - Manila was founded in 1571, the date Flynn & Giráldez give for the birth of world trade.
  - Urdaneta found the return route in 1565, and the last galleon sailed in 1815, so 250 years is correct.
  - The single whip reform was applied nationwide in 1581 under Zhang Juzheng.
  - Ming paper money had collapsed.
- **Yuan and yen:** both mean "round". OK.
- **Mexico City mint:** 1535, the first in the Americas. OK.
