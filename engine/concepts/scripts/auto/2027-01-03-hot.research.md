# Research brief: 2027-01-03-hot (Hot Take, Money Myth)
Topic: "New Year's money resolutions fail because of willpower."
Researched Wednesday, Nov 4, 2026. Posts Sunday, Jan 3, 2027, 6 PM Central. Evergreen: every number dated.
(Avoids the "84% of budgeters blew it / month-two trap" stat used in a recent video.)

## Angle
Myth: resolutions fail because people lack willpower. Fact: the research on defaults and automation shows that the
setup (what happens if you do nothing) moves behavior far more than motivation does. Same kinds of people, very
different results, depending on one setting. Timely hook: posting right after New Year's (the "fresh start" moment).

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. Fidelity 17th Annual New Year's Financial Resolutions Study (published Dec 11, 2025; online survey of 3,026 US adults):
   - 64% of Americans considering a financial resolution for 2026, up from 56% for 2025.
   - Top resolutions for 2026: save more money (44%), pay down debt (36%), spend less (30%).
   Sources: Fidelity/Business Wire release Dec 11, 2025; Fox Business coverage.
2. Vanguard, How America Saves 2026 (25th edition, data as of year-end 2025):
   - Plans with automatic enrollment: participant-weighted participation rate 94%, vs 64% in voluntary (opt-in) plans.
   - About 61% of Vanguard-administered plans had adopted automatic enrollment at year-end 2025 (79% among plans with 1,000+ participants).
   - Employees under 25: 25% join voluntarily vs 90% under automatic enrollment.
   - Earning $15,000 or less: 14% voluntary vs 77% automatic.
   Sources: Vanguard HAS 2026; InvestmentNews, PlanSponsor, NAPA coverage (June 2026).
3. Madrian & Shea (2001), Quarterly Journal of Economics 116(4): at a large US firm, 401(k) participation among new hires
   (3-15 months tenure) was about 86% under automatic enrollment vs about 37% before it. (A Treasury summary cites 49% to 86%
   for a different comparison; not used on screen.)
4. Thaler & Benartzi (2004), "Save More Tomorrow," Journal of Political Economy 112(S1): workers pre-committed to put part of
   future raises into savings; 78% of those offered joined; participants' average saving rate rose from 3.5% to 13.6% over 40 months.
5. Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014), "The Fresh Start Effect," Management Science 60(10): Google searches for "diet", gym visits and
   goal commitments (stickK) all rise at temporal landmarks such as the new year, new month, new week. (Context only.)

## Worked example (our math)
$50 moved automatically every paycheck, paid every two weeks: 26 paychecks x $50 = $1,300 a year (before any interest).

## Surprising fact
Same kinds of workers, same paperwork: 94% join when they're enrolled by default vs 64% when they must opt in (Vanguard, 2025).
The thing that changed was the default, not anyone's willpower.
