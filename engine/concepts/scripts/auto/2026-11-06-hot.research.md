# Research brief: Why are houses so expensive (posts Nov 6, 2026)

## Key numbers
- Harvard JCHS State of the Nation's Housing 2026 (June 17, 2026): price-to-income ratio 4.7 (5th straight rise), vs about 3.2 average in the 1990s; existing prices up 54% since 2020; payment on median-priced home ~$3,100/mo in Q4 2025 vs $1,700 in early 2020; income needed >$120,000 vs $66,000 in 2020.
- NAR August 2026 (Sept 10, 2026): median existing-home price $429,100 (+1.6% y/y; 38th straight annual gain); sales 3.98M SAAR; inventory 1.62M; 4.9 months supply.
- Census new-home median Q2 2026 $410,700.
- Freddie Mac PMMS: 30-year fixed 7.03% (Sept 24, 2026), highest of 2026.
- Freddie Mac: housing shortage about 3.7 million units (as of Q3 2024).
- Census: real median household income 2025 $87,460 (record).

## Worked example
$429,100 price, 10% down -> loan $386,190, 30 years.
- At 7.03%: about $2,577/mo principal and interest.
- At 3.00%: about $1,628/mo. Difference ~$949/mo.
