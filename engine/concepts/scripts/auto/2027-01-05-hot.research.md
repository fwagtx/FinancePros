# 2027-01-05-hot research brief: Face-off: HOA neighborhood vs no HOA
Researched Nov 6, 2026. Evergreen; every number dated. No timely news hook used (evergreen face-off).

## Key facts
- HOA price premium: Clarke & Freedman, "The rise and effects of homeowners associations," Journal of Urban Economics vol. 112 (2019), pp. 1-15: houses in HOAs are priced on average at least 4%, or about $13,500, above observably similar houses outside HOAs (near-national data). Premium larger where local government provides less; varies by location; largest when HOA is new.
  - Supporting: Meltzer & Cheung (2012, Lincoln Institute working paper, Florida data): HOA premium around 5-7%.
- Who pays: US Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2024 (story "Condo or Homeowners Association Fees Topped $500 Monthly for About 3 Million Households," Sept 2025): about 21.6 million of 86.6 million owner-occupied households paid a condo or HOA fee in 2024 (about 1 in 4; our math 21.6/86.6 = 24.9%). National median monthly fee: $135 (with a mortgage $120; without $184). About 3 million households paid more than $500/month; about 5.6 million (26%) paid under $50.
- Scale: Foundation for Community Association Research (CAI): 77.1 million residents in about 369,000 community associations (Fact Book 2024 estimate); 2025 Statistical Review reported ~373,000+ associations (78.1 million residents per secondary summaries; not confirmed from the PDF).
- Mortgage: Fannie Mae Selling Guide defines monthly housing expense (PITIA) as principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any owners' association dues (plus special assessments); this housing expense counts in debt-to-income (DTI). (Official page text not fully loaded; confirmed via secondary sources quoting the Guide.)
- Foreclosure: In most states an HOA can place a lien and foreclose over unpaid dues/assessments (Nolo, Redfin, Clark.com). Roughly 16-22 states plus DC give part of the HOA lien "super lien" priority over the mortgage (usually 6-9 months of dues). Some states set minimums (California: generally $1,800+ or 12 months delinquent before foreclosure). Rules vary by state and community.
- Pros: shared amenities (pools, parks), maintained common areas, rules that protect neighborhood look (and the price premium above). Cons: fees that can rise, special assessments for big repairs, rules and fines, foreclosure power.

## Worked example (our math)
- $300 per month HOA fee x 12 months x 10 years = $3,600/yr x 10 = $36,000 over 10 years (fee flat; real fees can rise).
- Compare to premium: 4% on a $337,500 home = $13,500 (consistent with study's $13,500).

## The surprising fact
In most states an HOA can foreclose on a home over unpaid dues, and in about 20 states part of that lien can even jump ahead of the mortgage.
