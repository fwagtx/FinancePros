# Research brief: How Berkshire Hathaway works (2026-12-08-lf-business)

Posts Tue Dec 8, 2026. Written Oct 9, 2026. Berkshire's Q3 2026 10-Q (early Nov 2026) will be out by posting day, so EVERY figure below is dated ("in 2025", "at the end of June 2026"). No "today/now/latest" phrasing for numbers.

Note on sourcing: berkshirehathaway.com and sec.gov could not be fetched directly from this environment; figures were confirmed via search results quoting the 10-K, 10-Q, annual letter and earnings release, cross-checked across outlets (Reuters/AP via local papers, CNBC, Morningstar, Insurance Business, Motley Fool, Yahoo Finance).

## Angle / hook
Berkshire invests a giant pool of money that is not really its own: insurance float, about $177.5 billion at June 30, 2026 (Berkshire Q2 2026 10-Q / earnings release, Aug. 2026). In 2025 its insurers also made an underwriting profit ($7.26B after tax), so that money cost less than nothing.
Hook line: "Berkshire invests $177 billion that isn't really its own." Cover: "Other People's Money."

## History (facts)
- 1965: Warren Buffett takes control of Berkshire Hathaway, a struggling New England textile maker (performance table starts 1965).
- 1967: buys National Indemnity (and National Fire & Marine), Omaha insurers, for about $8.6 million (Motley Fool, Weekend Investor; some sources say $8.4-8.5M -> say "under $9 million"). First step into insurance.
- 1967: Berkshire paid its only dividend ever: 10 cents a share (Motley Fool, Sept. 2026; dividendpower). Buffett later joked he must have been in the bathroom (secondhand; do not show as a quote).
- 1996 (Jan. 2): completes purchase of the rest of GEICO for $2.3 billion; it had owned about half (Berkshire filings).
- 2010 (Feb. 12): buys the 77.5% of BNSF it did not own for $26.5 billion (Berkshire 2010 10-K).
- Jan. 1, 2026: Greg Abel becomes CEO; Buffett remains chairman (board vote May 2025; PBS/AP, CNBC). Abel's first annual letter, Feb. 28, 2026: Buffett remains chairman and comes to the office five days a week (Reuters via newstribune, Mar. 1, 2026). Buffett born Aug. 30, 1930 (96 on posting day). At the May 2, 2026 annual meeting Abel took the stage; Buffett sat with the board (Yahoo/Reuters).
- Jan. 2026: closed ~$9.4B purchase of OxyChem (some sources $9.7B -> say "about $9.4 billion" or avoid). July 24, 2026: closed ~$6.8B purchase of homebuilder Taylor Morrison (Q2 2026 10-Q).

## The machine: insurance float
- Float = premiums collected now, claims paid later. Insurer holds and invests the money in between.
- Float: $39M (1970), $237M (1980), $1.632B (1990), $27.871B (2000), $65.832B (2010) (Buffett's letter table, quoted by Artemis and Motley Fool); $88B (2015), $171B (2024), ~$176B (2025) (Berkshire 2025 earnings release via Insurance Business, Mar. 2026); ~$177.5B at June 30, 2026 (Q2 2026 release, via XTB, Yahoo, others).
- 2025 insurance underwriting earnings (after tax): $7.26B, down from $9.02B in 2024. Insurance investment income 2025: $12.51B (vs $13.67B in 2024) (Insurance Business, Mar. 2026).
- Combined ratio 2025: 87.1% (agencychecklists headline; single source -> do not show).
- Underwriting profit means the float cost less than nothing in 2025: Berkshire was effectively paid to hold it.
- Q2 2026: after-tax underwriting earnings fell about 13% to ~$1.73B; GEICO underwriting profit $994M, down ~45% (Coverager, quarter). Float up $1.1B to $177.5B.

### Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
Insurer collects $1,000 of premiums in January, pays $950 of claims and costs over the year (hypothetical 95% combined ratio). Underwriting profit = $1,000 - $950 = $50. Meanwhile it invests the $1,000 at an assumed 4% for the year: $1,000 x 4% = $40. Total: $50 + $40 = $90, with no money of its own put in. If claims were $1,020 instead, underwriting loss = $20; it still earns $40 - $20 = $20: the float cost 2% but earned 4%.

## Operating businesses (2025, after-tax operating earnings, Berkshire 2025 10-K / annual report)
- Total operating earnings 2025: $44.49B (2024: $47.44B).
- Manufacturing, service and retailing: $13.65B (2024: $13.07B), up 4.4%. (Brands well known: See's Candies, Dairy Queen, Duracell, Fruit of the Loom, Clayton Homes, Precision Castparts, Lubrizol.)
- BNSF railroad: $5.48B (2024: $5.03B), up 8.8%. Revenue roughly flat.
- Berkshire Hathaway Energy: $3.98B, up 6.7%.
- Insurance underwriting: $7.26B. Insurance investment income: $12.51B.
- Net earnings 2025 $66.97B incl. investment gains and $8.255B impairment on Kraft Heinz and Occidental. Berkshire says quarterly investment gains are "usually meaningless" as a guide.
- Employees: about 387,800 at end of 2025 (2025 10-K).
- Q2 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026): operating earnings ~$13.0B vs $11.2B a year earlier (up ~16%); MS&R $4.47B, BNSF $1.56B, BHE $891M. Net earnings $25.7B.
- Abel's letter flagged BNSF as needing improvement and called Kraft Heinz disappointing.

## Stock portfolio (13F for June 30, 2026, filed Aug. 14, 2026)
- 13F equities: about $299 billion (Morningstar, insiderset).
- Net buyer in Q2 2026: about $19.8-20B net purchases, after 14 straight quarters of net selling (Morningstar, CNBC).
- Top holdings, end of June 2026 (aggregators; % of 13F value): Apple ~22% (~228M shares, ~$66B); American Express ~17%; Coca-Cola ~11%; Alphabet ~9% (bought ~$15B+ in Q2); Bank of America ~9% (trimmed for 5th straight quarter).
- Apple: Berkshire trimmed Apple in Q4 2025 (CNBC, Feb. 17, 2026); share count flat in Q2 2026.

## Cash pile
- Cash, cash equivalents and T-bills (insurance and other, net of payables, Berkshire's measure): $373.3B at end of 2025 (Abel letter / Reuters); $359.2B at June 30, 2026 (Q2 2026 10-Q). Broader measure peaked ~$397.4B at March 31, 2026 (10-Q) -> keep to $359.2B on screen.
- Most of it is short-term US Treasury bills.
- Buyback floor: no repurchases if consolidated cash, equivalents and T-bills would fall below $30 billion (10-Q).

## Buybacks / no dividend
- Repurchased $4.8B of stock in first half 2026, ~$4.5B in Q2 (10-Q; CNBC).
- Buybacks only when price is below intrinsic value (Abel letter, Feb. 2026; buybacks restarted the following week).
- Abel letter: no cash dividends as long as each retained dollar is "reasonably likely" to create more than a dollar of market value.
- Abel said he would use all his after-tax pay to buy Berkshire stock while CEO (Reuters, 2026).

## Long-run record (chartable / comparison) - 2025 annual letter table
- Compounded annual gain, per-share market value, 1965-2025: 19.7%. S&P 500 with dividends: 10.5%.
- Overall gain 1964-2025: Berkshire 6,099,294%; S&P 500 with dividends 46,061% (Motley Fool, Yahoo; letter table).
- Worked example: $100 at end of 1964 -> $100 x (1 + 60,992.94) = $6,099,394, about $6.1 million. S&P: $100 x (1 + 460.61) = $46,161, about $46,000.
- Recent decade: book value per share grew 12.4%/yr vs S&P 500 14.8%/yr (Morningstar analysis of 2025 letter; attribute to Morningstar).
- 1965-2024 (previous letter): 19.9% vs 10.4%.

## Surprising fact
Berkshire has paid exactly one dividend in its history: 10 cents a share in 1967.

## Risks
- Size: the bigger it gets, the harder to beat the market (Buffett has said so for years). Past decade book value growth trailed S&P (Morningstar).
- Insurance: catastrophes; GEICO profit fell ~45% in Q2 2026.
- Utilities: wildfire liability at PacifiCorp (BHE).
- Succession: first years after Buffett as CEO.
- Holdings concentration: top 5 holdings ~68% of the 13F portfolio (sum of approx %).

## Customer angle
GEICO drivers pay premiums (the float), BNSF shippers pay freight, BHE utility customers pay power bills, and people buy See's chocolates and Dairy Queen. Shareholders get no dividend; value shows up (or not) in the share price.
