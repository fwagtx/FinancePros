# Research brief: How moving companies price a move (posts Fri Dec 25, 2026, evergreen)

No timely news hook needed; evergreen explainer. Note: FMCSA site and HireAHelper could not be fetched directly from this environment; figures confirmed via search results quoting the official pages / regulation text (eCFR via Cornell LII, J.J. Keller).

## How the price is built
- Local moves: billed by the hour (crew size x hours, often door to door), usually with a minimum. This Old House (2025): most local movers charged $50-$150/hour, often with 2-4 hour minimums. Another mover guide: local crews bill per half hour with a two-hour minimum. Rates vary widely by source and are quoted per crew or per mover.
- Long-distance/interstate moves: priced mainly on shipment weight and distance (linehaul), per the mover's tariff (This Old House; FMCSA booklet: non-binding final bill based on actual weight, services and tariff).
- Extras ("accessorial services"): FMCSA glossary: services such as packing, appliance servicing, unpacking, or piano/stair carries, charged in addition to line haul. Mover accessorial forms itemize shuttle service (access restrictions), elevator, long carry, stair carry. Fuel surcharges are common in tariffs (industry practice; not separately verified with a number).
- Peak season: May through September is typically considered peak U.S. moving season (Thumbtack). No reliable source quantifying the summer premium was found -> don't put a number on it.

## FMCSA rules (interstate moves) - 49 CFR Part 375
- Binding estimate (49 CFR 375.403): written agreement in advance that sets the total cost for the listed services; after loading, the carrier can't collect more than the binding estimate (except for added services / changes covered in the rule).
- Non-binding estimate (49 CFR 375.405): must say it is not binding and must state the shipper "will not be required to pay more than 110 percent of the non-binding estimate at the time of delivery." FMCSA consumer page: mover is required to bill remaining charges not collected at delivery 30 days after delivery. Maryland AG: 110% cap does not excuse paying the full charges.
- Binding not-to-exceed: an industry term (not an FMCSA category); price capped at estimate, customer pays less if the shipment weighs less (HireAHelper, MoveBuddha, FreightWaves guides). Not every mover offers it.
- Impracticable operations charges at delivery capped at 15% of other charges due at delivery (FMCSA).
- Booklet: "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move" = Appendix A to 49 CFR Part 375; movers must give it to interstate customers.

## Valuation (FMCSA "Protect Your Move")
- Released Value Protection: no additional charge; mover liable for no more than 60 cents per pound per article. Customer must sign a statement choosing it.
- FMCSA's own example: a 10-pound stereo component worth $1,000 -> mover liable for no more than $6.00 (10 lb x $0.60).
- If you don't select released value, the shipment moves at Full Value Protection by default (mover liable for repair, replacement or cash value; cost varies by mover, deductibles possible). An FMCSA document: if no higher value declared, goods deemed worth $6.00 per pound, minimum $6,000 (not used in script).

## Average costs (label source + method)
- This Old House, 2025 survey of 1,000 moving customers: all moves avg $3,020; local $1,489; long-distance $3,129 (another TOH page says $3,124 - minor discrepancy, so not used on screen).
- HireAHelper (its own booking data; date not shown on page): U.S. local full-service avg $1,167.84; labor-only $451.90.
- Moving.com via SoFi (Dec 2025): long-distance avg about $4,890. AMSA (via Lugg, 2024 data): local ~$1,250, long-distance ~$4,300 - secondhand only.
- Takeaway: averages differ widely by method. Not used as a core on-screen number.

## Chartable data series (REAL)
Hostage-load complaints filed with U.S. DOT/FMCSA (FMCSA spokesman via AARP Fraud Watch, 2021):
- 2017: 364
- 2018: 409
- 2019: 495
- 2020: 899
- 2021: 932 through July 25 (partial year; not charted)
Later: FMCSA launched "Operation Protect Your Move" on April 4, 2023, a nationwide crackdown citing a rise in complaints about movers holding goods for extra fees. FMCSA OPYM 2024 report: 2024 sweep (Apr 1-19, 2024) addressed 380 HHG complaints incl. 128 hostage complaints.
Hostage-load penalty: up to $10,000 civil penalty per violation (49 U.S.C. 14915); registration suspension 12-36 months possible.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
Local move example: 3 movers x $45 per mover-hour x 5 hours = $675 before travel/truck fees. ($45/mover-hour is within the $25-$50 per mover range cited by a mover pricing guide; illustrative only.)
Valuation example (FMCSA's): 10 lb x $0.60 = $6.00 for a $1,000 stereo component.

## Surprising fact
The free "released value" coverage pays by weight, not value: 60 cents per pound per article, so FMCSA's own example is a $1,000 stereo paying $6.

## Weakly sourced / caveats
- Hourly rates vary widely by source; the $45 per mover figure is an assumption for the example.
- Hostage complaint counts come from an FMCSA spokesman quoted by AARP (2021), not a dataset download.
- Peak season premium unquantified.
