## Facts (September 2026 Producer Price Index, BLS, released Oct 15, 2026)
- Final demand PPI rose 1.3% in September 2026 (seasonally adjusted). Largest monthly advance since +1.7% in March 2022. (BLS release; ABA Banking Journal; Investing.com; Globe and Mail/Reuters "fastest rate in more than four years"; Reuters via rallies.ai "largest gain in 4-1/2 years")
- 12 months ended September 2026 (unadjusted): +6.3%, the largest 12-month increase since +6.4% in December 2022. (BLS release text quoted in search; Globe and Mail/Reuters gives 6.3%. ABA journal printed 6.4%, apparently the Dec 2022 comparison.)
- August 12-month: 5.4% as first reported Sept 10 (BLS); Reuters says revised 5.6%. Not used on screen.
- Final demand goods +2.6% in September (BLS text via search summary; one summary says largest since series began Dec 2009 -- NOT used on screen). WEAKER: single-path sourcing.
- Energy was the main driver: Reuters headline "Rising energy costs lift US producer prices in September"; Finimize "Energy was the driver". BLS: final demand energy +10.8% (search summary only; not used on screen).
- Services +0.8%; PPI less foods, energy and trade services +0.5% in Sept (search summary only; not used).
- Economists expected smaller gains (consensus roughly +0.5% to +0.6% m/m) -- not used.
- Context: second inflation report in two days after September CPI (Oct 14). Fed hiked Sept 16 (passing mention only, not used).
- What PPI is: BLS measures the average change over time in selling prices received by domestic producers for their output. Higher producer costs can be passed to consumers or absorbed in margins (general explanation, not a prediction).
