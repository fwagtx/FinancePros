# Research brief: 2026-10-19-hot (Why is baby formula so expensive?)

Format: "Why is X so expensive". Evergreen; every number dated. Education only; no brand recommendations, no price predictions.

## Timely angle (not used as hook)
- No US price news this month strong enough to anchor an evergreen short. 2026 formula news is about safety (FDA "Operation Stork Speed" review; ByHeart and Nara Organics infant botulism recalls; 2025-26 ARA oil contamination event abroad). UK prices rose sharply in 2026 (The Grocer), but that is the UK, not the US. Not used.

## Confirmed facts
1. Market concentration: As of 2022, four manufacturers (Abbott, Mead Johnson/Reckitt, Nestle/Gerber, Perrigo) made about 90% of US infant formula (CNBC, May 24, 2022, reporting on the FTC inquiry; Wikipedia "2022 US infant formula shortage" cites 89%). The FTC's March 2024 report "Market Factors Relevant to Infant Formula Supply Disruptions - 2022" describes the market as highly concentrated among these four for decades. (Some outlets cite 97% for the top four; we use the more conservative "about 90%".)
2. 2022 shortage: Abbott's Sturgis, Michigan plant recall (February 2022) triggered the 2022 shortage (FTC 2024 report).
3. Tariffs and imports: Most-favored-nation US tariff on infant formula is 14.9% to 17.5% depending on content; above tariff-rate-quota thresholds, duties on common formulas rise to $1.035/kg + 14.9%. 2012-2021: average effective duty on dutiable formula imports was 25.1% (CRS Insight IN11932, 2022). Imports were about 2% of US formula consumption (PolitiFact, May 24, 2022).
4. FDA rules: all infant formula marketed in the US must meet the same federal nutrient requirements (FD&C Act sec. 412(i), 21 CFR 107.100: 30 nutrients with minimums, maximums for some), and makers must notify FDA before marketing a new formula (FDA consumer Q&A; FDA industry FAQ). Applies to store brands and name brands alike.
5. WIC: the largest US buyer of infant formula; each state awards a sole-source contract to one manufacturer for the lowest net price after rebate (USDA ERS; GAO-25-106503). ERS estimated 57-68% of US formula was bought through WIC in 2004-06; GAO puts WIC at an estimated 44% in 2022. Rebates average roughly 85%+ of wholesale price (ERS). WIC rebate savings: $1.6 billion in 2023 (GAO, USDA estimates).
6. GAO-25-106503 (Jan 14, 2025), analysis of 2018-2023 retail sales: winning a WIC contract caused an average 1.7% price increase for the contract formula products, about 30 cents for a typical 12-ounce container of powder, for non-WIC shoppers; 0.3% for other same-brand products. GAO calls it "modest".
7. Feeding amount: AAP (HealthyChildren.org) says about 2.5 ounces of formula per pound of body weight per day; most babies average no more than about 32 ounces a day.
8. Price ranges (weak sources, used only to set example assumptions): store brands roughly 10-20 cents per prepared ounce; Similac/Enfamil standard roughly 20-35 cents per prepared ounce (parentcalc.com 2026; organiclifestart.com cost comparison).

## Worked example (exact math, hypothetical)
- 12-pound baby x 2.5 oz per pound = 30 oz a day (AAP guideline).
- 30 oz x 30 days = 900 prepared ounces a month.
- Example price A (store brand-range assumption): $0.12 per prepared oz x 900 = $108 a month.
- Example price B (name brand-range assumption): $0.25 per prepared oz x 900 = $225 a month.
- Difference: $225 - $108 = $117 a month. Both must meet the same FDA nutrient requirements.

## The surprising fact
- Every infant formula sold in the US, store brand or name brand, must meet the same federal nutrient requirements; and WIC's sole-source contracts nudge the winning brand's shelf price up only about 1.7% (GAO).
