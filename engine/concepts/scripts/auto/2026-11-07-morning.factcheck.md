# Fact-check: 2026-11-07-morning (Fed G.19 consumer credit, September 2026)

Checked Sat Nov 7, 2026, ~6:15 AM CT. federalreserve.gov and a direct pymnts.com fetch were blocked here (DNS). I read the PYMNTS article (Nov 6) and the Aktualita.co article (Nov 7) through Firecrawl, and checked them against search snippets from Investozora and an X post.

## Verified (no change needed)
- Total consumer credit rose about $27.8B in September (Aktualita: $334.1B SAAR flow / 12 = ~$27.8B; PYMNTS: "$28 billion increase"). The annual rate was 6.4%, up from 1.9% in August. Total outstanding was $5.224T.
- Revolving credit fell at a 4.4% annual rate in August (revised from -4.2%) and rose at a 14.5% annual rate in September (PYMNTS, Aktualita, Investozora).
- The revolving level was $1.369T (PYMNTS; Aktualita rounds it to ~$1.37T).
- "About a quarter / more than half": the revolving share is 1.369/5.224 = 26.2%. September's increase was +$16.3B revolving and +$11.5B nonrevolving, so revolving was 16.3/27.8 = 58.6% of it. PYMNTS states the same: "accounted for more than half of September's $28 billion increase despite making up about a quarter of outstanding balances."
- The card rate on accounts assessed interest was 22.36% in Q3 (PYMNTS: "third-quarter average annual rate at 22.36% for commercial bank card accounts charged interest"). "Last quarter" is correct on Nov 7, because Q3 (Jul-Sep) is the most recent completed quarter. Technically this is the Fed's mid-quarter (August) survey observation, which the Q3 column reports. That is acceptable.
- "Our math": $1,000 x 22.36% = $223.60, which rounds to ~$224. It is labeled, simple interest, and correct.
- Day words: the report came out Fri Nov 6, so "out Friday" is correct for a Saturday post.
- Limits: news headline 19 chars, board lines 18/17, split items 12 or fewer, compare values 6 or fewer, post.title 56. All OK.
