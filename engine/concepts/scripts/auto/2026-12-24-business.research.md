# Research: How Ryanair makes money on ultra-cheap flights (posts Dec 24, 2026)

All Ryanair figures are FY2026 = 12 months ended 31 March 2026, published 18 May 2026 (latest confirmed full year as of Oct 2026). Ryanair reports in euros; script uses € on screen, no USD conversion.

## Context (for US viewers)
- Ryanair Holdings is an Irish budget (low-cost) airline group flying mostly within Europe. FY25 it became the first EU airline to carry 200m passengers in a year (Ryanair release, May 2025).
- No timely news hook for Dec 24 (evergreen). Not about fuel (United fuel story ran Oct 24).

## FY2026 key numbers (Ryanair FY26 results, 18 May 2026; reported by Radiocor/Borsa Italiana, CAPA, TipRanks, Travel Extra, Il Sole 24 Ore)
- Passengers: 208.4 million, +4% (held back by delayed deliveries of 29 Boeing 737-8200s).
- Total revenue: €15.54bn, +11%.
- Scheduled (ticket) revenue: €10.56bn, +14%; fares about +10% (after a 7% fall in FY25).
- Average fare: €50.60 (Travel Extra); check: €10.56bn / 208.4m = €50.67 -> "about €51".
- Ancillary revenue: €4.99bn, +6%, about €24 per passenger (Radiocor; Travel Extra). Bags, seat selection, priority boarding, etc.
- Ancillary share of revenue: €4.99bn / €15.54bn = 32.1% -> "about a third".
- Profit after tax (before exceptional items): record €2.26bn, +40% from €1.61bn in FY25. Excludes an €85m provision for an Italian competition (AGCM) fine that Ryanair is appealing. (Post-exceptional ~€2.18bn by my arithmetic only; NOT used.)
- Load factor: 94%, unchanged (Radiocor).
- Fleet at 31 Mar 2026: 647 aircraft incl. 210 B737-8200 "Gamechangers" (aviospace/TipRanks summary). Group fleet is mostly Boeing 737s (subsidiary Lauda flies some Airbus A320s) -> say "mostly one plane type".
- Net cash about €2.1bn (CAPA/Grafa).

## Cost model (long-standing, Ryanair annual reports)
- One main aircraft type (Boeing 737): cuts training, spares and maintenance cost (secondary sources; Ryanair ARs).
- Secondary/regional airports with lower fees and less congestion (Ryanair 2000 annual report; ongoing).
- 25-minute turnarounds, called "legendary" in Ryanair's 2000 annual report and repeatedly cited by CEO Michael O'Leary (FlightGlobal; 2016 coverage). Long-standing target, not a measured FY26 average.
- Direct sales via its own website/app (2001 AR: 92% direct then). WEAKLY SOURCED for current share; not used with a number.

## Worked example (exact math, FY2026)
- Revenue per passenger: €15.54bn / 208.4m = €74.57 (= ticket €50.67 + extras €23.94).
- Profit per passenger (pre-exceptional PAT): €2.26bn / 208.4m = €10.84 -> "about €11".

## Surprising fact
About a third of Ryanair's revenue (€4.99bn of €15.54bn in FY26) comes not from tickets but from extras like bags and seats, about €24 per passenger on a ~€51 average fare.
