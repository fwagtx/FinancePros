## Key facts (College Board, Trends in College Pricing and Student Aid 2025)

Published (sticker) tuition and fees, 2025-26:
- Public four-year, in-state: $11,950 (up $340, or 2.9%, from 2024-25 before inflation).
- Private nonprofit four-year: $45,000 (up $1,750, or 4.0%, from $43,250 in 2024-25 before inflation).

Average net tuition and fees, first-time full-time students, 2025-26 (estimated, 2025 dollars):
- Public four-year, in-state: about $2,300 (peak $4,450 in 2012-13, in 2025 dollars).
- Private nonprofit four-year: about $16,910 (Higher Ed Dive: up 3.7% after inflation in 2025-26; it was $19,810 in 2006-07, in 2025 dollars).
- Definition: College Board's net price = published price minus the average grant aid received by first-time full-time students, *including students who got no grant aid*. (2025 report methodology, per search summaries.)

Average grant aid per first-time full-time student, 2025-26 (sticker minus net; also listed by secondary sites citing Figure CP-10):
- Public in-state: $11,950 - $2,300 = $9,650.
- Private nonprofit: $45,000 - $16,910 = $28,090.
- Private grants are about 2.9x public grants ($28,090 / $9,650 = 2.91).

Full student budgets (tuition, fees, housing, food, books, other), 2025-26:
- Public four-year in-state: $30,990. Private nonprofit four-year: $65,470. (College Board figures quoted by search summaries.)
- Net budget after grants (derived: budget minus average grant): public $30,990 - $9,650 = $21,340 (Summit Prep lists $21,340); private $65,470 - $28,090 = $37,380 (derived, not seen published). WEAKLY SOURCED; not used in the script.
- Housing-and-food sticker figures ($13,900 public, $15,920 private) only from secondary sites; NOT USED.

## Worked example (exact math, College Board 2025-26 averages, tuition and fees only)
- Sticker gap: $45,000 - $11,950 = $33,050 a year.
- Grant gap: $28,090 - $9,650 = $18,440 more grant aid at private, on average.
- Net gap: $16,910 - $2,300 = $14,610 a year. ($33,050 - $18,440 = $14,610, checks.)
- Net gap is 44% of the sticker gap ($14,610 / $33,050 = 0.442), so grants shrink the average gap by about 56%.
- Four years, if 2025-26 averages held flat (illustrative assumption): $14,610 x 4 = $58,440. Excludes housing, food and price increases.
- Ratios: sticker 3.8x ($45,000 / $11,950 = 3.77); net 7.4x ($16,910 / $2,300 = 7.35). The dollar gap shrinks but the ratio grows.

## The surprising fact
Private nonprofit colleges give the average first-time full-time student about $28,090 in grants, nearly three times the $9,650 at in-state public colleges, which cuts the yearly sticker-price gap from $33,050 to $14,610. And the average private freshman's tuition discount is estimated at 57.1% (NACUBO), meaning more than half the sticker isn't what most people pay.

## Who gets aid (NACUBO 2025 Tuition Discounting Study, preliminary 2025-26 estimates, released 2026)
- Average institutional tuition discount rate for first-time, full-time undergraduates at private nonprofit colleges: estimated 57.1% in 2025-26 (record/highest in the past decade). All undergraduates: 51.3%.
- About 90% of first-time undergraduates received some institutional aid; 84% of all undergraduates.
- Sample: private nonprofit colleges only (reported as 258 institutions). Preliminary estimates; final figures may change.
- Discrepancy: Higher Ed Dive says up from 54.5% the year before, while NACUBO's 2025-released study had put 2024-25 at 56.3% (preliminary). Don't use the year-over-year comparison.
- Sources: Higher Ed Dive, "Tuition discount rate reaches 57% for private nonprofits, NACUBO says" (2026); asumag.com press release coverage.

## Trade-offs (descriptive, not advice)
- In-state public: lower sticker, smaller average grants; net tuition and fees about $2,300 on average.
- Private nonprofit: higher sticker, much larger average grants/discounts; net about $16,910 on average; aid varies a lot by student and school (averages include students who get no grant).
- Averages are not offers: each school's net price calculator / aid offer gives an individual number.
