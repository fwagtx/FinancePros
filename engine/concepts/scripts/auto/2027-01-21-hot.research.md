# Research brief: Scam alert - "free lunch" investment seminars (2027-01-21-hot)
Made Nov 22, 2026; posts Thu Jan 21, 2027. Evergreen; every number dated.

## Key facts (with sources)
1. 2007 joint report "Protecting Senior Investors: Report of Examinations of Securities Firms Providing 'Free Lunch' Sales Seminars" by the SEC, NASAA (state regulators) and FINRA, released Sept 10, 2007 (SEC press release 2007-179).
   - 110 examinations of firms and branch offices that ran seminars with a free meal, April 2006 - June 2007. (SEC 2007-179; FINRA report page)
   - 100% of the "seminars" were sales presentations, though advertised as educational / "workshops". (SEC 2007-179)
   - 50% featured exaggerated or misleading advertising claims. (SEC 2007-179 key findings)
   - 23% involved possibly unsuitable recommendations. (SEC 2007-179)
   - "In some instances" possible fraud; SEC Chairman Cox summary: "half made misleading or exaggerated claims, and more than a third had unsuitable recommendations or outright fraud." Press (ThinkAdvisor 2007) reported 13% appeared fraudulent; a FINRA alert reportedly says 12%. UNCERTAIN -> fraud % NOT used in script.
   - Report also flagged poor firm supervision of seminar materials, and use of "senior specialist"-type designations that imply expertise with older investors.
2. SEC Investor.gov alert on free-meal seminars: the goal is typically to lure new clients and sell products, not educate; expect the "hard sell" in follow-up contacts even if the dinner itself is low-pressure.
3. Red flags (SEC/Investor.gov, FINRA, NASAA, state regulators): guaranteed or "no-risk" high returns; urgency ("limited time", sign today); high pressure; impressive-sounding "senior" designations; asking for personal financial info at sign-up.
4. How to check a seller: FINRA BrokerCheck (brokercheck.finra.org, 800-289-9999) for brokers; SEC Investment Adviser Public Disclosure (adviserinfo.sec.gov) for advisers; Investor.gov search; your state securities regulator (via NASAA). SEC investor help line 1-800-732-0330.
5. Scale today: FTC Consumer Sentinel: consumers reported losing $15.9 billion to fraud in 2025 (record; $12.5B in 2024). Investment scams were the top loss category at $7.9 billion in 2025, about half of all reported losses (FTC testimony to Joint Economic Committee, March 2026). Older adults (60+): $1.06B investment-scam losses in 2025, up 42% (FTC). Reported losses understate actual losses (FTC).

## Worked example (our math)
$7.9B / $3.8B = 2.079 -> reported investment-scam losses roughly doubled (about 2.1x) from 2022 to 2025.
50% of 110 exams = 55 seminars with exaggerated/misleading ads (our math). 23% of 110 = about 25 (our math).

## Surprising fact
Every single one of the 110 "educational" seminars regulators examined (2006-2007) was actually a sales presentation.
