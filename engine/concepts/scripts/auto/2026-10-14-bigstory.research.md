## Facts (BLS CPI release, September 2026 data, published Oct 14, 2026, 8:30 AM ET; USDL-26-1574)
- CPI-U all items: +0.6% month over month (seasonally adjusted) in September 2026.
- CPI-U all items: +3.7% over the 12 months ending September 2026 (not seasonally adjusted), up from 3.4% for the 12 months ending August.
- Consensus (Dow Jones): 0.6% m/m, 3.6% y/y -> annual rate came in above forecast; monthly matched (LSEG also had 0.6%).
- Core (all items less food and energy): +0.3% m/m, +2.6% y/y (Aug: 2.4% y/y). LSEG forecast: 0.2% m/m, 2.5% y/y.
- Gasoline index: +9.1% in September; accounted for over half of the monthly all-items increase; up about 34% (34.2% cited) over 12 months.
- Energy index: +5.2% in September after +2.1% in August.
- Food: unchanged in September; food at home -0.1% m/m, +1.8% over 12 months.
- Shelter: +0.4% m/m (Aug +0.3%), +3.2% over 12 months.
- Context: CNN called it the last CPI snapshot before the midterms. Fed's next decision: Oct 28, 2026 (two-day meeting ending Oct 28). 10-yr Treasury touched 5.364% intraday Oct 7, highest since 2002 (Kiplinger preview).

## What it means for a normal person
- 3.7% means a basket that cost $100 in Sept 2025 costs about $103.70 now (on average).
- Example (hypothetical): a 3% raise over the same year is less than 3.7% price growth -> buying power slips.
- Gas is the main driver; groceries were flat-to-down in September; core (ex food/energy) is much lower at 2.6%.
