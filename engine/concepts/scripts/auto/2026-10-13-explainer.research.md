## How the COLA is calculated (SSA Office of the Chief Actuary, "Latest COLA"; CRS RS 94-803 updated May 27, 2026)
- Index: CPI-W (Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers), published by BLS.
- Take the average CPI-W for the third calendar quarter (July, August, September) of the current year.
- Compare with the third-quarter average CPI-W of the last year a COLA became effective (typically the previous year).
- Percentage increase is rounded to the nearest one-tenth of 1%.
- If there is no increase, there is no COLA (benefits do not go down).
- The COLA is effective for December benefits, payable in January of the following year.

## Worked example: the 2026 COLA (SSA OACT Latest COLA)
- Q3 2024 average CPI-W: 308.729
- Q3 2025 average CPI-W: 317.265 (July 316.349, Aug 317.306, Sep 318.139)
- (317.265 - 308.729) / 308.729 x 100 = 2.765% -> rounds to 2.8%
- 2.8% COLA effective December 2025, paid starting January 2026.
- Average retired-worker benefit: $2,015 before COLA -> $2,071 after (SSA 2026 COLA Fact Sheet). Check: 2,015 x 1.028 = 2,071.42 -> about $56/month.
- Nearly 71 million Social Security beneficiaries got the 2.8% COLA starting January 2026; about 75 million including SSI. (SSA 2026 COLA announcement / fact sheet)

## The surprising fact
- Only 3 months of prices set the whole year's raise.
- No COLA was payable in 2010, 2011, and 2016 (since automatic COLAs began in 1975) because CPI-W did not rise. (CRS 94-803; SSA)
