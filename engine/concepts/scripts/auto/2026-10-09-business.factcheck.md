## Issues

1. **Line 3 scene contradicts its own source (factual).** The on-screen list says "Ticket: pays comics / Drinks: pay the bills". The Laugh Factory, quoted in that same line, says the *drink minimum* helps pay the comics. Line 9 ("the ticket pays the comedian, the drinks pay the club") has the same problem.
   **Fix:** change the line 3 items to "Drink minimum: helps pay comics" / "Keeps tickets cheap". Soften line 9 to "The ticket gets you in. The drinks keep the club open."

2. **Lines 5-6: the 70% comic split is a loaded assumption.** The example is clearly labeled ("Example:", "Picture an example club"), which is good. But the 70% figure is what creates the dramatic gap. Booking guides put comedian pay at roughly 15-30% of ticket revenue for typical club shows. Big door deals are for headliners. At a 30% split the club keeps $2,800 from tickets against $3,200 from drinks, which is close to even. The example also assumes a sold-out room, $20 tickets and everyone buying two $10 alcoholic drinks. In reality the minimum can be met with sodas or food, and food carries higher cost.
   **Fix:** use a 50% split (club keeps $2,000, text "If comics get half, the club keeps $2,000"), or say "if a big headliner takes 70%". Add "sold out" and "if everyone orders two $10 drinks".

3. **Line 5: on-screen value appears before it is spoken.** The line 5 compare scene already shows Drinks $3,200. That number is not narrated until line 6.
   **Fix:** show the right card as "?" or leave it blank in line 5, then reveal it in line 6.

4. **Lines 7-8: the year and the framing do not match.** Line 7 says "we do drink out" but cites the total ($643), which includes drinking at home. Line 8 then jumps back to 2023 for the away-from-home number, even though BLS has a 2024 figure ($374 away, $269 at home).
   **Fix:** line 8 should read "In 2024, $374 of it was away from home." Change the chart_note to x: 2024, "$374 away from home". Optionally reword line 7 to "And we spend on drinks. The average US household spent $643 on alcohol in 2024."

5. **Line 2: two-item vs two-drink wording mismatch (minor).** The spoken and caption text say "two-drink minimum". The board says "2-item minimum", and so does the cover label. Also, "how a lot of them stay open" is an unsourced generalization. The caption says "many of them" while the narration says "a lot of them".
   **Fix:** "And that two-item minimum is a big part of how clubs pay the bills." Use the same wording in text and say.

6. **Line 6 narration reads awkwardly.** "Drinks cost bars about twenty percent" is incomplete.
   **Fix:** "Bars pay about twenty cents per dollar for the booze, so the club keeps thirty-two hundred."

7. **Hook (line 1): acceptable but could be stronger.** It is true in spirit and matches the cover. It is 9 words, about 3.3 seconds at normal pace, so a little over the 3-second target. It is also a sweeping generalization.
   **Stronger option:** "That two-drink minimum? It's the real show." (about 2 seconds). Or "Comedy clubs make more on your drinks than your ticket." That version is only defensible if the example is kept.

8. **Post text.** "the club keeps $1,200 from tickets but $3,200 from drinks" needs updating if the split changes (issue 2). "US households" is fine; BLS technically says "consumer units".
