# Research brief: Why timeshares are so hard to sell (slot 2026-10-28-business)

Researched October 1, 2026. Posts October 28, 2026. Note: arda.org, consumer.ftc.gov, sec.gov and company IR sites were blocked for direct fetch in this session; every figure below was confirmed through search results quoting the official source, cross-checked across at least two results where marked.

## Key facts (with dates)

1. **Average new timeshare price, 2025: $24,740** (average U.S. transaction price).
   Source: ARDA (American Resort Development Association) / Ernst & Young, *State of the Vacation Timeshare Industry, 2026 Edition* (covers 2025, released mid-2026). Seen in two separate search results citing the ARDA 2026 edition.
   Prior year: $23,160 in 2024, $24,170 in 2023 (ARDA 2025 Edition).

2. **U.S. timeshare sales volume: $10.7 billion in 2025** (ARDA 2026 Ed.); $10.5 billion in 2024 (ARDA 2025 Ed.).
   About 1,434 resorts, ~188,700 units (2025); timeshare occupancy 80.0% vs. 62.3% hotel average in 2025 (ARDA 2026 Ed., per search results).

3. **Average annual maintenance fee (per weekly interval)**, ARDA State of the Vacation Timeshare Industry reports:
   - 2020: $1,090 (single source; not charted)
   - 2021: $1,120
   - 2022: $1,170
   - 2023: $1,260
   - 2024: $1,480 (ARDA 2025 Ed.; +17.5% vs 2023, reported by TimeshareFacts "Timeshare Fees Up 17.5% Last Year ARDA Says"; 1,480/1,260 = 1.175, consistent)
   - 2025: $1,550 (ARDA 2026 Ed.; +4.7% vs 2024; 1,550/1,480 = 1.047, consistent)
   Fees exclude property taxes and special assessments (per search summary of ARDA). 2021 to 2025: +38% (1,550/1,120 = 1.384).

4. **Marriott Vacations Worldwide (NYSE: VAC), fiscal year 2025** (Q4/FY2025 earnings release, February 2026; FY2025 10-K):
   - Consolidated contract sales: **$1.8 billion**, down 3% from 2024. (Contract sales is a company operating metric, not GAAP revenue.)
   - Marketing and sales expense (vacation ownership): **$943 million** (appeared in two separate search results).
   - Cost of vacation ownership products: $184 million; sale of vacation ownership products (GAAP revenue line): $1,464 million (single search result; not used on screen).
   - Ratio: $943M / $1.8B = 0.524, i.e. roughly half. Because $1.8B is rounded, describe as "about half," not an exact percentage.
   - Total FY2025 revenue: $5.03 billion.

5. **FTC guidance (exact wording)**, FTC consumer alert "If you have a timeshare, scammers might target you" (March 2024, consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/03/...):
   "Timeshares are hard to sell and only a scammer will tell you they already have a buyer or can find one fast."
   (Search results render the sentence with and without a comma after "sell"; on screen we quote only the second clause, which is identical in every rendering:
   "...only a scammer will tell you they already have a buyer or can find one fast.")
   Same alert: "Don't pay upfront fees"; "only scammers demand fees before they help you sell your timeshare"; owners who want out are pointed to contacting the timeshare company directly. The FTC also published an alert in November 2022, "Want to get rid of your timeshare? Read this before you hire someone to help."

## Why resale is hard (the business-model explanation)
- New timeshares are sold through costly marketing (tours, presentations, incentives). At Marriott Vacations Worldwide in FY2025, marketing and sales cost $943M against about $1.8B of contract sales: about half.
- A resale buyer is not paying for that sales machine, so the price an owner can get on resale reflects the vacation, not the sales cost.
- A resale buyer also takes on the annual maintenance fee, which averaged $1,550 in 2025 and rose every year 2021-2025 per ARDA.
- Resale listing prices: widely claimed ($1 eBay listings, "10% or less of original price") but only from exit-company/law-firm blogs, NOT clearly sourced. Not used.

## Worked example (labeled hypothetical)
Owner with the 2025 average fee, held flat for 10 years (assumption: no increases; actual fees rose every year 2021-2025):
$1,550 x 10 = $15,500 in maintenance fees, on top of a purchase price that averaged $24,740 new in 2025.
$24,740 + $15,500 = $40,240 over ten years.

## The surprising fact
At Marriott Vacations Worldwide, about half of what customers signed up to pay in FY2025 ($943M of ~$1.8B contract sales) went to marketing and sales, not to the resort itself.
