# Research brief: Brokerage vs savings account protection (2026-12-04-explainer, Acorn Economics)

## Core facts (confirmed Oct 5, 2026)
- FDIC: "insuring deposits to at least $250,000 per depositor, per ownership category at each FDIC-insured bank." Standard amount $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category (FDIC Understanding Deposit Insurance; FDIC Deposit Insurance FAQs). Limit $250,000 since Oct 2008 (permanent 2010).
- FDIC covers: checking, NOW, savings, money market deposit accounts, CDs, cashier's checks/money orders.
- FDIC does NOT cover: stock investments, bond investments, mutual funds, annuities, life insurance, safe deposit boxes, US Treasury bills/bonds/notes, municipal securities, crypto assets (FDIC Understanding Deposit Insurance).
- FDIC: "Since the FDIC was founded in 1933, no depositor has lost a penny of FDIC-insured funds." (FDIC Understanding Deposit Insurance, exact quote)
- SIPC: "The limit of SIPC protection is $500,000, which includes a $250,000 limit for cash." (sipc.org What SIPC Protects, exact)
- SIPC: "SIPC does not protect against the decline in value of your securities." Also not worthless stocks sold to you, not bad advice. "SIPC protection is not the same as protection for your cash at a ... FDIC insured banking institution because SIPC does not protect the value of any security." Money market mutual funds are protected as securities by SIPC. (sipc.org)
- SIPC protects customers' cash and securities when a SIPC-member brokerage firm fails and customer assets are missing (sipc.org; SEC Investor.gov SIPC bulletin).
- Cash sweeps: bank sweep programs move uninvested brokerage cash to program bank(s) where it is eligible for FDIC insurance (up to $250,000 per bank); swept funds held at the bank are not SIPC-protected (SEC Investor.gov Cash Sweep bulletin). Not used in script (word budget).
- Timely context (not in script): Main Street Depositor Protection Act (S.2999 / S.4198, 119th Congress) would let FDIC raise coverage for some non-interest-bearing transaction accounts up to $5M; introduced, hearing Feb 5, 2026, NOT passed as of Oct 5, 2026 (congress.gov; Semafor Mar 25, 2026). Limit still $250,000.

## Worked example (hypothetical)
- $10,000 in stocks at a brokerage falls 20%: 10,000 x 0.20 = $2,000 market loss; value now $8,000. Brokerage did not fail -> SIPC covers $0 of the $2,000.

## Surprise fact
- SIPC's $500,000 is bigger than FDIC's $250,000, but it pays nothing when prices fall; it only steps in when a member firm fails and assets are missing.
