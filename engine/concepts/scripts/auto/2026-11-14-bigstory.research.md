## The facts (October 2026 = first month of fiscal year 2027)
- Deficit: $404.1 billion in October 2026, the largest October deficit on record (US Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement, released Nov 12, 2026; reported by Reuters-based coverage, 24/7 Wall St Nov 13-14, mlq.ai, citybiz).
- A year earlier (October 2025): $284.3 billion. Increase: about $120 billion, or 42%.
- Receipts (money in): $368.9 billion. Outlays (money out): $773 billion.
- Calendar caveat: some payments normally due Nov 1 were shifted into October (weekend) in both years. Adjusted for timing shifts, October 2026 deficit = $285 billion vs $191 billion a year earlier, up $94 billion or 49% (Treasury estimate via Reuters-based reports). Not a record after adjustment.
- Interest: gross interest on federal debt $115 billion in October 2026, up about 11% from about $104 billion a year earlier (Reuters via reports). Net interest $103.1 billion vs $90.9 billion.
- Our math: $115B / 31 days = about $3.7 billion a day.
- Customs duties $19.2B, down about 39% from $31.4B a year earlier.
- CBO had estimated about $409B (Nov 10 estimate).
- Fiscal 2026 full-year deficit: about $1.99 trillion (5-year high, per Yahoo Finance/CBO).
- Context: 10-year Treasury yield ended Friday Nov 13 above 5% (Treasury par yield 5.10% per mlq.ai citing Treasury; Trading Economics ~5.15%); the government covers deficits by selling Treasury debt. Reports tie the week's yield rise mainly to hot inflation data (PPI +6.5% y/y) and Fed hike expectations, not to the deficit alone. Do not claim causation.

## What it means for a normal person
- A deficit is borrowed money; it is covered by selling Treasury bonds. Interest on that debt is now a top budget line.
- The 10-year Treasury yield is a benchmark that mortgage, car-loan and savings rates often move with.
