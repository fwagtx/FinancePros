# 2026-10-20-hot research brief: Scam alert, Medicare and open-enrollment scam calls

Posts Tuesday Oct 20, 2026 (inside Medicare Open Enrollment, Oct 15 - Dec 7, 2026). Researched Sept 30, 2026.
Note: medicare.gov, consumer.ftc.gov, ftc.gov blocked direct fetch from this environment; quotes confirmed through exact-phrase web searches whose top results are the official pages, and repeated verbatim by state/county consumer-protection sites.

## Timely angle
- FTC consumer alert "What to know ahead of Open Enrollment to avoid health insurance scams" (Sept 2026): health insurance scams happen year-round but pick up during Open Enrollment for Medicare and the Marketplace; fake look-alike .gov sites and paid search ads. Reports: ReportFraud.ftc.gov; Medicare scams at Medicare.gov or 800-633-4227.
- Nomorobo (call-blocking company, its own honeypot data, single company source): Medicare-related spam/scam calls up 25% between August and September 2026. In 2025, Medicare-related call volume went from about 190,000 (Aug) to 450,000 (Oct) and 465,000 (Nov). Company data; use only with attribution, if at all.

## Official statements (word-for-word)
- Medicare.gov (Reporting Medicare fraud & abuse): "Medicare will never call you to sell you anything or visit you at your home." Medicare or its representatives only call for personal info in limited situations (a plan you joined, returning your call, etc.).
- FTC consumer alert, Sept 2025 ("This Medicare Open Enrollment season, learn how to protect yourself from scams"): "Your caller ID might show Medicare's name or phone number. But caller ID can be faked. It could be anyone calling from anywhere in the world." "If you think a call might be real, hang up and call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) to check." Also: scammers say they need your Medicare, bank account, or credit card number for your "new" Medicare card; "Medicare sends its cards to you automatically for free." Scammers get more active around Open Enrollment (Oct 15 - Dec 7) every year.

## What scammers do (descriptive)
- Pose as Medicare / connected to Medicare; spoof caller ID; ask for Medicare number, SSN, bank/card numbers; charge for a "new" card; promise gifts for enrolling (FTC).
- HHS-OIG fraud alerts: "free" genetic testing (cheek swab kits) and "free" orthotic braces offered by phone/TV ads; once the beneficiary gives or verifies Medicare info, items are shipped and billed to Medicare even if not needed. With a Medicare number, scammers can submit more fraudulent claims, sell the Medicare identity, and inaccurate services accumulate on the person's Medicare record.

## Worked example (math)
- 2024 -> 2025: $7.75B / $4.885B = 1.586 -> about +59% (matches FBI-reported 59%).
- 2020 -> 2025: $7.75B / $0.966B = about 8 times.

## Surprising fact
- Real Medicare does not make sales calls at all; caller ID showing "Medicare" can be faked. And losses reported by people 60+ to the FBI were about 8x higher in 2025 than 2020.
