# Research brief: Why companies pay the agencies that grade their debt
Slot 2026-12-10-business (How They Make Money, posts Thu Dec 10, 2026). Researched Oct 11, 2026. Evergreen: every figure is dated.

## Angle / hook
The borrower pays the grader. Under the "issuer-pays" model, the company or government selling bonds pays S&P, Moody's or Fitch to rate them, and the rating is then given to investors free. It is a very high-margin business: S&P Global Ratings turned about 64% of its FY2025 revenue into operating profit (10-K).
No single news event this week; evergreen. (Related 2026 item: S&P Global recast its segments in a July 6, 2026 release; Ratings 2025 operating margin still 64%, adjusted 65% as recast.)

## Key numbers (all FY2025 = calendar 2025 unless noted)
- S&P Global Ratings (S&P Global FY2025 10-K / 2025 Annual Report, segment table):
  - Revenue: $4,724M in 2025, vs $4,370M in 2024 and $3,332M in 2023 (+8% in 2025).
  - Operating profit: $3,013M in 2025 (2024: $2,707M; 2023: $1,864M).
  - Operating margin: 64% in 2025 (2024: 62%; 2023: 56%).
  - FY2025 growth: Ratings revenue +8%, driven by +10% non-transaction and +6% transaction revenue (S&P Q4/FY2025 earnings release, Feb 10, 2026).
  - S&P Global total 2025 revenue: $15,336M.
- Moody's Investors Service (MIS) (Moody's FY2025 10-K and Q4/FY2025 earnings release, Feb 18, 2026):
  - MIS external revenue: $4,119M in 2025 vs $3,793M in 2024 (+9%; release says "$4.1 billion, up 9%").
  - MIS adjusted operating margin: 63.6% in 2025, up 350 bps (non-GAAP, company-defined).
  - Moody's Corp total 2025 revenue: $7,718M.
- Market concentration (SEC Office of Credit Ratings, "2025 Staff Report on NRSROs", posted April 2026): the three large NRSROs (S&P, Moody's, Fitch) accounted for 93.38% of all credit ratings outstanding as of Dec 31, 2024, down from 94.15% at Dec 31, 2023. (In 2007 the share was 98.8%.) Note: SEC measures share by number of ratings outstanding, not by revenue.

## Worked example (exact math, from S&P FY2025 10-K)
S&P Global Ratings 2025 operating margin = $3,013M / $4,724M = 0.6378 = 63.8% (filing rounds to 64%).
So about 64 cents of each revenue dollar was operating profit in 2025.
Comparison: Moody's MIS growth 2025 = 4,119 / 3,793 - 1 = 8.6% (company says 9%). MIS 2022 drop = 2,699 / 3,812 - 1 = -29.2%.
(A per-bond fee in basis points was NOT confirmed from an official source, so it is not used.)

## Why companies pay (the model)
- Agencies originally sold ratings to investors (subscriber/investor-pays). In the 1970s they moved to issuer-pays; Brookings and academic papers tie this to copying machines making it easy to share ratings for free (free-riding hurt agency revenue). Penn Central link is weakly sourced: NOT used.
- In 1975 the SEC began designating NRSROs, making ratings part of financial regulation (Brookings).
- Issuers pay because many bond buyers and rules rely on ratings; a rating makes a bond sellable to a wide pool of investors, and a higher rating generally means lower borrowing cost.
- Criticism: the payer is the company being graded, a potential conflict of interest (issuer-pays debate; S&P published a white paper defending it, Bond Buyer).

## 2008 crisis settlements (stated accurately)
- S&P: Feb 3, 2015, settled with the US Justice Department, 19 states and DC for $1.375 billion ($687.5M to DOJ, $687.5M to states/DC), over allegations it inflated ratings of mortgage securities and CDOs in 2004-2007; separate $125M CalPERS settlement. S&P acknowledged conduct in an agreed statement of facts; company release: "The settlement contains no findings of violations of law by the Company, S&P Financial Services or S&P Ratings."
- Moody's: Jan 13, 2017, nearly $864 million ($437.5M penalty to DOJ, $426.3M to 21 states and DC). Moody's said the settlement contains no finding of a violation of law or admission of liability and that it stands behind the integrity of its ratings.

## The surprise
The company borrowing the money pays for its own report card, and the grader keeps roughly 64 cents of profit on every revenue dollar (S&P Ratings, FY2025).
