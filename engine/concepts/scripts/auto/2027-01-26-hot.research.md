# Research brief: 2027-01-26-hot (Money Myth: you need an 850 credit score to get the best rates)
Posts Tue Jan 26, 2027, 6 PM Central. Evergreen: every number dated. Education only.

## Angle / hook
Myth: "You need a perfect 850 to get the best rates." Fact: the top pricing tiers for mortgages (Fannie Mae) and car loans (Experian tiers) start around 780-781. Above that, the extra points buy nothing on price.
No timely news angle needed; evergreen myth.

## Confirmed facts (with dates)
1. Perfect 850 is rare: As of June 2026, 1.71% of U.S. consumers had a FICO Score of 850, up from 1.67% in June 2025 (Experian, "How Many Americans Have a Perfect 850 Credit Score?", updated Aug 24, 2026). About 1 in 58. Spoken: "about one point seven percent"; screen "1.7%".
   - Context: 22.9% had 800-850 as of June 2026 (Experian).
   - Older history (not evenly spaced, from different sources): ~0.85% April 2009, ~1.6% April 2019 (FICO via Columbian 2019), 1.54% Q3 2023 (Experian via Motley Fool). Values barely move -> NOT charted; use stat instead.
2. Fannie Mae LLPA matrix (updated 09-30-2026), Table "Purchase Money Loans - LLPA by Credit Score/LTV Ratio": top credit score bucket is ">= 780" (Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0). Any score 780-850 gets the same price adjustment.
   - 75.01-80.00% LTV (20% down): >=780 = 0.375%; 760-779 = 0.625%; 740-759 = 0.875%.
   - <=60% LTV: 0.000% for 740 and above.
   - Older grids (before May 1, 2023) stopped at a 740 band (Fannie Mae / industry commentary).
3. Auto loans: Experian average APR by tier, Q2 2026 (VantageScore 4.0): Super prime (781 or above) new 4.41%, used 6.29%; Prime (661-780) new 6.15%, used 8.81%; Deep subprime (300-500) new 16.11%. (Experian "Auto Loan Rates and Financing for 2026"; Q2 2026 figures also reported by autoloanrate.com.) Spoken "about four point four percent"; screen "4.4%".
4. Individual lenders set their own pricing and cutoffs; these are the published tiers, not a guarantee.
5. myFICO loan savings calculator: live output not retrievable; secondary reproduction (May 2026 rates) shows a top "780+" bracket. Not used in script.

## Worked example (OUR MATH)
$400,000 purchase loan, 20% down (80% LTV), Fannie Mae LLPA grid updated Sept 30, 2026:
- 780 score: 0.375% x $400,000 = $1,500
- 850 score: same >=780 bucket: 0.375% x $400,000 = $1,500
- Difference from 780 to 850: $1,500 - $1,500 = $0
- For contrast, 760-779: 0.625% x $400,000 = $2,500 ($1,000 more than 780+).
(LLPAs are an upfront price adjustment; lenders often pass them through as fees or a slightly higher rate.)

## The surprising fact
Fewer than 2 in 100 people have an 850 (June 2026), yet the score that earns the top mortgage tier is 780, and the extra 70 points save $0 on that fee (our math).
