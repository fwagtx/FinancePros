# Research brief: Why Starbucks makes more from cold drinks than from hot coffee
Slot: 2027-01-16-business (How They Make Money), posts Sat Jan 16, 2027. Researched Nov 17, 2026. Evergreen: every number dated.

## Premise check (honest framing)
- Starbucks does NOT report cold vs hot revenue or profit in its 10-K. Profit by drink type is not disclosed anywhere -> never say cold drinks are "more profitable".
- What IS confirmed (company statements):
  1. Investor Day, Jan 29, 2026: "roughly two-thirds of the beverages sold in U.S. company-operated coffeehouses are cold" (Starbucks newsroom, Feb 26, 2026; Food Business News quoting global chief brand officer Tressie Lieberman). This is a share of drinks SOLD (units), US company-operated stores.
  2. Fiscal Q3 2023 earnings call (Aug 1, 2023): "our cold business reached 75% of U.S. beverage sales this past quarter" (dollar sales, one summer quarter). Same call: modifiers such as cold foam "now contribute to over $1 billion in revenue annually"; cold foam was the fastest-growing customization; over 60% of beverages customized.
  3. Earlier: ~74% in fiscal Q3 2021 (World Coffee Portal, Aug 2021). A 2024 report said cold has been about 70% of beverage sales for at least three years. Quarter-to-quarter the share moves (summer quarters peak), so do NOT chart these as a series.
- So "Starbucks makes more from cold drinks than hot coffee" holds in SALES terms (75% of US beverage sales in Q3 FY2023; about 2/3 of US drinks sold as of Jan 2026). Script frames it as "brings in more sales", not profit.

## Why cold brings in more per cup (management explanation)
- Q4 FY2026 call (Oct 29, 2026), CEO Brian Niccol, exact: "the combination of customers moving to cold, which also results in usually a little bit bigger size, and then the idea of modifying with cold foam, whether it's matcha, cold brew, iced coffee or iced Americanos, continues to see a really nice impact in the afternoon."
- Same call, CFO Cathy Smith: matcha "is now over $1 billion platform"; "now almost everyone puts a cold foam on it".
- Q4 FY2026 (quarter ended Sep 27, 2026): US comparable sales +7.7% = transactions +2.4% and ticket +5.2%; management said about half of the ticket increase came from pricing and delivery, half from attachment and mix (press/call summaries; Starbucks Q4 FY2026 release Oct 29, 2026).

## 10-K revenue split (company-operated stores; "Beverage" = drink sales in company-operated stores)
- FY2025 (ended Sep 28, 2025), FY2025 10-K: Beverage $22,539.9M (61%), Food $7,049.9M (19%), Other $7,594.6M (20%). Total net revenues $37,184.4M.
- FY2024: Beverage ~$21.88B (60%), Food ~$6.75B (19%). FY2023: Beverage ~$21.68B (60%), Food ~$6.59B (18%).
- FY2022: Beverage $19,555.3M (61%); FY2021: $18,317.0M (63%); FY2020: $14,337.5M (61%).
- FY2026 10-K (filed Nov 2026): total net revenues $37,910.6M (+2.0%). Beverage/food split NOT verified from the filing (secondary source says ~59% beverage) -> not used in script.

## Worked example (exact math, our math)
- FY2025 beverage share: 22,539.9 / 37,184.4 = 0.606 -> 61% (matches 10-K).
- Beverage vs food FY2025: 22,539.9 / 7,049.9 = 3.2 -> drinks brought in about 3.2 times what food did.
- Cold vs hot in US beverage sales, Q3 FY2023: 75% cold -> 25% hot -> cold = 3x hot dollars that quarter.

## Surprising fact
- At the coffee company, the classic hot cup is the minority: about two-thirds of US drinks sold are cold (Jan 2026), and in summer 2023 cold was 75% of US drink sales. Add-ons like cold foam alone were a $1B+ a year business (2023).

## Uncertain / not used
- Exact price of a cold foam add-on varies by store and market -> script says "usually costs extra", no dollar figure.
- FY2026 beverage/food split unverified (sec.gov not reachable from this session).
- No official cold vs hot profit margin data.
