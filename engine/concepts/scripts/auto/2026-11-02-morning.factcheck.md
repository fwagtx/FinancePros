## Issues

### 1. REQUIRED: line 7, time word wrong on the posting date
"this month" means November on Nov 2. The 23-cent drop happened over the past month (Oct 2 to Nov 2, $4.40 to $4.17).
- text: `Our math: a 15-gallon fill-up costs $17 more than last year, even after a 23-cent drop over the past month.`
- say: `Our math: a fifteen gallon fill-up costs seventeen dollars more than last year, even after a twenty-three cent drop over the past month.`
- board: no change. The spoken total becomes 132 words.

### 2. Optional: line 4, "still above $100" is present tense but the source is from Oct 29
WTI briefly traded below $100 this morning. The claim is safest if it is clearly dated.
- text: `Last week, AAA blamed crude oil, which was above $100 a barrel.`
- say: `Last week, AAA blamed crude oil, which was above a hundred dollars a barrel.`
- The stat scene can stay as it is (`>$100`, sub `a barrel, Oct 29`). The spoken total is about the same.

### 3. Optional: line 6, compare label
`Diesel, 2025` is vague. Change it to `Diesel, Nov 2025` (16 characters).

### 4. Optional: line 0, hook strength
The hook is true and specific, has 12 spoken words and takes about 3 seconds to say. But it states a fact without opening a question. A stronger version, also 12 words:
- text: `Filling your tank costs $17 more than last year. Here's why.`
- say: `Filling your tank costs seventeen dollars more than last year. Here's why.`
- If you use it, keep the hook scene value `+$1.13`, or change it to `+$17`, title `A fill-up is up [$17]`. Note that this would move the cover's payoff to line 7. The current hook is acceptable.

### 5. Optional: line 5, source tag
The claim is now confirmed by Reuters via Arab News, so the more recognizable source can be used: `source: "Reuters, Nov 2"`. The headline "Oil slips on talks" (4 words) is fine.
