# Research brief: When your W-2, 1099s and refund actually arrive (posts Wed Jan 20, 2027)

Scope: US federal individual income tax, tax year 2026 returns filed in 2027. Education only, not advice.
Note: IRS.gov could not be fetched directly from this session; IRS figures below were confirmed from IRS page search snippets plus reputable outlets (Accounting Today, CNN, CBS, PBS, Journal of Accountancy).

## Timely angle
- The video posts Jan 20, 2027, just before forms are due and the filing season opens. Evergreen framing: "these are the dates for the 2027 filing season".
- As of Nov 21, 2026, the IRS has NOT announced the 2027 filing-season opening date (latest IRS release found: IR-2026-140, Nov 16, 2026, "take steps now to make tax filing in 2027 easier", no opening date). Last four seasons opened on a Monday between Jan 23 and Jan 29. Do NOT state a 2027 opening date; use the 2026 one with its year.

## Key facts (with dates)
- W-2: employers must furnish W-2s to employees by January 31. When a deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, it moves to the next business day (IRC 7503). Jan 31, 2027 is a Sunday (Jan 1, 2027 is a Friday), so the 2027 deadline is Monday, Feb 1, 2027 (confirmed by TaxAct 2027 dates, TSCPA, savingtoinvest 2027 deadlines). Same date for filing Copy A + W-3 with SSA.
- Most 1099s to recipients (1099-NEC, 1099-INT, 1099-DIV, most 1099-MISC): due January 31 -> Feb 1, 2027.
- 1099-B (brokerage proceeds) and consolidated brokerage statements: due February 15. Feb 15, 2027 is Presidents' Day (Washington's Birthday, third Monday of February; a DC legal holiday), so the due date is Tuesday, Feb 16, 2027 (TaxAct, TSCPA, fedratecalc 2027 1099 deadlines). Corrected brokerage 1099s can still arrive in late February or March.
- 2026 filing season opened Monday, Jan 26, 2026 (IRS IR-2026-02, announced Jan 8, 2026; IR-2026-12 Jan 26, 2026; PBS, CBS, Journal of Accountancy). IRS expected about 164 million individual returns.
- Tax Day: April 15 (April 15, 2027 is a Thursday; statutory deadline).
- Refund speed: IRS says most refunds are issued in less than 21 days for e-filed returns with direct deposit; some returns need extra review and take longer (IRS Where's My Refund guidance; IR-2026-12).
- PATH Act (Protecting Americans from Tax Hikes Act of 2015): IRS cannot issue refunds that claim the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) or Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC) before mid-February (Feb 15). The hold applies to the ENTIRE refund, not just the credit portion. In 2026, the IRS said most EITC/ACTC refunds were expected in bank accounts by March 2, 2026 for early filers with direct deposit and no other issues (IRS via Credit Karma, Yahoo Finance; one outlet said about March 3).

## Surprising fact
- File on day one and claim the EITC or ACTC, and the law still holds your whole refund until mid-February; most of those refunds landed around March 2 in 2026.

## Worked example (our math)
- In 2026, the season opened Jan 26. Jan 26, 2026 + 21 days = Feb 16, 2026 (Jan 26 + 5 days = Jan 31; + 16 more = Feb 16). That is the earliest "within 21 days" window for a day-one e-filer with direct deposit, before any review.
- Refund growth: $3,275 - $2,942 = $333; $333 / $2,942 = 11.3% (matches IRS).
