# Research brief: How plumbers and electricians price a service call
Show: How They Make Money. Posts Sunday, Jan 17, 2027 (evergreen). Researched Nov 18, 2026.

Note: no single company 10-K covers this; most plumbing/electrical service shops are small private firms. The "official numbers" are BLS wage and compensation data; pricing practice comes from consumer cost guides (Angi) and trade press/software vendors, presented as ranges and attributed. No timely news hook this week; evergreen angle.

## Key facts (with dates)

### What the tech earns (BLS OEWS, May 2025; released May 15, 2026)
- Plumbers, pipefitters and steamfitters (SOC 47-2152): median $63,800 a year, $30.67 an hour, May 2025 (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, Pay tab; hourly confirmed via OOH Quick Facts as repeated by multiple sites).
- Electricians (SOC 47-2111): median $63,190 a year, $30.38 an hour, May 2025 (BLS OOH). 10th-90th percentile: $42,640 to $108,510 a year.
- Note: hourly = annual / 2,080, which is how BLS reports both.

### What the employer pays on top (BLS ECEC, June 2026; released Sep 9, 2026)
- Private industry: total compensation $46.89 per hour worked; wages and salaries $32.82 (70.0%); benefits $14.07 (30.0%). Exact quote verified from the BLS release text.
- Caveat: from the December 2026 ECEC onward BLS removes workers' compensation costs from ECEC, so later benefit shares may not compare 1:1.
- This is all private industry, not plumbers specifically (construction/trades breakdown not retrieved).

### What the customer is billed (industry ranges; vary widely, attribute)
- Angi cost guide (plumbers): rates range about $45 to $200 an hour depending on experience and job; average around $100 an hour; apprentices ~$45-$90, journeypersons ~$60-$110, masters ~$80-$200. Exact page date not confirmed (Angi pages fetched via search snippets only; angi.com blocked from this session).
- Angi (plumbers): trip fees "anywhere from $50 to $300" depending on location and distance (2023-era page); separate service/diagnostic fee $100-$250 also cited.
- Angi (electricians): $50-$130 an hour; service-call fee $100-$200 for the first hour; first hour priced higher because it includes a call-out fee covering gas, travel time and small parts.
- Software vendor benchmarks (ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro/others, 2026): residential plumbing $100-$200/hr, electrical $85-$165/hr; electrical trip fees ~$100-$200; many plumbers fold travel into labor instead of a separate fee. Vendor blogs: lower reliability.
- After-hours: commonly 1.5x-3x normal rates (Angi; vendor blogs).

### Flat-rate pricing
- Many service shops price from a "flat-rate price book": a fixed price per task (e.g., replace a faucet cartridge) set in advance rather than billing the clock. Price = estimated labor hours x shop hourly rate + parts x markup (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro guides). Flat rate is common for repairs/replacements; hourly/time-and-materials for diagnostics with unclear scope.

### Unbillable time
- Field Promax survey reported by ACHR News (trade press): only 65% of an HVAC technician's 8.8-hour day is billable (about 5.7 hours). HVAC, not plumbing/electrical specifically; date of article not confirmed.
- Other industry estimates range from about 30% (ServiceTitan calculator example: 2.4 billable hours of 8) to ~80%. Simpro: utilization = billed hours / paid hours (26 of 40 = 65%).
- Labor burden estimates (vendor): 28-35% on top of wages for trades.

## Worked example (OUR MATH, hypothetical)
1. Tech wage: $30.67/hr (BLS median plumber, May 2025).
2. Add benefits: if wages are 70% of total compensation (BLS ECEC private industry, June 2026), total = $30.67 / 0.70 = $43.81 per hour worked.
3. Unbillable time: if 65% of paid hours are billable (ACHR News/Field Promax HVAC figure), cost per billable hour = $43.81 / 0.65 = $67.40.
4. Still not included: truck and fuel, liability insurance, licensing, office/dispatch staff, tools, marketing, and profit. These push the billed rate toward the $100+ range industry guides cite (Angi: ~$100/hr average for plumbers).
Ratio: $67.40 / $30.67 = 2.2x the wage before any overhead or profit.

## The surprising fact
Before the truck, insurance, office or any profit, a $30.67-an-hour plumber already costs the shop about $67 per billable hour (our math), more than double the wage.
