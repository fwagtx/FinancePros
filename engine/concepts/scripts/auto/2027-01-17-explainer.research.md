# Research brief: Good debt vs bad debt: can borrowed acorns ever be smart?
Posts Sunday Jan 17, 2027 (evergreen). Researched Nov 18, 2026. Education only: how to judge debt (cost of the rate vs. what the borrowed money earns or saves). No borrowing advice.

## News angle (dated, not "this week")
- NY Fed Household Debt and Credit Report, Q3 2026 (released Nov 10, 2026): total household debt $19.1 trillion (+$334B, +1.8% in the quarter); credit card balances a record ~$1.285 trillion (+$22B q/q, +$52B y/y). Flow of card balances into serious (90+ day) delinquency 6.87% in Q3 2026 vs 7.05% in Q3 2025.
- Fed G.19 Consumer Credit (released Nov 6, 2026): credit card interest rate, accounts assessed interest, Q3 2026 = 22.36% (preliminary, reflects August 2026 sample), up from 22.15% in Q2 2026. All card accounts: 21.19%.

## Worked example (our math)
Borrow $1,000 for one year, simple interest, balance unchanged (ignores monthly compounding, which would make the card cost a bit more):
- Card at 22.36%: 1,000 x 0.2236 = $223.60
- Federal undergrad loan at 6.52%: 1,000 x 0.0652 = $65.20
- Ratio: 65.20 / 223.60 = 0.29, so under a third of the cost. (22.36 / 6.52 = 3.43x)
- Card vs 30-yr mortgage rate: 22.36 / 7.11 = 3.1x.

## The framework (definitions, not advice)
- "Good" vs "bad" is not a label on the loan type; it is a comparison: the interest rate (cost) vs what the borrowed money earns or saves, plus whether payments fit.
- Debt that can work: low, fixed rate, financing something expected to earn or save more than it costs (education, a home), with a clear payoff plan. Even this has a real cost (7.11% mortgage).
- Debt that hurts: high rate financing consumption that is gone soon (e.g., carrying a card balance).

## Surprising fact
The average card rate on accounts paying interest (22.36%, Q3 2026) is more than 3x the 2026-27 federal undergrad loan rate (6.52%), and the card rate jumped over 4 points in a single year (2022 to 2023 annual averages).
