# Research brief: Why vet bills are rising: corporate clinic chains
(Business / How They Make Money; posts Fri Jan 8, 2027. Evergreen: every number dated.)

## Angle / timely hook
No single week-of news; evergreen angle. Surprising fact: Mars Inc., the candy company (Snickers, M&M's), is the biggest owner of vet hospitals: Mars Veterinary Health says its network is about 3,000 vet clinics worldwide (company site; "nearly 3,000" in a 2023 Mars release). US brands: Banfield (~1,000+ locations, many inside PetSmart), VCA, BluePearl (~100 hospitals per Fortune, June 2024). Other big US groups: National Veterinary Associates (owned by JAB Holding; ~1,000-1,200 US hospitals, counts vary), Thrive Pet Healthcare.

## Key facts (with dates)
- US CPI "Veterinarian services" (BLS, annual-average % change; via officialdata.org BLS table; 2022 and 2023 also cited by dvm360 from BLS; 2024 cited by furverdict):
  2016 +3.65%, 2017 +2.20%, 2018 +2.55%, 2019 +4.14%, 2020 +3.70%, 2021 +4.21%, 2022 +8.83%, 2023 +9.44%, 2024 +7.40%, 2025 +6.46% (2025 less confirmed; other source shows 7.1%).
  All-items CPI comparison: 8.0% in 2022, 4.1% in 2023 (dvm360 citing BLS).
- Corporate ownership (US): about a quarter of general vet practices and about three-quarters of specialty/emergency practices are owned by large corporations / PE-backed groups (Brakke Consulting's John Volk, via Stateline, March 2024; PE Stakeholder Project: ~75% of specialty in 2021; Inquirer Oct 2026: 75-80% of specialty/ER). Consultant estimates, not official census.
- UK CMA vet market investigation: average vet prices rose 63% from 2016 to 2023, vs 31.3% general inflation (Bank of England) (CMA provisional decision Oct 15, 2025; final decision Mar 24, 2026). Large vet groups charged 16.6% more on average than independents (CMA, Oct 2025). Over 60% of UK practices owned by six large groups. Final remedies (Mar 2026): published price lists, prescription fee cap £21 first item / £12.50 each additional.
- Drivers (reported): staff wages/shortages, more advanced medicine (specialists, imaging), consolidation and pricing power (CMA: acquisitions linked to ~9% higher prices four years later).
- Pet insurance (NAPHIA): US written premium about $4.74B in 2024, 6.4M US pets insured end of 2024 (NAPHIA SOI 2025, Apr 2025); North America $6.2B gross written premium, 7.6M pets insured in 2025 (NAPHIA SOI 2026, June 2026).

## Worked example (our math)
Compound vet CPI 2016-2024: 1.0365*1.0220*1.0255*1.0414*1.0370*1.0421*1.0883*1.0944*1.0740 = 1.564 -> +56.4%.
A $200 vet bill at 2015 prices -> $200 x 1.564 = $312.80, about $313 by 2024.
All-items CPI-U annual average: 237.017 (2015) -> 313.689 (2024) = x1.3235 -> $200 -> $264.70, about $265.
