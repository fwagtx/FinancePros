# Research brief: Non-compete agreements: what they can and can't stop
Video posts Monday, January 4, 2027 (written November 5, 2026). Evergreen; every number dated. Not legal advice: rules vary by state.

## Timely angle
- Illinois' pay floor for new non-competes steps up from $75,000 to $80,000 on January 1, 2027 (820 ILCS 90/10, Illinois Freedom to Work Act, as amended effective Jan 1, 2022). In effect by the posting date.
- Washington State signed ESHB 1155 on March 23, 2026: near-total ban on non-competes (including existing ones) effective June 30, 2027 (Perkins Coie, Faegre Drinker, Holland & Knight alerts).

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. How common: about 18% of US workers are currently bound by a non-compete; 38% have signed one at some point (GAO-23-103785, May 2023, reviewing two national studies incl. Starr, Prescott & Bishara 2021). FTC also estimated ~30 million workers, about 18% (April 2024 rule release; CNBC 4/23/2024).
2. Federal status: FTC final rule banning most non-competes voted 3-2 on April 23, 2024. A federal district court (N.D. Texas, Ryan LLC v. FTC) set it aside on August 20, 2024, before its effective date. On September 5, 2025, the FTC (3-1 vote) dismissed its appeals in the 5th and 11th Circuits and acceded to vacatur; the rule is dead (FTC press release Sept 2025; Paul Weiss; JD Supra). FTC now acts case by case under Section 5, e.g., Gateway Services pet-cremation order (proposed Sept 4, 2025, finalized Nov 2025), covering ~1,800 workers barred nationwide for a year.
3. States that ban most worker non-competes (as of 2026): California (Bus. & Prof. Code 16600), Minnesota (agreements signed on/after July 1, 2023), North Dakota, Oklahoma. Wyoming voids most from July 1, 2025 (exceptions for executives, etc.; sources differ on calling it a full ban). Washington joins June 30, 2027.
4. Pay floors (thresholds) in other states:
   - Illinois: void unless earnings exceed $75,000 (2022-2026); $80,000 from Jan 1, 2027; $85,000 in 2032; $90,000 in 2037. Non-solicit floor $45,000 -> $47,500 in 2027 (820 ILCS 90/10; ILGA statute text).
   - Washington (2026): $126,858.83 employees; $317,147.09 independent contractors (WA L&I). 2027 figure not posted as of Oct 2026 check; thresholds repealed by ban June 30, 2027.
   - Oregon (2026): $119,541. Colorado (2026): $130,014. Tennessee: $70,000 threshold effective July 1, 2026 (Becker's; lower confidence, not used on screen).
5. What a non-compete can and can't do (general US common law, varies by state): it restricts working for a competitor or starting a competing business after you leave; it cannot stop you from quitting. Courts generally enforce only if it protects a legitimate business interest (trade secrets, client relationships, goodwill) and is reasonable in time, geographic area and scope of activity. Some courts "blue pencil" (narrow) overbroad terms; others void them. Separate tools (NDAs, trade secret law, non-solicits) remain even where non-competes are banned (e.g., Washington's ban does not cover non-solicits).

## Worked example (our math)
Illinois worker offered a new non-compete in 2027 earning $78,000:
$80,000 floor - $78,000 pay = $2,000 under the floor -> the non-compete is void and unenforceable under 820 ILCS 90/10 (earnings must EXCEED the floor). The same $78,000 in 2026 would have cleared the $75,000 floor by $3,000.

## The surprising fact
About 1 in 5 US workers is bound by one (GAO 2023), yet a non-compete can't stop you from quitting, and in four states (CA, MN, ND, OK) most are void outright; the national FTC ban never took effect.

## Caveats
- Not legal advice; rules vary by state and by contract; check your state's rules.
- Do not advise signing or breaking a contract.
