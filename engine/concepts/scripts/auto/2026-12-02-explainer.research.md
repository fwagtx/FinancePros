# Research brief: Financial infidelity (hiding money from a partner)
Slot: 2026-12-02-explainer (posts Wed Dec 2, 2026). Researched Oct 3, 2026. Evergreen; every number dated.

## Timely angle
No breaking news. Latest data: Bankrate survey published Jan 29, 2026 (fieldwork Dec 2-8, 2025). Generational "kept a secret" numbers are from Bankrate's Jan 27, 2025 release (fieldwork Dec 9-11, 2024); the 2026 release did not repeat that question.

## Key facts (with dates)
### Bankrate / YouGov, released Jan 27, 2025 (fieldwork Dec 9-11, 2024; 2,217 adults, 1,089 married/civil partnership/living with partner)
- 40% of US adults who are married, in a civil partnership or living with a partner are committing or have committed financial infidelity against their current spouse/partner.
- Examples (share of those in live-in relationships): spending more than partner would be okay with 33%; secret debt 23%; secret credit card 17%; secret savings account 15%; secret checking account 13%.
- By generation (in live-in relationships, committed or committing): Gen Z (18-28) 67%; millennials (29-44) 54%; Gen X (45-60) 33%; baby boomers (61-79) 30%.
- 45% of those in live-in relationships say financial secrets are as bad as or worse than physical infidelity (38% equally bad, 7% worse).
- 62% keep at least some money separate (27% completely separate, 34% mix).
- Ted Rossman quote (exact): "As long as you agree upon the parameters, maintaining separate accounts doesn't constitute financial infidelity."

### Bankrate / YouGov, released Jan 29, 2026 (fieldwork Dec 2-8, 2025; 2,564 adults, 1,208 married/living together/civil partnership)
- 43% of US adults say keeping financial secrets from a romantic partner is at least as bad as physical infidelity (38% equally bad, 5% worse); 31% say not as bad; 26% no opinion.
- By generation, at least as bad: millennials 47%, Gen X 44%, Gen Z 42%, boomers 39%.
- 45% of couples say they don't know everything about each other's finances; 55% say they do.
- Know everything, by generation: boomers 64%, millennials 53%, Gen X 51%, Gen Z 44%.
- 25% keep secret minor debt/expenses/income; 11% don't discuss credit scores/savings/investments; 9% keep secret major sources of debt, expenses or income.
- Reasons (among those not sharing): 28% feel they should be allowed to keep some info private; 15% don't want partner to know; 14% embarrassed.

### Bankrate, released Jan 22, 2024 (fieldwork Dec 18-20, 2023) - older, for context
- 42% in live-in relationships kept/are keeping a financial secret; Gen Z 67%, millennials 57%, Gen X 34%, boomers 33%.
- Secret spending 30%, secret debt 23%, secret savings 19%, secret credit card 18%, secret checking 17%.

### NEFE / The Harris Poll, released Nov 18, 2021 (fieldwork June 28-30, 2021; 2,073 adults, 1,248 ever combined finances)
- 43% of those who ever combined finances admit at least one financial deception.
- 39% hid a purchase, bank account, statement, bill or cash; 21% lied about finances, debt owed or money earned.
- 85% of those touched by a deception (by them or partner) say it affected the relationship; outcomes: argument 42%, less trust 32%, separation of finances or divorce 16% each.
- Earlier NEFE polls: 41% (2018), 42% (Jan 2016). No newer NEFE financial infidelity poll found as of Oct 2026.

## Worked example (exact math)
Average credit card APR: 19.63% (Bankrate weekly national average, as of Sept 30, 2026).
Hypothetical: a secret card carries a $3,000 balance for a full year, no payments, no new charges, no fees.
- Simple interest: $3,000 x 0.1963 = $588.90.
- With daily compounding (how most cards accrue): $3,000 x ((1 + 0.1963/365)^365 - 1) = about $650.48.
- So the hidden balance costs at least $588.90 in a year.

## The surprising fact
Gen Z is the most likely to keep money secrets (67% in Jan 2025) and, in that same survey, the most likely to call it at least as bad as cheating (63%).

## Weakly sourced / caveats
- "Financial infidelity" in Bankrate includes overspending, not only hidden accounts.
- A "December 2025 Harris Poll: 50% lied about or withheld financial info" claim surfaced only in a search summary; not confirmed, not used.
- APR changes weekly; label "as of Sept 30, 2026".
