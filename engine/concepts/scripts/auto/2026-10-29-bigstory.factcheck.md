# Fact-check: 2026-10-29-bigstory (Q3 2026 GDP advance estimate)
Checked Thu Oct 29, 2026 ~1:10 PM CT. The video posts today at 4 PM CT. bea.gov would not load (DNS blocked in this session), so the official figures below come from search excerpts of the BEA release plus several outlets that agree with each other.

## Verified
- Real GDP grew at a 3.4% annual rate in Q3 2026 (Jul-Sep). This is the advance estimate, released Oct 29, 2026. Sources: BEA release excerpt, AP (USNews/KSAT/KIRO7), CNBC, Business Standard, Breitbart, StockTitan.
- Q2 2026 was 2.2% (third estimate, Sept 30, after the annual update; revised up from 1.5%). Q1 2026 is 2.5% (revised from 2.1%). Sources: BEA Q2 third estimate, Advisor Perspectives, MishTalk.
- "Beat forecasts": the consensus was 3.0% (FactSet), 3.1% (CNBC/SBS) or 3.2% (Reuters/investinglive). All are below 3.4%.
- Consumer spending grew at a 3.8% annual rate. Sources: AP via inkl/NewsLooks, Breitbart, BEA coverage.
- AP headline: "US economy grows at brisk 3.4% pace in third quarter on strong consumer spending and AI investment". The line 4 paraphrase is fair. Equipment investment rose 20.9%, and information processing equipment rose 37.4%.
- The September PCE price index was up 3.6% y/y (core 3.1%, +0.5% m/m). This comes from the separate Personal Income and Outlays report released the same day. Source: CNBC Oct 29 ("Third-quarter U.S. GDP increases a better-than-expected 3.4% as September inflation accelerates"), plus fattail and zetik. "Fed's favorite/preferred gauge" is standard wording, and CNBC uses it.
- Attribution check: the GDP report's own quarterly PCE price index rose 2.1% annualized (core 2.5%), down from 5.0% in Q2. The script never cites 2.1%, and it presents 3.6% as a September, year-over-year figure. That is correct for the monthly report, so the figure is not misattributed. Fix 2 below makes the separation explicit so viewers don't think the GDP report said 3.6%.
- "GDP measures the whole economy's output after inflation" is correct: real GDP is inflation-adjusted.
- "Estimate one of three" is correct. The second estimate is due about Nov 25.
- Framing: "Why don't you feel it?" is posed as a question. The answer the script gives is explanatory rather than opinion: GDP is aggregate real output, and prices are up 3.6% y/y. NPR's Oct 29 coverage frames the release around affordability. "Prices still running hot" is supported: 3.6% is well above the Fed's 2% target, and CNBC says inflation "accelerates". The script makes no predictions, gives no advice and offers no political opinion. "Revisions to come" describes BEA's schedule, not a forecast.
- Spoken and on-screen values match on every line. There are no compare scenes (so the 7-character rule doesn't apply) and no keep:true lines.

## Issues
1. **lines[3] (text/say): "The biggest engine was shoppers." REQUIRED.** This claim is not supported as stated. Consumer spending contributed about 2.5 percentage points. Gross private domestic investment contributed about 3.3 points: equipment about 1.16, IP about 0.5, and inventories about 1.61 (investinglive and Reuters summaries; imports subtracted 2.64). So "biggest engine" depends on how you group the categories, and on the standard BEA grouping, investment was larger.
   Replacement text/say: "Shoppers did a lot of the work. Consumer spending grew at a 3.8% pace." / "Shoppers did a lot of the work. Consumer spending grew at a three point eight percent pace."
2. **lines[5] (text/say): attribution clarity. RECOMMENDED.** Make it clear that 3.6% comes from a separate report, not from the GDP report's own quarterly price index (2.1%).
   Replacement text: "But a separate report shows prices still running hot. The Fed's favorite inflation gauge was up 3.6% in September from a year earlier."
