# Research brief: Why is vanilla so expensive?
Slot: 2026-11-14-hot (Hot Take, posts Sat Nov 14, 2026). Researched Oct 2, 2026. Evergreen; every number dated.
Note: direct fetch of most pages (Wikipedia, trade-report sites, journals) was blocked by the egress proxy; numbers confirmed via search results quoting the sources, cross-checked where possible.

## Key facts
1. 2018 price spike: BBC (2018) reported vanilla at about $600 a kilo, more than silver. Quartz / weather.com (2017): record above $600/kg after Cyclone Enawo hit northeast Madagascar in March 2017 (strongest in 13 years), damaging vanilla vines. Gold.co.uk (Apr 2018), MoneyWeek, Marginal Revolution (Jun 2018) repeat "more expensive than silver".
2. Silver comparison: Silver averaged $15.71 per troy ounce in 2018 (Silver Institute, World Silver Survey 2019; USGS 2018 Minerals Yearbook). $15.71 x 32.1507 oz/kg = $505.09/kg -> about $505/kg. So ~$600/kg vanilla > ~$505/kg silver.
3. Madagascar's share: FAOSTAT data: Madagascar produced 3,114 tonnes in 2023, the world's largest producer; about 42% of world production in 2023 (Choices magazine, AAEA, citing FAOSTAT; Indonesia second at 24%). Top five producers = 85-91% of world output. (Popular "80%" figure refers to share of world supply/exports, older; script uses the FAO-based 42% of production.)
4. Hand pollination: outside its native Mexico, vanilla has no natural pollinator; each flower is pollinated by hand on the morning it opens, and it wilts the same day (Madagascar trade/grower sources; Smithsonian). Pods take about 9 months to mature on the vine. Curing (scalding, sweating, drying, conditioning) takes several months, up to ~6 months.
5. Weight loss: FAO Post-harvest Compendium (Vanilla): green-to-conditioned bean weight ratio 4.5:1 to 6:1 depending on grade.
6. Real vanilla vs vanillin: C&EN (American Chemical Society, 2016, "The problem with vanilla"): less than 1% of vanilla flavor comes from the vanilla orchid; of ~18,000 metric tons of vanilla flavor a year, about 85% is vanillin synthesized from petrochemical guaiacol, most of the rest from lignin.
7. Policy: Madagascar set a $250/kg minimum export price in July 2020; it was lifted in spring 2023 (April/May 2023 per Mintec and others), and low grades quickly fell below $20/kg (Mintec, DMNP/Monchy reports). For the 2025-2026 campaign the Ministry of Trade set reference export prices of $15/kg (cuts/powder), $25/kg (red/long), $50/kg (gourmet black) (Aust & Hachmann May 2026; Capmad). Late 2025: Madagascar removed its $4/kg vanilla export tax and US import tariffs on vanilla were removed (Aust & Hachmann May 2026). Aust & Hachmann (May 2026): inflation-adjusted prices at historic lows; oversupply; 2026 Madagascar crop projected 3,000-3,500 t.
8. Farmgate: Fairtrade 2024 Living Income Reference Price report: green vanilla farmgate price averaged about EUR 36/kg in 2018 vs about EUR 2.2/kg in 2023.
9. US standard: 21 CFR 169.3 / 169.175: vanilla extract must contain at least one "unit" of vanilla constituent per gallon = extractives of 13.35 oz of vanilla beans (<=25% moisture), in >=35% alcohol.

## Worked example (hypothetical, bean cost only)
One gallon of US standard vanilla extract needs 13.35 oz of beans = 13.35 x 28.3495 g = 378.47 g = 0.3785 kg.
- At ~$600/kg (2018 peak): 0.3785 x 600 = $227.08 -> about $227 of beans per gallon.
- At ~$48/kg (2024 Madagascar export average): 0.3785 x 48 = $18.17 -> about $18 of beans per gallon.
Ignores alcohol, bottling, shipping, retail margin.

## Surprising fact
Less than 1% of the world's vanilla flavor comes from actual vanilla beans (C&EN).
