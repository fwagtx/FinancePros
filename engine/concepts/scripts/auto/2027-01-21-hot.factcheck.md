# Fact-check: 2027-01-21-hot (free-dinner investment seminar scams)
Checked Nov 22, 2026 for posting Thu Jan 21, 2027, 6 PM CT (evergreen).

## Verified claims
- 2007 joint SEC / NASAA / FINRA report (SEC release 2007-179, Sep 10, 2007): examinations of **110 securities firms and branch offices** that sponsored "free lunch" seminars, April 2006 - June 2007. TRUE, but the 110 are firms/branch offices, not 110 seminars (fixed).
- 100% of the "seminars" were sales presentations. TRUE.
- 50% (of the exams) involved exaggerated or misleading advertising claims. TRUE; it measures ads/advertising, not seminar content (a separate 59% figure in press covers misleading seminar content).
- 23% (of the exams) involved possibly unsuitable **recommendations** (not "investments"). TRUE (fixed wording). 13% appeared fraudulent (not used).
- FTC Consumer Sentinel investment-scam reported losses: 2022 $3.8B (>40% of losses), 2023 $4.6B (largest category, +21%), 2024 $5.7B (largest category, +24%), 2025 $7.9B (nearly half of the record $15.9B total, so the top category). All TRUE.
- Math: 7.9 / 3.8 = 2.08, "about double in three years" (2022 -> 2025). CORRECT.
- Red flags (guaranteed returns, pressure to sign now, "senior" designations) and checking sellers on FINRA BrokerCheck, SEC IAPD (adviserinfo.sec.gov) and state securities regulators: consistent with SEC/FINRA/NASAA investor guidance.
- Timeliness: FTC 2026 annual data normally publishes ~Feb-Mar, so on Jan 21, 2027 the 2025 figures are still the latest full year. No relative time words remain.

## Issues found and fixes applied
1. Line 0 (hook) weak/vague: "That free steak dinner could be the priciest meal you ever eat." is a hedged tease, not a specific fact. -> text/say: "Regulators found every free investment dinner they checked was a sales pitch." (12 words, true, specific, opens the "how do I spot it?" question).
2. Line 1 inaccuracy: "examined 110 free-lunch investment seminars" -> "In 2007, US regulators examined 110 firms running free-lunch investment seminars." Stat label "Seminars examined" -> "Firms examined".
3. Line 2: "dressed up as classes" redundant with new hook -> "100% were sales presentations, not real classes."
4. Line 3: "advertising" -> "ads" (matches on-screen "50% misleading"; tighter).
5. Line 4 inaccuracy: "involved possibly unsuitable investments" -> "made possibly unsuitable recommendations."
6. Line 5: "Scams keep growing" is a loose generalization and figures are *reported* losses -> "FTC data show reported investment-scam losses went from $3.8 billion in 2022 to $7.9 billion in 2025."
7. Line 6: chart line had only 9-11 spoken words (rule: 15+) and text ("Our math:") differed from say ("By our math... just") -> "By our math, $7.9B over $3.8B is about 2, roughly double in three years." (spoken in words, 18 words).
8. Line 7: on-screen "SEC IAPD" differed from spoken "the SEC site" -> item "SEC adviser", spoken "the S E C adviser search"; simplified "Red flags are" to "Red flags:".
9. Line 8: narration was direct instruction ("sign nothing at the table") -> educational: "Regulators suggest checking a seller before you sign anything." Board "Eat the dinner. / Sign nothing." kept as consumer-safety guidance.
10. Cover value "110" not meaningful alone -> "$7.9B" (paid off on lines 5-6 and in the chart end label).
11. Post copy: title "110 checks" -> "110 exams"; IG/TikTok/YouTube rewritten to say 110 firms, "recommendations", "reported" losses, "about double", and "Regulators suggest checking ... before you sign anything". No companies or Anthropic named.

No advice/price predictions, no political opinion, no remaining math or on-screen/spoken mismatches. check.py: OK, 10 lines, 137 spoken words, hook 12 words. Last line unchanged.
