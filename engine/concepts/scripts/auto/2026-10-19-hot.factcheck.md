# Fact-check: 2026-10-19-hot ("Why Is Baby Formula So Expensive?")
Checked 2026-10-01 for posting 2026-10-19. Script JSON only; brief not read.

## Verified
- L1 / cover / posts: four makers (Abbott, Mead Johnson/Reckitt, Nestlé/Gerber, Perrigo) had about 90% of the US infant formula market in 2022. Source: FTC 2022 report, "Market Factors Relevant to Infant Formula Supply Disruptions". OK.
- L2 chart: BLS CPI-U baby food and formula annual averages: 2020 173.071, 2021 177.612, 2022 198.145, 2023 ~214.68, 2024 224.496, 2025 226.736. All chart points match to one decimal. 2020 to 2025 = +31.0% (python check). OK.
- L3: 2022 = +11.56%, so 11.6% is right. Year by year: 2021 +2.6%, 2022 +11.5/11.6%, 2023 +8.4%, 2024 +4.6%, 2025 +1.0%. 2022 is the biggest year. The Feb 2022 Abbott Sturgis recall and the shortage that followed are well documented. OK.
- L4: imports were about 2% of US formula use (CRS IN11932, PolitiFact). The 2022 MFN tariff was 14.9% to 17.5%. OK as a 2022 figure. **Problem with present tense, see Issue 1.**
- L5: GAO-25-106503 (Jan 2025) says winning a WIC contract raised retail prices of the contract formula by 1.7% on average (about 30 cents per 12-oz can). The regression used 2018-2023 data, so "GAO, 2018-2023 data" is right. GAO calls WIC "baby formula's biggest buyer". OK.
- L6: FDA nutrient requirements (21 CFR 107.100) apply to all standard infant formula, store brands included. OK, but see Issue 4.
- L7-8 worked example: it is labeled "Example" and "example price". 12 lb x about 2.5 oz/lb/day (AAP guidance) = about 30 oz/day, so about 900 oz in 30 days. 900 x $0.12 = $108 and 900 x $0.25 = $225, a $117 gap. The math is correct. The prices sit within reported per-prepared-ounce ranges (store brand about $0.10-0.20, name brand about $0.20-0.35). OK.
- On-screen captions match the narration (L6 says "federal" where the caption says "FDA". That is fine.)
- No price predictions, political opinion or explicit advice. The disclaimer is present.

## Issues
1. **L4 tariff claim in present tense is wrong or incomplete for Oct 2026. (FACT, must fix)** "Imports face tariffs up to 17.5%" gives the 2022 MFN ad valorem rate. It leaves out: (a) above the tariff-rate quota, the duty becomes $1.035/kg + 14.9% (CRS), which can be well above 17.5% effective; (b) 2026 surcharges. The Section 122 10-15% global tariff ran Feb 24-Jul 24, 2026, and exempted only *specialized* formulas. Since July 24, 2026, Section 301 "forced-labor" tariffs of 10% or 12.5% apply to imports from 60 economies. I could not confirm that standard infant formula is exempt. So "up to 17.5%" is likely too low as a current rate.
   Fix: put the claim in 2022, which matches the 2% import figure. Caption: "In 2022, imports faced tariffs up to 17.5% and were about 2% of US formula." Say: "In twenty twenty-two, imports faced tariffs up to seventeen and a half percent and were about two percent of US formula." Change the scene label to "2022 tariff: up to 17.5%". Make the same change in post.ig ("Imports faced tariffs up to 17.5%... in 2022"). Make it in yt_desc too: "Tariffs of up to 17.5% keep imports small" also claims cause. Use "In 2022, tariffs of up to 17.5% applied and imports were about 2%".
2. **L5 small wording fix.** WIC contracts go to one *manufacturer* per state agency, and some states contract as multi-state alliances. Suggested: "WIC, the biggest buyer, picks one formula maker per state."
3. **L7 example unit.** "12¢ vs 25¢ an ounce" should say *prepared* ounce. Powder per-ounce prices are much higher. Suggested: "At 12¢ vs 25¢ per prepared ounce". Optional: say which is store brand and which is name brand so L8's "Same nutrient rules" lands. Keep it descriptive, not an instruction.
4. **L6 overstated "every".** "Exempt" infant formulas for medical conditions can depart from the standard nutrient rules (21 CFR 107.50). Suggested: "every standard US infant formula" (or "every regular...").
5. **Hook (L1), minor.** It is true and specific and short (about 3 s). It is in past tense with no year until L2, which softens it. A stronger option: "Just four companies made about 90% of America's baby formula, and prices are up 31%." Not required.
6. **Time words.** None will be wrong on Oct 19. The 2022 and 2025 data are labeled with years. The present-tense tariff line is covered in Issue 1.
