## Facts (reported Wednesday Oct 21, 2026 after the close; Meta Q3 2026 press release, SEC 8-K Exhibit 99.1)
- Q3 2026 revenue: $64.601 billion ($64.6B), up 26% from $51.242B in Q3 2025. Analysts expected ~$63.3B. (SEC 8-K Ex 99.1 snippet; Yahoo Finance; stocktitan)
- Income from operations: $12.988B, down 37% (SEC Ex 99.1). Excluding the legal charge: $22.88B; CFO Susan Li said op income would have risen 11% y/y ex-charge.
- Diluted EPS: $3.83 (vs $1.05 a year earlier); $7.40 excluding the charge. Consensus ~$3.56 (one source cited $6.80 — inconsistent; not used).
- Legal charge: $9.89 billion in Q3, tied to Meta's August 2026 settlement with state attorneys general over teen mental health / social media (stocktitan, Vested Finance, TradingKey). NOTE: total settlement size is reported inconsistently ($17.1B per CA AG/MLex vs "up to about $18.59B") and number of states differs (52 vs 27) — NOT used in script.
- Capital expenditures incl. principal payments on finance leases: $34.91B in Q3 2026. Cash flow from operations: $32.79B. Free cash flow: negative $2.12B (stocktitan/vested; CNBC headline Oct 22: "Meta stock sinks 6% as AI spend pushes free cash flow negative").
- Cash and marketable securities: $82.45B as of Sept 30, 2026.
- Outlook: 2026 capex narrowed to $135–145B from $130–145B (low end raised; CNBC: "raised the floor of its expected capex spend"). 2026 total expenses $175–179B. Q4 revenue guide $73–76B.
- NEW CLASS C STOCK: Meta will distribute one nonvoting Class C share for each outstanding Class A and Class B share, as a one-time dividend, once an amended certificate of incorporation takes effect on Jan. 19, 2027 (Yahoo Finance, Yahoo/X post: trading expected to start late January 2027; SiliconANGLE; unite.ai). Effectively doubles share count, like a 2-for-1 split, but new shares have no vote. Purpose per Meta's board committee: capital raising, employee compensation, possible acquisitions and other corporate needs (The Information: "pave way for AI-related fundraising"). Zuckerberg retains voting control (voting % reported as 56.1% vs ~60.6% — differs; not used). Zuckerberg-related entities approved, so no shareholder vote. Only 20% of Zuckerberg's Class C can be sold without restrictions.
- Fortune/CNBC link the move partly to a proposed California wealth tax (Prop on Nov ballot) and Zuckerberg's residency — politically sensitive, NOT used.
- Analyst view: JPMorgan's Doug Anmuth on removing the overhang: "the sooner the better in our view" (Yahoo Finance).
