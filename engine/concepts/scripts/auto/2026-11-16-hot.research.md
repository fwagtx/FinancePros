# Research: Scam alert - fake home warranty and car warranty calls

## The big auto-warranty robocall case (FCC)
- FCC fined an auto-warranty robocall enterprise (Roy M. Cox Jr., Aaron Michael Jones and associates) $299,997,000 in Aug 2023, the largest fine in FCC history.
- The operation made more than 5 billion robocalls to more than 500 million phone numbers during a three-month span in 2021; used over 1 million different caller ID numbers (spoofing); sold vehicle service contracts under the false claim of selling auto warranties.
- In 2022 the FCC ordered US voice providers to stop carrying its traffic; these auto warranty robocalls then dropped 99%. (FCC; Fierce Network; CBS News)
- As of 2025, auto warranty calls no longer in the FCC's top five robocall complaints (ConsumerAffairs 2026) - not used on screen.

## How the scam works (FTC / FCC consumer guidance)
- Recorded message: your vehicle's manufacturer warranty is "expiring", "final notice", press 1; urgency; asks for payment or personal info.
- What's sold is often a vehicle service contract, not a manufacturer's warranty; callers aren't your carmaker/dealer.
- Home warranty version: letters/calls styled as "final notice" about home coverage, urgent language. (state AGs; FTC)
- FTC/FCC advice: don't press any numbers, hang up; contact the company using a number you look up yourself; report to FTC (ReportFraud.ftc.gov); Do Not Call Registry.

## Data (FTC Consumer Sentinel)
- Reported fraud losses: 2022 $8.8B; 2023 $10.0B; 2024 $12.5B; 2025 $15.9B (record, +27%). (FTC press releases Feb 2023, Feb 2024, Mar 2025; 2026 via Finextra/PYMNTS)
- Impostor scams: most-reported fraud category for the 5th straight year in 2025; $3.5B reported lost. (FTC, June 2026; CNBC June 26, 2026)

## Worked example
- 5 billion calls / 3 months (~92 days) = about 54 million calls a day (5,000,000,000 / 92 = 54,347,826).
- FCC fine per call: $299,997,000 / 5,000,000,000 = about 6 cents per call.

## Surprising fact
- One operation made 5 billion robocalls in three months.
