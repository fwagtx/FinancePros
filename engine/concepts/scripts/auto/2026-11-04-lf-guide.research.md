# Research brief: How to choose a health insurance plan during open enrollment (long-form, posts Nov 4, 2026)

## Key numbers
- KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey (Oct 22, 2025): average annual premium $26,993 family (worker pays $6,850), $9,325 single (worker pays $1,440). Family premium +6% vs 2024; +26% vs 2020; +53% vs 2015. Average single deductible (plans with a general deductible) $1,886.
- Family premium series (KFF EHBS): 2015 $17,545 | 2016 $18,142 | 2017 $18,764 | 2018 $19,616 | 2019 $20,576 | 2020 $21,342 | 2021 $22,221 | 2022 $22,463 | 2023 $23,968 | 2024 $25,572 | 2025 $26,993
- ACA out-of-pocket maximum (self-only), chartable: 2014 $6,350 | 2016 $6,850 | 2018 $7,350 | 2020 $8,150 | 2022 $8,700 | 2024 $9,450 | 2025 $9,200 | 2026 $10,600 | 2027 $12,000 (family 2027 $24,000). 2027 from CMS 2027 Notice of Benefit and Payment Parameters (May 2026).
- HSA 2027 (IRS Rev. Proc. 2026-24, May 2026): contribution $4,500 self / $9,000 family; +$1,000 catch-up at 55+. HDHP minimum deductible $1,750 / $3,500; HDHP out-of-pocket max $8,700 / $17,400. (2026: min deductible $1,700/$3,400; HSA $4,400/$8,750.)
- One Big Beautiful Bill Act: from Jan 1, 2026, marketplace bronze and catastrophic plans count as HSA-eligible (IRS Notice 2026-05).
- Health FSA 2026 limit $3,400 (IRS); FSA is generally use-it-or-lose-it (plans may allow a carryover or grace period).
- ACA open enrollment for 2027 coverage: Nov 1, 2026 - Jan 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov (CMS confirmed Aug 2026); pick by Dec 15 for Jan 1 coverage. Some state exchanges differ.
- Enhanced premium tax credits expired Dec 31, 2025; for 2026 the 400% of poverty cap on subsidies returned. A House-passed bill to restore them (Jan 2026) has not become law (as of Sept 2026).
- Metal tiers (actuarial value): bronze ~60%, silver ~70%, gold ~80%, platinum ~90%. Cost-sharing reductions only on silver plans for eligible incomes.
- Outside open enrollment you generally need a qualifying life event (special enrollment period: typically 30 days for employer plans, 60 days on the marketplace).
- Plan types: HMO (network only, usually referrals), PPO (out-of-network covered at higher cost), EPO (network only, no referrals usually), HDHP (high deductible; HSA-eligible).

## Worked example (hypothetical, self-only, simplified: no copays)
Plan A: $150/mo premium ($1,800/yr), $1,000 deductible, 20% coinsurance, $4,000 OOP max.
Plan B (HSA plan): $50/mo ($600/yr), $3,000 deductible, 20% coinsurance, $6,000 OOP max, employer puts $500 in HSA.
- $500 of care: A = 1,800 + 500 = $2,300. B = 600 + 500 - 500 = $600.
- $5,000 of care: A = 1,800 + (1,000 + 20% x 4,000 = 1,800) = $3,600. B = 600 + (3,000 + 20% x 2,000 = 3,400) - 500 = $3,500.
- $40,000 of care: A = 1,800 + 4,000 (cap) = $5,800. B = 600 + 6,000 (cap) - 500 = $6,100.
- HSA tax: $1,000 contributed in the 22% bracket saves $220 of federal income tax (more if via payroll, which also avoids FICA).
