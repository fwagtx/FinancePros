# Research brief: What happens if you die without a will? (US intestacy)
Slot: 2026-11-12-hot (posts Thu Nov 12, 2026). Researched Oct 1, 2026. Education, not legal advice.

## No timely news hook
No major news peg found; evergreen. Latest Caring.com survey is the 2025 edition (published Jan 31, 2025; YouGov, 2,500+ US adults). No 2026 Caring.com survey found as of Oct 2026. (Trust & Will published a separate 2026 report: 56% of Americans have no estate plan; different methodology, not used in script.)

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
- Caring.com 2025 Wills and Estate Planning Study: 24% of respondents said they have a will (13% living trust, 4% other documents). Down from 33% in 2022. 43% of those without a will "just haven't gotten around to it." [Caring.com 2025 survey; WHSV Oct 15, 2025]
- Dying without a valid will = "intestacy." State intestate-succession law decides who inherits the probate (intestate) estate. Rules vary by state.
- Assets with a named beneficiary (life insurance, retirement accounts), joint property with survivorship, etc., generally pass outside intestacy. [NY EPTL commentary: nyestateslawyer.com, nycprobate.com]
- Uniform Probate Code (model law; full UPC adopted by about 18 states, others adopt parts):
  - UPC 2-102: spouse gets the ENTIRE intestate estate if no descendant or parent survives, or if all descendants are also the spouse's (and spouse has no other descendants). Smaller shares (bracketed dollar amounts + fraction) in blended-family / surviving-parent cases.
  - UPC 2-105 "No taker": if no heir qualifies, the intestate estate passes to the state (e.g., Mass. Gen. Laws 190B 2-105; Hawaii 560:2-105; Utah 75-2-105 "for the benefit of the state school fund").
- Concrete state example: New York EPTL 4-1.1: spouse + children -> spouse gets $50,000 plus one-half of the residue; children share the balance by representation. Spouse and no children -> spouse gets everything. [EPTL 4-1.1 via nycprobate.com, nyestateslawyer.com, Nolo]
- New York gives unmarried partners no intestate share; NY does not recognize common-law marriage. [Nolo "Intestate Succession in New York"; nyestateslawyer.com]

## Surprising fact
In New York, a married parent who dies without a will does NOT leave everything to the spouse: the kids get part of it. And a long-term unmarried partner gets $0 under intestacy.

## Worked example (hypothetical, New York, spouse + children, $250,000 intestate estate after debts and costs)
- Spouse: $50,000 + 1/2 x ($250,000 - $50,000) = $50,000 + $100,000 = $150,000
- Children (shared): $250,000 - $150,000 = $100,000
- Unmarried partner (if instead unmarried): $0
