# Fact-check: 2026-10-11-business (posts Oct 11, 2026)

## Verified claims
- **Prob. Code 10810 schedule** (L4, post, yt_desc): 4% of the first $100,000, 3% of the next $100,000, 2% of the next $800,000, 1% of the next $9,000,000, 0.5% of the next $15,000,000, and a court-set amount above $25M. The script's figures are correct. (FindLaw / Justia / onecle, PROB 10810)
- **Gross value** (L2, L7, post): 10810(b) values the estate "without reference to encumbrances or other obligations on estate property." So the claim that a mortgage doesn't shrink the fee is correct.
- **Executor, 10800** (L8): the personal representative gets the same schedule. "Can claim" is accurate because executors may waive the fee.
- **Extraordinary services, 10811** (L9): the court may allow additional compensation "in an amount the court determines is just and reasonable." The line "Extra work... needs a judge's OK" is accurate, and litigation is a standard example of extraordinary services.
- **Math** (python): $900K comes to 4,000 + 3,000 + 14,000 = $21,000. Chart points 100→4,000; 200→7,000; 300→9,000; 500→13,000; 700→17,000; 900→21,000; 1000→23,000 all check out. The effective rate falls from 4.0% to 2.3%, so "fee rises, rate falls" is true.
- **$900K example**: C.A.R. puts the August 2026 statewide median at $901,420 (July: $887,210; Aug 2025: $900,620). The example is realistic and close to the median.
- **Small estates** (L9 "Some small estates"): the personal-property affidavit limit under Prob. Code 13100 is $208,850 for deaths on or after April 1, 2025. A separate petition under 13151 covers a primary residence up to $750K. The hedge "some" is accurate.
- **"Most states: reasonable fee"**: correct. Only a handful of states set statutory percentage schedules.
- **Quotes**: no direct statutory quotes are used. The single quote marks around 'reasonable' paraphrase the standard, which is acceptable.
- No stale time words, advice, predictions or politics. The cover ($900K / $21,000) is paid off in L2 and L5.
