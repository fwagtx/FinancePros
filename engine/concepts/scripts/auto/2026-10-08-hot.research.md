# Research: Why is a mattress so expensive when it's always on sale?

Angle: "Why is X so expensive" + myth check on the "sale" price. No single news peg this week; evergreen, using the latest full-year filings (Sleep Number fiscal 2025, filed March 2026) and the 2025 Mattress Firm pricing settlement.

## Key numbers (latest available)
- Sleep Number fiscal 2025 (53 weeks ended Jan 3, 2026): net sales $1,411.5 million (about $1.4 billion), down 16% from about $1.7 billion in 2024. (10-K FY2025; Q4/FY2025 release, Mar 2026; TradingView)
- Gross profit rate 2025: 59.0% of net sales (59.6% in 2024). So cost of sales = 41.0% of each sales dollar. (Sleep Number FY2025 release / 10-K; Motley Fool Q4 2025 call transcript "full year gross margin was 59%")
- Sales and marketing expense 2025: 47.1% of net sales (45.6% in 2024). (Sleep Number 10-K FY2025, MD&A). Consistent with quarterly 2025 figures of 48.1% (Q1) and 48.8% (Q3).
- Operating result 2025: operating LOSS of $47 million (vs operating income of $23 million in 2024). Net loss $132.0 million. (10-K FY2025; FY2025 release)
- THE SURPRISE: a company that keeps 59 cents of gross profit on every sales dollar still lost money in 2025, because selling the mattress (stores, salespeople, ads, promotions) cost more than making it: 47.1 cents vs 41.0 cents per dollar.

## Worked example (exact math, using Sleep Number's 2025 ratios, illustrative $2,000 sale)
- Cost to make and deliver (cost of sales, 41.0%): 2,000 x 0.410 = $820
- Sales and marketing (47.1%): 2,000 x 0.471 = $942
- Left for everything else (G&A, R&D, restructuring, profit): 2,000 - 820 - 942 = $238
- Company-wide, those other costs exceeded that remainder in 2025: operating margin = -47 / 1,411.5 = about -3.3%.
- Check: 41.0% + 47.1% = 88.1% of every sales dollar before overhead.

## Why "always on sale"
- FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, 16 CFR 233.1: a "former price" in a price-reduction ad must be one at which the item was "openly and actively offered for sale, for a reasonably substantial period of time, in the recent, regular course of business, honestly and in good faith." If not, the "reduction" is fictitious.
- Hampton v. Mattress Firm (California class action): alleged Mattress Firm's website showed fictitious crossed-out "original" prices next to sale prices. Settlement preliminarily approved May 16, 2025; covers California buyers on MattressFirm.com from Aug 1, 2020 to July 16, 2024; $40 per qualifying mattress order (cash or store credit); up to $5,476,000 in settlement awards (reported as $6.4M+ total by ClassAction.org). Claim deadline Aug 5, 2025. Allegations only; settlements are not findings of wrongdoing. (ClassAction.org; TopClassActions; hamptonvmattressfirmlitigation.com)
- Mattress Firm is now owned by Somnigroup (formerly Tempur Sealy), acquisition completed Feb 5, 2025. Somnigroup 2025 net sales $7,476.5 million; Q4 2025 gross margin 44.0%. (Somnigroup FY2025 release)

## Takeaway (education, not advice)
- The price tag mostly pays for selling, not the foam. A "sale" price is only a discount if the higher price was ever really charged.
