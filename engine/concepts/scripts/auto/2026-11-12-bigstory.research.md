## The facts (Freddie Mac PMMS, released Nov 12, 2026, 12:00 ET)
- 30-year fixed-rate mortgage averaged 7.11% as of Nov 12, 2026, down from 7.18% last week. A year ago: 6.24%.
- 15-year FRM averaged 6.45%, down from 6.57% last week. A year ago: 5.49%.
- Fourth consecutive weekly decline; lowest level in seven weeks (lowest since Sep 24, when it was 7.03%).
- Quote (Sam Khater, Freddie Mac chief economist, Nov 12, 2026): "Mortgage rates dropped to their lowest level in seven weeks, a welcome development that gives borrowers greater flexibility as they navigate the current housing market."
- PMMS covers conventional, conforming, fully amortizing purchase loans for borrowers who put 20% down and have excellent credit.

## Context
- Rates had climbed since late February 2026 (Iran conflict pushed oil and inflation up, per AP); 10-year Treasury yield hit highest since 2002 in October, then pulled back (AP, Oct 15).
- Mortgage rates tend to track the 10-year Treasury yield rather than the Fed's rate directly.
- Fannie Mae projects 30-yr averaging 7.4% in Q4 2026 and 7.5% in 2027 (per AP/MPA) - forecast, not used on screen.

## Our math (principal + interest, $400,000, 30-year fixed)
- 7.40%: $2,769.52/mo -> $2,770
- 7.11%: $2,690.83/mo -> $2,691  (about $79/mo less than at the October peak; ~$944/yr)
- 6.24% (year ago): $2,460.27/mo -> $2,460  (about $231/mo less than today)

## What it means
- Buyers and refinancers get a little relief vs. October, but borrowing still costs much more than a year ago.
- Individual quotes vary with credit score, down payment, loan type.
