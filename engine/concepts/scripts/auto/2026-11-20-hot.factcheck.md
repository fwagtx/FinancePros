## Verified
- FTC Consumer Sentinel 2025: $15.9B total reported fraud losses (FTC testimony, Mar 25, 2026); imposter scams $3.5B. The FTC press release (June 2026), "FTC Data Show People Reported Losing $3.5 Billion to Imposter Scams in 2025", says losses rose "nearly three times since 2020". 3.5/1.2 = 2.92, so "nearly triple" is correct.
- Imposter-loss series: 2020 ~$1.2B (FTC Feb 2021) OK; 2022 $2.6B, 2023 $2.7B, 2024 $2.95B OK; 2025 $3.5B OK. **2021 is wrong:** the script has $2.3B, the first-release figure. The FTC's Feb 2023 release revised it: "$2.6 billion, up from $2.4 billion in 2021".
- Amazon Prime: $2.5B settlement (Sept 2025). The revised order raised the cap from $51 to $200, paid automatically, with more than $845M paid as of Sept 2026 (FTC press release, Sept 2026). Quote "No one from the FTC or Amazon will ask you for money to get a refund." matches FTC refund-page excerpts word for word.
- FTC refund FAQ: the FTC "never requires you to pay upfront fees or asks you for sensitive information, like your Social Security number or bank account information".
- BBB 2026 red flags (upfront fee, SSN/bank info, vague case details, act-now pressure): found only in local TV and secondary reports citing the BBB (WALB, WGEM, KYMA, 23ABC, July/Mar 2026). No bbb.org primary source was found, so the wording must attribute them to news reports.
- FTC staff report "Consumers and Class Actions" (Sept 2019): median calculated claims rate 9% is confirmed. The "spam" finding comes from the report's survey of *sample* email notices: many respondents saw them as spam or ads. It is not about real notices in general.
- Claims administrators: administration costs are commonly, but not always, paid from the settlement fund, so the script should say "typically". Filing a claim is free.
