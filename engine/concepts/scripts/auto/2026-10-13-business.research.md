# Research brief: How florists price flowers on Valentine's Day (slot 2026-10-13-business)

Show: How They Make Money. Posts Tuesday, October 13, 2026. Evergreen: all figures dated.
Note: no single public company dominates US florist retail; this is an industry story (retail florists + growers). No 10-K numbers used. Company/association figures are named.

## Timely angle
No breaking news this week. Evergreen angle: Valentine's Day 2026 price data (February 2026) and the florist pricing formula.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)

1. Retail price of a dozen roses (FinanceBuzz annual florist price survey, February 2026):
   - National average for a dozen long-stem red roses for Valentine's Day 2026: $93.07, up nearly 3% from 2025.
   - Up $12.91 since 2023, a 16% increase (implies 2023 = $80.16).
   - Most expensive state: Hawaii $126.65; cheapest: Indiana $71.99 (2026).
   - Sources: FinanceBuzz "The Cost of a Dozen Roses in Each State"; reported by National Today (Feb 9, 2026); CBS News (2025 edition).

2. Chartable data series: FinanceBuzz national average price, dozen long-stem red roses, Valentine's Day
   | Year | Price |
   |---|---|
   | 2023 | $80.16 (derived: $93.07 - $12.91) |
   | 2024 | $88.61 |
   | 2025 | $90.50 |
   | 2026 | $93.07 |
   Check: 93.07/80.16 = 1.161 (+16%). 93.07/90.50 = 1.028 (nearly 3%). 90.50/88.61 = 1.021 (2%). Sources: FinanceBuzz via CBS News (2025: $90.50, 2024: $88.61), National Today (2026).

3. Florist pricing rule of thumb (trade guidance, not a law):
   - "A common rule-of-thumb in the floral industry is to mark up fresh product 3.5x and hard goods 2.5x, with a labor charge of 25 percent." Florists' Review ("Pricing For Profit" / "Key Markup Tactics"). Range cited: 3x to 5x on fresh flowers, norm 3.5x.
   - Florists' Review worked example: $20 flowers x 3.5 = $70; $10 hard goods x 2.5 = $25; subtotal $95; + 25% labor = $118.75. (Checks out.)
   - Also: Lacy Bird pricing guide, hanafloristpos (3.5x fresh, 2.5x hard goods, ~25% labor).

4. Worked example (hypothetical, labeled as example; assumptions stated):
   - Assumption: florist pays $1 per rose stem wholesale in a normal week, $2 in Valentine's week. (Bulk/wholesale rose prices commonly cited $0.65-$2.50 per stem; Valentine's is the peak of that range. Sources: Whole Blossoms 2026 bulk price guide; WREG/floraldaily report $1-$3 per stem.)
   - Normal: 12 x $1 = $12 wholesale; x 3.5 = $42 flower price (before vase and labor).
   - Valentine's: 12 x $2 = $24 wholesale; x 3.5 = $84.
   - Difference: $84 - $42 = $42. A $1 per stem wholesale rise = 12 x $1 x 3.5 = $42 more per dozen at retail.
   - THE SURPRISE: the markup is a multiplier, so a wholesale spike gets multiplied at the counter.

5. Growers' side: Colombia (Asocolflores, 2026 season)
   - Asocolflores projected 2026 flower exports of about US$2.4 billion, of which about US$480 million from the Valentine's season (about 980 million stems). $480M / $2.4B = 20%. Sources: El Colombiano (Feb 2026, "Dolar barato amarga el San Valentin"), Teleantioquia (San Valentin 2026).
   - Colombia One (Feb 14, 2026): shipments Jan 15 - Feb 9 represent 20% of annual sales; ~65,000 tons, ~900 million stems to the US, up 13% vs 2025.
   - About 80% of Colombia's flower exports go to the US.
   - Other growers' cost pressures cited (Feb 2026): 10% US tariff on Colombian flowers since April 2025; Colombian minimum wage up 23%. (Not used in script; policy-neutral.)

6. Demand (NRF / Prosper Insights, released Jan 27, 2026):
   - Record $29.1 billion total Valentine's Day spending projected for 2026 (vs $27.5B in 2025).
   - $3.1 billion expected on flowers in 2026 (2025: $2.9B; 2024: $2.6B).
   - 41% planned to buy flowers. Average budget $199.78.
   - Sources: NRF press release via StreetInsider/GlobeNewswire, Talk Business & Politics, Empower.
