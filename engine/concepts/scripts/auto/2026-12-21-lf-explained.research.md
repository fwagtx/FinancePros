## Worked examples (our own math; ASSUMPTION: 7% a year return, roughly the long-run after-inflation stock return, so results are in today's dollars; yearly deposits at year end; no taxes or fees unless stated)
Formula (future value of yearly deposits): FV = P x ((1 + r)^n - 1) / r
- $60,000 salary, save 10% = $6,000/yr:
  - 30 yrs: $566,765; 35 yrs: $829,421; 40 yrs: $1,197,811
- Save 15% = $9,000/yr:
  - 30 yrs: $850,147; 35 yrs: $1,244,132; 40 yrs: $1,796,716
- Start at 25 vs 35 (to 65), $6,000/yr: 40 yrs = $1,197,811 (put in $240,000) vs 30 yrs = $566,765 (put in $180,000).
- SURPRISE FACT: saving $6,000/yr only from 25 to 35 (10 years, $60,000 total), then stopping and letting it grow 30 more years: $631,046 -- more than someone who saves $6,000 every year from 35 to 65 ($180,000 total) and ends with $566,765.
- Doubling: 1.07^10 = 1.967 -> roughly doubles every ~10 years (Rule of 72: 72/7 = ~10.3). 1.07^30 = 7.61; 1.07^40 = 14.97.
- Employer match example: 50% match on up to 6% of pay = $1,800/yr on $60,000. Match alone over 30 yrs: $170,029. Save 10% + $1,800 match = $7,800/yr -> $736,794 in 30 yrs.
- Fees: $9,000/yr for 30 yrs at 7% minus 0.05% fee (6.95%) = $842,537; minus 1% (6%) = $711,524. Gap: $131,013. (ICI 2024 avg equity mutual fund 0.40% -> $791,352.)
- Credit card: $5,000 balance at 22.36% = about $1,118 interest in a year (simple, ignores monthly compounding/payments). Versus 7% assumed return: paying off 22% debt is a guaranteed "return" of the rate avoided.
- Lifestyle creep example: $60,000 + 3% raise = $61,800; raise is $1,800; sending half ($900) to savings bumps the savings rate. $900/yr extra over 30 yrs at 7% = $85,015 (900*94.4608).
