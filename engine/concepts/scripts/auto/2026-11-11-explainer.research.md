# 2026-11-11-explainer research brief: ETFs explained with acorns

Posts Wednesday, November 11, 2026. Evergreen; every figure dated. Education only; no fund recommendations.

## Definitions (stable)
- ETF (exchange-traded fund): a pooled fund holding a basket of securities (e.g., stocks or bonds) whose shares trade on a stock exchange throughout the trading day at market prices, like a single stock.
- Mutual fund: also a pooled basket, but investors buy and redeem shares directly with the fund at the net asset value (NAV) computed once per day, after the market close (forward pricing).
- Creation/redemption: large institutions ("authorized participants") create ETF shares by delivering a basket of the underlying securities to the fund and redeem by receiving securities back (in-kind), rather than cash. Because the ETF hands out securities instead of selling them for cash, it usually does not realize capital gains to meet redemptions. Mutual funds typically pay redemptions in cash, so managers may sell holdings, and realized gains are distributed to all remaining shareholders (taxable in taxable accounts). (State Street SPDR education; Morningstar; iShares; Schwab.)
- Caveat: tax advantage matters in taxable accounts; ETFs can still distribute gains, and investors owe tax on their own sales at a gain.
- Expense ratio: the annual fund operating cost as a percent of assets, taken out of fund assets (not billed separately).

## Key numbers (with dates)
- US ETF industry, end of July 2026 (ETFGI, press release Aug 2026): 5,590 ETFs, $15.74 trillion in assets, 493 providers, 3 exchanges; record $1.23 trillion YTD net inflows. (Confirmed via ETFGI release title + Funds Society + Mondo Visione.)
- ICI: combined US ETF assets $15.67 trillion in July 2026 (ICI release Aug 28, 2026). Close to ETFGI; different coverage.
- First US ETF: SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY) commenced operations Jan 22, 1993; began trading on the American Stock Exchange Jan 29, 1993 (State Street; Wikipedia; etf.com).
- One share of an S&P 500 index ETF holds a slice of all 500 companies in the index (the index has 500 companies, about 503 share classes).

## Taxes: the surprising fact
- In 2025, 7% of ETFs paid a capital gain distribution vs. 52% of mutual funds; since 2016 the averages are 9% vs. 53% (State Street Global Advisors, "Tax efficiency is structural," 2026; repeated by Western & Southern). Note: State Street is an ETF issuer.
- Morningstar (late 2025): only about 6% of ETFs surveyed estimated a capital gains distribution for 2025.

## Expense ratios (ICI, "Trends in the Expenses and Fees of Funds, 2025," March 2026; asset-weighted)
- Index equity ETFs: 0.14% (unchanged in 2025)
- Index equity mutual funds: 0.05%
- Actively managed equity mutual funds: 0.64%
- Equity mutual funds overall: 0.40%
- Takeaway: cost tracks the strategy (index vs active), not just the wrapper. Index mutual funds averaged lower than index ETFs.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
$10,000 held for one year, ignoring growth:
- at 0.14%: 10,000 x 0.0014 = $14
- at 0.64%: 10,000 x 0.0064 = $64
- difference: $50 a year.
Acorn version: 10,000 acorns at 0.14% = 14 acorns a year to the basket keeper.

## Hook
"One share can hold five hundred companies. That's an ETF." (true for an S&P 500 index ETF; paid off by the basket explanation.)
