# Research brief: Holiday debt - how BNPL and credit cards add up in January
Slot: 2026-12-01-explainer (posts Tue Dec 1, 2026). Researched Oct 2, 2026. Many primary sites (lendingtree.com, news.adobe.com, federalreserve.gov, consumerfinance.gov, newyorkfed.org) block direct fetch here; figures confirmed from search results quoting the primary source, cross-checked with a second outlet where possible.

## Timely angle
Dec 1, 2026 sits right after Black Friday / Cyber Monday (Nov 27 / Nov 30, 2026). No new 2026 holiday-debt data exists yet, so the video uses the latest completed season (2025 holidays, surveyed Jan 2026).

## Key facts (dated)
1. LendingTree holiday debt survey (published Jan 2026, about the 2025 season): 37% of Americans took on holiday debt; average $1,223 (up from $1,181 in the Jan 2025 survey; highest since 2022). Parents of kids under 18: 48% borrowed, avg $1,324.
   - Where the debt sits (among those with holiday debt, multiple answers allowed): credit cards 62%, BNPL loans 35%, store credit cards 32%.
   - 63% expect it to take 3 months or longer to pay off; 40% are paying 20% interest or more.
   - Sources: lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/holiday-debt-tariffs/ ("Survey: Holiday Debt Hits $1,223"); San.com "Nearly 40% of Americans took on holiday debt"; KSAT (Jan 2, 2026); Debt Discipline recap.
2. Credit card APR: Federal Reserve G.19 (released Aug 7, 2026): commercial bank interest rate on credit card plans, accounts assessed interest = 22.15% in Q2 2026 (up from 21.52% in Q1 2026). All accounts: 20.94% (Q2 2026). Sources: federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/20260807 (via search); The Money Overview; LendingTree credit card debt statistics.
3. Pay-in-4 structure (CFPB definition): four-installment, no-interest loan, typically 25% down at purchase, remaining three payments every two weeks, so paid off over six weeks; no interest but may carry late fees. Sources: CFPB BNPL reports (2022, Jan 2025); CRS R48858 (Feb 18, 2026).
4. Adobe holiday data (released Jan 7, 2026): US online spend Nov 1-Dec 31, 2025 = $257.8B (+6.8% YoY). BNPL drove $20.0B of online holiday spend, up 9.8% YoY, an all-time high; 82.2% of BNPL purchases on smartphones. Cyber Monday (Dec 1, 2025): $14.25B online, $1.03B via BNPL (+4.2% YoY). Sources: Adobe newsroom (Jan 7, 2026 and Dec 2, 2025); MarketScreener; Digital Commerce 360; Payments Dive; Fortune (Dec 5, 2025).
5. CFPB BNPL stacking (report released Jan 13, 2025, data from six large BNPL lenders): in 2022, about 63% of borrowers originated multiple simultaneous BNPL loans at some point during the year; 33% did so across multiple firms. Avg originations per borrower 9.5 in 2022 (8.5 in 2021). About 20% were heavy users (>1 loan/month on average). Sources: files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_BNPL_Report_2025_01.pdf; Payments Dive; Yahoo Finance.
6. New York Fed Household Debt and Credit, credit card balances: Q2 2026 = $1.26T ($1.263T), up $21B in the quarter (released Aug 11, 2026). Record $1.28T in Q4 2025 (rose $44B in Q4; +5.5% YoY; released Feb 10, 2026). Q1 2026: $1.25T (CNBC May 12, 2026).

## Worked example (hypothetical; stated assumptions)
Balance $1,223 (LendingTree average), APR 22.15% (Fed G.19, Q2 2026, accounts assessed interest), monthly rate = 0.2215/12 = 1.8458%. Interest computed monthly, rounded to cents.
A) Minimum payment only. Assumed minimum = interest + 1% of balance, with a $25 floor (a common issuer formula; issuers vary). Month 1 interest = $1,223 x 0.018458 = $22.57; payment = 22.57 + 12.23 = $34.80.
   Result: 91 months (about 7.6 years), total paid $2,409.94, interest $1,186.94.
B) Paid off in 4 months with equal payments: P = 1223 x r / (1 - (1+r)^-4) = $319.99/month.
   Interest by month: $22.57, $17.08, $11.49, $5.80 -> total interest $56.94; total paid $1,279.94.
C) Same $1,223 as a Pay-in-4 BNPL plan: four payments of $305.75 over six weeks, $0 interest if paid on time (late fees possible).
Difference A vs B: $1,130.00 more interest.

## Surprising fact
At minimum payments, the interest ($1,186.94) nearly equals the original holiday debt ($1,223): the balance roughly doubles in cost and takes about 7.6 years.
