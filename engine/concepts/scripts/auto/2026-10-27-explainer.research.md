# Research brief: Pay yourself first: automating savings (Explainer, posts Oct 27, 2026)

- Madrian & Shea, "The Power of Suggestion: Inertia in 401(k) Participation and Savings Behavior" (Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2001; NBER w7682): at one large company, 401(k) participation was 86% for employees hired under automatic enrollment vs 37% for a comparable cohort hired before it. The cause is inertia; the plan's economics were unchanged.
- BEA Personal Income and Outlays, August 2026 (released Sept 30, 2026): personal saving rate 4.1% of disposable personal income; personal saving $990.2B (annual rate).
- Pay yourself first: move savings out automatically on payday, before spending. Methods: split direct deposit, scheduled transfers, automatic 401(k) contributions, automatic increases.
- Worked example: $100 every two weeks x 26 paydays = $2,600 a year.
- Surprising fact: changing the default (opt-out vs opt-in) more than doubled participation, 37% to 86%.

SOURCES:
- Madrian & Shea (QJE 2001; NBER Working Paper 7682)
- BEA, Personal Income and Outlays, August 2026 (Sept 30, 2026)
