## Issues

1. **Line 8: says the leftover is profit (logic error, must fix).** "A price tag isn't profit. What's left is." This calls the $44,400 profit. That is wrong in both directions. The leftover still ignores taxes, insurance and repairs (line 6), and inflation (line 7). It also ignores the rent the owner didn't pay, which offsets much of the interest. Interest is a cost of borrowing, so subtracting it is fair only if you call the result "gain left after costs to own and sell", not profit. The $53K of principal repaid is correctly left out, because that is the owner's own money (equity).
   - text: "Owning does skip rent. But a higher price isn't all profit."
   - say: "Owning does skip rent. But a higher price isn't all profit."
   - scene lines: ["Owners skip rent", "Not all profit"]

2. **Line 7: "inflation hit 34%" is misleading.** It sounds like a 34% annual inflation rate. It is the total rise from 2016 to 2025. It also repeats "And" from line 6.
   - text: "Overall prices rose 34% from 2016 to 2025: 2016's $300,000 is about $402,000 in 2025 dollars."
   - say: "Overall prices rose thirty-four percent, so twenty sixteen's three hundred thousand is about four hundred two thousand in twenty twenty-five dollars."
   - Scene values unchanged ($300,000 / $402,000 / "2025 dollars").

3. **Years don't line up (minor, disclose rather than rework).** The home is valued in 2026, interest runs 10 years from 2016 to 2026, but the index and CPI stop at 2025, the latest full years. That is acceptable, but:
   - The example's +50% ($300K to $450K) is well below the index's +82% (the index would put it near $545K). That makes the example lean toward "not profit". It is fine as a stated hypothetical ("Say..."), but don't present it as typical.
   - $402,000 is in 2025 dollars, and 2026 inflation would add a few thousand more. Keep the "2025 dollars" label (already on screen). Don't subtract inflation from the $44,400. Inflation also shrinks the real cost of later interest payments, so stacking it would double-count.
   - The example implies a 20% down payment ($60K), which isn't stated. That's optional to show; no fix required.

4. **Line 5: wording and "Our math." tacked on.** "That leaves about $44,400. Our math." Read aloud, "Our math." is a fragment and sounds awkward. "Leaves" also needs an object: it's what's left of the gain, not cash in hand. The seller's actual cash at sale is about $236K.
   - text: "That leaves about $44,400 of the gain (our math)."
   - say: "That leaves about forty-four thousand four hundred of the gain."
   - board lines unchanged: ["Gain: $150,000", "Left: $44,400"]

5. **Line 0: hook can be stronger and more exact.** "That's not your profit" is too absolute, since part of it can be profit. The spoken line is 12 words, about 3 s, which is borderline. A specific stake makes the viewer need the answer.
   - text: "Home up $150K? Costs can eat most of it."

   - scene title: "Up $150K? Not all [profit]"; label and value unchanged.

6. **Line 4: spoken words differ from the screen.** The screen says "2016's 3.65% average ... (our math)", but the spoken line never says the rate.
   - text: "Interest on a $240,000 loan at 2016's 3.65% average: about $78,600 in 10 years (our math)."
   - say: "Interest on a two hundred forty thousand dollar loan at three point six five percent? About seventy-eight thousand six hundred in ten years."

7. **Line 3: awkward possessive.** "$27,000's gone" / "twenty-seven thousand's gone".
   - text: "Selling often costs 6% to 10%. At 6%, that's $27,000."
