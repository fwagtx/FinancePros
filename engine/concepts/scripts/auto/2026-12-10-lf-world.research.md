## Birth of the peg (1983)
- Hong Kong dollar floated before 1983. Amid confidence worries in 1983, it hit a record low of HK$9.60 per US$1 on Saturday, Sept 24, 1983 ("Black Saturday"); about 15% depreciation over two days of trading. Sources: HKMA reference material "1-1" (record low HK$9.60, Sept 1983); HKMA Insight Nov 4, 1999; Wikipedia "Black Saturday (1983)".
- Oct 17, 1983: the Linked Exchange Rate System started: a currency board, HK$7.80 = US$1. Source: HKMA "Milestones of Monetary Reform"; HK Yearbook.
- By Oct 17, 2026, the peg turned 43 years old (1983 -> 2026). At posting (Dec 10, 2026) it is 43 years old.
- Math: 9.60 -> 7.80 means each US dollar bought HK$1.80 less; HK dollar 9.60/7.80 = 1.2308 → about 23% stronger than the low.

## How the machine works
- Currency board: monetary base must be fully (100%+) backed by US dollar reserves; changes in the monetary base matched by changes in US dollar reserves at a fixed rate. Source: HK Yearbook 2004, HKMA.
- Notes: issued by three commercial banks: HSBC, Standard Chartered Bank (Hong Kong), Bank of China (Hong Kong). The government issues the $10 note (paper since 2002, polymer since 2007). Note-issuing banks must hold Certificates of Indebtedness issued by the Exchange Fund; notes are issued/redeemed against US dollars paid to/from the Exchange Fund at US$1 = HK$7.80. Source: HKMA "History of note-issuing banks"; HKMA notes page.
- May 18, 2005 three refinements: (a) strong-side Convertibility Undertaking (CU): HKMA buys US dollars from licensed banks at 7.75; (b) weak-side CU moved from 7.80 to 7.85 (phased, 7.81 on May 23, 2005 to 7.85 on June 20, 2005); (c) Convertibility Zone between them where HKMA may do market operations. Band centered on 7.80. Source: HKMA press release May 18, 2005.
- Weak side 7.85: HKMA sells US$ / buys HK$ → Aggregate Balance shrinks by same amount. Strong side 7.75: HKMA sells HK$ / buys US$ → Aggregate Balance grows. Source: LegCo paper FA 2018-05-15; HKMA 2020 release.
- Aggregate Balance = total clearing account balances commercial banks keep with the HKMA (interbank liquidity). Source: J.P. Morgan AM (May 24, 2023); HKMA.
- Self-correcting: smaller Aggregate Balance → HIBOR rises toward US rates → carry trade incentive shrinks → HK$ firms. (HKMA; Eddie Yue, July 2025.)
- Currency Board Account end-July 2026: Monetary Base HK$2,080.1B; Backing Assets HK$2,326.8B; Backing Ratio 111.86% (111.84% end-June 2026). Source: HKMA press release Aug 31, 2026.

## 1997-98 Asian financial crisis
- Oct 23, 1997: interbank liquidity squeeze; overnight HIBOR briefly reached 280% (HKMA Annual Report 1997; LegCo minutes cite 300% intraday). Closed near 100%, about 5% the next day.
- Aug 1998: speculators' "double play": short HK stocks and futures while selling HK$ to push up interest rates (BIS paper; HKMA 1998 Annual Report).
- Aug 14-28, 1998: government used HK$118 billion (about 18% of Exchange Fund assets at the time) to buy 33 Hang Seng Index stocks over 10 trading days. Hang Seng closed at 7,830 on Aug 28, 1998, 18% above level where intervention began on Aug 14. Source: HKMA Insight Sept 18, 2019; Goodhart & Lu Dai (Central Banking review). HK$118B ≈ US$15.1B (Webb-site).
- Shares later sold via Tracker Fund; HKMA said in 2000 it had recouped HK$72.5B of the HK$118B with about HK$175B still held at market value (HKMA, Aug 2000). Controversial at the time (critics vs. HKMA).
- Sept 5, 1998: HKMA's seven technical measures incl. a clear convertibility undertaking for banks' clearing balances at 7.75 (then moved toward 7.80 from April 1999 at one pip a day). Sources: LegCo, BIS, HKMA milestones.

## 2020s pressure
- 2020: strong-side CU triggered starting April 21, 2020 (first since Oct 2015); HKMA sold HK$383.5B in 2020 in total (HKMA Annual Report 2020).
- 2022-2023 (Fed hiking): weak-side CU triggered 41 times May-Nov 2022 (HKMA Annual Report 2022); e.g. HK$104.28B bought Apr 13-Jun 23, 2022 period; HK$23.12B Oct 11-Dec 30, 2022 (Aggregate Balance ~HK$96B). 2023: triggered 8 times mid-Feb to early May 2023 (HKMA AR 2023). Aggregate Balance fell to HK$44.8B (May 2023), lowest since 2008 (J.P. Morgan AM, May 24, 2023). Full 2022 total not confirmed: script avoids a total.
- 2025 (both sides in one year):
  - Strong side: triggered four times in early May 2025 (first since Oct 2020; first on May 2, HK$46.539B); HKMA sold HK$129.4 billion total; Aggregate Balance rose from HK$44.7B (end-Feb 2025) to HK$174.1B (May 8, 2025). Source: HKMA Half-Yearly Monetary and Financial Stability Report Sept 2025; EFAC Currency Board Sub-Committee report.
  - Flood of liquidity → 1-month HIBOR fell below 1% in late May 2025 (UBS: 0.57% on May 26, 2025) while fed funds 4.25-4.50% → carry trade.
  - Weak side: HK$ touched 7.85 on June 20, 2025 (first since 2023); weak-side CU triggered 12 times late June to mid-Aug 2025; HKMA bought HK$119.9 billion; Aggregate Balance back to about HK$54B. Source: HKMA March 2026 Half-Yearly report chapter 4; China Daily HK (July 2, 2025).
- 2026: Aggregate Balance HK$54.0B at end-Feb 2026, CUs not triggered in that review period (HKMA Mar 2026). HK$ traded 7.7818-7.8387 through Apr 22, 2026 (EFAC). Mid-2026: HK$ near weak side (The Standard / Bloomberg reports; Aggregate Balance ~HK$49B mid-July 2026: single outlet, NOT used). No confirmed 2026 trigger found → not claimed.

## Worked example (hypothetical, exact math)
- $1,000 converted at the 7.80 linked rate = HK$7,800. At the strong edge 7.75 = HK$7,750. At the weak edge 7.85 = HK$7,850. Edge-to-edge difference HK$100 = 100/7,750 = 1.29% (about 1.3%).
- A HK$780 hotel night: 780/7.80 = $100.00; 780/7.75 = $100.645 → $100.65; 780/7.85 = $99.363 → $99.36. Worst-to-best swing $1.29.
- Ignores bank/card FX fees (stated assumption).

## Surprising fact
- Most Hong Kong banknotes aren't printed by the government: three commercial banks issue them, and they must hand over US dollars at 7.80 for every HK$7.80 of notes.
- Hong Kong's interest rates are effectively set by the Fed: the HKMA Base Rate is mechanically Fed lower bound + 0.5 points (Sept 2026: 3.75 + 0.50 = 4.25).

## How it reaches an American's wallet
- Travel/shopping: price in HK$ converts at 7.75-7.85; max ~1.3% swing, unlike floating currencies.
- Fed decisions also set borrowing costs in Hong Kong: a Fed hike in Sept 2026 → HK Base Rate up the next day.
- Hong Kong's reserves are held largely in US dollar assets (Backing Assets must be US dollar assets under the currency board) → a steady holder of US dollar assets.
