# Research brief: Scam alert, fake bank fraud department calls (slot 2026-10-15-hot)

Researched September 30 - October 1, 2026. Posting date October 15, 2026. Evergreen: no "this week".

## Timely angle
- FTC released 2025 Consumer Sentinel imposter data in June 2026: people reported losing $3.5 billion to imposter scams in 2025; imposter scams were the most-reported fraud category for the fifth straight year (nearly 1 in 3 fraud reports). [FTC press release June 2026; CNBC June 26, 2026]
- FCC issued a consumer alert on bank impersonation scams on July 21, 2026: bank impersonation accounts for the highest losses of any impersonation category; scammers use calls, texts and caller ID spoofing, and phony "fraud alerts". [FCC; ABA Banking Journal July 2026]
- September 2026: FTC sought public comment on updating its Impersonation Rule to address platforms' role in impersonation scam ads (neutral background only).

## Key numbers (FTC Consumer Sentinel, reported losses)
- Imposter scams 2025: $3.5 billion reported lost; about 1 million imposter reports; ~80% reported no loss. [FTC; CNBC]
- Business impersonators 2025: nearly $1 billion (up from $866 million in 2024); FTC says the highest reported losses within it were to BANK impersonators. Compliance Week headline: "Bank imposter fraud jumped in 2025". NOTE: a precise bank-only dollar figure was NOT confirmed (sources conflict on wording) - do not state one.
- Government impersonators 2025: about $920 million (up from $789 million in 2024).
- Total fraud losses reported 2025: $15.9 billion (some outlets round to $16B).

## How the scam works (FTC consumer alerts 2024; FCC alert July 2026)
- Caller says there is fraud on your account; offers to "protect" money; may ask for verification codes; always ends with moving money (to a "safe account", gold, or crypto). Some claim the bank is working with a government agency.
- FTC: "Banks will never call or text and ask you to move your money to protect it." (FTC consumer alert, "Never move your money to 'protect it.' That's a scam", March 2024 - exact wording per search snippets; use as paraphrase, not quote)
- FTC: if you move money out of your account in this scam, you won't be protected and probably won't get it back.
- FTC 2025 data: losses in these fake-security-alert scams are often limited only by the victim's available funds.
- Caller ID can be spoofed to show the bank's real name/number (FCC).
- What protects people: hang up and call back using the number on the back of the card or on the statement (FTC, FCC).

## Surprising fact
The real fraud department never asks you to move money to protect it; the request itself is the tell. And the caller ID showing your bank's name proves nothing (spoofing).

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
Example: someone has $5,000 in checking and $3,000 in savings. A "fraud department" caller says both are compromised and asks them to move it all to a "safe account".
$5,000 + $3,000 = $8,000 moved. Amount protected by the "safe account": $0. Loss = $8,000, i.e. 100% of available funds (FTC: losses often limited only by available funds).
