## Facts (Korea, October 2026; preliminary data from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources (MOTIR), released Nov 1, 2026)
- Total exports: $115.28B (reported as $115.3B), up 93.7% from Oct 2025. Record for any October; second only to September 2026's all-time monthly record ($120.9B).
- Semiconductor exports: $56.44B, up 259.4% (reported "259%"), about 49% of all exports.
- Imports: $72.78B (+36%); trade surplus $42.5B.
- Exports to the US: $24.9B, up 185.8% (AI-related goods: chips, computers, electrical equipment).
- Exports to China: $26.98B, up 133.6% (Global Times).
- Autos: $6.55B, +18.1% (record October).
- Calendar effect: the Chuseok holiday fell in October 2025 but in September 2026, so October 2026 had more working days. Average daily exports rose 76% to $5.24B.
- Ministry's reason: strong global chip demand plus big tech companies' continued investment in AI infrastructure.
- Jan-Oct 2026 cumulative exports: about $930B (Reuters via secondary; Jan-Sep was $814.5B + Oct $115.3B = $929.8B, our math). Ministry expects the $1 trillion annual mark as early as November. Korea would be the 4th country ever to export $1T in a year, after the US, China and Germany (SBS, Seoul Economic Daily).

## Context
Korea is the world's big memory-chip maker (Samsung, SK Hynix). AI data centers need huge amounts of memory, so Korean chip exports are a real-time read on AI spending by big US tech firms. Monthly exports were ~$55-60B for years; 2026 broke $80B (March), $100B (June), $120B (September).

## What it means for a normal person
- The AI build-out is showing up as real money in trade data, not just stock prices. The big US tech firms doing the spending are large parts of common index funds (education, not advice).
- Lesson in reading headlines: part of the 93.7% jump is the calendar (more workdays); per-day growth was 76%.
