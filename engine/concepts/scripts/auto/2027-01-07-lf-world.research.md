# Research brief: India's 1991 crisis: the near-default that opened an economy
World Money, long-form. Posts Thu Jan 7, 2027. Evergreen; every number dated. Researched Nov 8, 2026.

No breaking news hook needed; evergreen history story. Recent context: India's forex reserves were about $717.06B in the week ended Oct 30, 2026 (RBI weekly data).

## The surprising fact
In July 1991, India's central bank flew 46.91 tonnes of its gold to the Bank of England as collateral to borrow about $405 million, because the country was weeks away from being unable to pay for imports. (Manmohan Singh statement to Lok Sabha, July 18, 1991; RBI history via press.)

## 1. Running on empty (1990-91)
- Iraq invaded Kuwait in August 1990. Oil prices roughly doubled within months in 1990 (Brent about $17 in July 1990 to a peak above $40 in October 1990; EIA history).
- India's petroleum import bill jumped by more than half in 1990-91 (Oxford GEG working paper, Ghosh 2004: about $5.7B, up over 50%; Scroll: $6B vs $3.8B).
- Remittances from Indian workers in Kuwait and Iraq stopped. India airlifted more than 170,000 citizens from Kuwait, Aug 13 - Oct 20, 1990, in 488 flights over 59 days (Guinness record for Air India; The Week, 2023; Onmanorama 2020).
- Current account deficit about 3% of GDP in 1990-91 (Scroll; indiabefore91).
- Forex reserves: about $1.2 billion in January 1991; by June 1991 enough for only about two weeks of imports (Springer chapter; 1991 Project/Mercatus; many outlets say "two weeks", some "two to three weeks"). Singh later spoke of about 15 days of reserves (Bloomberg, July 26, 2011).
- Political context (neutral): Rajiv Gandhi assassinated May 21, 1991 during elections; P.V. Narasimha Rao sworn in as PM June 21, 1991; Manmohan Singh named finance minister.

## 2. Gold on a plane
- May 16, 1991: the government leased 20 tonnes of confiscated gold to State Bank of India, which sold it to Union Bank of Switzerland with an option to buy it back; about $200 million raised (Rajya Sabha reply, Feb 28, 1994; press).
- July 4-18, 1991: RBI pledged 46.91 tonnes of gold to the Bank of England (and Bank of Japan), shipped in four tranches, raising about $405 million (Singh statement to Lok Sabha, July 18, 1991: total 46.91 tonnes, first shipment July 4; RBI history quoted by Business Today, Aug 16, 2026). Total pledged about 67 tonnes.
- A van carrying the gold to the airport broke down in Mumbai (Business Standard, June 21, 2021) - colorful detail.

## 3. The rupee drops
- Devaluation in two steps: July 1 and July 3, 1991. Combined: about 18.7% against the US dollar, 17.38% against the pound (RBI History, Vol. 4). Steps roughly 9% then 11% (press).
- Worked example (our math, hypothetical import): if the rupee loses 18.7% of its dollar value, a dollar now costs 1 / (1 - 0.187) = 1 / 0.813 = 1.23 times as many rupees, so a dollar-priced import costs about 23% more in rupees. Example: an import costing 1,000 rupees before costs 1,000 x 1.23 = 1,230 rupees after.
- Gold loan math (our math): $405M / 46.91 tonnes = about $8.6M per tonne.

## 4. The big rewrite (July 24, 1991)
- July 24, 1991: Industrial Policy statement tabled; same day Manmohan Singh's 1991-92 budget speech.
- Industrial licensing ("License Raj": needing government permits to open or expand a factory) abolished for all but 18 industries (mostly defense, hazardous, strategic) (Industrial Policy 1991; multiple histories).
- Automatic approval for foreign direct investment up to 51% in high-priority industries (Industrial Policy 1991).
- Peak import tariff cut from more than 300% to 150% in the 1991-92 budget (multiple histories; Business Standard 2011: "150 per cent in 1991-92").
- Quote, closing the budget speech July 24, 1991, Singh quoting Victor Hugo: "No power on earth can stop an idea whose time has come." Followed by: "I suggest to this august House that the emergence of India as a major economic power in the world happens to be one such idea." (Business Standard, July 24, 2011; Gulf News; wording variants "whole world"). Note: the Hugo attribution itself is a paraphrase (Quote Investigator, 2023).
- IMF: Stand-by arrangement of SDR 1,656 million, approved Oct 31, 1991, 20 months (Ministry of Finance reply to Parliament; IMF archives EBM/91/145). Earlier 1991 IMF drawings also happened (Jan 1991, ~$1.8B incl. compensatory facility; less certain). Conditions in Letter of Intent dated Aug 27, 1991.

## 6. Your wallet
- Generic drugs: Indian companies supplied 47% of all US generic prescriptions in 2022 (IQVIA Institute, "U.S.-India Medicine Partnership", April 2024; funded by Indian Pharmaceutical Alliance). About 4 in 10 of all US prescriptions filled in 2022. Estimated $219B savings to US health system in 2022 (same report).
- IT services: TCS got 48.5% of revenue from North America in Q4 FY2026 (Jan-Mar 2026, TCS release); FY2026 revenue $30.0B. Infosys: about 55.7% from North America in Q4 FY2026; FY2026 revenue $20.16B.
- Trade: 2025 US-India goods trade about $149.1B; US imports from India $103.8B (up 18.9%), exports $45.4B; goods deficit about $58.4B (USTR); Census: $58.2B.
- H-1B: 71% of approved H-1B petitions in FY2024 were for people born in India (283,397 of 399,395; USCIS report to Congress). China second, 11.7%.
- Neutral framing: no position on trade or immigration policy.
