# Research brief: How prepaid debit cards make money (posts Sun Dec 27, 2026, 2 PM CT)

Evergreen angle (no "this week" news hook). Example company: Green Dot Corporation (NYSE: GDOT), one of the largest US prepaid card issuers (Green Dot Bank). Neutral, education only.

## Core facts (with dates and sources)

### Green Dot FY2025 revenue mix (Form 10-K for year ended Dec 31, 2025)
- Total operating revenues: $2,080.5 million (FY2025) vs $1,723.9 million (FY2024).
- Card revenues and other fees: ~$1,565.9 million = ~75.3% of total. 10-K says the increase came mainly from higher program management fees from B2B Services partners (companies that run accounts/cards on Green Dot's bank and platform), partly offset by LOWER accountholder fees (the fees charged to consumers).
- Cash processing revenues: ~$240.2 million (11.5%) vs $231.8M in 2024 (fees for processing cash, e.g., cash deposits/reloads at retail; formerly labeled "processing and settlement service revenues"). Description moderately sourced.
- Interchange revenues: ~$184.6 million (8.9%) vs $198.3M in 2024.
- Interest income, net: ~$89.8 million (vs ~$62M in 2024) = "float": interest earned on customer balances/deposits.
- GAAP net loss FY2025 ~$98.9M (not used in script).
Source: Green Dot FY2025 Form 10-K (sec.gov, gdot-20251231.htm), via search excerpts; Q4/FY2025 earnings release.

### Consumer fee menu (Green Dot cardholder agreements on greendot.com; vary by card version)
- Monthly fee: $7.95, waived if $1,000+ loaded in the prior monthly period (Green Dot Prepaid Visa/Mastercard and OneMain agreements; some versions $5.95 or $500 waiver).
- Cash reload: up to $5.95 per load at retail reload agents ("can be lower depending on how and where you reload").
- Out-of-network ATM withdrawal: $2.50 in several versions, $3.00 in one (ATM operator may add its own fee). Weakly consistent - not used as a number in script.
- Card purchase fee: e.g., $3.95 on a Load & Go agreement (may be lower by retailer). Not used as a number.
- Netspend (competitor): cash reload up to $3.95 at its reload network; Netspend discontinued monthly fee plans effective May 18, 2024 (moved to pay-as-you-go). Not used in script.
- Inactivity fees exist on some prepaid products (not verified for a named current card; not used).

### Worked example (labeled EXAMPLE; Green Dot card with $7.95 monthly fee, user loads under $1,000/month)
- Monthly fee $7.95 + 2 cash reloads x $5.95 (maximum reload fee) = $7.95 + $11.90 = $19.85 per month.
- Over 12 months: $19.85 x 12 = $238.20 (not used in script).
- Assumptions: fee not waived; both reloads at retailers charging the full $5.95; no ATM use.

### Interchange (swipe fees)
- Merchants pay interchange on every card purchase; the issuer (here Green Dot Bank) earns it.
- Durbin amendment / Fed Regulation II (since Oct 1, 2011): debit interchange for issuers with $10B+ in assets capped at 21 cents + 0.05% of the transaction + 1-cent fraud-prevention adjustment. Fed proposed (Nov 2023) lowering to 14.4 cents + 0.04% + 1.3 cents; final status/litigation as of late 2026 unclear in search results -> NOT used in script.
- Reg II 12 CFR 235.5(c): certain general-use reloadable prepaid cards (not marketed as gift cards, sole means of access to funds) are exempt from the cap, BUT 235.5(d): the exemption is lost if the card may charge an overdraft fee or a fee for the first ATM withdrawal per month in the issuer's designated network. Small issuers (<$10B assets) are exempt regardless. Not used in script (too nuanced to state per-card).

### CFPB Prepaid Rule
- Prepaid Accounts Rule (amends Reg E and Reg Z) effective April 1, 2019 (after delays from 2017 and 2018). Requires pre-acquisition short-form disclosure (table of key fees, incl. the most common fees) and long-form fee list; error resolution and limited liability protections for prepaid accounts once registered/identity verified.
- PayPal challenge: district court vacated short-form requirement (Dec 2020); D.C. Circuit reversed on Feb 3, 2023 (Rule does not impose mandatory model clauses). A later 2024 district ruling vacated the short-form disclosure as applied to digital wallets only. Prepaid CARDS still covered.
Sources: CFPB effective-date document; Goodwin, Venable, Consumer Finance Monitor.

### Who uses prepaid cards (FDIC National Survey of Unbanked and Underbanked Households)
- 2023: 5.9% of all US households used prepaid cards (at survey time), down from 6.9% in 2021. Use fell about one-third among unbanked households vs one-tenth among banked households (2021-2023).
- 96% of US households were banked in 2023 (FDIC press release, Nov 2024).
- Earlier GPR prepaid use: 10.2% (2015), 9.7% (2017), 8.5% (2019) (2019 report; definition differs slightly).

## Surprising fact
At Green Dot, swipe fees (interchange) fell from $380.0M in 2021 to $184.6M in 2025 (from 26.6% to 8.9% of revenue), and FY2025 growth came from B2B partner program fees while consumer accountholder fees fell.
