# Research brief: Why rent is so much higher in some cities (posts Dec 15, 2026)

Researched Oct 16, 2026. Direct page fetches were blocked in this environment; figures below were confirmed through search results quoting the sources. Each is dated.

## Core idea
Rent is a price set by supply and demand. Demand: jobs and wages pulling people (and their paychecks) into a metro. Supply: how many homes get built, which depends on land cost and availability, zoning rules (what can be built where: e.g. single-family-only lots, height limits, density caps) and permit approvals. Where high-paying jobs grow faster than housing, rents rise; where building keeps pace or outpaces demand, rents flatten or fall. (Policy described neutrally; no side taken.)

## Key numbers (dated)
- San Francisco median 1-bedroom asking rent: $4,400, an all-time high, up 25.4% year over year (Zumper National Rent Report, September 2026, published Sept 28, 2026; also reported by KPIX/KION, Sept 29, 2026). SF 2-bedroom median $6,340, highest in the US (same report).
- Zumper attributes SF's increase to AI-sector hiring adding demand while new supply stays scarce (KPIX/KION coverage of Zumper, Sept 2026).
- New York City 1-bedroom median: $4,580, #1 in the US (Zumper, Sept 2026).
- National median 1-bedroom (Zumper, Sept 2026): $1,518.
- Houston median 1-bedroom: $1,050, down 14.6% year over year; 2-bedroom $1,400, down 5.4% (Zumper, August 2026, via CultureMap Houston). Austin, Houston and Dallas had some of the steepest 1BR declines nationally.
- Ratio: $4,400 / $1,050 = 4.19, "about 4x" (note: SF figure is Sept 2026, Houston is Aug 2026; both Zumper medians).
- Houston metro permitted 65,075 housing units in 2025; Dallas-Fort Worth 66,179 (Census Building Permits Survey, via BusinessStats compilation; separate reporting also says Houston and DFW each topped 65,000 units in 2025).
- US total: 1,431,616 units authorized by permits in 2025, down 3.1% from 2024 (Census annual highlights).
- Bay Area (all 9 counties) issued permits for about 9,100 new homes in 2024, one of the lowest in 15 years (MTC Vital Signs, CIRB data).
- HUD FY2026 Fair Market Rent (Oct 1, 2025 - Sept 30, 2026), San Francisco HMFA 2-bedroom: $3,604 (HUD FY2026 FMR schedule). FY2027 FMRs took effect Oct 1, 2026 (not used).

## Worked example
$4,400 (SF 1BR, Sept 2026) / $1,050 (Houston 1BR, Aug 2026) = 4.19, about 4 times. Difference: $4,400 - $1,050 = $3,350 a month; x 12 = $40,200 a year.
Permits drop: 2,519 (2021) to 770 (2024) = -69%.

## Surprising fact
The country's priciest rent market and one of its fastest-rising are the same place: SF 1BR rents rose 25.4% in a year (Sept 2026) while Houston's fell 14.6% (Aug 2026), in the city that permitted among the most homes in the US in 2025.
