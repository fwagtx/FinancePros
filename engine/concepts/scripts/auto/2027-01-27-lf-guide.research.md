## Surprising fact
- FTC study (Report to Congress under FACTA Section 319, dated Dec 2012, press release Feb 11, 2013): 1,001 participants, 2,968 reports; 1 in 5 consumers had an error on at least one of their three reports that was corrected after a dispute; 5% had errors that could lead to paying more for loans/insurance; about 1 in 10 saw a score change after fixes; about 1 in 20 saw a max score change of more than 25 points. (Sources: FTC press release Feb 2013; Washington Times, CBS, Fox Business Feb 2013.) [Date note: study dated Dec 2012, released Feb 2013; script says "published in 2013".]

## Step 1: Get the reports
- AnnualCreditReport.com is the only authorized site for free reports under federal law; phone 1-877-322-8228.
- Free weekly reports from Equifax, Experian, TransUnion; the bureaus made weekly access permanent in Sept 2023 (FTC consumer alert, Oct 13, 2023).
- Reports do not include credit scores. Checking your own report does not affect your score (FTC).
- Each bureau's report can differ (not every lender reports to all three) -> check all three.

## Step 2: What to check, line by line (FTC "Disputing Errors on Your Credit Reports"; CFPB "common errors" blog)
- Personal info: name, address, SSN digits, employer; mixed files (someone else's info).
- Accounts: accounts you don't recognize (possible identity theft), status (open/closed), balances, credit limits, payment history (late marks you didn't earn), duplicate accounts/debts listed twice, closed accounts reported as open.
- Collections and public records (bankruptcy).
- Inquiries: hard inquiries you didn't authorize. Hard inquiries can stay up to 2 years (Experian) [CHECK: industry source].
- Why balances/limits matter: FICO weights (myFICO): payment history 35%, amounts owed 30%, length of history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%.

## Worked example (our math)
- Two cards: Card A limit $3,000, real balance $600. Card B limit $2,000, real balance $400. Total limit $5,000.
- Correct utilization: ($600 + $400) / $5,000 = $1,000 / $5,000 = 20%.
- Error: Card B reported at an old balance of $1,400 (you paid it down to $400). Reported total: $600 + $1,400 = $2,000. $2,000 / $5,000 = 40%.
- The error doubles reported utilization from 20% to 40%. Amounts owed is about 30% of a FICO score.

## Step 3: Dispute (FCRA, 15 U.S.C. 1681i; CFPB Ask CFPB; FTC)
- Disputes are free. Dispute online, by mail, or by phone with each bureau showing the error. FTC: also dispute directly with the furnisher (the lender/collector that supplied the info), with the same documents.
- Letter should include: full name and address, each item you dispute and why, copies (not originals) of supporting documents (statements, payment confirmations), ask for correction or deletion. Keep copies; mail certified with return receipt (FTC suggestion).
- Timing: bureau generally must investigate within 30 days of receiving the dispute; can extend by 15 days (to 45) if you send more info during the window; 45 days for disputes filed after receiving the free annual report (CFPB). Bureau must forward the dispute to the furnisher (within 5 business days, statute). Results to you within 5 business days of completing the investigation, plus a free copy of the report if it changed.
- If information is inaccurate or can't be verified, it must be deleted or corrected (FTC).
- Furnisher must also investigate disputes sent directly to it (Regulation V, 12 CFR 1022.43) generally on the same 30-day timeline [CHECK].

## Step 4: What can't be removed (true negatives) - timelines (CFPB; Experian; Nolo)
- Most negative info (late payments, collections, charge-offs): generally up to 7 years.
- Chapter 7 bankruptcy: up to 10 years from filing. Chapter 13: bureaus generally remove after 7 years.
- Positive info can stay longer.
- Accurate, timely information can't legally be forced off; credit repair firms can't remove accurate items. Credit Repair Organizations Act: it's illegal for credit repair companies to charge before performing services (FTC).

## Step 5: Protect the file
- Credit freeze: free at all three bureaus since Sept 21, 2018 (Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act; FTC "Free credit freezes are here"). Contact each bureau separately. Doesn't affect score. Online/phone: placed within 1 business day; lift within 1 hour (USA.gov).
- Initial fraud alert: free, lasts 1 year, renewable; contact one bureau and it tells the other two. Extended fraud alert: 7 years, for identity theft victims with an Identity Theft Report (IdentityTheft.gov) (FTC).

## Step 6: If the dispute fails
- Ask the bureau to include a statement of dispute in your file; FCRA 1681i(b): the agency "may limit such statements to not more than one hundred words if it provides the consumer with assistance in writing a clear summary of the dispute." Must note the dispute in future reports (1681i(c)).
- Ask that the statement/correction be sent to anyone who pulled your report recently.
- Re-dispute with new evidence; dispute with furnisher; file a complaint with the CFPB (consumerfinance.gov/complaint) or state attorney general; right to sue under FCRA.

## Common mistakes
- Checking only one bureau. Using look-alike "free report" sites. Disputing accurate items hoping they vanish. Sending originals. Not keeping copies or dates. Skipping the furnisher. Paying a credit repair firm upfront. Forgetting to lift a freeze before applying.
