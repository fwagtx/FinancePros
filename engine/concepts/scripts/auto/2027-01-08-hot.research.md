# Research brief: Why is beef so expensive? (2027-01-08-hot)

Researched November 9, 2026. Direct fetches of usda.gov, bls.gov, fred.stlouisfed.org and news sites were blocked by the network proxy. Every figure below was confirmed through search results quoting the official source, and checked against a second source where possible. Angle: this is about CATTLE SUPPLY (herd size, biology, imports), not general grocery inflation (covered Nov 8).

## Timely angle
- No single news event is tied to the Jan 8, 2027 posting date. USDA's next January 1 Cattle report comes out in late January 2027, after the post. The evergreen hook is the price jump plus the herd low.
- August 2026: BLS said ground beef (all uncooked ground beef series) averaged $7.158/lb, a record, up 7.9% from a year earlier (Farm Progress; UkrAgroConsult, Sept 2026). NOTE: that is a DIFFERENT series from the 100% ground beef series charted below. Do not mix them.
- September 2026, all uncooked ground beef: $7.062/lb, down 1.3% from August (secondary report quoting BLS). Not used.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. US herd size. On Jan 1, 2026, USDA NASS counted all cattle and calves at 86.2 million head, down about 0.3-0.4% from 86.5-86.7 million a year earlier. That is the lowest since 1951, a 75-year low (Farm Progress "U.S. cattle herd stays at 75-year low"; Univ. of Kentucky gives 86.16M; farmdoc daily). Jan 1, 2025: 86.66 million (86.7M), the lowest since 1951 at that point (farmdoc daily; Pro Farmer; Bloomberg "74-year low", Jan 31, 2025).
   - Beef cows on Jan 1, 2026: 27.6 million, down 1%, the lowest since 1961.
   - For contrast, Jan 1, 2019: 94.8 million head (USDA NASS Feb 2019 report). The last cyclical peak was 96.6 million in 2017.
2. Drought liquidation. In 2022, drought dried up pastures and hay, and ranchers sold cows they could not feed. USDA reported more beef cows slaughtered in July 2022 than in any month since record-keeping began in 1986 (NPR, Sept 1, 2022, "Droughts are hitting cattle ranchers hard - and that could make beef more expensive"; Texas Farm Bureau). High feed, fuel and fertilizer costs also played a part. Analysts describe five or six straight years of herd contraction through 2025-26.
3. Rebuilding takes years (biology). Cattle gestation is about 9 months (about 283 days; extension sources). Heifers are bred at about 13-15 months and first calve at about 2 years old (Univ. of Wisconsin extension: bred by ~13 months to calve at ~22-23 months). UF/IFAS extension (Oct 2025 Florida Cattle Market Update): "A heifer retained in the fall of 2025 and bred in 2026 will calve in 2027 with that calf entering beef production in 2028-2029." Keeping a heifer to breed also means she is NOT sold for beef now, which tightens supply in the short run. As of 2026, analysts (CoBank, Oklahoma State's Derrell Peel, WLJ) say the rebuild has barely started.
4. Imports: Mexico. USDA halted live cattle imports from Mexico over the New World screwworm (a flesh-eating parasite) in May 2025 (after a brief late-2024 suspension). A planned 2025 phased reopening was reversed and the border stayed mostly shut for about a year. Imports resumed at Douglas, Arizona on August 24, 2026, with every animal screened; Santa Teresa, NM followed Sept 24, 2026; other ports were planned later in 2026 (AZFamily/KOLD Aug 24, 2026; Boston Globe/Reuters July 25, 2026; Drovers Oct 2026). Through Oct 16, 2026, about 39,600 head had crossed (USDA AMS data via news). Neutral wording: a pause for animal-health reasons.
5. Imports: Brazil. A 50% US tariff on Brazilian goods (10% baseline plus an extra 40%) took effect Aug 6, 2025. An executive order signed Nov 20, 2025 removed the extra 40% on Brazilian beef, coffee and other farm goods, retroactive to Nov 13, 2025 (Beef Central; WLJ; Supply Chain Dive; Fortune). Brazilian beef still pays a 26.4% over-quota duty once the "other countries" quota fills (Beef Central). In Sept 2026, the administration said it would allow up to 300,000 tons of ground beef imports at lower tariff rates (UkrAgroConsult, Sept 2026; not used).
6. Packers (contested, NOT used in the script). ERS chart: the top-four packers' share of fed-cattle slaughter rose from 36% in 1980 to 85% by 2012. A Meat Institute fact sheet (Oct 2025) citing USDA Packers and Stockyards says about 81% of fed cattle. DOJ/USDA have an ongoing antitrust investigation (2025-26). The figure depends on the year and measure, so it is left out.
7. Demand. USDA all-fresh retail beef demand index for Q2 2025 was the highest in at least 25 years (Farms.com, Aug 2025); the LMIC demand index was 147 in Q1 2026 (RFD-TV). Shoppers kept buying despite record prices. (Trade press only; used only as "strong demand" with no number.)

- These came from an earlier brief (2026-10-05-business), which was fact-checked, and they agree with today's searches. FRED snapshot: Aug 2025 6.318. FRED: Jun 2026 6.825, Jul 2026 6.885; a 0.55% rise from Jul to Aug gives 6.923, which matches the ~0.6% rise BLS reported for August. Other points: June 2025 $6.12, the first month above $6 since the series began in the 1980s (news reports).
- Change: Aug 2022 to Aug 2026 = 6.923 / 4.937 = 1.4023, so +40.2% (about 40%). Aug 2025 to Aug 2026: 6.923 / 6.318 = 1.0958, so +9.6%.

## Worked example (our math)
- A household that buys 4 lb of 100% ground beef a month buys 48 lb a year.
- At Aug 2022 prices: 48 x $4.937 = $236.976, about $237 a year.
- At Aug 2026 prices: 48 x $6.923 = $332.304, about $332 a year.
- Difference: $332.304 - $236.976 = $95.328, about $95 more a year.

## Surprising fact
- The US had fewer cattle on Jan 1, 2026 (86.2 million) than in any year since 1951. And the fastest way for a rancher to rebuild (keep heifers to breed) means selling FEWER cattle at first, so supply gets tighter before it improves. A calf from a heifer kept in 2025 doesn't become beef until about 2028-2029.
