# Research brief: Combining finances with a partner: joint, separate or both (Explainer, posts Nov 19, 2026)

No timely news hook needed; evergreen. Latest data: Bankrate couples-finances survey (published February 2026) and Census Bureau SIPP story (September 2025).

## Key facts
- Bankrate couples finances survey (YouGov online panel, fieldwork Dec 2-8, 2025; 2,564 US adults, of whom 1,208 married/civil partnership/living with a partner; published Feb 2026):
  - 38% of couples completely combine their finances (all joint).
  - 36% have a mix of joint and separate accounts (hybrid).
  - 26% keep their financial accounts completely separate.
  - 62% keep at least some accounts in their name only.
  - Completely separate by generation: Gen Z (ages 18-29) 51%, millennials (30-45) 34%, Gen X (46-61) 23%, baby boomers (62-80) 15%.
  - By income: 39% of couples with household income under $50,000 completely separate vs 17% at $100,000+; 47% of $100,000+ couples use a hybrid.
- Prior Bankrate survey (CNBC, Jan 27, 2025): 38% exclusively joint, 34% combination, 27% completely separate; 62% keep at least some money separate.
- US Census Bureau, Survey of Income and Program Participation (SIPP), story "Almost a Quarter of Married Couples Didn't Have Joint Accounts in 2023, Up From 15% in 1996" (Sept 2025), universe: householder married couples who owned any assets at financial institutions:
  - No joint account at all: 15% (1996) -> 23% (2023).
  - At least one joint account: 85% (1996) -> 77% (2023).
  - All accounts joint: 53% (1996) -> 40% (2023).
  - Both joint and separate accounts: 9% (1996) -> 17% (2023).
  - Census context: median age at first marriage 24.8 (women) / 27.1 (men) in 1996 vs 28.4 / 30.2 in 2023; marrying later with established finances may explain the shift.
- FDIC (fdic.gov, Joint Accounts, 12 CFR 330.9): each co-owner of a joint account is insured up to $250,000 for their combined interests in all joint accounts at the same insured bank, so two co-owners can be covered up to $500,000. Joint accounts are a separate ownership category from single accounts. Co-owners are assumed equal owners unless bank records say otherwise.

## Chartable data series
- Census SIPP, married couples with NO joint account: 1996 15%, 2023 23% (only endpoints published in the story; the full table "Joint Bank Account Ownership Within Couples: 1996-2023" was not reachable). Use as a compare, not a line chart.
- Bankrate "completely separate" share: 27% (survey published Jan 2025), 26% (published Feb 2026).
- Bankrate Feb 2026 breakdown (categorical): all joint 38 / mix 36 / all separate 26.

## Worked example (hypothetical, proportional-to-income split)
- Partner A earns $6,000/month, Partner B $4,000/month. Total $10,000.
- Shares: A 6,000 / 10,000 = 60%; B 4,000 / 10,000 = 40%.
- Shared bills $3,000/month: A pays 0.60 x 3,000 = $1,800; B pays 0.40 x 3,000 = $1,200. Check: 1,800 + 1,200 = 3,000.
- Left over: A 6,000 - 1,800 = $4,200 (70% of pay); B 4,000 - 1,200 = $2,800 (70% of pay). Both keep the same 70%.
- Versus a 50/50 split ($1,500 each): A keeps $4,500 (75%), B keeps $2,500 (62.5%).

## Surprising fact
- More than half of Gen Z couples (51%) keep money completely separate (Bankrate, Feb 2026), and among married couples the share with no joint account rose from 15% in 1996 to 23% in 2023 (Census).
