# 2026-12-22-hot research brief: Money Myth, "You can't get a credit card with no credit history"

Researched Friday, October 23, 2026, for posting Tuesday, December 22, 2026 (evergreen; no news hook needed).

## The myth vs. the fact
- MYTH: No credit history means no credit card (the "need credit to get credit" trap).
- FACT: Several products are built for people with no file: secured credit cards, student cards, authorized-user status on someone else's card, and credit-builder loans. Issuers generally report secured-card activity to the credit bureaus, which is how it builds a file (Capital One, Chase, Bankrate education pages; CFPB consumer guidance as cited by them).

## How many people have no credit file (CFPB) - verify which estimate is current
- OLD (superseded): CFPB 2015 Data Point "Credit Invisibles": 26 million (11.0%, 25.9M) credit invisible + 19 million unscorable, December 2010 data. Widely quoted, but CFPB itself corrected it.
- CURRENT: CFPB "Technical Correction and Update to the CFPB's Credit Invisibles Estimate," June 2025:
  - December 2010 corrected: 5.8% of adults = 13.5 million credit invisible (was 11.0% / 25.9 million).
  - December 2020: 2.7% of adults = about 7.0 million credit invisible.
  - Unscored records: 12.7% in 2010 (revised up from 7.4%), 9.8% (about 25.4 million) in December 2020.
  - Scored records: 81.6% (191.3M) in 2010 -> 87.5% (225.3M) in 2020.
  - Reason for correction: records with only deferred student loans, collections or closed accounts were missing from the Consumer Credit Panel used in 2015.
  - Confirmed via CFPB report page/PDF titles in search results plus Orrick InfoBytes (June 27, 2025), PYMNTS / NASCUS (July 2025), The MortgagePoint (July 9, 2025), Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor (July 2025). No newer CFPB estimate found as of October 2026.
  - Note: the 25.4 million unscored count is from secondary summaries; the share 9.8% is consistent across sources. Not used on screen.

## How the paths work
- Secured card: you put down a refundable cash deposit; the deposit usually equals the credit limit (e.g. $200 deposit -> $200 limit). Some issuers allow a smaller deposit for a $200 line (Capital One: $49/$99/$200 deposit for at least a $200 line; SoFi). Deposit is usually refunded when the account is closed in good standing or upgraded (SoFi, Chase).
- Student cards: unsecured cards marketed to college students (issuer pages); still subject to under-21 rule below.
- Authorized user: added to someone else's card; not liable for the balance; benefit depends on the primary holder's habits and whether the issuer reports authorized users (LendingTree / Self / Capital One education pages).
- Credit-builder loan: lender holds the loan amount in a locked account while you make fixed monthly payments that are reported to bureaus; you get the money at the end. A CFPB 2020 study (2014-2015 enrollment data) found credit-builder loans raised the likelihood of having a score (secondary: Self blog). Not used on screen.
- Under-21 rule: CARD Act / Regulation Z 12 CFR 1026.51(b): applicants under 21 must show independent ability to pay (own income/assets) or have a cosigner/guarantor 21 or older (CFPB eCFR text, 2013 amendment kept this for under-21s).

## When a FICO Score can exist
- myFICO FAQ "What are the minimum requirements for a FICO Score?": at least one account opened six months or more, at least one account reported to the bureau within the past six months, and no "deceased" indication. One account can satisfy both. Minimums can vary slightly by FICO version.
- So a new card opened with zero history can typically produce a FICO Score after about six months.

## Utilization rule of thumb (attributed)
- Experian: no single point where utilization goes from good to bad, but 30% is where it starts to have a more pronounced negative effect. Chase calls staying below 30% a "general rule of thumb"; people with the highest scores tend to be under 10% (Chase; Experian data via Bankrate: 800-850 scores about 7%).

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- $200 deposit -> $200 limit.
- 30% x $200 = $60. Keeping the reported balance under $60 stays under 30% utilization.
- 10% x $200 = $20 (where top scorers tend to sit).

## The surprising fact
- CFPB's famous "26 million credit invisible" figure was cut roughly in half in June 2025 (to 13.5 million for 2010), and by December 2020 only 2.7% of adults (about 7 million) had no credit file at all.
