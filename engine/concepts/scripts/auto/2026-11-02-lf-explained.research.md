# Research brief: The U.S. national debt, explained (long-form, posts Nov 2, 2026)

Timely angle: gross national debt passed $40 trillion on Aug 18, 2026 (Treasury Debt to the Penny; CRFB, CNN, Washington Post, Aug 19, 2026).

## Size (Treasury FiscalData, Debt to the Penny)
- Sept 25, 2026: total public debt outstanding $40,097,178,119,751 (~$40.1T): debt held by the public $32,385,874,118,414 (~$32.4T); intragovernmental holdings $7,711,304,001,337 (~$7.7T).
- Milestones: $37T on Aug 11, 2025; $38T on Oct 21, 2025 (73 days later); $39T in March 2026; $40T on Aug 18, 2026 (less than five months after $39T). CRFB: roughly $1T every five months since Jan 2025.
- Debt first topped $1 trillion in 1981 (Treasury historical data).
- Per person: $40.097T / 341,784,857 (Census, July 1, 2025) = about $117,317.

## Deficit and interest (CBO Monthly Budget Review, FY2025 summary, Nov 2025)
- FY2025 (Oct 2024-Sep 2025): revenue $5.2T, outlays $7.0T, deficit $1.8T.
- Net interest $970B, more than national defense ($917B). Net interest is now the second-largest category after Social Security (behind it only), passing Medicare and defense.
- Interest per person: $970B / 341.8M = about $2,838.

## Rates (Treasury, Average Interest Rates on U.S. Treasury Securities)
- Average interest rate on total marketable debt: 1.42% in Jan 2022 (low); 3.48% in Aug 2026.
- Illustration: 1 percentage point on $32.4T held by the public = about $324B a year once the debt rolls over at the new rate. The 2.06-point rise (1.42% -> 3.48%) on $32.4T = about $667B a year (illustrative; actual interest also depends on timing and mix).

## Who holds it
- Intragovernmental (~$7.7T): mostly federal trust funds led by Social Security.
- Federal Reserve: about $4.4T of Treasuries (System Open Market Account, 2026).
- Foreign investors: about $9.35T (April 2026, Treasury TIC), roughly 30% of debt held by the public. Top: Japan $1.21T, United Kingdom $937.5B, China $651.1B (TIC; custodial attribution).

## Debt limit
- Statutory limit on total debt; Congress raised it by $5 trillion to $41.1 trillion in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025). CBO: that law's tax and spending provisions add about $3.4T to deficits over 10 years before extra interest.

## Projections (CBO, The Budget and Economic Outlook: 2026 to 2036, Feb 2026)
- Debt held by the public: about 101% of GDP at end of FY2026; 107.7% in FY2030, above the 106.1% record of 1946; 120% by 2036. (Projections, not predictions of prices.)

## Worked example: the household version (divide FY2025 flows by 100 million)
- Earns $52,000 (revenue $5.2T), spends $70,000 ($7.0T), borrows $18,000 ($1.8T), pays $9,700 of interest ($970B).
- Owes about $324,000 (debt held by the public $32.4T, Sept 2026) -- note: flows are FY2025, stock is Sept 2026.
- Limits of the analogy: a government issues its own currency, doesn't retire, and its income grows with the economy; that's why economists use debt as a share of GDP.
