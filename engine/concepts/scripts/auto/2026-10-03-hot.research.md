# Research brief: 2026-10-03-hot (Money Myth: you need a lot of money to start investing)

Format: Money Myth (myth, then fact). Evergreen; no "this week" wording. Education only; no product recommendations.

## Timely angle
- Gallup's April 2026 Economy and Personal Finance survey reportedly found 58% of US adults own stock, down from 62% in 2025 (reported by Stacker syndication, June 30, 2026, and The Motley Fool, Sept 23, 2026). Not used on screen because Gallup's own page could not be opened to confirm; script uses confirmed 2016-2025 figures.

## Confirmed facts
1. Fidelity "Stocks by the Slice": fractional shares of US stocks and ETFs for as little as $1 per order; more than 7,000 US stocks and ETFs; $0 commission online. (Fidelity, fidelity.com/trading/fractional-shares)
2. Schwab Stock Slices: any S&P 500 company for as little as $5 per slice; up to 30 companies per order; commission-free online. (Schwab press release 2020; aboutschwab.com/stock-slices)
3. Fractional shares receive dividends proportional to the fraction held. (Fidelity; Schwab)
4. TreasuryDirect: minimum purchase for any Treasury bill, note, bond, TIPS or FRN is $100, in $100 increments. Electronic EE and I savings bonds: minimum $25. (TreasuryDirect FAQs)
5. Gallup: 62% of Americans reported owning stock in 2025, same as 2024 (so 38% did not). Question counts individual stocks, mutual funds and retirement accounts like a 401(k) or IRA. Ownership only 28% in households earning under $50,000 vs 87% at $100,000+. (Gallup, May 2025)

## Worked example (exact math)
- Hypothetical share price $250. $10 / $250 = 0.04 share (four hundredths of a share). If that share paid a $1.00 dividend, the slice gets 0.04 x $1.00 = $0.04.
- $5 a week x 52 weeks = $260 a year (contributions only, no return assumed).

## The surprising fact
- The minimum at big US brokers is $1 (Fidelity) or $5 (Schwab), and the US Treasury sells I bonds from $25 - yet 38% of Americans owned no stock in 2025, and just 28% of households earning under $50,000 did.
