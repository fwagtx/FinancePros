# Research brief: How surgery centers (ASCs) make money
Slot 2026-12-14-business (How They Make Money, vertical short). Researched Oct 15, 2026. Evergreen; every number dated.
Avoid: Nov 12 "hospital-run practices lose ~$314K per doctor" angle; Dec 12 cancer drug prices angle.

## Definition
- Ambulatory surgery center (ASC): a facility for same-day (outpatient) surgery, with patients going home the same day; no inpatient beds, no emergency room. Common cases: cataract surgery, colonoscopy, orthopedic/knee scopes.
- For CY 2026, CMS added 560 procedures to the ASC covered procedures list (CY 2026 OPPS/ASC final rule, Nov 2025; via Surgery Business / Federal Register).

## Medicare pays ASCs less than hospital outpatient departments (CY 2026 final rule, released Nov 21, 2025)
- 2026 OPPS (hospital outpatient department) conversion factor: $91.415 (up 2.6% from $89.169 in 2025), before a 340B remedy offset that lowers it to $90.967. Source: ACR detailed summary of CY2026 HOPPS final rule; Team-IHA summary; Surgery Business.
- 2026 ASC conversion factor: $56.322 for ASCs meeting quality reporting (ASCRS; Surgery Business). Both systems got the same 2.6% update for 2026.
- The conversion factor is the dollar value of one "payment unit" (relative weight). On screen rounded: ASC $56.32 vs hospital $91.42.
  Caveat: ASC relative weights are also scaled, so the CF ratio is not exactly the payment ratio per procedure; label it "base rate per payment unit".
- Named procedure: cataract surgery (CPT 66984), 2026 Medicare ASC facility payment: $1,255.73 (vs $1,214.31 in 2025). Sources: ASCRS "2026 ASC Final Rule Released"; Ophthalmology Management (Mar 2026). Two outlets agree.
- WEAK: 2026 hospital outpatient payment for 66984 not found on an official source (CMS Addendum B not reachable). A consumer site (CoveredUSA) cites ~$2,370; Becker's older undated Medicare figure: $977 ASC vs $1,917 HOPD. Do NOT put an HOPD cataract dollar figure on screen.

## Tenet Healthcare FY2025 (Q4/FY2025 earnings release, Feb 11, 2026; SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1)
- Ambulatory Care segment (USPI) FY2025: net operating revenues $5,172M (2024: $4,534M); adjusted EBITDA $2,026M; adjusted EBITDA margin 39.2% (2024: 39.9%).
- Hospital Operations FY2025: net operating revenues $16,138M; adjusted EBITDA $2,540M; margin 15.7% (2024: 13.5%).
- USPI footprint Dec 31, 2025: interests in 533 ASCs (401 consolidated) and 26 surgical hospitals, 37 states.
- WORKED EXAMPLE: $2,026M / $5,172M = 0.3917 = 39.2% (USPI). Hospitals: $2,540M / $16,138M = 0.1574 = 15.7%.
  Spoken: "two point zero two six billion" / "five point one seven two billion".

## Surgery Partners FY2025 (10-K FY2025; Q4/FY2025 results release)
- Revenue ~$3.3 billion in 2025, +6.2% from $3.1 billion (2024). Surgical cases ~670,000 (+2.0%). Adjusted EBITDA $526.2M (2024: $508.2M). Net loss $77.9M for 2025.
- As of Dec 31, 2025: owned or operated, "primarily in partnership with physicians," 176 surgical facilities: 157 ASCs and 19 surgical hospitals in 30 states; majority interest in 90; consolidated 121. (FY2025 10-K)

## Physician ownership model (neutral)
- Operators like Surgery Partners and USPI typically own centers jointly with local physicians (FY2025 10-Ks). Physician owners are paid their professional (surgeon) fee separately, and as part-owners they can also receive a share of the facility's profits (distributions to noncontrolling interests).
- Surgeon payment for 66984 in 2026: $462.94, down about 11% from $521.75 in 2025 (Review of Ophthalmology / ophthalmology outlets) - context only.

## Why the margin is higher (industry explanation)
- No emergency room to staff, no inpatient beds, scheduled/elective cases and quick room turnover keep costs lower than a hospital. (General industry description; Becker's ASC; MedPAC ASC status reports describe ASCs as lower-cost settings.)

## Surprise fact
Medicare pays a surgery center less than a hospital for the same outpatient surgery, yet Tenet's surgery-center arm kept a 39.2% adjusted EBITDA margin in 2025, vs 15.7% for its hospitals.
