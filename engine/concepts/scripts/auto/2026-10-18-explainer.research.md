# Research brief: What a recession actually is (posts Oct 18, 2026)

## Official definition
- In the US, the NBER Business Cycle Dating Committee (a private, nonprofit research group) dates recessions. Its definition: "a significant decline in economic activity that is spread across the economy and lasts more than a few months" (NBER FAQ; BEA glossary points to NBER).
- The indicators it watches include real personal income less transfers, nonfarm payroll employment, household employment, real consumer spending, wholesale-retail sales and industrial production.
- "Two consecutive quarters of falling GDP" is a popular rule of thumb, not the official test. NBER "has never accepted the two-quarters idea". The 2001 recession did not have two straight negative GDP quarters.
- 2022: real GDP fell in Q1 and Q2 2022, but NBER did not declare a recession (NPR, CNBC, Time, July 2022).

## Dating lags (the surprising fact)
- Great Recession: December 2007 to June 2009, 18 months. NBER announced the start on Dec 1, 2008 (about a year later) and the end on Sep 20, 2010.
- COVID recession: February 2020 to April 2020, 2 months, the shortest on record (NBER, July 19, 2021; CNBC, Axios).

## Worked example
- Monthly unemployment rate: 5.0% in Dec 2007 -> peak of 10.0% in Oct 2009 (BLS). 10.0 / 5.0 = 2, so it doubled.
