# Research brief: 401(k) vs IRA (explainer short, posts Sat Dec 12, 2026)

Angle: evergreen comparison. One account comes through your job (401(k)); one you open yourself (IRA). Describe differences only; never say which to choose. Keep distinct from Roth IRA how-to (Nov 11), 401(k) investment picking (Nov 18), year-end checklist (Dec 9).

## Confirmed facts (with dates)
- 2026 401(k)/403(b)/gov 457/TSP employee deferral limit: $24,500 (up from $23,500 in 2025). IRS IR-2025-111, Nov 13, 2025 / Notice 2025-67.
- 2026 401(k) catch-up, age 50+: $8,000 (up from $7,500); total $32,500. Ages 60-63 higher catch-up: $11,250. (IRS IR-2025-111)
- 2026 IRA limit: $7,500 (up from $7,000). IRA catch-up 50+: $1,100 (up from $1,000). (IRS IR-2025-111)
- Ratio: $24,500 / $7,500 = 3.27, i.e. "over 3x" the IRA limit (our math).
- 2026 Roth IRA income phase-out (MAGI): single/head of household $153,000-$168,000 (2025: $150,000-$165,000); married filing jointly $242,000-$252,000 (2025: $236,000-$246,000); married filing separately $0-$10,000. (IRS IR-2025-111 via Journal of Accountancy, Nov 2025; Alliant CU)
- Roth 401(k) (designated Roth account): no income limitation to participate. (IRS "Roth comparison chart")
- Deadlines: 401(k) salary deferrals come from payroll, so 2026 deferrals must come out of paychecks by Dec 31, 2026. IRA contributions for tax year 2026 can be made until the filing deadline, generally April 15, 2027 (an extension does not extend it). (Partner Colorado CU Jan 2026; PensionBee; IRS Pub 590-A rule)
- Employer money: only a workplace plan can carry an employer match; IRAs have no match. Vanguard How America Saves 2026: 96% of Vanguard plans provided an employer contribution in 2025 (match or nonelective). Average total contribution rate (employee + employer) 12.1% in 2025 (preliminary).
- Most common match formula: 50 cents per dollar on the first 6% of pay (Vanguard How America Saves, 2020 data via ASPPA 2021; also cited for 2024 report by Motley Fool). NOT confirmed for the 2026 report, so the script uses it only as a labeled example.
- Investment choice: 401(k) limited to the plan's fund menu; an IRA at a brokerage can hold nearly any stock, fund or ETF (general rule; IRAs bar a few items like collectibles/life insurance).
- You can have both: 401(k) participation doesn't bar IRA contributions (it can limit the traditional IRA deduction; e.g. covered MFJ phase-out $129,000-$149,000 in 2026).

## Surprising fact
The 401(k) holds over three times the IRA limit in 2026 ($24,500 vs $7,500), and a Roth 401(k) has no income limit while a Roth IRA does.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
Salary $60,000; employee contributes 6% = 0.06 x 60,000 = $3,600. Employer matches 50% up to 6% of pay = 0.5 x 3,600 = $1,800. Total going in = $5,400.

## Chartable series: 401(k) employee deferral limit (IRS)
2020 $19,500; 2021 $19,500 (Notice 2020-79); 2022 $20,500; 2023 $22,500; 2024 $23,000; 2025 $23,500; 2026 $24,500.
