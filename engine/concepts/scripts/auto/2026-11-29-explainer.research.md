# Research brief: The cost of raising a child: where the money goes (posts Nov 29, 2026)

No hard-news angle needed; evergreen. Most recent data releases: LendingTree 2026 report (mostly 2024 data); Child Care Aware of America "Child Care in America: 2025 Price & Supply" (released May 2026).

## Key facts (with source and year)
1. USDA "Expenditures on Children by Families, 2015" (released Jan 9, 2017; Lino & Kuczynski, CNPP): a middle-income, married-couple (two-child) family will spend **$233,610** (2015 dollars) on a child born in 2015, **from birth through age 17** (= 18 years). Annual: $12,350-$13,900. Lower income: $174,690; higher income: $372,210.
2. USDA 2015 category shares (middle-income, married-couple): **housing 29%, food 18%, child care/education 16%, transportation 15%, health care 9%, miscellaneous 7%, clothing 6%** (sums to 100%). Housing largest across income groups (26-33%). Clothing is the smallest share.
3. Brookings Institution (Aug 2022, Lindsay/Kearney-era article "It's getting more expensive to raise children..."): adjusting the USDA estimate for higher inflation (2.23%/yr 2015-2020, 4%/yr from 2021), a child born in 2015 costs **$310,605** through age 17; $26,011 more than earlier inflation-projected estimate. Reported by CNN, Axios (Aug 2022).
4. LendingTree 2026 cost-of-raising-a-child report (mostly 2024 data): **$303,418** over 18 years, up 1.9% from $297,674 in the prior report; ~$16,857 a year. Includes housing (extra rent), food, infant day care, clothing, transportation, health insurance premiums; after tax exemptions/credits. Infant child care estimated at $17,264. Hawaii highest ($412,661). Sources: LendingTree; The Hill; CBS News; WSAW (Aug 20, 2026).
5. Child Care Aware of America (CCAoA), 2025 Price & Supply (May 2026): national average annual price of child care **$13,184 in 2025** (up from $13,128 in 2024). That's **10%** of median income for a married/two-parent household and **33%** for a single-parent household. Child care prices +23% 2021-2025 vs overall prices +24%. Sources: CCAoA press release (PR Newswire/Morningstar, May 14, 2026).

## Worked example (exact math)
Brookings $310,605, birth through age 17 = 18 years (ages 0-17).
- $310,605 / 18 = $17,255.83 -> **$17,256 a year**
- $17,255.83 / 12 = $1,437.99 -> **about $1,438 a month**
(Note: dividing by 17 would be wrong; "through age 17" covers 18 birthdays-years.)
Also: housing + food + transportation = 29 + 18 + 15 = **62%** of USDA total.

## Surprising fact
The biggest cost is not daycare or diapers: it's housing (29% of USDA total). Clothing is just 6%.

## Caveats
- Estimates differ by method: USDA (2015 dollars, projected), Brookings (inflation-adjusted USDA), LendingTree (different expense list, after tax credits). Label each.
- USDA has not published an update since the 2017 report (2015 data).
- LendingTree publication date: articles dated Aug 2026 (WSAW) and Apr 2026 (Fortune); label as "2026 report".
