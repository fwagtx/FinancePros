# Research brief: Use-it-or-lose-it: FSA deadlines, HSAs and year-end benefits explained
Researched Friday, October 23, 2026. Posts Tuesday, December 22, 2026, 12 PM Central. Evergreen; every number labeled with year/source.

## Timely angle
Late December is the last stretch before most calendar-year health FSA plan years end on December 31. Money not used (or carried over / covered by a grace period) is forfeited to the employer.

## Core rules (IRS)
- Health FSA "use-it-or-lose-it": unused amounts at the end of the plan year are forfeited, unless the employer's cafeteria plan adopts ONE of two optional exceptions (IRS Notice 2013-71; Prop. Treas. Reg. 1.125-5):
  1. Carryover of unused amounts into the next plan year, up to an indexed cap, OR
  2. Grace period of up to 2 months and 15 days (2.5 months) after plan year end to incur new expenses with old-year money (calendar-year plan: up to March 15).
  A plan cannot offer both a carryover and a grace period for the same plan year (Notice 2013-71). Employers may also offer neither, and may set a lower carryover cap than the IRS maximum.
- Run-out period: a plan-set window after the plan year ends for SUBMITTING claims for expenses incurred during that plan year. It is not extra time to spend. Length varies by plan (often ~90 days; plan-specific).
- Plans differ: plan year, deadlines, and which exception (if any) apply are in each plan's documents. Education, not advice.

## Limits (with years)
- Health FSA salary-reduction limit: $3,300 (2025); $3,400 (2026) - Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (Oct 9, 2025).
- Max carryover: $660 (plan years beginning in 2025); $680 (plan years beginning in 2026, carried into 2027) - Rev. Proc. 2025-32. Carryover cap = 20% of the salary-reduction limit.
- 2027 health FSA limit: NOT announced as of Oct 23, 2026 (projections like $3,500 / $700 carryover are third-party estimates; not used in script).
- Dependent care FSA: $7,500 per household ($3,750 married filing separately) starting 2026, up from $5,000, under Public Law 119-21 (2025 "One Big Beautiful Bill Act"), amending IRC sec. 129; not inflation-indexed (Polsinelli, NIS Benefits, Minnesota House Research summaries). Secondary sources only, but consistent.
- HSA limits: 2026 $4,400 self-only / $8,750 family (Rev. Proc. 2025-19); 2027 $4,500 / $9,000 (Rev. Proc. 2026-24, released ~May 29/June 1, 2026); $1,000 catch-up age 55+ (statutory, not indexed).
- HSA vs FSA: HSA balances carry over year to year (IRS Pub. 969: "Amounts that remain at the end of the year are generally carried over to the next year"); the account is owned by the individual and portable when changing jobs. Requires HDHP coverage.

## Surprising fact / data point
- EBRI, "Vital Statistics on Flexible Spending Accounts, 2022: Forfeitures on the Rise" (press release May 8, 2024): analysis of 3.2 million+ FSAs in 2022; roughly half (52%, per Money.com coverage of the report) of accountholders forfeited funds; average forfeiture $441 in 2022 (vs $339 in 2019). Total for accounts reviewed ~$1.4B (Plansponsor). Extrapolated national totals ($5.1B Money; $4B+ Empower) are estimates with different methods - NOT used.

## Worked example (hypothetical, calendar-year plan, 2026)
- Elect the 2026 max: $3,400. Balance left at Dec 31, 2026: $900 (assumed).
- No exception: $900 forfeited.
- Carryover plan with IRS max cap: $680 carries into 2027; $900 - $680 = $220 forfeited.
- Grace period plan (full 2.5 months): $900 can pay for expenses incurred through March 15, 2027; $0 lost if all spent by then.
