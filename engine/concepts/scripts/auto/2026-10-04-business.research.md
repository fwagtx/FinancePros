# Research brief: How software companies make money on subscriptions (case study: Adobe)

Show: How They Make Money. Posts Sunday, October 4, 2026. Evergreen: every number carries its fiscal year or date.
Note: sec.gov / adobe.com pages were blocked for direct fetch in this environment; figures below were confirmed via web search results that quote Adobe's SEC filings (10-K FY2025, 10-K FY2021-FY2024, 8-K earnings releases). The fact-checker should re-verify against the filings.

## Timely context (not used as hook, to stay evergreen)
- Adobe reported Q3 fiscal 2026 revenue of $6.76 billion on September 10, 2026 (up 13% year over year). Source: Adobe newsroom "Adobe Reports Record Q3 Results" (news.adobe.com, Sept 2026).

## Core business model
- A subscription company sells access, not ownership. Customers pay monthly or yearly; revenue is recognized ratably over the subscription term (Adobe 10-K revenue recognition policy).
- The switch: In May 2013 Adobe announced it would stop selling new Creative Suite perpetual licenses and move to Creative Cloud subscriptions only (TechCrunch, May 6, 2013; DPReview, May 2013). Users protested; a Change.org petition "I do not want to subscribe to use Photoshop" gathered tens of thousands of signatures.
- Before: Creative Suite 6 Master Collection list price $2,599 (one-time). After: Creative Cloud full membership $49.99 per month for individuals (2013 launch pricing). Sources: ProDesignTools CS6 price sheet; Digital Camera World; Tech & Learning CS6 review.

## Key numbers (Adobe 10-K, fiscal 2025, year ended November 28, 2025)
- Total revenue: $23,769 million (~$23.77B), up 11% from FY2024 ($21,505M).
- Subscription revenue: $22,904 million (~$22.9B) = 96% of total revenue. FY2024: $20,521M = 95%.
- Cost of revenue (total): $2,551 million. Gross profit: $21,218 million. Gross margin = 21,218 / 23,769 = 89.3% (~89%).
- Remaining performance obligations (contracted revenue not yet recognized): $22.52 billion as of November 28, 2025, up 13% from $19.96 billion as of November 29, 2024.

## Worked example (exact math, 2013 prices)
- One-time Creative Suite 6 Master Collection: $2,599.
- Creative Cloud: $49.99 per month.
- Break-even: $2,599 / $49.99 = 51.99, so about 52 months (a bit over 4 years). After that, every month is extra revenue compared with the old one-time sale.
- One year of subscription: $49.99 x 12 = $599.88 (~$600).

## The surprising fact
- 96% of Adobe's fiscal 2025 revenue came from subscriptions, and it already had $22.52 billion of signed-but-not-yet-recognized revenue (RPO) at the end of fiscal 2025, close to a full year of subscription revenue ($22.9B).

## Why it matters to viewers (education)
- Small monthly charges add up: $49.99 a month is about $600 a year. Subscriptions are designed to be predictable for the company and easy to forget for the customer.
