# 2026-12-10-hot research brief: Money Myth, "bank error in your favor = finders keepers"

Researched Sunday, October 11, 2026. Posts Thursday, December 10, 2026, 6 PM Central. Evergreen; no news hook. Every figure dated.
Format: Money Myth. MYTH: if the bank puts money in your account by mistake, it's yours. FACT: banks can reverse erroneous credits, and knowingly spending money that isn't yours has led to theft/criminal charges. Neutral, educational, no legal advice.

## Rules (what the rules say)
- US OCC (HelpWithMyBank.gov, "bank error remove funds"): a bank that credited money to an account in error does not need the customer's permission to remove it; it may correct the error (including by offset, per the deposit agreement) and may temporarily freeze the account up to the amount of the erroneous deposit while it's fixed.
  https://www.helpwithmybank.gov/help-topics/bank-accounts/banking-errors-disputes/bank-error-remove-funds.html
- Deposit account agreements commonly reserve this right. Example (CIBC, Canada, personal account agreement): "We may, without notice to you, reverse or correct any debit or credit transactions which were posted in error to your account at any time." Wording varies by bank; the bank's own current agreement controls.
- Nacha Operating Rules (ACH), subsection 2.9 Reversing Entries: an Originator/ODFI may send a reversing entry for an erroneous entry (duplicate, wrong receiver, wrong amount, etc.); it must be made available to the receiving bank within FIVE BANKING DAYS after the settlement date of the erroneous entry; originator must make a reasonable attempt to notify the receiver by the reversal's settlement date. https://www.nacha.org/rules/reversals-and-enforcement
- UCC Article 4A (wire transfers), section 4A-303(a) and (c): when a bank sends too much, a duplicate, or pays the wrong beneficiary, it "is entitled to recover from the beneficiary ... the excess payment received to the extent allowed by the law governing mistake and restitution." https://law.cornell.edu/ucc/4A/4A-303 (NY codification: nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/4-A-303)
  - Note: outcome after 5 days / under restitution law depends on facts (e.g. notice of mistake, change of position). Do not overstate.

## Big real examples
1. Citibank / Revlon (US):
   - Aug 11, 2020: Citi (as loan agent) meant to send about $7.8 million in interest but wired about $900 million (mostly Citi's own money, ~$894M) to Revlon's lenders.
   - Some lenders returned roughly $385M (reports vary; "about $400M"); 10 asset managers held on to about $500M+.
   - Feb 2021: Judge Jesse Furman (SDNY) ruled the lenders could keep it ("discharge for value" defense).
   - Sept 8, 2022: US Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit reversed (lenders had constructive notice of the mistake; discharge-for-value didn't apply to loans not yet due).
   - Sources: Torys (Sept 2022), Business Standard (Sept 9, 2022), Motley Fool (Feb 16, 2021), Fox Business, Institutional Investor.
2. Citigroup $81 trillion near miss:
   - April 2024: Citi erroneously credited a client account with $81 trillion instead of $280; missed by two employees, caught by a third ~90 minutes after posting; reversed hours later; no funds left the bank; reported to the Fed and OCC as a "near miss". Citi said no impact on the bank or client.
   - Reported by the Financial Times on Feb 28, 2025 (also Bloomberg, Gulf News, Business Standard citing FT).
   - FT, citing internal report: Citi had 10 near misses of $1 billion+ in 2024, down from 13 the year before.

## Criminal cases (accurately stated)
- Crypto.com (Australia): in May 2021 a staff member, processing a small refund (reported as $100; some reports say $68/$65), typed an account number into the amount field and sent A$10,474,143 (about A$10.5M) to Thevamanogari Manivel's account. Found in a December 2021 audit. Civil: Supreme Court of Victoria ordered repayment; a Melbourne house bought with funds ordered sold. Criminal: Manivel pleaded guilty to recklessly dealing with proceeds of crime; sentenced to time served (209 days) plus an 18-month community corrections order. Her then-partner Jatinder Singh pleaded guilty to theft and was sentenced to 3 years' jail (Victorian County Court, Judge Martine Marich). Sources: Fortune (Aug 31, 2022), Decrypt, AAP, The Nightly.
  - UNCONFIRMED: exact sentencing dates (Manivel ~2023; Singh ~2024). Don't state years for sentences in script.
- US: Robert and Tiffany Williams (Pennsylvania): BB&T teller error deposited $120,000 on May 31, 2019; police alleged they spent much of it; charged Sept 2019 with theft and receiving stolen property (allegations). Final disposition NOT found -> don't use as outcome.
- Australia: Christine Jiaxin Lee (Westpac): alleged ~A$4.6M withdrawn 2014-2015 from an overdraft error; charges withdrawn by prosecutors late Nov 2017. Shows outcomes vary.

## Worked example (math)
- Citi near miss: intended $280; credited $81,000,000,000,000. Ratio = 81e12 / 280 = about 289 billion times the intended amount.
- Revlon: intended ~$7.8M; sent ~$900M. 900 / 7.8 = about 115x.

## Surprising fact
- A judge first let Revlon's lenders keep about $500M (Feb 2021) before the appeals court reversed in Sept 2022.
