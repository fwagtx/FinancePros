# Research brief: How extended warranties make money: the claim-rate math (posts Oct 17, 2026)

## Seller economics
- Consumer Reports, Extended Warranty Buying Guide: stores keep 50 percent or more of what they charge for extended warranties, much more than they make selling products.
- Retail margins on service contracts are often cited at 40-70% gross vs single digits on electronics (secondary sources, not used on screen).
- Allstate FY2025 10-K: Protection Plans (SquareTrade) revenues were $2,300M in 2025 vs $1,987M in 2024 (+15.8%). Adjusted net income was $179M vs $157M. Context only.

## Claim rates / the key math
- Abito & Salant, "The Effect of Product Misperception on Economic Outcomes: Evidence from the Extended Warranty Market," Review of Economic Studies 86(6), 2019:
  - When a product's actual failure rate was about 5%, consumers acted as if it were about 13%.
  - In a counterfactual with correct perceptions, warranty profits fell by more than 90%, even with sellers' market power unchanged.
  - Giving buyers failure-rate information cut their willingness to pay.
- Consumer Reports 2014 survey: 55% of people with extended car warranties never used them for repairs.

## Worked example (hypothetical plan, using the study's 5% failure rate)
- Plan price $80; covered repair costs $300; failure chance 5%.
- Expected payout = 0.05 x $300 = $15. Seller keeps on average $80 - $15 = $65 before costs.
