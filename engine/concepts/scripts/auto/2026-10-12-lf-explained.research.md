# Research brief: How banks create money (2026-10-12-lf-explained)

Prepared September 30, 2026. Posts October 12, 2026. Evergreen wording; every number dated.

## Timely angle
- Fed H.6 (released Sept 22, 2026): M2 (seasonally adjusted) rose $124.9 billion from July to a record $23.3428 trillion in August 2026, about 5.7% above a year earlier; the 5.7% was the largest year-over-year rise since June 2022 (Kobeissi Letter / Yahoo Finance citing H.6).
- Fed raised the federal funds target range 1/4 point to 3.75%-4% on Sept 16, 2026 (12-0 vote), its first hike since 2023. Interest on reserve balances (IORB) raised to 3.90%, effective Sept 17, 2026 (Fed implementation note; CNBC).

## The surprising fact
- Currency component of money stock, August 2026 (H.6): $2,475.6 billion. As a share of M2 ($23,342.8B): 2,475.6 / 23,342.8 = 10.6%. So about 89% of M2 is not paper cash or coins (the rest is mostly bank deposits, plus retail money market funds and small time deposits).
- UK comparison (Bank of England, 2014 Q1 Quarterly Bulletin, "Money creation in the modern economy"): bank deposits make up 97% of broad money; notes and coins about 3%.
- BoE exact quote: "Whenever a bank makes a loan, it simultaneously creates a matching deposit in the borrower's bank account, thereby creating new money." (McLeay, Radia, Thomas, BoE Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1)
- BoE: "the majority of money in the modern economy is created by commercial banks making loans." Repaying loans destroys money.
- BoE three limits on bank money creation: market forces/profitability, prudential regulation, monetary policy (the ultimate limit).

## Rules / numbers
- Reserve requirements: Fed announced March 15, 2020 it cut reserve requirement ratios to 0%, effective March 26, 2020 (Fed Reserve Requirements page; Federal Register). Still 0%.
- Capital: large US banks need minimum CET1 ratio 4.5% plus stress capital buffer of at least 2.5% (Fed, Large Bank Capital Requirements, June 2026). GSIB surcharge at least 1.0%.
- Commercial bank loans and leases: about $14.0 trillion (seasonally adjusted), mid-September 2026 (Fed H.8, Sept 25, 2026 release).
- Reserve balances at Federal Reserve Banks: about $2.9 trillion ($2.930T) in September 2026 (Fed H.4.1).
- FDIC: standard insurance $250,000 per depositor, per insured bank, per ownership category (FDIC). FDIC created by Banking Act of 1933 (signed June 1933); about 9,000 banks failed in the Depression (History.com / LOC).
- SVB: March 9, 2023 customers withdrew $42 billion in one day; seized March 10, 2023; second-largest bank failure in US history (Wikipedia citing Fed OIG/FDIC; CNN; NPR).

## Worked examples (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
1. Textbook money multiplier (the myth): $1,000 deposit, 10% reserve requirement. Lends 900, then 810, ... sum = 1,000 / 0.10 = $10,000. With a 0% requirement (since March 2020) the formula gives infinity; lending did not become infinite, so the reserve ratio is not the real constraint.
2. Example car loan: "Maya" borrows $30,000 at 7% APR for 60 months.
   - Bank books: asset +$30,000 (loan), liability +$30,000 (Maya's deposit). New deposit = new money; no one else's balance fell.
   - Payment = 30,000 x (0.07/12) / (1 - (1+0.07/12)^-60) = $594.04/month.
   - First month interest = 30,000 x 0.07/12 = $175.00; principal = $419.04.
   - Total interest over 60 months = 594.04 x 60 - 30,000 = $5,642.16 (exact: $5,642.16 using unrounded payment $594.0360).
   - When Maya pays the dealer at another bank, her bank sends $30,000 of reserves to the dealer's bank (why reserves still matter for settlement).
   - Each principal repayment deletes deposit money; interest becomes bank income.

## History
- 1600s London: goldsmiths stored gold, issued receipts that circulated as banknotes; they issued more receipts than gold held (fractional reserve origin). Charles I seized ~GBP200,000 of bullion in the Tower of London in 1640.
- Dec 23, 1913: Federal Reserve Act signed by Wilson.
- 1933: Banking Act creates FDIC.
- Aug 15, 1971: Nixon suspends dollar convertibility into gold.
- March 26, 2020: US reserve requirements set to 0%.
