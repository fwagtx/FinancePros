# Fact-check: 2026-12-29-hot (Budget face-off: 3 methods, same pay)

Checked Oct 30, 2026 for posting Dec 29, 2026. I verified the claims independently with web search and recomputed the math in python. Two inputs disagreed on what to read. The caller told me to treat the research brief as leads only, while checker-rules.md says not to read it. I read it but did not rely on it for any claim.

## Verified claims
- **Fidelity 64%:** Fidelity's 17th annual New Year's Financial Resolutions Study (Business Wire, Dec 11, 2025) found that 64% of Americans were considering a financial resolution for 2026, up from 56% in 2025. The sample was 3,026 US adults. Fox Business, 401k Specialist, KUTV and the Fidelity newsroom all report the same figure.
- **50/30/20:** The rule comes from *All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan* (2005) by Elizabeth Warren and her daughter Amelia Warren Tyagi. It splits after-tax income into 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings and debt repayment. "Popularized" is the safe verb because some sources debate whether Warren invented it. Acorns, Fox Business and Wealthsimple agree.
- **Zero-based:** Peter Pyhrr of Texas Instruments wrote "Zero-base Budgeting" for Harvard Business Review (Nov-Dec 1970, pp. 111-121). Wikipedia, D Magazine (1976) and Mind Tools agree. "Written up for companies in 1970" is accurate.
- **Pay yourself first:** George S. Clason's *The Richest Man in Babylon* is dated 1926 by Wikipedia and other sources. It began as thrift pamphlets that banks and insurers distributed. The book's rule is to keep one-tenth of what you earn. Some sources date the pamphlets to 1920-24 and an illustrated edition to 1930, but "a 1926 classic" is acceptable.
- **Math (recomputed):** $4,000 x 50/30/20 gives $2,000 + $1,200 + $800 = $4,000. 10% of $4,000 is $400, leaving $3,600. Zero-based ends at $4,000 to $0. All correct. Spoken and on-screen values match on every line.
- **Neutrality:** There is no advice and no single winner. Line 7 says "No universal winner", and the post fields include a not-advice disclaimer. The examples are labeled "our math" / "our example".
- **Other checks:** Compare values ("$400", "$3,600") are 7 characters or fewer. No line has keep:true. There are no quotes, allegations or price predictions.

### 2. post.yt_desc and post.ig: "first written up" / "dates to" overstates the origin of zero-based budgeting
- **Problem:** The US Department of Agriculture tried a zero-base budgeting approach around 1962-64, before Pyhrr's article. The 1977 Congressional Record and Wikipedia's zero-based budgeting page both mention it. Pyhrr's 1970 HBR article is the famous corporate write-up, but it was not the first.
- **Fixes:**
  - post.yt_desc: `zero-based, written up for companies in a 1970 Harvard Business Review article;`
  - post.ig: `Zero-based: every dollar gets a job until $0 is left. A 1970 Harvard Business Review article popularized it for companies.`
