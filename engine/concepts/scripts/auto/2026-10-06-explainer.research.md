# Research brief: 2026-10-06-explainer
Topic: Open enrollment is coming: how to read a health plan (premium, deductible, out-of-pocket max)
Angle (differs from the Oct 16 video, which covers basics + the $12,000 2027 cap): compare two plans with a worked total-cost example. Why the plan with the cheaper premium can cost more in a bad year, and less in a quiet year.

## Timely angle
- Fall is open-enrollment season for most employer plans (dates vary by employer). Medicare's annual enrollment runs October 15 - December 7, 2026 (Medicare.gov / Kiplinger). HealthCare.gov open enrollment for 2027 coverage starts November 1, 2026 (end date reported differently by sources, so not used on screen).

## Definitions (HealthCare.gov glossary; ACA rules)
- Premium: what you pay every month to have the plan, whether or not you use care.
- Deductible: what you pay for covered care before the plan starts paying its share.
- Coinsurance: your percentage share of a covered bill after the deductible (for example 20%).
- Out-of-pocket maximum: the most you pay in a plan year for covered in-network care (deductible + copays + coinsurance). Premiums do NOT count toward it; neither does out-of-network care in most plans.

## Key numbers (with dates)
- 2025 average deductible, single coverage, workers with a general deductible: $1,886 (KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey, released Oct 22, 2025). Up 43% over 10 years (2015: $1,318).
- 34% of covered workers in 2025 had a single-coverage deductible of $2,000 or more (32% in 2024). (KFF 2025)
- 2025 average annual premium: $9,325 single, $26,993 family; workers contributed $1,440 (single) and $6,850 (family). (KFF 2025)
- 2025 average single premium by plan type: HDHP/SO $8,620 vs PPO $9,818. (KFF 2025)
- Large firms 2025 average single deductible: HMO $1,156, PPO $1,123, HDHP/SO $2,121. (KFF 2025)
- Average coinsurance for a hospital admission: 20% (KFF 2025).
- 33% of covered workers were in HDHP/SO plans, 46% in PPOs in 2025 (KFF 2025).
- ACA out-of-pocket cap: $10,600 self / $21,200 family for 2026; $12,000 / $24,000 for 2027 (HHS NBPP 2027 final rule, Jan 29 2026; Milliman). NOT the hook (used by Oct 16 video).
- The 2026 KFF employer survey has not been released as of Sept 30, 2026 (usually late October), so 2025 is the latest.

## Worked example (hypothetical plans, round numbers, both 20% coinsurance, in-network)
Plan A (low deductible): $120/month premium, $1,000 deductible, 20% coinsurance, $4,000 out-of-pocket max.
Plan B (high deductible): $40/month premium, $3,000 deductible, 20% coinsurance, $7,000 out-of-pocket max.
Premiums per year: A = $120 x 12 = $1,440. B = $40 x 12 = $480. B is $960 cheaper in premiums.

Quiet year, $500 of covered care (all under both deductibles):
- A: $1,440 + $500 = $1,940
- B: $480 + $500 = $980  -> B costs $960 less.

Big year, one $20,000 covered bill:
- A: $1,000 deductible + 20% x $19,000 = $3,800 -> $4,800, capped at $4,000 max. Total = $1,440 + $4,000 = $5,440
- B: $3,000 deductible + 20% x $17,000 = $3,400 -> $6,400 (under $7,000 max). Total = $480 + $6,400 = $6,880
- A costs $1,440 less ($6,880 - $5,440 = $1,440).
Formula: yearly cost = premium x 12 + your share of care (deductible, then coinsurance, up to the max).

## Surprising fact
The plan with the lower paycheck deduction can cost more in total: in the example, the $80/month cheaper plan costs $1,440 more in a $20,000 year. And premiums never count toward the out-of-pocket max.
