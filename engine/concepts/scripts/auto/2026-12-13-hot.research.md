# 2026-12-13-hot research brief: Money myth - "crypto yield is just like savings account interest"

Researched Wednesday, October 14, 2026. Posts Sunday, December 13, 2026. Evergreen; every figure dated.

## The myth vs the fact
- Myth: a crypto "Earn"/"interest" account works like a bank savings account, just with a higher rate.
- Fact: bank savings interest is paid on an insured deposit at a bank. Crypto yield comes from the platform lending your coins out (to institutions, traders) or staking them on a blockchain. It carries coin-price risk and counterparty (platform) risk, and FDIC deposit insurance does not cover it.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. **Advertised yields, early 2022.** Bloomberg Law (Jan 27, 2022): Celsius founder Alex Mashinsky's livestreams pitched interest of as much as 18% a year ("Celsius's 18% Yields on Crypto Are Tempting and Drawing Scrutiny"). Coin Bureau / other coverage of Celsius's app listed "up to 17% APY". Rates varied by coin; headline figures were ceilings.
2. **Average bank savings rate, same time.** FDIC national rate for savings: 0.06% (Dec 2021 and Jan-Mar 2022). (FDIC national rates, via FRED series SNDR / third-party table reproduction.)
3. **Bankruptcies, 2022.** Voyager Digital filed Chapter 11 on July 6, 2022; Celsius Network on July 13, 2022 (SDNY); BlockFi on November 28, 2022 (D.N.J.). (Vermont DFR investor alert May 2, 2023; Blockworks; The Block.)
4. **Celsius Earn ruling.** On January 4, 2023, Chief Judge Martin Glenn (US Bankruptcy Court, SDNY) ruled that crypto deposited in Celsius "Earn" accounts was property of the bankruptcy estate (Celsius's), based on the terms of use. About 600,000 Earn accounts held about $4.2 billion in crypto shortly before the July 2022 filing. Earn customers were unsecured creditors; on Jan 6, 2023 the judge denied Earn customers' motions to be treated as secured. (Weil restructuring blog; Bracewell; Schulte Roth & Zabel; The Defiant; Kitco Jan 5, 2023.)
5. **Recoveries.** Celsius plan disclosure (2023) estimated Earn holders would recover about 67% (range up to ~85.6% for Borrow participants; ~47% in a liquidation), paid in BTC/ETH and cash/stock; actual recoveries are individualized. (FinanceFeeds, FXStreet Aug 2023, Stretto distribution site.) Use only as "estimated".
6. **FDIC: crypto not insured.** FDIC fact sheet, July 28, 2022, "What the Public Needs to Know About FDIC Deposit Insurance and Crypto Companies": exact text: "FDIC deposit insurance does not apply to financial products such as stocks, bonds, money market mutual funds, other types of securities, commodities, or crypto assets." Also: FDIC insurance "does not protect against the default, insolvency, or bankruptcy of any non-bank entity" (crypto custodians, exchanges, brokers, wallet providers, neobanks).
7. **Voyager FDIC claims.** July 28, 2022: Federal Reserve Board and FDIC jointly sent Voyager a cease-and-desist letter, calling its statements that it was FDIC-insured / that customers would get FDIC coverage "false and misleading". Voyager's only FDIC-linked account was at Metropolitan Commercial Bank, and that coverage did not extend to Voyager's customers against Voyager's own failure. (FDIC PR-22-058; The Block; NJBIZ.)
8. **FTC, October 2023.** FTC settled with Voyager (permanent ban from handling consumer assets; $1.65 billion judgment suspended) and sued former CEO Stephen Ehrlich, alleging he falsely told customers their accounts were FDIC-insured and "safe". Ehrlich did not settle at that time; these are allegations. FTC says customers lost more than $1 billion in crypto assets (alleged). (FTC press release Oct 12, 2023; TechCrunch.)
9. **BlockFi SEC settlement.** February 14, 2022: BlockFi agreed to pay $100 million ($50M to the SEC, $50M to 32 states) over its BlockFi Interest Accounts (BIAs), which the SEC order found were unregistered securities; the SEC also found BlockFi misrepresented the risk of its loan portfolio (claimed institutional loans were "typically" over-collateralized). BlockFi did not admit or deny the findings; it stopped offering BIAs to new US customers. BIAs paid up to 9.25% APY (one source). (SEC order; Simpson Thacher; Ropes & Gray; Crowdfund Insider; The Defiant.)
10. **Staking risk.** Staking locks coins to help run a proof-of-stake network; rewards are paid in that coin, so a price drop can outweigh the reward; tokens can be locked and unsellable; validators can be "slashed" (penalized) for misbehavior or downtime; custodial staking adds platform risk. (CNBC Feb 11, 2023; Elliptic.)
11. **GENIUS Act (stablecoins).** Signed into law July 18, 2025 (Public Law 119-27; passed Senate 68-30, House 308-122). Section 4(a)(11): "No permitted payment stablecoin issuer or foreign payment stablecoin issuer shall pay the holder of any payment stablecoin any form of interest or yield (whether in cash, tokens, or other consideration) solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention of such payment stablecoin." The ban names issuers only; whether exchanges/affiliates/third parties may pay "rewards" is disputed. OCC proposed rule (2026) adds a rebuttable presumption covering issuer arrangements with affiliates/related third parties (proposal, not confirmed final). CRS "The Stablecoin Yield Debate" (IF13174, updated Mar 6, 2026). May 1, 2026: Senators Tillis and Alsobrooks released CLARITY Act compromise language to bar stablecoin rewards that work like deposit interest while allowing some activity-based rewards; banks said it falls short, Coinbase backed it. Final status as of Oct 2026 NOT confirmed - describe only as "debated in Congress in 2026". Neutral.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
$1,000 for one year, rates held constant, no compounding:
- Bank savings at the FDIC national average 0.06% (Jan 2022): $1,000 x 0.0006 = $0.60 of interest; principal is an insured deposit.
- Crypto account at an advertised 18%: $1,000 x 0.18 = $180 of "yield", paid in crypto.
- But if the platform fails and estimated Earn recovery is ~67% (Celsius plan estimate, 2023): $1,000 x 0.67 = $670 back, i.e. a $330 loss before any yield, received years later.
- And if the coin itself falls 50% in price, a 18% coin yield leaves 1.18 x 0.5 = 0.59, i.e. $590 of value.

## The fact that surprises people
In Celsius's bankruptcy, a judge ruled (Jan 4, 2023) that the coins customers put in "Earn" accounts legally belonged to Celsius, not the customers - per the terms of use they had agreed to. Customers were unsecured creditors.
