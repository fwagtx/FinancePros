## Core definitions (textbook, stable)
- Economies of scale: average (per-unit) cost falls as output/volume grows.
- Fixed costs: do not change with how much you make/sell in the period (rent, a machine, a warehouse; in our story the storage tree).
- Variable costs: rise with each unit (materials, packaging; in our story a sack per acorn).
- Average cost = (fixed cost + variable cost per unit x units) / units = fixed/units + variable per unit. The fixed part gets spread thinner as units grow.
- Diseconomies of scale: past the most efficient size, long-run average cost per unit rises again, because of slower/more complex communication, coordination problems, bureaucracy, weaker motivation. Gives the U-shaped long-run average cost curve. (Standard economics; sources: Economics Online, Save My Exams revision notes, WallStreetMojo. Educational sites, not primary; concept is textbook-standard.)

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, clearly labeled; made-up numbers)
Assumptions: a squirrel's storage tree costs $100 (fixed, same no matter how many acorns); each acorn needs a $1 sack (variable).
- 10 acorns: total cost = $100 + 10 x $1 = $110. Average = $110 / 10 = **$11.00 per acorn**.
- 1,000 acorns: total cost = $100 + 1,000 x $1 = $1,100. Average = $1,100 / 1,000 = **$1.10 per acorn**.
- Drop: ($11.00 - $1.10) / $11.00 = 90% cheaper per acorn. Variable cost per acorn is unchanged ($1); the whole drop is the fixed $100 spread over more acorns ($10.00 each at 10 acorns vs $0.10 each at 1,000).

## Real example 1: Costco (company filings)
- Fiscal 2026 (52 weeks ended August 30, 2026): net sales **$297.247 billion** (up 10.1% from $269.9B in FY2025). Merchandise costs $264.279B. Gross margin = $32.968B = **11.09% of net sales** (FY2025: 11.12%). Source: Costco FY2026 Form 10-K (SEC, cost-20260830.htm) and Costco Q4/FY2026 release Sept 24, 2026 (via search excerpts; math checks: 32,968 / 297,247 = 11.09%).
- FY2026 net income $9.226 billion ($20.76/diluted share). Source: Costco FY2026 release.
- Costco's 10-K (business description, paraphrased, NOT a quote): its model of low prices on a limited selection, high sales volume and rapid inventory turnover, combined with volume purchasing and efficient no-frills self-service warehouses, lets it operate profitably at gross margins significantly lower than most other retailers.
- Caveat: Costco also earns membership fees, which help it run thin merchandise margins; scale is one part of the model.

## Real example 2: Kroger (comparison grocer)
- Fiscal 2025 (year ended around January 31, 2026): gross margin **22.9% of sales** (22.3% in FY2024). Source: Kroger "Fourth Quarter and Full-Year 2025 Results" release, March 5, 2026 (Table 9), via thekrogerco.com / Business Wire reprints. Kroger's full fiscal 2026 ends ~Jan 2027, so FY2025 is still its latest full year on December 18, 2026.
- Caveat: Kroger is also very large; fiscal years differ; gross margin definitions differ slightly by company. Script compares the two named companies only, does not generalize to "all grocers".

## Surprising fact
Costco's gross margin (about 11.1% in fiscal 2026) is roughly half of Kroger's (22.9% in fiscal 2025), yet Costco earned $9.2 billion in fiscal 2026: thin margin times enormous volume.
