## Issues

1. **Line 41: wrong fact (blocking).** The line says state gas taxes average "about 22 cents" and the stat shows "~40¢". EIA puts the average of total state taxes and fees at **33.27 cents per gallon** (Jan 2026; about 33 cents through 2025). Adding the 18.4-cent federal tax gives about 52 cents. That is also the only total that fits the 17% tax share on line 40: 16.6% of $3.10 is 51.5 cents, while 40 cents would be about 13%. Fix the caption and narration to "18.4 cents federal, plus about 33 cents from states on average". Change the scene value to `~52¢` and the sub to `18.4¢ federal + ~33¢ state avg (EIA)`.

2. **Line 71: the caption and the narration give different reasons.** The caption says the byproducts ("asphalt, lubricants...") are "why the total comes out to about 45 gallons". That is not true. The narration gives the right cause: processing makes the volume expand (processing gain). Fix the caption to: "The rest becomes asphalt, lubricants and plastic feedstocks. Processing makes it expand, so the total is about 45 gallons."

3. **Cover title "Why Is Gas $4.47?" will read as dated on Oct 8.** It is present tense, but by Oct 8 the Oct 5 EIA weekly price will be out. Brent fell to $102.59 on Sept 29 amid SPR releases and recovering Gulf exports, so the pump price will likely be lower. Change the cover title to "Why Did Gas Hit $4.47?", which matches the post title and the hook. The label "Sept 28 '26" already dates it. The hook and post text say "hit" in past tense and are fine.

4. **Line 1: the hook is a little over 3 seconds.** It is true and specific (net petroleum exporter, $4.47). But the spoken version is 12 words ("America's a net oil seller. So why did gas hit four forty-seven?"), which is about 3.5 to 4 seconds. A tighter version: say "America sells oil. So why's gas four forty-seven?" (about 2.5 seconds). Or keep the tense safe for Oct 8 with "America sells oil. So why did gas hit four forty-seven?" Accuracy note: the US is a net exporter of *petroleum* (crude plus products) but still a net *crude* importer. "Net oil seller" is fine colloquially because line 3 clarifies "petroleum".

5. **Line 67: unclear and awkward to say aloud.** "Oil importers feel it even harder, since they buy that oil in dollars they earn first" is hard to follow. The caption "Importers pay twice" is not explained. Suggested rewrite: "Oil importers like Japan and Germany feel it even more, because oil is priced in US dollars. When their own currency is weaker, the same barrel costs them extra." Change the caption to "Priced in dollars".

6. **Line 19: the referent of "That's" is unclear.** Line 18 is about geography, so "That's about a fifth of all the oil..." no longer points clearly at the 20M b/d figure. Say "Those twenty million barrels are about a fifth of all the oil the world uses..."

7. **Line 38: "a record since 1987" can be misheard as "the highest since 1987".** Platts began publishing Dated Brent in 1987, so this is an all-time record for that series. Say "the highest since Platts began publishing it in 1987", or just "a record high". The on-screen headline "highest on record" is fine.

8. **Line 25 (minor): "nearly quadrupled" is slightly off.** $11.65 / $2.90 = 4.02x. Say "roughly quadrupled" or "quadrupled".

9. **Line 72 (minor, not independently verified).** I could not find a source for "polyethylene up an estimated 70-80% Feb-Apr 2026 (Plastics Today)". ICIS (Apr 14, 2026) reports European PE spot prices "up 100% on the low end since end-February" and heading for records, and the ethylene contract rose a record ~40% in April. The claim points the same way and is labeled an estimate, so it can stay if the Plastics Today source is on file. Otherwise use "European polyethylene spot prices roughly doubled at the low end from late February to April (ICIS)".

10. **Line 6 (minor clarity).** The US character says "I pump my own!", and all three countries say they pay the "same price". Viewers could take this to mean pump prices are the same, which they are not (taxes differ). Consider "Every country pays roughly the same price for crude" in the caption and narration.

11. **Line 51 (read-aloud nit).** "Four forty-six and a half" is okay. "Four dollars and forty-six and a half cents" is clearer, or round to "about four forty-seven" to match the hook (then 15 x 4.47 = $67.05, so keep $4.465 on screen if you want $66.98).

12. **Lines 27-28 (minor).** $147.27 was the intraday high; the July 11, 2008 settle was $145.08. "Hit" is accurate. The scene label "WTI, July 11, 2008: $147.27" could add "intraday". "Around $32 by December" is acceptable (Dec 19, 2008 low $32.40).
