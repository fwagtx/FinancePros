# Fact-check: 2026-11-05-hot ("Heir to Millions? It's a Scam")
Checked 2026-10-01 for posting 2026-11-05. Source: script JSON only. Official sites (consumer.ftc.gov, uspis.gov, unclaimed.org, nast.org) block direct fetch here, so these checks rely on search-result excerpts of those official pages plus news coverage.

## Verified
- **Line 2 / news card.** The FTC consumer alert "Unclaimed life insurance money? It's a scam" is dated July 9, 2026 (consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2026/07/...). It describes letters from a supposed law firm saying a client with your last name died, that you are heir to a life-insurance policy worth millions, and that the money would be split between you, charities and the firm. The FTC calls it a scam that seeks personal and financial information (SSN, bank account numbers), money, or both. News coverage notes that the client "died without heirs" and that the scam is a revival of earlier letters.
- **Line 3 board.** "Client, your last name" / "Split: you, firm, charity" / "Then: pay the 'fees'" fit the alert. The split card's "Asks for SSN" matches the alert's mention of Social Security numbers.
- **Line 4 USPIS quote.** "Legitimate law firms or executors of wills do not request you to pay a fee to find out about your share of an estate." matches the USPIS Inheritance Scams page word for word.
- **Line 5.** NAUPA's FY2024 report (released Oct 29, 2024) says $4.49 billion was returned to owners. Correct, and labeled FY2024.
- **Line 6, average $2,080.** This is the FY2024 average claim paid. Confirmed as FY2024, not FY2022.
- **Line 7.** Searching state programs, unclaimed.org (the NAUPA hub) and MissingMoney.com is free. Correct.
- **Line 8 quote wording.** "Never send money or information to a stranger who promises big rewards." matches the FTC text word for word. The alert continues "That's always a scam."
- **Hook (line 1).** "A stranger with your last name left you millions?" is 9 words (under 3 seconds). It is specific and is exactly the pitch the FTC describes. It is fair, and the cover and title are paid off.
- **Time words.** "July 2026" and "fiscal 2024" are absolute dates, and the script never says "latest", "most recent", "new" or "this year". NAUPA's FY2025 report may come out in late October 2026, before the post date. Because the script labels FY2024 explicitly, it stays accurate.
- **Instruction framing.** None in the script's own voice. The "never send money" line is an attributed FTC quote.
- **Sub-labels.** All 28 characters or fewer (the longest, "Returned to owners, FY2024", is 26). The two `source` strings are 32 and 31 characters, but they are source lines, not sub-labels.

## Issues
1. **WRONG FACT: median claim (line 6; post.ig; post.yt_desc).** $144.30 is the median from NAUPA's inaugural FY2019 report (when $3.14B was returned). NAUPA's FY2024 report gives a median claim of **$100.00** alongside the $2,080 average. As written, the "Real claims, FY2024" card pairs an FY2019 median with FY2024 figures.
   Fix:
   - text: "But the typical claim was $100, not millions."
   - say: "But the typical claim was about one hundred dollars, not millions."
   - compare.left.value: "$100"
   - In post.ig and post.yt_desc, change "The median claim was $144.30" and "the median claim was $144.30" to "$100".
2. **WRONG SOURCE: FTC quote (line 8 scene.source "FTC consumer alert, August 2022").** Search finds this sentence in the July 9, 2026 alert. It does not tie the sentence to any August 2022 alert. Fix: source "FTC consumer alert, July 9, 2026" (or "FTC alert, July 2026").
3. **Minor wording (line 8 say).** "The F T C's version?" is a little vague out loud, and the screen says "The FTC:". Suggest saying "The F T C puts it simply: never send money or information to a stranger who promises big rewards."
4. **Minor (line 6 right.sub "a few big ones").** An average about 21 times the median does mean a skewed distribution, so this is interpretive but fair. Optional: "skewed by big claims".
5. **Note on the average.** The NAUPA release describes the average and median as claims "paid" in FY2024; one excerpt attributes them to claims paid through missingmoney.com. "Real claims" is fine. Do not say "every state paid this".
