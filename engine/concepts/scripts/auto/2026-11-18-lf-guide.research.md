# Research brief: How to pick investments in your 401(k) (The Guide, posts Nov 18, 2026)

Research date: Oct 1-2, 2026. Education, not advice: describe options, costs and rules; never recommend funds.

## Hook / surprising fact
- Vanguard How America Saves 2026 (25th edition, June 2026; data as of year-end 2025, ~5 million participants in Vanguard-administered plans): 69% of participants were in a professionally managed allocation (record, up from 67%); 61% were invested in a single target-date fund; 1% a balanced fund; 7% a managed account. 96% of plans offer target-date funds. Nearly 2 of every 3 contribution dollars went to target-date funds.
- Hook: "61% of Vanguard 401(k) savers own exactly one fund." (a single target-date fund, year-end 2025)
- Other HAS 2026 data (year-end 2025): 61% of Vanguard plans had automatic enrollment; average balance $167,970, median $44,115; 79% of contribution dollars in equities.

## 2026 IRS limits (IRS Notice 2025-67; IRS "Retirement topics" pages)
- Employee elective deferral (402(g)): $24,500 (2025: $23,500).
- Catch-up age 50+: $8,000 (total $32,500).
- Ages 60-63 (turning 60-63 in the calendar year), SECURE 2.0 "super catch-up": $11,250 (total $35,750), if plan allows.
- Total annual additions (415(c), employee + employer): $72,000 (or 100% of comp).
- Roth catch-up rule (SECURE 2.0 sec. 603; final regs Sept 2025; threshold IRS Notice 2025-67): starting Jan 1, 2026, workers whose prior-year (2025) FICA wages from that employer exceeded $150,000 must make catch-up contributions as Roth (after-tax). Applies only to the catch-up portion.

## Fees
- ICI, The Economics of Providing 401(k) Plans: Services, Fees, and Expenses, 2024 (July 2025): 401(k) participants in equity mutual funds paid an average (asset-weighted) expense ratio of 0.26% in 2024, down from 0.76% in 2000 (a 66% decline); industrywide equity mutual fund investors paid 0.40%.
- ICI, Trends in the Expenses and Fees of Funds, 2025 (March 2026): equity mutual funds average 0.40% in 2025; index equity mutual funds 0.05%; actively managed domestic equity mutual funds 0.64% (industry average).
- Morningstar 2026 Target-Date Landscape (data year-end 2025): asset-weighted average expense ratio of target-date mutual funds 0.27% (27 bps), down from 0.29% in 2024; target-date assets $4.8 trillion; CITs 54% of target-date assets.
- Expense ratio = annual % of your balance the fund keeps for running costs; it's taken out of the fund's returns, not billed.

## Worked example (hypothetical, exact math)
$50,000, no more contributions, 30 years, hypothetical 7% annual return before fees, compounded annually.
- Fee 0.05%: net 6.95%. 50,000 x 1.0695^30 = $375,313.
- Fee 0.75%: net 6.25%. 50,000 x 1.0625^30 = $308,204.
- Gap: $67,109. First-year fee: $25 vs $375.
- Gap by year: 0: $0; 5: $2,260; 10: $6,222; 15: $12,850; 20: $23,591; 25: $40,608; 30: $67,109.

## Fund types (descriptions)
- Target-date fund: one fund with a mix of stocks and bonds that shifts more conservative as the target year nears (the "glide path"). Two funds with same year can hold different mixes. Designed to be a whole portfolio on its own.
- Index fund: tracks a market index; usually lowest cost (ICI 0.05% avg index equity MF, 2025).
- Actively managed: managers pick securities trying to beat a benchmark; higher costs (0.64% avg active domestic equity MF, 2025).
- Stable value: conservative option common in 401(k)s; generally bonds wrapped with insurance contracts to steady value and credit interest; aims at principal preservation; can have rules on transfers.
- Company stock: concentration risk (your job and savings tied to one company). IRC 401(a)(35): plans holding publicly traded employer stock must let participants divest stock from their own deferrals at any time, and from employer contributions after 3 years of service; at least quarterly.

## Default investments (DOL)
- QDIA rule, 29 CFR 2550.404c-5 (DOL, 2007): if you don't choose, money goes to the plan's qualified default investment alternative. Types: target-date/lifecycle fund, balanced fund, managed account, and a capital-preservation option limited to the first 120 days after first contribution. Plans must give notice.
- SECURE 2.0: new 401(k)/403(b) plans set up after Dec 29, 2022 must auto-enroll starting 2025 at 3%-10%, rising 1 point a year to at least 10% (max 15%); exemptions for small (10 or fewer employees) and new businesses, church and governmental plans.

## Vesting (IRC 411; IRS)
- Your own deferrals: always 100% vested.
- Employer match can follow a schedule: 3-year cliff (0% until 3 years, then 100%) or 2-to-6-year graded (20% at 2 yrs, 40% at 3, 60% at 4, 80% at 5, 100% at 6). Many plans vest faster.

## Diversification / rebalancing (SEC Investor.gov beginners' guide)
- Diversification: spreading money across and within asset classes so one loss doesn't sink everything.
- Rebalancing: bringing a mix back to its original target after markets move; by trading or redirecting new contributions; some plans offer automatic rebalancing. Target-date funds rebalance internally.
- Holding a target-date fund plus other funds changes the overall mix; the target-date's design assumes it's the whole portfolio.

## Timely angle (neutral)
- DOL proposed rule, March 30-31, 2026: safe harbor for how plan fiduciaries select designated investment alternatives, including alternatives like private equity, crypto, real estate; follows Aug 2025 executive order; comments were due June 1, 2026. Proposal, not final (as of Oct 2026). Mention only briefly, neutrally.
