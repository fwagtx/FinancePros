# Research brief: How to file your taxes for the first time (The Guide, posts Wed Dec 2, 2026)

Researched October 3, 2026. The viewer files the TAX YEAR 2026 return in early 2027. All dollar rules below are tax year 2026 unless labeled.

## Timely angle
- First-time filers in early 2027 file without IRS Direct File: the IRS told states in a November 3, 2025 email that Direct File "will not be available in Filing Season 2026" and that "no launch date has been set for the future." No relaunch has been announced for the 2027 season (searches as of Oct 3, 2026 show none). Sources: Nextgov/FCW (Nov 2025), Federal News Network (Nov 2025), CPA Practice Advisor (Nov 5, 2025), Tax Notes (Nov 5, 2025); Treasury report cited low participation (under 0.5% of ~146M returns in 2025) and cost ($41M for its final season).
- Hook angle: a refund is not a bonus; it is mostly your own over-withheld money. Average refund through April 24, 2026: $3,268 (up 11.0% from $2,945 a year earlier). Source: IRS Filing Season Statistics, week ending April 24, 2026 (irs.gov, scraped); CNBC (Apr 24, 2026).

## Dates (tax year 2026 return)
- Filing season opening for 2027: NOT announced yet (as of Oct 3, 2026). Say "usually late January". For reference, 2026 season opened Monday, Jan 26, 2026 (IR-2026-02, Jan 8, 2026; CNBC Jan 8, 2026).
- Deadline: Thursday, April 15, 2027 (weekday confirmed; Emancipation Day is Fri Apr 16, 2027, no shift). Jackson Hewitt / multiple outlets list Tax Day for TY2026 as April 15, 2027.
- W-2 and 1099-INT due to recipients: Feb 1, 2027 (Jan 31, 2027 is a Sunday). Sources: 1099online (2026 deadlines), prior W-2 brief (IRS 2026 General Instructions for W-2/W-3).
- Extension: Form 4868 gives an automatic 6 months to FILE (to Friday, Oct 15, 2027), NOT to pay; tax owed is still due April 15. IRS Free File page: "The extension is only for filing your return." Sources: irs.gov Free File page (scraped); H&R Block; Instead.
- Penalties: failure to file 5% of unpaid tax per month, max 25%; failure to pay 0.5% per month (max 25%), plus interest. Sources: Instead/levyio (citing IRS); IRS penalty pages.

## Who must file (2026)
- Single, under 65: must file if gross income is at least $16,100 (= standard deduction). Net self-employment earnings of $400 or more: must file regardless. Sources: TaxSlayer, Jackson Hewitt ("2027" page), FreshBooks.
- Even below the threshold, filing is the only way to get withheld tax back (refund of Box 2 withholding) and to claim refundable credits like the EITC.

## 2026 numbers (IRS IR-2025-103, Oct 9, 2025; Rev. Proc. 2025-32; includes One Big Beautiful Bill Act amendments) - scraped from irs.gov, confirmed by NerdWallet, Forbes, MOAA, CRS RL34498
- Standard deduction: $16,100 single; $32,200 married filing jointly; $24,150 head of household.
- Single brackets: 10% to $12,400; 12% over $12,400; 22% over $50,400; 24% over $105,700; 32% over $201,775; 35% over $256,225; 37% over $640,600.
- EITC max 2026: $8,231 (3+ qualifying children), up from $8,046 in 2025.

## Forms
- W-2: from employer; Box 1 wages, Box 2 federal income tax withheld; state boxes 15-17.
- 1099-NEC: nonemployee (freelance/gig) pay. OBBBA raised the reporting threshold from $600 to $2,000 for payments made after Dec 31, 2025 (inflation-indexed later). Income is taxable even with no form. Sources: CPA Practice Advisor (Jul 30, 2025), Sovos, Instead.
- 1099-K: OBBBA restored $20,000 AND 200 transactions threshold (IRS FAQ news release).
- 1099-INT: issued when interest is $10 or more; all interest is taxable even under $10. Sources: 1099online, efile4biz.
- First-time e-filer signature: IRS Free File page: "If you've never filed before, you can create a new self-select PIN or put '$0' as your AGI." (irs.gov, scraped)

## Filing status
- Five statuses: single, married filing jointly, married filing separately, head of household, qualifying surviving spouse. Status is set by situation on Dec 31. Wrong filing status is one of IRS's listed common errors.

## Standard vs itemized
- Itemize only if itemized deductions (mortgage interest, state and local taxes, charitable gifts, large medical costs) exceed the $16,100 standard deduction.
- About 9 in 10 returns took the standard deduction in tax year 2022 (IRS SOI: 88.6%; Tax Policy Center ~91%). Source: Tax Policy Center briefing book; IRS SOI Bulletin.

## Timeline: e-file history (IRS FS-2011-10, June 2011, scraped from irs.gov; IRS filing stats 2026)
- 1986: pilot, 25,000 returns e-filed. 1990: nationwide, 4.2 million. 2003: Free File debuts. 2005: 68.4 million (over 50%). By April 24, 2026: 139.2 million e-filed returns received (of 142.2 million total).

## Credits vs deductions (math)
- A deduction lowers taxable income; worth (amount x your bracket). $1,000 deduction in the 12% bracket = $120 less tax.
- A credit lowers tax dollar for dollar. $1,000 credit = $1,000 less tax. Refundable credits (like EITC) can be paid out even beyond the tax owed.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL) - single, tax year 2026, no dependents, standard deduction
- W-2 Box 1 wages: $40,000. W-2 Box 2 federal withholding: $3,100. 1099-INT interest: $200.
- Total income: 40,000 + 200 = $40,200.
- Taxable income: 40,200 - 16,100 = $24,100.
- Tax: 10% x 12,400 = $1,240; 12% x (24,100 - 12,400 = 11,700) = $1,404; total $2,644.
- Effective rate on total income: 2,644 / 40,200 = 6.58% (about 6.6%); top bracket is 12%.
- Interest effect: the $200 of interest adds 12% x 200 = $24 of tax (wages-only tax would be $2,620).
- Withholding 3,100 - tax 2,644 = refund $456.
- Variant: if Box 2 withholding were $2,400: 2,644 - 2,400 = $244 balance due by April 15, 2027.
- (Simplified: ignores credits and state tax. Actual 1040 uses IRS Tax Table for income under $100,000, which can differ by a few dollars from the exact bracket math.)

## How to file (options and trade-offs, neutral)
- IRS Free File guided software: for AGI of $89,000 or less in the 2026 season (TY2025), 8 partner companies; each partner sets own eligibility; some offer free state returns; must start at IRS.gov/freefile. The 2027 season AGI limit is not announced yet. IRS-Free File Inc. agreement runs through October 2029 (IR-2024-145, May 2024; CPA Practice Advisor). Free File opened Jan 9, 2026 for the 2026 season.
- Free File Fillable Forms: any income, no guidance, no state filing.
- VITA: free volunteer help, generally for people earning $69,000 or less (IRS/Taxpayer Advocate; county VITA pages for 2026 season).
- Paid software or paid preparer: costs money; trade-off is guidance/support. (No specific product named.)
- Direct File: discontinued; not available in the 2026 season; no future launch date set (IRS email, Nov 3, 2025).
- Refunds: IRS issues most e-filed, direct-deposit refunds in under 21 days; refund status about 24 hours after e-filing; IRS phasing out paper refund checks (executive order; IR-2026-02). EITC/ACTC refunds held until mid-February by law.

## State returns
- Most states with an income tax need a separate state return. Nine states have no tax on wages in 2026: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming (Tax Foundation 2026; Kiplinger). Washington has a capital gains tax; a separate high-earner income tax is scheduled for 2028.

## Records
- IRS: keep records 3 years generally; 6 years if you underreport income by more than 25%; 7 years for worthless securities/bad debt claims. (irs.gov "How long should I keep records?", scraped)

## Common mistakes (IRS lists, Kiplinger, CNBC, Forbes Apr 2026)
- Wrong/missing Social Security number or name mismatch; wrong filing status; math errors (about 2.7 million returns had math errors in 2024 per IRS via Kiplinger); wrong bank account/routing number; unsigned return; missing a form (forgotten 1099); filing before all forms arrive; confusing extension to file with extension to pay.

## Surprising facts
- A refund is your own overpaid money; a $3,268 average refund is about $272 a month over-withheld.  (3,268 / 12 = 272.33)
- 2025 Direct File users can't use it anymore.
- First-time e-filers sign with "$0" as last year's AGI or a new PIN.
