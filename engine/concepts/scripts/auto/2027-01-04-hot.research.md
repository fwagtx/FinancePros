# Research brief: What happens if you win a car on a game show? (Hot Take, posts Jan 4, 2027)
Format: Money Myth. Myth: "A game show car is free." Fact: it is taxable income, and you can usually decline it.
No timely news angle needed (evergreen). Researched Nov 5, 2026.

## Confirmed facts (with dates and sources)
1. Prizes are taxable income. IRS: amounts from a lucky number drawing, quiz program, contest etc. generally must be included in income; noncash prizes at fair market value (FMV). Treas. Reg. 1.74-1: prizes and awards are included in gross income unless an exclusion applies. [IRS ITA "Prizes and Awards"; IRS Pub 525; 26 CFR 1.74-1]
2. Refusing a prize: IRS Pub 525 / IRS help page: "If you refuse to accept a prize, don't include its value in your income." Refusal must be complete and timely (secondary sources).
3. Reporting: the payer reports prizes in Form 1099-MISC box 3. Threshold was $600; the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, sec. 70433) raised it to $2,000 for payments made after Dec 31, 2025 (inflation-indexed after 2026). A car is far above either threshold. Income is taxable even with no form. [Sovos; CPA Practice Advisor; JD Supra, 2025]
4. 2026 federal brackets (Rev. Proc. 2025-32): rates unchanged at 10/12/22/24/32/35/37%; single filer 22% bracket = taxable income about $50,401 to $105,700 for 2026. [CBS News, Money.com, Oct 2025]
5. Oprah, Sept 13, 2004: 276 audience members each got a Pontiac G6, sticker price $28,500. CNN/Money (Sept 22, 2004): value counted as income; winners must pay up to $7,000 or forfeit the car. Pontiac covered most local charges incl. state sales tax and licensing fees, not income tax. Jalopnik later: about $6,000 for some. [CNN/Money 2004; History.com; Jalopnik]
6. The Price Is Right (tapes in California): winners report paying California sales tax on prizes plus federal and state income tax on retail value; they can forfeit prizes; cannot swap a car for cash. [Mental Floss; ABC News 2012/2013; Jalopnik]
7. Other costs of owning a won car: registration, title fees, insurance (general; vary by state).
8. FMV can be disputed if documented as lower than the sponsor's figure (secondary source; mention only as "value is usually the retail value").

## Worked example (our math)
Car worth $30,000, winner in a 22% federal bracket (whole prize taxed in that bracket):
$30,000 x 0.22 = $6,600 federal income tax. Plus state income tax where applicable.
(If the prize pushes income into the 24% bracket, part is taxed at 24%.)
Oprah check: $28,500 x 0.22 = $6,270; x 0.25 (2004 bracket) = $7,125, consistent with "up to $7,000".

## Surprising fact
You can owe thousands in cash for a "free" car, before you ever drive it, and turning the prize down is the legal way to owe nothing.
