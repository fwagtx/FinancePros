# Research brief: How stand-up comedy clubs make money on drink minimums
Slot: 2026-10-09-business (How They Make Money). Researched Sept 30, 2026.

## News angle
No specific breaking news this week; evergreen explainer. (Comedy clubs are mostly private; no 10-K reports the drink-minimum economics directly, so the model is explained with official club policies, BLS data, trade benchmarks and a clearly labeled hypothetical example.)

## Confirmed facts (official club policies, checked on club websites via search, Sept 2026)
- The Comedy Store (West Hollywood), official Information page: "2 item minimum per person"; "It's how we pay the bills"; non-alcoholic drinks, water, coffee and food count; per person, not per table. [thecomedystore.com/information]
- Laugh Factory, official FAQ: each guest must buy at least two food or beverage menu items; drinks can be alcoholic or non-alcoholic; the club says the policy helps it pay comics and keep ticket prices low. [laughfactory.com/hollywood/faqs]
- Helium Comedy Club, official FAQ: two-item-per-person minimum in the showroom, any food or drink; a $10 charge ($5 per item) if a guest orders nothing. [philadelphia.heliumcomedy.com/pages/faq-2021]
- Comedy Cellar (NYC): cover charge per person plus two-item minimum, paid after the show (reservations page and visitor reviews via search).
- Trade/press: MEL Magazine "The Lubricated Economics of Two-Drink Minimums": ticket money largely goes to comics (some comics get 100% or 90% of ticket revenue, some a flat fee); drink minimum pays rent, staff, overhead. Two-drink minimum is standard in NYC and across the US.

## Bar margins (trade benchmarks, multiple trade sources agree)
- Healthy total bar pour cost is about 18%-24% of the drink price; liquor 15-20%, draft beer 20-25% (BarSociety, Backbar, Sculpture Hospitality). So roughly 20 cents of each drink dollar is the cost of the drink itself.

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, labeled "Example club")
- 200 seats, $20 ticket: 200 x $20 = $4,000 in tickets.
- Example deal: comedians get 70% of the door: 0.70 x 4,000 = $2,800; club keeps $1,200.
- Two-item minimum at $10 per item: 200 x 2 x $10 = $4,000 in drinks.
- Pour cost 20% (trade benchmark): 0.20 x 4,000 = $800; club keeps $3,200 after drink cost.
- So in this example the club keeps $3,200 from drinks versus $1,200 from tickets (before rent, staff, etc.).

## Surprising fact
The ticket often mostly pays the comedians; the club's own business is the bar. Water and soda count toward the minimum at clubs like The Comedy Store and Laugh Factory.
