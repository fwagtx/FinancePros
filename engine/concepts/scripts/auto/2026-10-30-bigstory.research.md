## Context
- Refiners turn crude into gasoline, diesel and jet fuel; their profit is the "crack spread" margin between crude cost and fuel prices. When refinery supply is knocked out (strikes on Russian refineries, Middle East conflict), fuel prices can rise faster than crude, so refining profits jump even while oil-production profit falls (Exxon Upstream fell this quarter on lower crude prices).
- Normally refining is the smaller, lower-margin business for integrated majors.

## What it means for a normal person
- Diesel moves trucks, trains and farm equipment, so higher diesel costs can feed into shipping and grocery prices.
- The same supply squeeze that raises fuel prices is what boosted refiner profits this quarter.

## What to watch
- Weekly EIA diesel prices; whether refining margins stay high if supply disruptions ease (analysts flag this). No predictions made.
