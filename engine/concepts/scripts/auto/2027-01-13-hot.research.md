# 2027-01-13-hot research brief: Face-off: side hustle vs overtime (the tax side)
Researched Saturday, November 14, 2026. Posts Wednesday, January 13, 2027, 6 PM CT. Evergreen (no news peg); every number dated.
Format: Face-off (A vs B). Education only: no advice on which to choose. Neutral on politics (describe what the 2025 law does, no opinion).

Angle: the same extra $300 is taxed differently. Overtime (W-2) pays the employee half of payroll tax (7.65%); side-hustle profit pays
self-employment tax (15.3% on 92.35% of net earnings), nearly double. But "no tax on overtime" only covers the premium half and only
federal income tax, while a side hustle is taxed on profit after business expenses. Neither is simply "better".

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. FLSA overtime (US Dept. of Labor, dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa, overtime pay pages): covered nonexempt employees must get overtime of at
   least 1.5x their regular rate of pay for hours over 40 in a workweek. Each workweek stands alone; no double-time or weekend rule federally.
   Some states have their own overtime laws.
2. "No tax on overtime" deduction (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025; new IRC section 225; IRS "What to know about the No Tax on
   Overtime deduction" and IRS/Treasury guidance on tax year 2025; Fidelity, H&R Block, Keeper):
   - Deducts only "qualified overtime compensation": the FLSA-required PREMIUM portion (the "half" in time-and-a-half). Example from
     IRS-cited explainers: worker at $20/hr paid $30/hr overtime -> the $10/hr premium counts, not the full $30.
   - Cap: $12,500 per year ($25,000 married filing jointly). Tax years 2025 through 2028.
   - Phase-out: reduced by $100 for each $1,000 of modified AGI above $150,000 ($300,000 joint); fully gone at $275,000 single at the cap
     (secondary sources citing section 225(b)(2); Keeper, TaxAct, Baker Tilly).
   - Available whether you itemize or take the standard deduction (claimed on Schedule 1-A).
   - It is a federal INCOME tax deduction only. Overtime is still subject to Social Security and Medicare (payroll) taxes, and employers
     still withhold. State income tax treatment varies: many states start from federal AGI (computed before this deduction) and do not
     pass it through; a few conform (state counts differ by source; say "depends on your state").
   - Starting tax year 2026, employers report qualified overtime on Form W-2, Box 12, code TT.
3. Employee payroll tax (IRS Topic 751): Social Security 6.2% + Medicare 1.45% = 7.65% employee share; employer pays a matching 7.65%.
   Social Security wage base for 2026: $184,500 (SSA, Journal of Accountancy Oct 2025). Additional 0.9% Medicare above $200,000 (employee only).
4. Self-employment tax (IRS Topic 554; Schedule SE): 15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare), generally on 92.35% of net SE earnings;
   applies when net SE earnings are $400 or more. Half of SE tax is deductible in figuring AGI. The SS part stops at the $184,500 (2026) base,
   combined with W-2 wages. Reason it is about double: an employee pays half and the employer pays the other half; self-employed pays both.
5. Business expenses: a side hustle reports on Schedule C and is taxed on net PROFIT after ordinary and necessary business expenses
   (supplies, business share of phone, business mileage, platform fees). IRS 2026 standard mileage rate: 72.5 cents/mile from Jan 1, 2026
   (IR-2025-128); secondary sources report a mid-year raise to 76 cents from July 1, 2026 (Announcement 2026-11) -- NOT used on screen.
   (Also: the 20% qualified business income deduction may further reduce income tax on side-hustle profit; not used in the script.)
6. Form 1099-K (IRS IR-2025-107 / Fact Sheet 2025-08, Oct 23, 2025; OBBBA section 70432): payment apps/marketplaces must send a 1099-K only
   when payments exceed $20,000 AND more than 200 transactions (reverted from the planned $600; applies for 2025 and 2026). IRS FAQ: the
   threshold does not change what is taxable; all income is taxable unless the law says otherwise, even if you get no 1099-K.
7. Estimated taxes (IRS Form 1040-ES 2026; IRS estimated tax FAQ; Taxpayer Advocate Service 2026 calendar): generally required if you
   expect to owe at least $1,000 for the year after withholding and refundable credits. 2026 due dates: April 15, June 15, September 15,
   2026 and January 15, 2027 (two days after this video posts). Alternative: more W-2 withholding can cover side-hustle tax.
8. Side-hustle earnings, Bankrate survey (press release July 9, 2025; YouGov, June 2-4, 2025; 2,616 US adults, 699 with a side hustle):
   27% of US adults have a side hustle (down from 36% in 2024 and 39% in 2023; lowest since 2017's 19%). Average side-hustle income
   $885/month, median $200/month. 25% of side hustlers earn more than $500/month; 14% more than $1,000.

## Worked example (OUR MATH; single filer, same $300 extra, tax year 2026, ignores income tax brackets and state tax)
Overtime: $20/hr base, 10 overtime hours at 1.5x = $30/hr -> 10 x $30 = $300 gross.
  Employee payroll tax: $300 x 7.65% = $22.95.
  Qualified overtime premium (deductible from federal taxable income): 10 x ($30 - $20) = $100. The other $200 stays taxable.
Side hustle: $300 net profit (after expenses).
  SE tax: $300 x 0.9235 = $277.05; $277.05 x 15.3% = $42.39 (42.38865).
  Ratio: $42.39 / $22.95 = 1.847 -> "nearly double" / "1.85x".
  Half of SE tax ($21.19) is deductible from income.
Assumes W-2 wages below the $184,500 Social Security wage base and side-hustle net SE earnings of $400+ for the year (SE tax applies to the
whole year's net earnings once $400 is reached; $300 here is one slice of that).

## Surprising fact
"No tax on overtime" is not no tax: it skips federal income tax on the extra half only, and payroll tax still comes out of every overtime
dollar. And the same dollar of side-hustle profit pays nearly double the payroll-type tax, because no employer pays half.
