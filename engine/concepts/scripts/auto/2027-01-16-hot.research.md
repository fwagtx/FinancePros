# Research brief: Scam alert - "make money online" course upsell funnels
Prepared Nov 17, 2026 for a Jan 16, 2027 post (evergreen; every number dated).
Note: ftc.gov could not be fetched directly from this session (network block); facts below were confirmed via web search results quoting FTC press releases, complaints and orders, plus secondary coverage. Allegations vs. outcomes are labeled.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. FTC Consumer Sentinel, calendar 2024 (released March 2025): reported losses in the "business and job opportunities" category = $750.6 million, up nearly $250 million from 2023. Second-largest loss category in that grouping. Total reported fraud losses in 2024 = $12.5 billion. Median loss in this category: $2,250 (2024), $2,137 (2023).
2. FTC, 2025 data: total reported fraud losses about $15.9 billion (record; ~27% above 2024). A 2025 category figure for business & job opportunities was NOT found - do not use one.
3. Job/employment-agency scam subcategory (FTC): losses grew from $90M (2020) to $501M (2024); reports nearly tripled.
4. The funnel pattern (FTC complaints): low-priced entry product -> pricier program -> telemarketer/"coach" calls pushing costly add-ons ("upsells"). The FTC's Apply Knowledge complaint describes a phase where defendants "direct and urge consumers to purchase costly add-on business services, known as 'upsells'."
5. FTC v. Growth Cave (C.D. Cal., filed Feb 2025; announced March 2025): FTC ALLEGED the operation sold its "Knowledge Business Accelerator" via YouTube ads with false income claims, and upsold a "done-for-you" service ("Digital Freedom Mastermind") for an additional $30,000 to $50,000. Complaint alleges most purchasers, including those who bought the upsell, did not make a single sale; about $50 million in alleged consumer harm. Outcome: January 2026 FTC announced settlement orders permanently banning the defendants (including both co-CEOs) from marketing/selling business opportunities and credit repair; co-CEOs must liquidate assets for redress. (Settlement, not a trial verdict.)
6. FTC v. Lurn (Maryland online business coaching seller; settlement Sept 2023): FTC ALLEGED unfounded income claims; telemarketers pitched buyers on "coaching"/consulting programs costing up to $10,000; complaint alleges more than $65 million taken from consumers 2019-May 2022. Settlement: $2.5 million to FTC for refunds; $14,077,121 judgment partially suspended for inability to pay. FTC says Lurn continued after receiving an Oct 2021 Notice of Penalty Offenses. FTC later sent refunds (over $2.4 million reported).
7. Notice of Penalty Offenses on money-making opportunities (October 2021): sent to more than 1,100 businesses; civil penalties then up to $43,792 per violation (amount inflation-adjusted yearly). Example enforcement: DK Automation $2.6 million penalty for continuing deceptive earnings claims after notices. (Note: the ~670-company notice of April 2023 is about product-claims substantiation, NOT money-making - do not conflate.)
8. FTC Business Opportunity Rule (16 CFR Part 437): covered sellers must give a one-page disclosure document at least 7 calendar days before the buyer signs or pays; any earnings claim needs a separate "Earnings Claim Statement Required by Law". Caveat: many pure coaching/course sellers may fall outside the Rule's definition, so the script does not lean on it.
9. FTC Fiscal Year 2025: 40 law enforcement actions involving fraudulent schemes such as business opportunities, investment or other money-making schemes (FTC JEC testimony, 2026).
10. FTC consumer red flags: pressure to buy now; promises/guarantees of big income; "no experience needed"; after you pay, more calls pushing you to pay for another level; ask for proof of typical earnings; report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

## Chartable data series (real)
FTC job scam losses (Data Spotlight Dec 2024 + March 2025 release): 2020 $90M; 2021 $131M; 2022 $179M; 2023 $286M; 2024 $501M (full year).
Caveat: this is job scams, not course funnels; the script uses stat/list/split instead of a chart to stay on topic. No year-by-year series for the business-opportunity/coaching slice alone was confirmable.

## Worked example (our math, hypothetical funnel, labeled as such)
$27 intro product + $497 course + $9,997 coaching = $10,521. Last step / first step = 9,997 / 27 = about 370x. (Not used on screen; illustrates why the cheap front end is the bait.)
Real-case version: Growth Cave alleged upsell $30,000-$50,000 vs. 2024 median category loss $2,250 -> $30,000 / $2,250 = 13.3x the median reported loss.

## Surprising fact
FTC alleged that in Growth Cave most buyers - even those who paid tens of thousands for the upsell - never made a single sale.

## Hook
"That cheap make-money-online course is the bait, not the product." (paid off by funnel explanation + FTC case)
