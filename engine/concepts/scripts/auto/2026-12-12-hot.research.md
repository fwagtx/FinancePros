# Research brief: Why are cancer drugs so expensive? (2026-12-12-hot)

Posts Saturday, December 12, 2026. Evergreen: every figure dated. Education only, no medical advice, neutral on policy.

## Hook angle
Ibrance (palbociclib, Pfizer, breast cancer) had a 2024 list price of $15,741 for a 30-day supply (CMS, Nov 2025 negotiated-price release). Medicare's negotiated price, effective Jan 1, 2027: $7,871 per 30-day supply (50% lower). The surprising fact: one month of one pill lists for more than many people's annual rent increase; and Medicare's negotiated price cuts it in half.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. **Global spending on cancer medicines (IQVIA, list/invoice prices), chartable series:**
   - 2020: $164B (IQVIA Global Oncology Trends 2021 press release, June 2021)
   - 2021: $185B (IQVIA Global Oncology Trends 2022, June 2022)
   - 2022: $196B (IQVIA Global Oncology Trends 2023, May 2023)
   - 2023: $223B (IQVIA Global Oncology Trends 2024, June 2024)
   - 2024: $252B (IQVIA Global Oncology Trends 2025, ~June 2025); projected $441B by 2029.
   Caveat: each year comes from the edition that first reported it; later editions may restate. No 2026 edition (2025 data) found as of Oct 2026.
   - US was $99B (45% of global) in 2023 (IQVIA 2024).
2. **Launch prices:** Median annual cost of new oncology medicines launched in 2022 was $260,000, up from $63,534 ten years earlier; 44% of launches in the prior five years cost >$200,000/yr (IQVIA Global Oncology Trends 2023, via Managed Healthcare Executive, June 2023). Could not confirm a 2023 or 2024 launch median.
3. **R&D cost estimates (range, not a single number):**
   - Wouters, McKee, Luyten, JAMA, March 2020: median $985M per new drug, mean $1.3B (63 drugs approved 2009-2018, public SEC data, skewed toward smaller firms). Median for antineoplastic/immunomodulating agents: $2,771.6M (~$2.8B), highest of any area.
   - DiMasi et al. (Tufts CSDD), J Health Econ, 2016: $2.558B (~$2.6B) per approved drug, capitalized, 2013 dollars, all therapeutic areas; $2.87B including post-approval R&D.
4. **Failure rates:** BIO / Informa Pharma Intelligence / QLS, "Clinical Development Success Rates 2011-2020" (Feb 2021): likelihood of approval from Phase I = 5.3% for oncology vs 9.3% non-oncology (7.9% overall in that report per most summaries; one table shows 7.5%). Earlier BIO 2006-2015 report: oncology 5.1%, lowest of 14 areas. Confirmed via secondary sources (Statista, Nature Reviews Drug Discovery table); primary PDF not reached.
5. **Patents / exclusivity:** US utility patent term is 20 years from filing date (35 U.S.C. 154); clock starts before clinical trials, so effective market life is shorter. Generic/biosimilar competition generally can't launch until patents/exclusivities expire.
6. **US vs other countries:** RAND (for HHS ASPE), Feb 2024, using 2022 data: US prices for all drugs 2.78x those of 33 OECD countries; brand-name drugs 4.22x; at least 3.22x after adjusting for estimated US rebates. US unbranded generics: 67% of other countries' prices.
7. **Medicare drug price negotiation (Inflation Reduction Act):**
   - First cycle (prices effective Jan 1, 2026): includes Imbruvica (blood cancers), negotiated $9,319 per 30-day supply vs 2023 list $14,934 (-38%). NOTE: Imbruvica was in the 2026 list, NOT the 2027 list.
   - Second cycle (announced Nov 2025, effective Jan 1, 2027; 15 drugs; CMS said 44% / ~$12B savings had they applied in 2024): cancer drugs include Ibrance $7,871 vs $15,741 (-50%), Xtandi (prostate cancer) $7,004 vs $13,480 (-48%), Pomalyst (multiple myeloma) $8,650 vs $21,744 (-60%), Calquence $8,600 vs $14,228 (Calquence from secondary reporting). List prices are 2024, per 30-day supply.
8. **Part D out-of-pocket cap:** $2,000 in 2025; $2,100 in 2026; $2,400 in 2027 (CMS CY2027 Rate Announcement, April 6, 2026; reported by Becker's, AHA proposed-stage, Kiplinger). 2027 deductible $700 (from $615).

## Both sides (neutral)
- Industry view: prices fund expensive, high-failure R&D (5.3% success from Phase I; $2.6B Tufts estimate) and a limited patent window.
- Critics' view: independent estimates are lower ($985M median, Wouters 2020); US pays far more than peers for the same brand drugs (RAND 2024); prices reflect lack of competition during exclusivity.

## Worked example (labeled EXAMPLE)
12 months of Ibrance, one Medicare patient:
- At 2024 list price: $15,741 x 12 = $188,892 (about $189K)
- At 2027 negotiated price: $7,871 x 12 = $94,452 (about $94K)
- Difference: $94,440 per year.
- Patient's own Part D out-of-pocket in 2027 capped at $2,400 for the year (excluding premiums), regardless of which price.

## Surprising fact
One month of a common breast cancer pill listed at $15,741 in 2024, and Medicare's negotiated 2027 price is half that.
