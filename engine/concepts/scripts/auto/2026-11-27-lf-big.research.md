## Context / history
- "Overheating": demand (spending, hiring, investment) running faster than the economy can produce, which tends to push prices up. Growth of 4.3% is about double the 2.2% of Q2.
- The 2021-22 episode: strong demand plus supply problems pushed PCE inflation to 5% (Oct 2021) and 6% (Oct 2022); the Fed hiked fast. Inflation fell to 2.3% by Oct 2024, then rose again: 3.8% now, with energy (Iran war began Feb 28, 2026; brief of Nov 26) a big part.
- A hot economy is good news for jobs (unemployment 4.1%, payrolls +165,000) but bad news for anyone whose pay trails prices and for borrowers, since inflation pushes the Fed to raise rates.

## What it means for a normal person (education, not advice)
- Paycheck: average pay +3.1% vs prices +3.8%: the average raise lost ground.
- Spending: dollars spent rose, but what those dollars bought (real spending) slipped in October; part of the "spending boom" is higher prices.
- Borrowing: card APRs ~22.36%, record $1.29T card debt, BNPL $6.1B so far this season with payments due after the holidays; mortgage 7.21% vs 6.23% a year ago.
- Saving: saving rate rose to 4.1%; higher rates usually mean savings accounts and CDs pay more (no specific rate cited).
- Winners/losers: workers with jobs, savers, people with fixed-rate loans locked earlier; vs. borrowers on variable rates, new homebuyers, people whose pay lags prices.
