# Research brief: 2026-10-06-hot — Money Myth: "Carrying a credit card balance builds credit"

Posts Tuesday, October 6, 2026 (6 PM Central). Research done September 30, 2026. Evergreen wording: every number carries its date.

## The myth and the fact
- MYTH: you need to carry (revolve) a credit card balance and pay interest to build credit.
- FACT (FICO, the company that makes the score): FICO calls this a myth. FICO Scores "don't take into account if an individual is carrying a balance from month to month"; carrying a balance "will not increase your FICO Scores" and will likely cost money in interest. You can pay in full and still show a low-but-not-zero utilization. (myFICO blog "Myth Busting – You Don't Need to Carry Credit Card Balances to Improve Your FICO Scores"; FICO press release via Business Wire, May 9, 2023.)
- CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau), "Credit score myths" blog: paying the balance in full keeps utilization low; carrying a balance is not required.
- How reporting works (myFICO): issuers generally report account info to the credit bureaus once a month, on the statement closing date. So the statement balance shows up as "usage" even if you then pay it in full by the due date and pay no interest.

## Surprising fact
- In a February 2023 U.S. News & World Report survey, 48% of people (just over 48%) believed carrying a credit card balance increases your credit score. (U.S. News, "Survey: Over 48% Say Carrying a Credit Card Balance Increases Your Credit Score"; also cited by myFICO.)

## What actually builds a FICO Score (myFICO "What's in your credit score")
- Payment history 35%, amounts owed 30% (includes utilization), length of history 15%, new credit 10%, credit mix 10%.
- Interest paid is not a factor.

## Cost of the myth (latest official number)
- Fed G.19 Consumer Credit: average interest rate on credit card accounts assessed interest (people who carry a balance) = 22.15% in Q2 2026 (May 2026 billing cycle), up from 21.52% in Q1 2026; 22.25% in Q2 2025. All-accounts average APR was 20.94% in Q2 2026. (Federal Reserve G.19; reported by The Money Overview and LendingTree.) This is the latest available quarter as of September 30, 2026 (Q3 2026 comes with the November release).
- Definition (Fed): rate for accounts assessed interest = annualized ratio of total finance charges to average daily balances on which finance charges were assessed.

## Timely angle
- September 16, 2026: the Fed raised the federal funds target range by 0.25 point to 3.75%-4.00%, its first increase since 2023 (FOMC statement Sept 16, 2026; CNBC; Charles Schwab). Most credit card APRs are variable and tied to the prime rate, so they tend to move with Fed changes.

## Worked example (exact math)
- $2,000 balance carried at 22.15% APR (Q2 2026 average for accounts assessed interest).
- One month: 2,000 x 0.2215 / 12 = $36.92 -> "about $37 a month".
- Held all year (simple, no compounding): 2,000 x 0.2215 = $443.00. With monthly compounding: about $490.86.
- Credit score benefit from that interest: zero (FICO).
