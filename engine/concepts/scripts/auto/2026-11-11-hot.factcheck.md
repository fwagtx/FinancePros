# Fact-check: 2026-11-11-hot (posts Nov 11, 2026; checked Oct 1, 2026)

## Verified claims
- **Realization rule (L3):** IRS Topic 409 says a capital gain arises when you sell a capital asset (amount realized minus adjusted basis). "Generally taxed only once it's sold" is correct.
- **Hypothetical (L4):** It is labeled "Example (hypothetical)" on screen and "Say..." in narration, so it reads as an example. The math is consistent: $1B unrealized, $1M salary.
- **Forbes 400 (L5):** 2021 $4.5T; 2022 $4.0T (down $500B, per Forbes 9/27/2022); 2023 $4.5T; 2024 $5.4T; 2025 $6.6T; 2026 $8.0T (record; list released Sept 15, 2026). The 2026 figure is "up $1.4T", and python gives 8.0 - 6.6 = 1.4. All six chart points are correct. "Record" in the IG post is correct.
- **CEA/OMB (L6):** The Sept 23, 2021 report by Leiserson and Yagan estimates that the 400 wealthiest (billionaire) families paid an *average* *federal individual* income tax rate of 8.2% over 2010-2018, counting unrealized gains as income. The number, date and years are correct. The wording on screen and in narration is imprecise (see issue 3).
- **Critics (L7):** Cato (Chris Edwards) says unrealized gains are not part of any of 11 federal income measures and that the 8% is at odds with Treasury, CBO, IRS and JCT data. That supports "wealth growth isn't income" and "higher rates under standard measures." Tax Foundation ("Latest White House Report Tells Incomplete Story...") says the main problem is that the report leaves out corporate income taxes, which supports "Skips corporate taxes." Each objection is accurate, but the narration pins the "isn't income" point on both groups (see issue 4).
- **Moore v. United States (L8):** Decided June 20, 2024, 7-2, opinion by Kavanaugh. The Court held that the income was realized by KisanKraft and expressly declined to decide whether the Constitution requires realization. "Left that open" is accurate.
- **Sub-labels:** All are 28 characters or fewer. The longest is "CEA/OMB, Sept 2021; 2010-18" at 27.
- **Timeliness:** "in 2026", "up in a year" and "June 2024" will all still be correct on Nov 11, 2026. There is no advice and no price prediction.
- **Hook (L1):** True for unrealized gains, specific, and under 3 seconds. Lines 2-3 pay it off right away, so it is fair. The cover ("A $1B Gain, $0 Taxable?") is also paid off.

## Issues
1. **L2 neutrality (substantive).** "The income tax taxes income, not wealth" and the board "Taxed: INCOME / Not taxed: WEALTH" state the critics' position as fact. Whether paper gains count as "income" or as "wealth" is the exact point in dispute: the CEA counts them as income, and Cato and the Tax Foundation say they are not. Line 7 then presents the same idea as a critics' view, so the script contradicts itself and leans to one side.
   - Fix: "Not a loophole. The income tax generally taxes realized gains, not paper gains."
   - Board: "Taxed: SOLD gains" / "Not taxed: PAPER gains".
   - Also change the IG and YT text ("The income tax taxes income, not wealth") to match.
2. **L4 wording.** "Only her $1 million salary is income" takes the same side as issue 1. Fix: "...is *taxable* income." The text and the say line should both say "taxable".
3. **L6 precision.** The 8.2% is an *estimate* of an *average federal individual income tax* rate for the *400 wealthiest* families. "Top 400 families' rate" leaves out "average" and "estimate", and "top 400" is ambiguous (it could mean top 400 by income).
   - Fix text/say: "Sept 2021: White House economists, counting paper gains as income, estimated the 400 wealthiest families' average federal income tax rate at 8.2% for 2010-2018."
   - Stat label: "Avg federal income tax rate" (27 characters).
   - Optional: say "Biden White House economists" so the source is clear.
4. **L7 attribution.** The spoken line gives "wealth growth isn't income" to both groups. The Tax Foundation's main objection was that the report leaves out corporate taxes.
   - Fix say/text: "Critics like Cato and the Tax Foundation say paper gains aren't income, and the estimate leaves out corporate taxes."
   - The list item "Standard rates: higher" is vague. Use "Official data: higher rates" (27 characters).
5. **Minor.** "So it comes down to one rule" slightly oversimplifies, because the dispute also involves measurement (corporate taxes). This is acceptable.
