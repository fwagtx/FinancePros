# Fact-check: 2026-11-12-hot (dying without a will / US intestacy)
Checked Oct 1, 2026 for posting Nov 12, 2026.

## What checks out
- **Caring.com series.** 2022: 33% (2022 study, YouGov, 2,600+ adults). 2023: 34% (2023 study, 2,400+ adults). 2024: 32% (2024 study, described as "a 6% decline from 2023"). 2025: 24% (2025 study, YouGov, 2,500+ adults; 13% trust, 56% no plan). The 2025 study itself says it is "down from 33% in 2022".
- **2023 "+3% since 2022" does not conflict.** Caring.com describes changes as relative percent changes: 33 to 34 is about +3% relative, and 34 to 32 is about -6% relative, which matches the 2024 study's "6% decline". The chart value of 33 for 2022 is correct.
- **No 2026 Caring.com edition.** None was found as of Oct 2026, so 2025 is the latest and the hook is not stale. Optionally re-check before Nov 12.
- **NY EPTL 4-1.1(a)(1).** The statute says "fifty thousand dollars and one-half of the residue to the spouse, and the balance thereof to the issue by representation." This is confirmed (nysenate.gov, Justia, FindLaw).
- **The $250,000 math is correct.** $50,000 + ($200,000 / 2) = $150,000 to the spouse, and the kids get $100,000. The compare scene is labeled "(hypothetical)".
- **Unmarried partners.** NY intestacy gives an unmarried partner no share, however long the relationship lasted. NY does not recognize common-law marriages contracted in NY.
- **UPC 2-105 ("No taker").** The intestate estate passes to the state. Confirmed.
- **UPC 2-102.** The spouse takes the entire estate if no descendant or parent survives, or if all descendants are shared with the spouse. Confirmed. This makes the list scene misleading; see fix 3.
- **Advice wording.** The script describes the law and does not instruct. "Not legal advice" is in the IG post. The state-variation caveat is present in line 4.

## Required fixes
1. **Line 3 chart title is 31 characters (max 28).**
   - Change `"title": "Adults with a will (Caring.com)"` to `"title": "Have a will (Caring.com)"` (24 characters).
2. **Line 6 has two problems.** The "partner of 20 years" reads as an unlabeled hypothetical, and the stat label is 30 characters (max 28). Reword it as a general rule, which is true regardless of how long the couple was together.
   - text: `An unmarried partner, even after 20 years? Under New York's intestacy law, $0.`
   - say: `And an unmarried partner, even after twenty years together? Under New York's intestacy law, zero dollars.`
   - scene label: `Unmarried partner (NY)` (22 characters).
3. **Line 7 UPC list is misleading.**
   - It implies that the kids share with the spouse. Under UPC 2-102 the spouse often takes everything.
   - It also skips grandparents and their descendants (aunts, uncles, cousins), who come before the state under UPC 2-103.
   - Replace the items with: `["Spouse (often all)", "Kids and grandkids", "Parents, then siblings", "Grandparents' families", "The state"]` and set `"reveal": 5`. Every item is 5 words or fewer.
   - The spoken and on-screen line can stay as written.
4. **Line 2 and the IG post overclaim with "Everyone else gets a default plan".** Surveyed people with trusts (13%), beneficiary designations, or joint accounts don't fall entirely into intestacy.
   - text: `Without one, state law has a default plan.`
   - say: `Without one, state law has a default plan.`
   - IG post: change "Everyone else gets a default plan written by state law." to "Without one, state law has a default plan."

## Recommended (not blocking)
5. **The hook takes about 4 seconds to say** because the year comes first. A punchier version:
   - text: `Only 24% of Americans surveyed had a will.`
   - say: `Only twenty-four percent of Americans surveyed had a will.`
   - The chart already dates it to 2025. If you keep the year, put it at the end ("...had a will in 2025").
   - "Surveyed Americans" is a fair description: it was a YouGov sample of US adults.
6. **Line 8:** "skip all of this" could become "usually skip all of this", because an account falls back into the estate if the named beneficiary has died.
7. **Line 7 caveat (optional):** in the say line, change "Under the Uniform Probate Code" to "Under the Uniform Probate Code, a model many states use". This keeps viewers from reading it as NY law.
