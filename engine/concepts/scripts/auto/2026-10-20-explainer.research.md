# Research brief: How the jobs report moves markets (posts Tue Oct 20, 2026)

Angle: the two summer 2026 reports show the "good news is bad news" mechanic in both directions. The September 2026 report is scheduled for Fri Oct 2, 2026 (BLS schedule) and will be out before posting, so the script does NOT quote September numbers; every report cited is labeled with its month. Shutdown risk: Congress passed a CR funding the government through Dec 11, 2026 (signed Sept 2026), so no shutdown delay is expected for the Oct 2 release.

## What the jobs report is (mechanics, evergreen)
- Official name: The Employment Situation, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (Labor Department). Usually released the first Friday of the month at 8:30 a.m. Eastern (e.g. July 2026 report on Fri Aug 7; August 2026 report on Fri Sept 4; September 2026 report scheduled Fri Oct 2, 2026, 8:30 a.m. ET).
- Two surveys: establishment (payroll) survey, about 121,000 businesses and government agencies covering about 631,000 worksites -> nonfarm payrolls, hours, earnings; household survey (CPS), about 60,000 households -> unemployment rate (BLS technical note).
- Markets react to the surprise (actual vs. economists' consensus), because prices already reflect the expected number.
- Chain: stronger-than-expected jobs -> more pressure on the Fed to keep rates high or raise them -> Treasury yields rise (2-year most sensitive) -> stocks can fall. Weaker-than-expected -> the reverse. In 2026 the debate is about hikes: the Fed raised the federal funds rate 0.25 pt to 3.75%-4.00% on Sept 16, 2026, its first hike since 2023 (CNBC, Fox Business, Sept 16, 2026).

## Worked example 1: July 2026 report (released Fri Aug 7, 2026)
- Payrolls: -23,000 first print vs. Dow Jones consensus +83,000 (CNBC; Reuters poll had +80,000). Miss = 83,000 - (-23,000) = 106,000 jobs.
- 2-year Treasury yield fell more than 5 basis points to 4.193%; 10-year down more than 3 bp to 4.639% (CNBC).
- Stocks: S&P 500 +0.62% to 7,757.64, a record close; Nasdaq +1.30%; Dow +0.28% (Reuters via Detroit News/Yahoo). Reason cited: dampened expectations of a September rate hike; CME odds of a September move fell to 44%.

## Worked example 2: August 2026 report (released Fri Sept 4, 2026)
- Payrolls +162,000 vs. consensus about 55,000 (CNBC; CNBC preview had 53,000). 162,000 / 55,000 = about 2.9x. Strongest month since March. Unemployment rate unchanged at 4.1% (BLS).
- 2-year yield rose more than 4 bp to 4.377%, highest since January 2025 intraday (CNBC).
- Stocks: S&P 500 -0.38% to 7,718.60; Dow -0.51% (-271.86 pts); Nasdaq -0.29% (Yahoo Finance / Washington Post/AP). CME odds of a September hike rose to about 58%.

## The surprising fact: the first number isn't final
- In the August 2026 report, July was revised from -23,000 to +21,000 (+44,000 swing: 21,000 - (-23,000) = 44,000); June revised from +20,000 to +31,000; combined +55,000 (BLS Employment Situation, Sept 4, 2026; Yahoo Finance).
- June 2026 was first reported as +57,000 (NBC, JEC), cut to +20,000 a month later, then +31,000.
- February 2026 first print -92,000 (CNBC, Mar 6, 2026), later -133,000 (March report). Strike at Kaiser Permanente and winter weather cited.
