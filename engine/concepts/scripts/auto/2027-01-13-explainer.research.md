# Research brief: What an IPO is (Acorn Economics: squirrel's orchard sells slices to strangers)
Evergreen, posts Wed Jan 13, 2027, noon CT. Made Nov 14, 2026. Latest full-year IPO data is calendar 2025 (Jay Ritter, IPO Statistics, dated Nov 5, 2026; Table 1 dated July 13, 2026). No dated news hook used (keeps it evergreen). Anthropic deliberately NOT used as an example (conflict of interest).

## Definition
- IPO = initial public offering: the first time a private company sells its shares to the public, after which they trade on an exchange (NYSE / Nasdaq). Company hires underwriters (investment banks) who set the offer price, sell shares to (mostly institutional) buyers at that price the night before trading, and take a fee (the gross spread). SEC / Investor.gov glossary.
- Analogy: squirrel's private orchard (company) sells slices (shares) to strangers (public) for the first time to get acorns (cash) to plant more trees. After that, strangers trade slices among themselves; the orchard only gets cash from the first sale.

## Key numbers (with dates)
- Money left on the table, 2025: $13.11 billion aggregate (Ritter Table 1: first-day closing price minus offer price, times shares offered; 90 IPOs). On screen "$13.1B"; spoken "about thirteen billion dollars".
- Average (equal-weighted) first-day return, 2025: 29.3% (Ritter Table 1). Median 13.7%. Proceeds-weighted 33.6%. 1980-2025 average: 19.0%.
- Share of 2025 IPOs with a negative first-day return: 24.4% (Ritter Table 1a) -> "about one in four closed day one below their offer price". (2023: 53.7%.)
- Number of IPOs, Ritter's strict definition (operating companies, offer price >= $5, NYSE/Nasdaq, excluding SPACs, ADRs, REITs, units, banks, penny stocks): 2025 = 90; 2021 = 311; 2022 = 38.
- Broader count (Renaissance Capital, 2025 US IPO Review): 202 IPOs raised $44.0 billion in 2025 (four-year high for deal count). Different definitions explain the gap; Ritter says "at least 352 counting all offerings" in 2025 including 144 SPACs.
- Aggregate proceeds 2025 (Ritter): $38.97 billion.
- Underwriter fee (gross spread): exactly 7% on most moderate-size US IPOs. Chen & Ritter, "The Seven Percent Solution," Journal of Finance 2000 (>90% of $20-80M deals in the late 1990s at exactly 7%); Ritter SEC IPO data appendix (2018): 96.6% of moderate-size IPOs at exactly 7%, 50.4% of large IPOs; Ritter 2026 Table 10 title: "Gross Spreads Continue to Remain at 7% on Moderate-Size Deals, 2001-2025". Large deals often pay less.
- Lockup: insiders typically barred from selling for 180 days after the IPO (Investor.gov glossary "Initial public offerings, lockup agreements"; SEC "Lockup agreements" Q&A). Not required by law; terms vary and are disclosed in the prospectus; underwriters can release early.

## Worked example (our math, hypothetical orchard)
- Orchard sells 2,000,000 slices at a $20 offer price: 2,000,000 x $20 = $40,000,000 raised (a moderate-size deal, where 7% is the norm).
- Bankers' 7% gross spread: $40,000,000 x 0.07 = $2,800,000. Orchard nets $40,000,000 - $2,800,000 = $37,200,000.
- Day one, slices close at $26: pop = (26 - 20) / 20 = 30% (close to 2025's 29.3% average).
- Money left on the table = ($26 - $20) x 2,000,000 = $12,000,000, gained (on paper) by the first buyers who got shares at $20, not by the orchard.

## The surprising fact
- The "pop" everyone celebrates is money the company did NOT get: in 2025, about $13.1 billion went to buyers who got shares at the offer price (Ritter). And pops are not guaranteed: 24.4% of 2025 IPOs closed day one below the offer price. Renaissance Capital: 2025 IPOs overall returned about 2% from offer price by year-end (not used on screen).

## Compliance notes
- Education only; no "buy IPOs", no predictions. Note ordinary investors usually buy after trading starts, at market price, not at the offer price (general knowledge; not stated as a number).
