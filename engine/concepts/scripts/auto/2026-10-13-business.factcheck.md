# Fact-check: 2026-10-13-business ("How Florists Price Valentine's Roses")

Checked on 2026-09-30 for posting on 2026-10-13. I read only the raw script JSON and redid all the math in python.

## Verified claims
- **L2 chart:** FinanceBuzz's national average for a dozen long-stem red roses was $80.16 in 2023, $88.61 in 2024, $90.50 in 2025 and $93.07 in 2026. All four chart points match. FinanceBuzz priced a standard dozen long-stem red roses from at least 3 florists per state. (FinanceBuzz; CBS News; National Today, 2026-02-09)
- **L3 "+16% since 2023":** 93.07 / 80.16 = +16.1%, a rise of $12.91. FinanceBuzz also reports 16.1%. Correct.
- **L4 board (flowers x3.5, hard goods x2.5, +25% labor):** Florists' Review ("Pricing For Profit") calls this a common industry rule of thumb. "Florists often" is fair wording.
- **L5–L7 math:** 12 x $1 x 3.5 = $42. 12 x $2 x 3.5 = $84. The difference is $42. Correct.
- **L8 Colombia:** Asocolflores projected about US$2.4B in 2026 exports, with US$480M from the Valentine's season. 480 / 2400 = 20%. It was framed as "expected", which is right. (El Colombiano, Teleantioquia, Portafolio.) La República cites about 18% as a typical-year share, so "about 20%" for 2026 is fine.
- **On-screen vs spoken:** every value matches: $93, $93.07, 16%, 3.5, $12, $42, $2, $84, 20%.
- **Other checks:** no quotes, allegations, politics, advice or price predictions. The cover and title ("Why Roses Cost About $93") are paid off. The post text numbers match the script.

## Issues and fixes
1. **L9 board "Wholesale spike / x 3.5 markup / = the $93 dozen" (math mismatch, medium).** The script's own example gives $84 from flowers alone. Adding the vase and the 25% labor charge would push it above $105, not to $93. The board shows an equation that the example does not produce.
   - Fix: change the last board line to "= the pricey dozen", or "= a $90+ dozen".
2. **L3 "That's up 16% since 2023, and the answer is one multiplier" (unsupported causal link, medium).** This reads as if the markup explains the rise since 2023. No source ties the 2023–2026 increase to wholesale changes.
   - Fix: "That's up 16% since 2023. The price comes down to one multiplier."
3. **L6–L7 hypotheticals (labeling, minor).** The screen labels "$1/stem example" and "$2/stem example", which is good. The spoken line "So a one-dollar bump at wholesale adds forty-two dollars per dozen" states it as a general rule. It also leaves out the 25% labor charge that L4 mentions.
   - Fix: say "In our example, a one-dollar bump at wholesale adds forty-two dollars in flowers alone."
4. **Timing and staleness (minor).** The piece posts on Oct 13, eight months after Valentine's Day, using February 2026 data. The past tense ("did cost", "expected") keeps every statement true, and no time word will be wrong on the posting date. The topic is out of season, but that is not a factual problem.
5. **Opening line (OK, could be stronger).** "Why did a dozen Valentine's roses cost about $93?" is true and specific, and it takes about 2.5 seconds to say. In October it creates less urgency.
   - Stronger option: "A dozen Valentine's roses averaged ninety-three dollars. Here's the multiplier behind it."
   - Or lead with the example: "Twelve dollars of roses can become forty-two at the counter."
6. **Narration (minor).** "Same math, eighty-four dollars" reads fine. No awkward lines.
