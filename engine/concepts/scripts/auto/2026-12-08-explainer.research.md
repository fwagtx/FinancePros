# 2026-12-08-explainer research brief: How life insurance amounts are calculated (income-replacement math)

Evergreen (posts Tue Dec 8, 2026). No news hook needed. Latest survey data: 2026 Insurance Barometer Study (LIMRA + Life Happens, fielded January 2026). This is the newest edition; the next one is normally released in spring, so it is still current on Dec 8, 2026.

Framing: education only. Describe the methods planners and insurers commonly use; never tell viewers what to buy or how much.

## Method 1: income multiple (rule of thumb)
- Common rule of thumb: coverage of about 10 times annual pre-tax income (military.com: "have 10 times your income"); ranges quoted by others: 5-10x (Haven Life), 7-10x as an older agent rule (Kiplinger).
- Weaknesses (Kiplinger, Haven Life): ignores specific debts, number/age of dependents, partner's income, existing savings; gives zero for a stay-at-home parent even though childcare etc. would cost money.
- Employer group life is often 1-2x salary (Haven Life).

## Method 2: DIME
- D = Debt (non-mortgage debts: cards, car, student loans; some versions add final expenses), I = Income (annual income x number of years the family would need it; ~10 years is a common choice), M = Mortgage payoff balance, E = Education (estimated future college costs). Sum = estimate. (OneDigital and multiple calculator guides.)
- Basic DIME does not subtract existing coverage or savings; many versions do.

## Method 3: needs-based (capital needs)
- Needs = debts + years of income replacement + future costs (mortgage, college, final expenses) MINUS existing assets (savings, existing policies incl. work coverage). The difference is the "coverage gap." (OneDigital; standard planner approach.)

## Worked example (HYPOTHETICAL, my numbers, clearly labelled)
- Earner makes $60,000/yr.
- 10x rule: 10 x $60,000 = $600,000.
- DIME: Debt $20,000 + Income 10 yrs x $60,000 = $600,000 + Mortgage $250,000 + Education $100,000 = $970,000.
  Check: 20,000 + 600,000 + 250,000 + 100,000 = 970,000.
- Gap between the two methods: $970,000 - $600,000 = $370,000.
- Needs-based (subtract existing assets): savings $50,000 + workplace policy $60,000 (1x salary) = $110,000. $970,000 - $110,000 = $860,000.
- Assumptions stated: 10 years of income, no inflation/investment return adjustment, illustrative only.

## Real data: 2026 Insurance Barometer Study (LIMRA & Life Happens)
- Survey of 5,237 adult financial decision-makers, ages 18-75, fielded January 2026, margin of error +/-3 pts (LIMRA 2026 member PR toolkit).
- 52% of US adults own life insurance (individual and/or group), up 1 point from 2025.
- Need gap: 38% of adults say they need life insurance or need more = about 92 million adults (LIMRA toolkit; LOMA MarketFacts Sept 2026 "92 Million Reasons to Talk About Life Insurance").
- Split: 29% non-owners who say they need it (~70M) + 9% owners who want more (~22M) (toolkit; LIMRA conference slides show slightly different rounded counts).
- Only 29% could stay financially secure more than two years if a primary wage earner died (toolkit).
- Surprising fact: adults under 31 overestimate the cost of a term policy by roughly 5-6x; about three-quarters of consumers overestimate cost (toolkit/LOMA).

## Chartable series: share of US adults saying they need (more) life insurance, Insurance Barometer
- 2023: 41% (InsuranceNewsNet summary of 2023 Barometer: "41% ... say they don't have sufficient coverage")
- 2024: 42%, ~102 million adults, record high (LIMRA news release 2024)
- 2025: 40%, ~100 million (2025 Barometer summaries)
- 2026: 38%, ~92 million (2026 toolkit / LOMA)
- Caveat: 2023 figure from secondary summary; some sources show 2024 as 41%. Safer to use 2024-2026 as a compare or stat.
