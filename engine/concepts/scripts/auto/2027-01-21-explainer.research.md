## Definition (what a split is)
- A stock split is a board decision that increases the number of shares outstanding by issuing new shares in a set proportion; shares outstanding rise but market capitalization (shares x price) stays the same at the moment of the split. (FINRA "Stock Splits" investor page, via search excerpt)
- A split lowers the price of each share without changing the company's market cap or fundamentals. (Charles Schwab; Morningstar worked example: 500,000 shares at $100, 2-for-1 -> 1,000,000 at $50, still $50 million)
- Reverse split: the mirror image. Shareholders swap several shares for one; fewer shares, higher price per share, same total value. Often used by small companies that think their price is too low (FINRA; Nasdaq; Robinhood Learn). Investor.gov has glossary entries for both "stock splits" and "reverse stock split" (page text not retrievable here).
- Pizza/pie analogy is a common teaching device (Britannica Money): more slices, same pizza. Caveat: the company can grow or shrink after the split; the split itself does not change value.

## Chartable real series: shares from 1 Apple share bought at the 1980 IPO
Apple's five splits: 2-for-1 June 16, 1987; 2-for-1 June 21, 2000; 2-for-1 Feb 28, 2005; 7-for-1 June 9, 2014; 4-for-1 Aug 31, 2020 (first split-adjusted trading days). 2 x 2 x 2 x 7 x 4 = 224 (Motley Fool table citing Apple Investor Relations; Yahoo Finance).
Shares held at year end (from 1 IPO share), 5-year steps:
1985: 1 | 1990: 2 | 1995: 2 | 2000: 4 | 2005: 8 | 2010: 8 | 2015: 56 | 2020: 224
(The chart shows share COUNT, not value; each split left the holding's value unchanged at that moment.)

## Worked example (our math)
- 1 share at $1,000 -> 10-for-1 split -> 10 shares at $100 = 10 x $100 = $1,000. Same pie, more slices.
- Nvidia: $1,208.88 / 10 = $120.888, shown as $120.88 (restated close). 10 x $120.88 = $1,208.80 (rounding).
- Reverse 1-for-10 (our math): 10 shares at $5 = $50 -> 1 share at $50 = $50.

## Surprising fact
The price per share can drop 90% in a single weekend (Nvidia, June 2024) and no holder loses a cent from it: they just hold 10x as many shares. One Apple IPO share is 224 shares after five splits.

## Guardrails
Education only. Do not say splits make stocks rise; research on post-split returns is mixed; avoid.
