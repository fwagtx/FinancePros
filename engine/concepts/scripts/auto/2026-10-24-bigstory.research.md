## Facts (all Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey unless noted)
- Thursday, Oct 22, 2026: 30-year fixed averaged 7.32%, down from 7.40% the week before (Freddie Mac release "Mortgage Rates Decrease to 7.32%"; AP via US News / Washington Post / Washington Times).
- First weekly decline since late August (AP). Bloomberg: "first time in nine weeks". (Streak length varies by outlet: 8 weeks / 9 weeks / two months; 7.40% was flat Oct 8 -> Oct 15. Use "first drop since late August".)
- 7.40% (Oct 8 and Oct 15) was the highest level in nearly three years (AP); Fox/Trading Economics: highest since 2023.
- Year ago (Oct 23, 2025): 6.19% (Freddie Mac; AP).
- 15-year fixed: 6.68%, down from 6.71% (Freddie Mac).
- Sept 24, 2026: 7.03%, first weekly average at/above 7% since January 2025.
- Drivers (AP): rates have been rising since late February as higher oil prices pushed inflation higher; 10-year Treasury yield climbed to its highest since 2002, then has mostly eased the last couple of weeks.
- Forecast cited by AP (not ours): Fannie Mae projects 30-year averaging 7.4% in Q4 2026 and 7.5% in 2027. Do NOT present as prediction; optional attribution only.

## Worked example (our math, labeled example)
$400,000 30-year fixed loan, principal and interest only:
- at 6.19% (year ago): $2,447/month
- at 7.32% (now): $2,748/month
- difference: about $300 more per month (~$301)

## Context / what it means
- Mortgage rates follow the 10-year Treasury yield more than the Fed's rate directly.
- A small weekly dip; still more than a full point above a year ago, so monthly payments for a new buyer remain much higher.
- Existing-home sales Sept: 3.99M SAAR (NAR, Oct 13); NAR's Yun said rising rates mean fewer buyers can qualify.
