## Specific concerns, answered

**1. Chart basis (first-reported vs revised).** I could not open the 2025 Data Book's multi-year chart, because every ftc.gov host is blocked. So I cannot independently confirm a revised series such as 3.4 / 6.1 / ... from here. Here is what I can confirm:
- The script's values (3.3, 5.8, 8.8, 10.0, 12.5, 15.9) are exactly the **as-first-reported** figures. One search excerpt citing the FTC also listed 2021 as $5.9B.
- Mixing the two bases is the problem. The FTC revises earlier years upward as late reports come in, so the latest Data Book's chart will show higher values for 2020-2024 than the first releases did.
- Ruling: keep the verified first-reported values and **label the basis on screen**. If the 11-24 video's revised series is confirmed from the 2025 Data Book PDF, using it here would be more consistent with the channel. In that case the line 3 narration ("nearly five times") still holds at 4.7x, but every point must come from that one Data Book.
- Exact values to keep: `[[2020,3.3],[2021,5.8],[2022,8.8],[2023,10.0],[2024,12.5],[2025,15.9]]`
- New chart title: `"Fraud losses, as first reported"` (31 chars). Shorter option: `"As first reported"`.

**2. "Lost" vs "reported losing."** The figure is right. Many outlets write "lost," so the voice is defensible, but it does not match the caption, and the FTC wording is "reported losing." Make the voice and the caption match (fix 1).

**3. Hawaii/Lowell.** The dates are correct, and the release date in the source is right. "Hit Kaua'i" is common press usage (Civil Beat), but the storm did not make landfall, so "battered" is safer. On Nov 27 the event will be about 11 weeks old. That is OK because it is dated explicitly ("September 2026") and works as an example ("Disasters bring ..."), not as breaking news. No time words become wrong.

**4. Deduction line.** Saying "up to $1,000" with no qualifier is incomplete: joint filers get $2,000. The post text includes it, but the spoken line and the board do not. Add it (fix 4).

**5. Hook.** It is 12 words, true and specific. But it is generic fraud and never mentions charity, while the cover asks "Is That Charity Even Real?". Spoken length is 13 words, about 3.2 s. A rewrite below ties it to the topic within 12 words.

**6. "You" voice / advice.** There is no "you should" anywhere. "Shows which charities qualify" is informational. The post carries "not financial advice." OK.
