# Research brief: FHA vs conventional loans: the trade-offs
Prepared Nov 1, 2026 for a Dec 31, 2026 post. Evergreen: every number carries its date or rule source.

## News angle
- No hard news peg. FHFA normally announces next year's conforming limit in late November (2026 limits were announced Nov 25, 2025). As of Nov 1, 2026, the official 2027 conforming and FHA limits were NOT yet announced; only lender early-adoption figures (Rocket/CrossCountry $845,000, Pennymac $850,000, UWM $847,440) and forecasts exist. Do NOT use 2027 numbers in the script; use 2026 limits, dated.

## Key facts (with dates/sources)
FHA (insured by HUD's Federal Housing Administration)
- Minimum down payment: 3.5% (max LTV 96.5%) with a credit score of 580+; 10% down (90% LTV) for scores 500-579 (HUD Handbook 4000.1, via Chase, Experian, PenFed). Lenders often set higher score floors (overlays).
- Upfront MIP (UFMIP): 1.75% of the base loan amount, usually financed into the loan; unchanged by ML 2023-05 (HUD ML 2023-05; fha.com, Aug 30, 2026: no new MIP change announced, UFMIP still 1.75%).
- Annual MIP: cut 30 basis points by HUD Mortgagee Letter 2023-05 (Feb 22, 2023; loans endorsed on/after Mar 20, 2023). Most common case: 30-year loan at or below $726,200 with less than 5% down = 0.55% per year; 5-10% down = 0.50%. Full range 0.15%-0.75% depending on term, size, LTV (Wells Fargo). No HUD change found as of Aug-Oct 2026.
- Duration: since HUD ML 2013-04 (case numbers from June 3, 2013), annual MIP lasts the LIFE OF THE LOAN if LTV > 90% at origination (under 10% down); with 10%+ down it lasts 11 years. Only way out with <10% down: refinance into a non-FHA loan (NerdWallet; NAR, Sep 2025 confirms rule still in effect).
- 2026 FHA one-unit loan limits: floor $541,287, ceiling $1,249,125 (HUD ML 2025-23; for case numbers on/after Jan 1, 2026).

Conventional (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac)
- Minimum down: 3% (97% LTV) for one-unit primary residence purchases, e.g. Fannie Mae HomeReady / standard 97% LTV, Freddie Mac Home Possible (Fannie Mae lender guide, Feb 2026; NerdWallet).
- PMI required below 20% down. Fannie Mae homebuyer FAQ: PMI typically costs 0.2% to 2% of the loan amount per year; cost depends heavily on credit score and down payment.
- Homeowners Protection Act (1998; CFPB Compliance Bulletin 2015-03): borrower can request PMI cancellation when the balance reaches 80% of original value (20% equity) if current and in good standing; servicer must automatically end it when the balance is scheduled to reach 78% of original value (22% equity), if current. Also final termination at loan midpoint. Applies to loans from July 29, 1999 on.
- 2026 baseline conforming loan limit (one-unit): $832,750, up from $806,500 in 2025 (+$26,250, +3.26%); high-cost ceiling $1,249,125 (FHFA, announced Nov 25, 2025).

## The surprising fact
- With less than 10% down, FHA mortgage insurance never cancels on its own: it lasts the life of the loan (rule since 2013). Conventional PMI legally must drop off automatically at 22% equity.

## Worked example (OUR MATH), $350,000 home, 30-year
- FHA 3.5% down: 350,000 x 0.035 = $12,250 down. Base loan = 350,000 - 12,250 = $337,750.
- Upfront MIP: 337,750 x 0.0175 = $5,910.63 (about $5,911), usually added to the loan.
- Annual MIP (0.55%, under 5% down, under $726,200): 337,750 x 0.0055 = $1,857.63 in year one; / 12 = $154.80 a month (about $155). (Approximation on the starting balance; HUD computes it on the average outstanding balance each year, so it declines slightly.) With <10% down this lasts the life of the loan.
- Conventional 3% down: 350,000 x 0.03 = $10,500 down; loan $339,500. PMI cost varies (0.2%-2%/yr per Fannie Mae), so no single number.
- PMI exit points on the $350,000 original value: request at 80% = $280,000 balance; automatic at 78% = $273,000 balance.
