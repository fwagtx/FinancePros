# Fact-check: 2026-11-14-hot ("Why is vanilla so expensive?")

Checked Oct 2, 2026 for a Nov 14, 2026 post. Math was recomputed in Python.

## What checks out
- 2018 vanilla at about $600/kg is BBC's 2018 report of the peak. It is widely echoed by Moneyweek, The Takeout and gold.co.uk. OK.
- Silver's 2018 average was $15.71/troy oz (USGS 2018 Minerals Yearbook, Engelhard price). 15.71 x 32.1507 = $505.09/kg. "More than silver" is correct for 2018. OK.
- Cyclone Enawo hit Madagascar's SAVA vanilla region (Antalaha, Sambava) on Mar 7, 2017. OK.
- Hand pollination works only on the one day a flower is open. Pods take about 9 months to mature, and curing takes about 3-6+ months. OK. Nearly all commercial vanilla is hand-pollinated, so "each flower" is acceptable.
- The $250/kg minimum export price ran from late 2020 (it replaced a $350 floor) until May 2023, when it was liberalised (Mintec, Global Trade Alert). OK.
- Madagascar produced 3,114 t in 2023 out of a world total of 7,433 t (FAOSTAT). That is 41.9%, so "about 42%" is OK. The ~80% figure that people often quote refers to exports and older years. The on-screen source "FAOSTAT" makes 42% defensible, but see fix 2 for the label and the causal wording.
- C&EN (Sept 12, 2016, "The problem with vanilla") says less than 1% of vanilla flavor comes from the vanilla orchid and about 85% is vanillin synthesized from guaiacol. OK.
- 21 CFR 169.3(b) sets a unit weight of vanilla beans at 13.35 oz, which is 0.37847 kg. $600 x 0.37847 = $227.08 and $48 x 0.37847 = $18.17. The math is correct.
- Spoken and on-screen values match, apart from allowed rounding.
- There is no advice, no price prediction and no political opinion. The cover and title pay off in lines 1-2.
