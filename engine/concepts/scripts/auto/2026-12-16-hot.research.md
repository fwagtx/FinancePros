# 2026-12-16-hot research brief
Topic: Money myth: freezing your credit lowers your credit score
Format: Money Myth (myth, then fact). Evergreen; posts Wednesday, December 16, 2026.
Researched Saturday, October 17, 2026.

## The myth and the fact
- MYTH: Freezing your credit report hurts or lowers your credit score.
- FACT: A credit freeze does not affect your credit score. FTC: "A freeze is free to place and lift and it doesn't affect your credit score." FTC also: "Neither fraud alerts nor credit freezes affect credit scores." CFPB: security freezes "do not impact your credit scores in any way whatsoever." (FTC consumer article "Credit Freeze or Fraud Alert: What's Right for Your Credit Report?"; FTC consumer alert Feb 2020 "Fraud alerts, credit freezes: What's the difference?"; CFPB Ask CFPB #1341 "What is a credit freeze?")
- Why: a freeze only restricts who can PULL your report to open new credit. It does not change your payment history, balances, account ages or anything a score is built from. It is also not a hard inquiry.
- What still moves the score while frozen: your existing accounts keep reporting. Creditors can still report late/delinquent payments, which can hurt the score (FTC-derived summaries; CFPB).

## The law (cost and timing)
- Free nationwide since September 21, 2018, under the Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act (signed May 2018). Free to place and to lift at all three nationwide bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). (FTC press release Sept 21, 2018 "Starting today, new law allows consumers to place free credit freezes"; Bloomberg Law; Foley Hoag Oct 2018)
- Timing rules in the law (FTC Sept 2018 release):
  - Freeze requested online or by phone: bureau must place it within 1 business day.
  - Lift requested online or by phone: bureau must lift it within 1 hour.
  - Mail requests: 3 business days.
- Same law: initial fraud alert extended from 90 days to 1 year, still free; identity-theft victims can get an extended fraud alert for 7 years. (FTC 2018; Foley Hoag)
- A freeze stays in place until the consumer lifts it (no expiration).
- Each bureau is separate: freezing one does not freeze the other two (FTC).

## What a freeze does and does not block
- Blocks: new creditors pulling the report, so in practice nobody can open most new credit accounts in your name (FTC: "nobody can open a new credit account").
- Does NOT block:
  - Charges or fraud on EXISTING accounts (FTC 2018 release: freezes "don't prevent thieves from making charges to your existing accounts").
  - Existing creditors and debt collectors acting for them (CFPB: entities that can see a frozen file include "creditors of accounts you currently hold").
  - Government agencies acting on a court or administrative order, subpoena or search warrant (FTC-derived summaries; weaker sourcing).
  - Under the federal law, reports pulled for employment, tenant screening or insurance purposes (CFPB #1341; Wisconsin DATCP freeze publication says employment, tenant, background screening).
  - Prescreened credit offers (separate opt-out exists) (Wisconsin DATCP).

## Freeze vs fraud alert vs lock (describe, not instruct)
- Credit FREEZE: a legal right under federal law (FCRA as amended 2018). Free. Placed separately at each bureau. Blocks new-credit pulls until lifted. Lift timing set by law (1 hour online/phone).
- FRAUD ALERT: free; you keep access to credit; creditors must take extra steps to verify identity before opening new credit. Placing it at one bureau is enough: that bureau must tell the other two (FTC). Lasts 1 year (initial) or 7 years (extended, for identity-theft victims).
- Credit LOCK: a bureau PRODUCT governed by its own terms/contract, not the federal freeze law. Usually toggled instantly in an app. Some are free (Equifax Lock & Alert), others come with paid subscriptions (Experian's lock is part of a paid plan; TransUnion's pricing is reported inconsistently). Sources: NerdWallet, Navy Federal, credit.com, MoneyLion (May 2026). Weakly sourced on exact prices; do not put prices on screen.
- None of the three affects the credit score.

## Worked example (exact math)
- Cost to freeze at all three bureaus and later lift all three: 3 freezes x $0 + 3 lifts x $0 = $0 (free under the 2018 law).
- Score points lost from freezing: 0 (FTC, CFPB).
- Timing example: an online lift request at 2:00 PM must be done by 3:00 PM (2:00 + 1 hour, per law). The same request by mail: up to 3 business days.
- Bureaus to contact: a fraud alert needs 1 (it is passed to the other 2); a freeze needs 3 (each separately).

## The surprising fact
- Freezing your credit has zero effect on your score, costs $0 since 2018, and by law a phone or online lift must happen within one hour. Yet a freeze still doesn't stop fraud on cards you already have.
