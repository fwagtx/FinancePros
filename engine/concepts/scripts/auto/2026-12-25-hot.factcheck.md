The line 2 paraphrase "HP loses money on printers" is fair, and it is not presented as a quote. The post.ig wording "makes it on supplies" is also a fair paraphrase.
- **Which? 2019:** The Drinks Business reported Which?'s 2019 finding of about £1.89 per ml (£1,890 per litre) for branded ink bought from a brand's website, and its "10 times more than vintage champagne" comparison. Confirmed, though only through a secondary source.
- **Dynamic Security US case:** HP agreed to pay $1.5M without admitting wrongdoing, and the court gave final approval on April 25, 2019. The class was owners of certain OfficeJet / OfficeJet Pro printers from March 2015 to December 2017. The firmware update came out in 2016 (class counsel says "early 2016"; it was widely reported in September 2016), so the "2016" timeline entry is fine.
- **2025 toner case:** Settled in March 2025 (The Register, March 19, 2025). Customers got no monetary relief. HP agreed to disclosure and to let customers opt out of firmware updates. The settlement notice says "HP denies that it did anything wrong." The post.ig wording ("settled with HP denying wrongdoing") is accurate and neutral.

- **Hook:** "Your printer is cheap on purpose. The ink is the product." is 11 words, specific, under 3 seconds, and fairly supported by the CEO's remark and the 65% supplies share. Strong opener.

## Issues and fixes

### 1. Line 6: allegation stated as fact (MUST FIX)

"HP's Dynamic Security firmware blocked some non-HP cartridges, leading to a … settlement" presents the plaintiffs' claim as fact. It also implies HP was found liable. HP settled without admitting wrongdoing.

Replace line 6 `text` with:
`Lawsuits alleged HP firmware blocked rival cartridges. HP admitted no wrongdoing, settling in 2019 for $1.5 million.`

Replace line 6 `say` with:
`Lawsuits alleged HP firmware blocked rival cartridges. HP admitted no wrongdoing, settling in twenty nineteen for one point five million dollars.`

The new `say` is 21 words, the same as the old one, so the total stays at 138. The scene can stay as it is.

### 2. post.ig and post.yt_desc: same framing problem (MUST FIX)

In post.ig, replace:
`HP's Dynamic Security firmware led to a $1.5M US settlement in 2019;`
with:
`Lawsuits alleged HP's Dynamic Security firmware blocked non-HP cartridges; HP settled a US case for $1.5M in 2019 without admitting wrongdoing;`

In post.yt_desc, replace:
`and HP's Dynamic Security firmware led to a $1.5 million US settlement in 2019.`
with:
`and HP settled a 2019 US lawsuit alleging its Dynamic Security firmware blocked non-HP cartridges for $1.5 million, admitting no wrongdoing.`

### 3. Cover and line 0: "$10.9B" next to "INK" reads as the whole ink industry (minor)

The figure is HP's FY2025 supplies revenue, which also includes toner. To tie it to HP within the prop label limit (≤5), change `cover.label` and the line 0 `scene.label` from `INK` to `HP`. Optional, but it keeps HP's number from being generalized to the industry. The cover title is paid off by the video.

### 4. Line 8: split item over the limit (minor)

In `scene.left.items`, "Pricier printer" is 15 characters, over the 14-character limit. Replace it with `Pricier buy` (11 characters).

### 5. Line 4: chart title not labeled as HP / fiscal years (minor)

Replace the `scene.title` value `Supplies share` with `HP supplies share` (17). Optional.

### 6. Line 5: "UK group Which?" reads awkwardly out loud (minor, optional)

Spoken, "Which?" can sound like a question. Replace `UK group Which?` with `UK consumer group Which?` in both text and say, and drop "about" to keep the word count at 138. Note that "£1.89" is still approximate.

- text: `In 2019, UK consumer group Which? found branded ink cost £1.89 per millilitre.`
- say: `In twenty nineteen, UK consumer group Which? found branded ink cost one pound eighty-nine per millilitre.`

The text/say differences on the other lines are only numerals versus spoken words. That is consistent.
