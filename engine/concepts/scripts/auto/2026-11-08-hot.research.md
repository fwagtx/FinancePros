# Research brief: Why grocery prices stay high even when inflation cools (posts Nov 8, 2026)

## BLS CPI food at home, 12-month change, December to December
2020 +3.9% | 2021 +6.5% | 2022 +11.8% | 2023 +1.3% | 2024 +1.8% | 2025 +2.4%  (BLS "CPI in review" 2024, 2025)
- August 2026: +2.2% over 12 months (July 2026 +2.7%) (BLS CPI, Sept 11, 2026).
- Cumulative Dec 2019 -> Dec 2025: 1.039 x 1.065 x 1.118 x 1.013 x 1.018 x 1.024 = 1.306, about +31%.

## Key idea
- "Inflation cooling" (disinflation) means prices rise more slowly, not that they fall (deflation). The price level stays at its new, higher base.
- Prices that rose fast rarely fall back across the board; broad grocery price declines are rare.

## Worked example
A $100 grocery cart in Dec 2019 cost about $130.64 by Dec 2025. Then +2.2% a year (Aug 2026 pace): on $130.64 that's ~$2.87 more, not a drop.
