## Verified claims (OK)
- **FRA is 67 for people born in 1960 or later.** Confirmed by NerdWallet, CRS R41962 and Yahoo Finance. Anyone turning 62 in late 2026 was born in 1964, so the hook's 30% figure applies to everyone who can newly claim at 62.
- **62 pays 70% (a 30% cut).** The reduction is 5/9 of 1% a month for the first 36 months and 5/12 of 1% for each month after. 36 x 5/9 = 20% and 24 x 5/12 = 10%, so the total is 30%. Source: SSA Pub 05-10035, via secondary sources (ssa.gov was blocked in this session).
- **Chart points.** 63 = 75%, 64 = 80%, 65 = 86.7%, 66 = 93.3%, 67 = 100% are all correct. Delayed credits are 8% a year (2/3 of 1% a month), giving 108% at 68, 116% at 69 and 124% at 70. All correct.
- **Worked example.** $2,000 x 0.70 = $1,400 and $2,000 x 1.24 = $2,480. Correct.
- **2027 COLA is 3.6%, announced Oct 14, 2026.** Confirmed by the SSA press release (ssa.gov/news/en/press/releases/2026-10-14.html), CNBC and Fox Business. It starts with January 2027 payments. On Dec 31, 2026 it is still current and correctly called "for 2027". It is not stale.

## Issues

### 1. Line 0 hook, line 5 and POST: "for life" / "never resets. It's permanent" overstates the rule (accuracy)
SSA calls the reduction "permanent", but there are real exceptions:
- You can withdraw the claim within 12 months (Form SSA-521, repaying benefits, once per lifetime) and the cut is erased.
- You can voluntarily suspend at FRA to earn delayed credits until 70, which offsets the cut.
- If earnings-test withholding applies before FRA, SSA recalculates the benefit at FRA and credits the withheld months, which shrinks the cut.

"Never resets" is therefore false for some viewers. The hook is also 12 words, at the cap, and runs about 4 seconds at TTS pace, so it should be shorter.

**Line 0 replacement:**
- text: "Claim Social Security at 62? Your check shrinks 30%."
- say: "Claim Social Security at sixty-two? Your check shrinks thirty percent."
- Spoken words drop to 10 (about 3 s). Keep the scene as is (value "-30%").
- It is stronger because it opens with a question aimed at the viewer, plus one concrete number. Line 1's "Same check, just sooner" then creates the need to hear the answer.
