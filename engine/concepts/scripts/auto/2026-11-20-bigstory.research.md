## The facts (University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers, final November 2026, released Nov 20, 2026)
- Year-ahead (1-year) median inflation expectations: 3.7% in November final, down from 4.7% in October (a 1.0-point drop). Preliminary November (Nov 6) was 4.3%; consensus for final was 4.3%. (Newsquawk, Rio Times, Helious, Kitco, Axios)
- 3.7% is the lowest since February 2026 (RISMedia/Axios coverage). Hsu: "Median year-ahead inflation expectations plunged from 4.7% last month to 3.7% this month. Still, it remains meaningfully above the 3.4% seen in February before the Iran conflict began."
- Long-run (5-10 yr) expectations: 3.3% in November, down from 3.5% in October.
- Consumer Sentiment Index: 51.5 final November (prelim 51.0), up from 48.0 in October; a three-month high (highest since August). Bloomberg median estimate was 51.6.
- Current Conditions index 49.4 (from 46.7); Expectations index 52.9 (from 48.8).
- Drivers per director Joanne Hsu: post-election expectations and lower expected gas prices; gas price expectations "broadly comparable to pre-Iran readings."
- Wealth split: lower- and middle-wealth households reported improved finances; consumers with the largest stock holdings said their finances worsened, likely tied to AI-related stock market swings.
- Sentiment still about 9% below February (Axios).

## Context / why it matters
- What people expect inflation to be can shape real prices: workers ask for bigger raises, businesses feel freer to raise prices. The Fed watches this survey closely.
- Expectations are not actual prices: actual CPI is a separate report.
- Watch next: final sentiment in December; the Fed's December meeting (markets pricing a possible hike); actual inflation data.

## For a normal person
- A lower expected inflation rate is a mood reading, not a price cut. 3.7% is still above February's 3.4%.
