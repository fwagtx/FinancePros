## Structure and history (federalreserve.gov)
- Created by the Federal Reserve Act, signed Dec 23, 1913 (President Woodrow Wilson).
- 12 regional Federal Reserve Banks + Board of Governors in Washington.
- Board: 7 governors, 14-year terms, nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate; chair and vice chair 4-year terms.
- FOMC: 12 voting members = 7 governors + New York Fed president + 4 of the other 11 regional presidents on a rotating basis. 8 scheduled meetings a year.
- Mandate (Federal Reserve Reform Act of 1977): maximum employment, stable prices, moderate long-term interest rates ("dual mandate").
- 2% inflation goal (PCE) formally adopted January 2012.
- Tools: fed funds target range; interest on reserve balances (IORB, since 2008); overnight reverse repo; discount window; balance sheet (QE/QT).
- The Fed does not set mortgage rates or credit card rates directly; prime rate conventionally = fed funds upper bound + 3 points; card APRs are typically prime + a margin.

## Worked example (exact math)
- $400,000 30-year fixed mortgage: at 3% -> $1,686.42/month; at 7% -> $2,661.21/month. Difference $974.79/month.
- Total interest over 30 years: 3% -> $207,109.81; 7% -> $558,035.59.
- Context: Freddie Mac PMMS 30-yr average 2.96% in 2021 and 6.81% in 2023; record low 2.65% (Jan 7, 2021).
- Credit card: a 0.25-point rise on a $5,000 balance = $12.50 more interest a year (5,000 x 0.0025).

## Surprising fact
- The Fed doesn't set your mortgage rate, but when it moved from near zero (2021) to 5.5% (2023), average 30-year mortgage rates went from about 3% to about 7%, adding about $975/month on a $400,000 loan.
