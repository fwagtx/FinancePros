# Fact-check: 2026-11-09-bigstory (NY Fed SCE, October 2026 survey)

Primary source: NY Fed press release "Inflation Expectations Stable; Labor Market Expectations Mixed", Nov 9, 2026
(https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261109, read via Firecrawl scrape; newyorkfed.org is blocked for direct fetch in this session).
Second source: Reuters, "NY Fed survey finds Americans more worried over future jobless levels" (Nov 9, 2026; syndicated on investing.com and 1027wbow.com).
Context: the Sept survey (released Oct 7) had 43.9%; the August reading of 44.4% was the earlier "highest since April 2020". 45.3% beats it, so the claim holds.

Verified against release: 45.3% (+1.4 pts, highest since April 2020, driven by those without a college degree); job-finding 46.5% (+0.4, highest since Nov 2025);
missing a minimum debt payment 13.8% over the next three months (+1.6, highest since Dec 2025); rent 7.7% (+0.9); medical care 9.3% (+0.1);
fielded Oct 1-31, 2026; released Nov 9, 2026 (so "out today" is correct for a Nov 9 post).

  TEXT: Americans see a 45.3% chance unemployment will be higher a year from now.
  SAY: Americans see about a forty-five percent chance unemployment will be higher a year from now.
  SCENE: OK.

1 - OK. Highest since April 2020 is confirmed, and "out today" is correct (released Nov 9, 2026). Scene source "NY Fed, Nov 9, 2026" is OK.

2 - OK. +1.4 percentage points, Sept to Oct, driven by people without a college degree ("led by" is fine).

3 - OK. 46.5%, highest since Nov 2025. "About forty-six and a half percent" matches 46.5%.
  Optional scene tweak (not required): left label "Jobs worse" -> "Unemployment up" (more precise).

  The scene also declares "winner": "right" with a happy mood. That spins a mixed report as good news, when the worry measure rose 1.4 pts against 0.4 pt
  and job-loss odds also rose (13.9%).
  TEXT: So people expect a softer job market, but feel a bit better about bouncing back.
  SAY: So people expect a softer job market, but they feel a bit better about bouncing back.

5 - OK. 13.8% over the next three months (the scene sub says so). "About fourteen percent" is a sensible rounding.

  Optional scene tweak: title "Expected next year" -> "Next 12 months" (avoids the calendar-year reading).
