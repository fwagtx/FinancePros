## Company numbers (Live Nation Entertainment, FY2025 10-K / Q4 2025 earnings release, Feb 2026)
- Total revenue FY2025: $25.2 billion (up 9%, +$2.0B). Consolidated AOI FY2025: $2.4 billion (up 10%, +$220.5M).
- Ticketing segment revenue FY2025: $3,081.2 million (FY2024: $2,988.7M), up 3%.
- Ticketing AOI FY2025: $1,134.4 million (FY2024: $1,123.6M), up 1%.
- Fee-bearing tickets FY2025: 346 million (FY2024: 340 million), up 2%. Fee-bearing GTV FY2025: $37.1 billion, up 6%.
- Concerts AOI FY2025: $687 million, record margin 3.3%.
- Derived: Ticketing share of revenue = 3,081.2 / 25,200 = 12.2% (~12%). Ticketing share of AOI = 1,134.4 / ~2,400 = ~47% ("nearly half"). Ticketing AOI margin = 1,134.4 / 3,081.2 = 36.8% (~37%).
- 10-K: Ticketing is "primarily an agency business" that sells tickets on behalf of clients and "retains a portion of the service charge as its fee"; it pays clients upfront payments and a portion of service fee revenue.

## Fee split (company's own claim)
- Ticketmaster spokesperson / Live Nation "Facts" page (cited by Marketplace, May 15, 2026): Ticketmaster's share of service fees is typically about 5-7% of the total ticket price; "on a $100 all-in ticket, Ticketmaster gets $5"; venues typically keep over two-thirds of the service fee, and set the fee rate card. Attribute as Ticketmaster's claim.

## Worked example (exact math, labeled rough average)
- Ticketing AOI / fee-bearing tickets, FY2025: $1,134.4M / 346M = $3.28 per fee-bearing ticket. Rough average only: AOI also includes non-fee income (e.g. advertising, sponsorship), and tickets vary.

## FTC rule (Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees, 16 C.F.R. Part 464), effective May 12, 2025
- Covers live-event tickets and short-term lodging.
- 464.2(a), word-for-word: "It is an unfair and deceptive practice and a violation of this part for any Business to offer, display, or advertise any price of a Covered Good or Service without Clearly and Conspicuously disclosing the Total Price." (Script quotes the contiguous excerpt starting "for any Business to offer...".)
- 464.2(b): total price must be disclosed more prominently than other pricing information.

## Antitrust case (status as of late September 2026)
- May 2024: DOJ and states sued Live Nation/Ticketmaster (SDNY), alleging monopolization.
- Trial opened March 2, 2026 before Judge Arun Subramanian.
- March 9, 2026: DOJ (and some states) settled: $280,388,297 settlement fund for state claims; 15% service-fee cap at Live Nation amphitheaters; up to 50% of amphitheater tickets sellable via other platforms; exclusive deals capped at 4 years; 8-year term; no Ticketmaster divestiture. Requires court approval under the Tunney Act; public comments closed Sept 4, 2026; judge indicated decision mid-September to October 2026.
- 33 states + DC continued. April 15, 2026: jury found Live Nation and Ticketmaster liable on every count submitted (monopolization of primary ticketing markets, illegal tying/bundling of amphitheaters and promotion); found overcharge of $1.72 per ticket (in 21 states + DC).
- May 21, 2026: companies moved for judgment as a matter of law (Rule 50(b)) or a new trial (Rule 59); replies filed July 2, 2026. States seek remedies incl. Ticketmaster divestiture. As of late Sept 2026 no remedy ordered; separate remedies proceeding pending (Digital Music News May 8, 2026: penalties could arrive in 2027).
- Unrelated: Sept 28, 2026, a judge denied Ticketmaster's motion to dismiss an FTC BOTS Act suit (not used in script).
