## Math
- 80 x 117 = 9,360. OK.
- 12,900 + 9,360 = 22,260, and 22,260 / 34,200 = 65.1%. OK. **Caveat:** this is an estimate the script builds by combining separate averages. The venue average covers only couples who booked a venue, and $80 x 117 multiplies two averages together. The Knot did not publish a 65% share, so the script needs to say it is approximate.
- 20 x 292 = 5,840. OK. **Caveat:** $292 is the total cost divided by the number of guests. It is not the amount a couple saves per guest they cut. The Knot uses the same framing ("every 25 guests shifts... about $7,300"), so the number can stay if it is worded as a rough average.
- Every spoken number matches the number on screen.
- Both keep:true lines (lines 4 and 5) follow list scenes, so the templates match. The script has no compare scenes.

## Issues and fixes

### 1. Weak opening (line 1)
"The average twenty twenty-five wedding cost thirty-four thousand two hundred dollars" is 11 words, about 4 seconds spoken. It is a plain statistic and gives viewers no reason to need an answer. A per-guest price is more surprising and fits in under 3 seconds. The $34,200 total moves to line 2.
- text: `Every wedding guest costs about $292.`
- say: `Every wedding guest costs about two ninety-two.`
- scene: `{"t":"hook","title":"Per guest: [$292]","prop":"tag","label":"2025 avg per guest","value":"$292","mood":"shock"}`

### 2. Non-sequitur and unsourced claim (line 2)
"Most of it isn't the dress. It's $292 per guest" does not follow: a per-guest price says nothing about the dress, and the dress claim has no source. Replace the line with the total:
- text: `The average 2025 wedding cost $34,200, for 117 guests (The Knot).`
- say: `The average twenty twenty-five wedding cost thirty-four thousand two hundred dollars, for a hundred seventeen guests, per The Knot.`
- scene: `{"t":"stat","label":"Average wedding, 2025","value":"$34,200","tone":"down","sub":"117 guests avg, The Knot 2026","mood":"think"}`

### 3. Derived share presented as fact, and awkward phrasing (line 6)
The 65% is an estimate built from separate averages (see Math), but the line presents it as a measured share. "Twenty-two thousand two sixty" is also hard to say aloud.
- text: `Venue plus food comes to roughly $22,260, about 65% of the average bill.`
- say: `Venue plus food comes to roughly twenty-two thousand two hundred sixty dollars, about sixty-five percent of the average bill.`
- scene: `{"t":"stat","label":"Venue + catering, est.","value":"~65%","tone":"down","sub":"$22,260 of $34,200 (est.)","mood":"shock"}`

### 4. Awkward phrasing (line 7)
"Thirty-four two" is unclear when spoken.
- text: (unchanged) `Costs rebounded fast: $19,000 in 2020, $35,000 in 2023, and $34,200 in 2025.`
- say: `Costs rebounded fast: nineteen thousand in twenty twenty, thirty-five thousand in twenty twenty-three, and thirty-four thousand two hundred in twenty twenty-five.`
- scene: unchanged. The chart years are correct.

### 5. Unsupported superlative and overstated savings (line 8)
"Guest count moves the total most" has no source. The per-guest number is an average, not what cutting a guest actually saves, and "20 fewer guests" reads close to advice.
- text: `So guest count matters: at that average, 20 guests works out to about $5,840.`
- say: `So guest count matters. At that average, twenty guests works out to about fifty-eight forty.`
- scene: `{"t":"board","lines":["20 guests x $292 avg","≈ $5,840"],"mood":"think"}`

### 6. Post captions
- TikTok: replace "Two-thirds is venue and food." with `Venue and food are roughly 65% of it (estimate).`
- IG: replace "Venue plus food: about 65% of the bill." with `Venue plus food: an estimated 65% of the bill.`
- YT: replace "make up about 65% of the bill" with `make up an estimated 65% of the bill`
