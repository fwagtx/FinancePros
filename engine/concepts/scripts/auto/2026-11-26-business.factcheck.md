## Issues and fixes

**1. Line 1 (hook): spoken words don't match the screen, and the spoken claim is undated.** The screen says "kept ... in Q3 2025". The narration says "keeps", which is present tense and may be stale after Q3 2026 results. The hook itself is true, specific and fast (the first clause takes about 2 s), so it is fine.
- text: `Uber says it kept 21% of rider payments worldwide in Q3 2025. A 2026 study of three US drivers' trips says over 50%.`

- scene: unchanged.

**2. Line 3: "Uber keeps the gap, minus insurance and fees" is muddled, and the board mixes the two definitions.** In most US markets Uber books the gap between rider price and driver pay (service fee plus booking fee) as revenue. Insurance and other third-party costs are paid out of that revenue. "Fees" is ambiguous, because booking fees are part of the gap.
- text: `The rider pays an upfront fare. The driver gets a separate upfront offer. Uber keeps the gap, and pays insurance from it.`
- say: `The rider pays an upfront fare. The driver gets a separate upfront offer. Uber keeps the gap, and pays insurance from it.`
- scene: `{"t":"board","lines":["Rider fare","- driver pay","= Uber's gap"],"mood":"think"}`

**3. Line 4: the two numbers are compared without saying they measure different things, and the study's narrow sample is left out (fairness).** Uber's 21% is global and after insurance and third-party costs. The study's >50% is US, before insurance, and comes from three drivers in three Sun Belt cities. As written, "US take rates topped 50%" presents a three-driver sample as a national fact. Uber disputes it.
- text: `Uber says its take, after insurance and other costs, was 21% worldwide in Q3 2025. A study by Columbia's Len Sherman with GigU, using three US drivers' trips, says the gap topped 50%.`
- say: `Uber says its take, after insurance and other costs, was twenty-one percent worldwide in the third quarter of twenty twenty-five. A Columbia professor's study of three US drivers' trips says it topped fifty percent.`
- scene: title `Uber's take, two measures`; left sub `global, after insurance`; right label `Study says`, value `>50%`, tone `neutral` (not `down`, to stay neutral), sub `3 US drivers, pre-insurance`. If the sub length limit is tight, use `3 drivers, 2026`. Values `21%` and `>50%` are 7 characters or fewer.

**4. Line 5: wrong launch framing.** Rider upfront pricing dates from 2016. In 2022 the change was upfront fares for drivers.
- text: `The study points to upfront fares for drivers, rolled out in the US in 2022.`
- say: `The study points to upfront fares for drivers, rolled out in twenty twenty-two.`
- scene: timeline events `[{"when":"2016","what":"Rider upfront prices"},{"when":"2019","what":"Uber IPO"},{"when":"2022","what":"Driver upfront fares"},{"when":"2025","what":"Uber: 21% take"}]`, reveal `4`. If the timeline allows only 3 events, drop 2019.

**5. Line 6: the $25 example labels both numbers "Uber keeps", but the two percentages are not comparable.** At the study's 50%, the $12.50 is the gap before insurance, not what Uber keeps. The math itself is correct.
- text: `Hypothetical $25 ride: at Uber's 21% (after insurance), Uber keeps $5.25. A 50% gap is $12.50, before insurance.`

**6. Line 2 (minor): the "Uber's share" sub overstates.** The $52B revenue includes Freight and gross-recorded revenue in some markets (for example the UK), so it is not a clean "share" of bookings.
- scene: right sub `Uber's revenue` (instead of `Uber's share`). text/say unchanged.

**7. Cover and post (minor, fairness).** The cover value "21%?" puts a question mark only on Uber's figure. Suggested cover value: `21–50%?`. Add the study's scope and Uber's rebuttal to the captions:
