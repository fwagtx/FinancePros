# Research brief: Scam alert, the "we refunded you too much" refund scam (posts Nov 3, 2026)

## How it works (FTC consumer guidance)
- Contact out of the blue (call, email, pop-up) from a "support agent" (posing as e.g. Microsoft, Apple, Norton, Geek Squad, Amazon) offering a refund for a renewal/subscription/overpayment.
- To "process the refund" they ask you to install remote-access software and log into your bank account.
- They create the false impression of an over-refund by moving money between YOUR accounts (e.g. savings to checking) or editing the web page (HTML), then say they refunded too much and demand the difference back.
- Payment demanded: gift cards, wire transfer, bank transfer, cryptocurrency or payment app.
- FTC: real companies won't ask you to pay to get a refund; anyone asking for remote access to process a refund or asking you to pay back an "over-refund" with gift cards/crypto is a scammer. Report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

## Numbers
- FBI IC3 2025 annual report: tech/customer support scams 47,794 complaints, ~$2.1 billion losses (all ages). People 60+: $1.04 billion of tech/customer support losses in 2025 (2nd largest category for older victims).
- IC3 total 2025: 1,008,597 complaints, $20.877 billion losses.
- FTC: 3 million fraud reports and $15.9 billion reported losses in 2025 (vs $12.5B in 2024). Imposter scams were the top fraud report category for the 5th straight year in 2025, $3.5 billion losses (FTC, Jun 2026).

## Worked example
Real "refund" promised: $50. Screen shows $5,000 "refunded" (actually $5,000 moved from your savings to your checking).
"Over-refund" they demand back: $5,000 - $50 = $4,950, usually in gift cards. You lose $4,950 of your own money.

## Surprising fact
The "extra money" is usually your own money, moved from your savings to your checking.
