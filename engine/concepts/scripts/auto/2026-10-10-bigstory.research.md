## Facts — Federal Reserve Board, 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF), released Friday, October 9, 2026
Report: "Changes in U.S. Family Finances from 2022 to 2025". Survey run every 3 years (NORC for the Fed). All dollar values inflation-adjusted (2025 dollars), comparing the 2022 and 2025 surveys.
- Median family income: +7% to $82,200. Mean income: -6% to $145,200. (Fed press release)
- Median net worth (all families): +2% to $215,900 (a record high, per CBS). Mean net worth: +7% to $1.24 million. (Fed press release)
- Under 35 (household head): median net worth fell 23% to $33,000 (CBS News; Daily Wire; IndexBox). Fed attributes younger families' losses primarily to "the unwinding of business-equity gains from 2019 to 2022" (paraphrase of report as quoted by CBS).
- Age 75+: median net worth rose 37% to $504,900, "primarily driven by an increase in the value of retirement accounts" (CBS News; IndexBox). Now the wealthiest age group by median.
- Bottom quartile of wealth: net worth fell from $3,800 to $1,700 (Fed press release via Yahoo/Detroit News).
- Late payments: Fed: "Families were more likely to be behind on their financial obligations than at any point since the 2010 survey." CNBC and Quartz: share of families behind on loan payments rose from about 12% (2022) to nearly 20% (end of 2025), i.e. nearly 1 in 5. Families at least two months behind: more than 8%, from 5% in 2022 (CNBC, Quartz, CU Today).
- Debt payments over 40% of income: 6.5% -> 8.6%, a level last seen in the 2013 survey (Fed press release, quoted).
- Stock ownership (direct or indirect): 58% -> 56%. Homeownership about 66%, about unchanged (Fed release via Mirage News).

## Context
- 2019-2022 was a big wealth-gain period for young families (St. Louis Fed); the 2025 survey shows part of that reversing, mainly business equity.
- Older households' gains came mainly from retirement accounts (stock market rally).
- Ties to 2026 backdrop: high borrowing costs (10-year near highest since 2007), record-low current-conditions sentiment (Oct 9 UMich).

## What it means for a normal person
- The typical under-35 household has $33,000 in net worth; the typical household overall $215,900.
- Debt stress is rising: nearly 1 in 5 families behind, more than 8% at least two months behind.
- Retirement accounts drove older families' gains: owning assets that grow is what separated the groups (describe, not advise).

## What to watch
- Next SCF in 2028 (2028 survey). Nearer term: NY Fed household debt report (Q3) in November; bank earnings week of Oct 12 (credit-card charge-offs).
