# 2027-01-20-hot research brief
Money Myth: "Rate shopping with several mortgage lenders wrecks your credit."
Made Nov 21, 2026; posts Wed Jan 20, 2027. Evergreen; no news hook needed (scoring rules below are long-standing).
Note: Firecrawl was out of credits and freddiemac.com was not reachable by fetch; facts were confirmed through web search results quoting the official pages.

## Verdict: MYTH (mostly). Credit scoring models are built to let you shop.

## Facts (with sources)
1. FICO groups rate-shopping inquiries: multiple mortgage (also auto, student loan) inquiries within a window count as ONE inquiry.
   - Newer FICO versions: 45-day window. Older FICO versions: any 14-day span. (myFICO, "How to Rate Shop", myfico.com/credit-education/blog/rate-shop; myFICO inquiries FAQ)
   - Mortgage lenders commonly use older "classic" FICO versions: FICO Score 2 (Experian), FICO 5 (Equifax), FICO 4 (TransUnion). (Chase mortgage education; Experian) -> safest to finish shopping within 14 days.
2. FICO ignores mortgage, auto and student loan inquiries made in the 30 days before scoring. (myFICO inquiries page; CFPB)
3. CFPB: "Within a 45-day window, multiple credit checks from mortgage lenders are recorded on your credit report as a single inquiry. The impact on your credit is the same no matter how many lenders you consult." Also: "Even if a lender needs to check your credit after the 45-day window is over, shopping around is usually still worth it. The effect of an additional inquiry is small." Shopping beyond 45 days, or for two different loan types (mortgage + auto), generally counts as multiple inquiries. (consumerfinance.gov, Ask CFPB "What exactly happens when a mortgage lender checks my credit?")
4. VantageScore: rolling 14-day window; inquiries in it treated as one. No 30-day buffer like FICO. (Experian, "How rate shopping affects your credit score"; VantageScore consumer blog)
5. One hard inquiry usually takes fewer than 5 points off a FICO Score (Experian "What is a hard inquiry"; American Express Credit Intel). FICO counts inquiries only from the last 12 months; they stay on the report up to 2 years. (myFICO, "How long do hard inquiries stay on your credit report")
   - Caveat: "fewer than 5 points" is Experian's/Amex's wording, not a myFICO page. Larger effect for thin/short credit files.
6. THE SURPRISE: the danger isn't the lenders, it's other credit. A credit card or car loan application during mortgage shopping counts separately (CFPB: two loan types = multiple inquiries).

## Savings from shopping (official research)
- Freddie Mac Economic & Housing Research Insight, April 2018: one additional rate quote saves about $1,500 on average over the life of the loan; five quotes about $3,000 ($2,914 in the press release). 80% of borrowers with one more quote save $966-$2,086. (freddiemac.com 201804-Insight.pdf; GlobeNewswire release Apr 17, 2018)
- Freddie Mac Insight, Feb 16, 2023 ("When Rates Are Higher, Borrowers Who Shop Around Save More"): in Oct-Nov 2022, two quotes could save as much as $600 a year; at least four quotes more than $1,200 a year. Rate reduction from shopping two lenders averaged 10 basis points 2010-2021, 20 basis points in 2022. Assumes FICO 740+, 20% down, 30-year fixed conventional purchase.
  (one mirror says "five" quotes; official page says four - use "four or more" cautiously, or use the 2018 figures.)

## Worked example (OUR MATH, illustrative rates, not current quotes)
$350,000, 30-year fixed, standard amortization M = P*i/(1-(1+i)^-n), i = r/12, n = 360.
- 6.50%: i = 0.0054167 -> M = $2,212.24/month; total paid $796,405.71
- 6.25%: i = 0.0052083 -> M = $2,155.01/month; total paid $775,803.67
- Difference: $57.23/month; $20,602.04 over 30 years (360 x 57.23 = 20,602.80 using rounded payments; exact difference $20,602.04).
- Spoken: "about fifty-seven dollars a month", "about twenty thousand six hundred dollars over thirty years". On screen: $57/mo, $20,602.
