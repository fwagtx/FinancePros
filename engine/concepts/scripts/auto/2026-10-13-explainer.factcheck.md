# Fact-check: 2026-10-13-explainer ("How Social Security gets its raise")

Checked 2026-09-30 for posting on 2026-10-13. I read only the raw script JSON.

## Claims verified
- The COLA uses the Jul-Sep (Q3) average of CPI-W, compared with the Q3 average of the last year a COLA was paid, and rounded to 0.1%. **Correct** (SSA COLA fact sheet; CRS 94-803).
- The September 2026 CPI report is due October 14, 2026. **Correct**: the BLS 2026 schedule lists it for Oct 14, 8:30 a.m. ET.
- The 2025 Q3 CPI-W average was 317.265 and the 2024 average was 308.729. **Correct** (SSA; AAF).
- The 2026 COLA was 2.8%. **Correct.** Recomputed: 317.265 / 308.729 - 1 = 2.765%, which rounds to 2.8%.
- The average retired worker went from $2,015 to $2,071. **Correct** (SSA 2026 COLA Fact Sheet). Recomputed: 2,015 x 1.028 = 2,071.42, an increase of $56.
- The chart values 2017 0.3, 2018 2.0, 2019 2.8, 2020 1.6, 2021 1.3, 2022 5.9, 2023 8.7, 2024 3.2, 2025 2.5 and 2026 2.8 are **correct** when each year is the year the COLA was paid (January).
- There was no COLA in 2010, 2011 or 2016 (payment years). **Correct.**
- The cover and title ("3 Months Set Social Security's Raise") are paid off by lines 1-3.

## Issues (none blocking)
1. **Line 7 chart, year labels (minor).** SSA's own table labels each COLA by its December effective year (for example, "2016: 0.3%"). The script labels by the year the COLA was paid, which is consistent with the rest of the script. Fix: retitle the chart "Social Security raise by year paid" so a viewer comparing it with SSA's table isn't confused.
2. **Line 2 timeline, "After: SSA announces raise" (minor).** SSA normally announces the COLA the same morning the September CPI comes out. Fix: change it to "Same day: SSA announces raise".
3. **Staleness and timing.** The video posts Oct 13, one day before the 2027 COLA is set. The script correctly uses a date ("October 14, 2026") rather than "tomorrow". It will go stale quickly after Oct 14 because the 2.8% figure will no longer be the latest raise. Optional: add "that sets the 2027 raise" to line 2 so the date's relevance is explicit.
4. **Line 4, spoken vs on-screen values (nit).** The narration says "about 317 ... about 309" while the screen shows 317.265 and 308.729. These are consistent roundings, so this is acceptable.
5. **Line 8, "No price rise means no raise" (nit).** This is simplified. Strictly, there is no raise when the Q3 CPI-W average does not exceed the last COLA year's Q3 average. It is fine as a summary.
6. **Opening.** The hook is true, specific and speakable in under 3 seconds. It could be stronger if it were timely: "Your 2027 Social Security raise gets locked in on October 14, from just three months of prices."
7. **Other checks.** The script contains no advice, price predictions, political opinion, direct quotes, allegations or generalizations from a single company. The only worked example uses real SSA data, so no hypothetical label is needed. Line 9 is an observation, not an instruction. The narration reads naturally out loud.

Sources: bls.gov/schedule/news_release/current_year.asp; ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html; congress.gov/crs-product/94-803; americanactionforum.org (2026 COLA insight); COLA history tables (colacalculator.com, wealthvieu.com).

VERDICT: PASS
