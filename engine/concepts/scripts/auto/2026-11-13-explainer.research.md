# Research brief: Net worth, explained with acorns (Acorn Economics, posts Nov 13, 2026)

Researched Oct 1, 2026. Federal Reserve sites block direct fetch from this environment; figures confirmed through search results quoting the Fed and cross-checked against news coverage (CNBC, Washington Examiner/Denver Gazette, KPMG, Calculated Risk).

## Definition
- Net worth = total assets (what you own: cash, savings, investments, retirement accounts, home, car, business) minus total liabilities (what you owe: mortgage, car loan, student loans, credit cards). Can be negative. (Standard definition used in the Fed's Survey of Consumer Finances.)
- Accounting point: paying a debt out of savings does not change net worth (assets fall by the same amount as debt). Net worth rises when assets grow (saving new income, investment gains) or debt falls using new income.

## Key numbers (latest available)
- Fed Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF) 2022, published October 2023 ("Changes in U.S. Family Finances from 2019 to 2022"), 2022 dollars:
  - Median family net worth 2022: $192,900 (up 37% in real terms from 2019, the largest three-year rise in the modern SCF's history).
  - Mean (average) family net worth 2022: $1,063,700 (up 23% from 2019).
  - Mean is about 5.5x the median: a small number of very wealthy families pull the average up.
  - Median 2019 (2022 dollars): about $141,100.
  - Median net worth, families with head under 35, 2022: $39,000 (bulletin shows 39.0 thousand; third parties list $39,040).
- Is there newer SCF data? The 2025 SCF was fielded March-December 2025; the Fed says summary results will be published "in late 2026". As of Oct 1, 2026, not released. ## Worked example (hypothetical, for the script)
Squirrel owns: $8,000 savings + $12,000 car = $20,000 in assets.
Squirrel owes: $25,000 student loans.
Net worth = $20,000 - $25,000 = -$5,000.
(A negative net worth is common early in adult life when student loans are large.)

## Surprising fact
The "average" US family net worth topped $1 million in 2022 ($1,063,700), but the typical (median) family had $192,900, and families under 35 had a median of $39,000. Averages are skewed by the very rich, so the median is the better "typical" number.
