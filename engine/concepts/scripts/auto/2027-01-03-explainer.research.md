# Research brief: Interest is rent (Acorn Economics) - posts Sun Jan 3, 2027

Evergreen explainer. No timely news hook needed; every number below is dated.

## Core idea (definition)
- Interest is the price of using someone else's money for a period of time - "rent" on money.
- The lender charges it for three reasons:
  1. Waiting (time value of money): the lender gives up using the money now.
  2. Inflation: money paid back later may buy less than it does today.
  3. Risk: the borrower might not pay it back (default risk). Riskier borrowers pay higher rates.
- Same institution pays and charges rent: a bank pays depositors interest (it is renting your money) and charges borrowers interest.
- (Standard textbook definitions; see Fed / St. Louis Fed educational material in SOURCES.)

## Surprising fact
- The same banking system that paid savers an average of 0.38% (Oct 2026, FDIC) charged card balances an average of 22.36% (Aug 2026, Fed G.19). When you deposit, you are the landlord collecting tiny rent; when you carry a card balance, you pay big rent. The gap reflects risk (cards are unsecured) and costs.
- Annual mortgage rate more than doubled from 2.96% (2021) to 6.81% (2023).

## Worked example (our math)
- Squirrel lends 100 acorns for 1 year at 5% simple interest.
- Interest = 100 x 0.05 x 1 = 5 acorns. Total repaid = 100 + 5 = 105 acorns.
- (Illustrative; 5% is our chosen rate, not a market rate.)
