## The story (all from the official NY Fed release, Nov 9, 2026; October 2026 survey, fielded Oct 1-31, 2026; ~1,300 household heads)
- Mean probability the US unemployment rate will be higher one year from now: 45.3%, up 1.4 percentage points (so September = 43.9%, our math). Highest reading since April 2020. Increase driven by those without a college degree.
- Mean probability of finding a job if current job was lost: 46.5%, up 0.4 pt; highest since November 2025.
- Mean probability of losing one's job in next 12 months: 13.9%, up 0.4 pt (below 12-month trailing avg 14.3%).
- Expected quit rate: 19.4%, down 0.5 pt.
- Median inflation expectations unchanged: 1-yr 3.9%, 3-yr 3.3%, 5-yr 3.0%.
- Expected price changes next year: gas 4.2% (down 0.6 pt), food 5.5% (unchanged), medical care 9.3% (+0.1), college 7.6% (+0.1), rent 7.7% (+0.9).
- Median earnings growth expectation: 2.6% (unchanged). Household income growth expectation: 3.0% (-0.1). Spending growth: 5.3% (-0.2).
- Average probability of missing a minimum debt payment over next 3 months: 13.8%, up 1.6 pts, highest since December 2025.
- Probability savings account rates higher in 12 months: 31.6% (+1.3), highest since April 2023.
- Probability US stock prices higher in 12 months: 40.0% (-0.2).
- Headline of release: "Inflation Expectations Stable; Labor Market Expectations Mixed".

Second outlets: Reuters (via finwire.io / 1027wbow.com, Nov 9, 2026) "NY Fed survey finds Americans more worried over future jobless levels"; ABA Banking Journal (Nov 2026) "New York Fed: Inflation expectations held steady in October".

## Context
- September 2026 survey (released Oct 7, 2026): 1-yr inflation expectation rose to 3.9% from 3.6%, highest since May 2023 (CNBC Oct 7, 2026).
- Government October jobs report (covered Nov 6): +165,000 jobs, unemployment rate 4.1% (per Reuters-based report; not used in script).
- Expectations are survey opinions, not forecasts; the Fed watches them because expectations can shape spending and wage behavior.

## What it means for a normal person
- People expect a weaker job market, but also feel slightly better about finding new work if laid off -- a mixed signal.
- Expected rent up 7.7% and medical care 9.3% over the next year; more households expect trouble making minimum debt payments (13.8%).
- Takeaway (education): expectations are not facts; a good moment to check your emergency cushion.
