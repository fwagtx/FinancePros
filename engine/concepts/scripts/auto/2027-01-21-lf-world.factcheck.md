## Hook
- Line 0, "The first bitcoin nation, and about ninety-two percent skipped it.": TRUE. UCA/IUDOP survey (Dec 2024 fieldwork, published Jan 2025): 91.8% said they did not use bitcoin in 2024. It is 10 words and specific, and line 1 pays it off right away with the 2024 year and the source. Kept as is.

## Issues found and fixed
1. Line 1: "didn't use bitcoin at all" overstated the survey question, which asked about using it to buy or pay for something. FIXED: "didn't use bitcoin to buy or pay for anything in 2024" (text and say).
2. Line 3: "it signed a $1.4 billion IMF loan deal" in 2025. Staff agreement was Dec 2024, board approval Feb 26, 2025, and the law was reformed by congress. FIXED: "And in 2025 the IMF approved a $1.4 billion loan, and much of the bitcoin law was rolled back."
3. Lines 13-14 chart: 2023 = 24.7% did not match the World Bank series (about 24.5%). 2025 = 27.5% could not be confirmed in World Bank WDI, only on a third-party ranking table; BCR quarterly figures run 25-28%. FIXED: chart uses verified, evenly spaced World Bank points 2018 20.7, 2020 24.1, 2022 24.6, 2024 24.0. End and chart_note are 24.0% (2024). Narration was rewritten to match.
4. Line 84: "remittances are now over a quarter of its economy" was no longer supported. FIXED: "about a quarter".
5. Line 17: "The average transfer topped $340 a month." The BCR $340+ figure is the average per send. The average household receives about $179.54 a month (EHPM 2025), so "a month" misleads. FIXED: "The average single transfer topped $340 in 2025", sub "BCR, per send".
6. Line 29: "the app was briefly pulled". Sources say the app was missing from Apple and Google stores at launch and the system was temporarily disconnected to add capacity. FIXED: "the system was briefly taken offline".
7. Line 41: the "14 uses a year" figure comes from the UCA director (Laura Andrade), but it followed an NBER line, so the source was unclear. FIXED: "In the UCA survey, those who used it averaged about 14 uses a year", say "...its director said".
8. Lines 49, 50, 56: the exact "$16,672 (Kitco)" figure could not be confirmed. Kitco's Nov 17, 2022 article confirms the announcement but not that price. CoinMarketCap snapshots put BTC at about $16,600-16,710 around Nov 16-19, 2022. FIXED: on screen now reads "~$16,700" / "~$16.7K" (approx.). The spoken "about sixteen thousand seven hundred" already matched. Math: 16,700 x 365 = $6.10M, so "about $6.1 million" still holds.
9. Line 65: "over $3.5 billion more expected from other lenders" was wrong. The IMF says the EFF is expected to catalyze a combined package of over $3.5 billion, IMF money included. FIXED: "part of an expected package of over $3.5 billion with other lenders."
10. Line 68: the population figure had no date. FIXED: "about 6.4 million people (UN, 2025)". UNFPA gives 6.40M for 2025. 1.4B / 6.4M = $218.75, about $219, which is correct.
11. Line 79: "The donors haven't been named" is a present-tense claim that could be stale by Jan 2027. FIXED: "The IMF didn't name the donors" (past tense, ties it to the Sept 2026 statement).
12. Line 87: "IMF deal made bitcoin optional" was imprecise. The congressional reform made merchant acceptance voluntary, and the law reportedly still calls bitcoin legal tender but drops "currency". FIXED: "under a $1.4 billion IMF deal, accepting bitcoin became optional."
13. YouTube description: updated to match. Reform wording is now "made accepting bitcoin voluntary for businesses"; the World Bank chart years (2018-2024), the UN population source and CoinMarketCap are added.
