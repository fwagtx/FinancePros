# Research brief: How renters insurance makes money (posts Wed Oct 7, 2026)

Show: How They Make Money. Neutral, educational. No timely news hook found this week; evergreen angle: "cheap for you, still profitable for them."

## What a renters (HO-4) policy covers
- Standard renters policy (HO-4) covers the renter's personal belongings, personal liability, and additional living expenses ("loss of use") if the unit becomes unlivable. It does NOT cover the building itself; the landlord's own policy covers the structure. (NAIC consumer guide; Triple-I)
- This is the core of the business model: the insurer carries no roof, walls or structure risk, so the exposure per policy is small.

## Key number: average premium (NAIC)
- Countrywide average HO-4 (renters) premium, 2023 data: $173 per year. Source: NAIC "Dwelling Fire, Homeowners Owner-Occupied, and Homeowners Tenant and Condominium/Cooperative Unit Owner's Insurance Report: Data for 2023" (reported published July 2026). NOTE for fact-check: the $173 (2023) figure was found via secondary sites citing the NAIC report (statecalc.com); NAIC site not directly fetchable. Fallback: 2022 data $171 (NAIC, +0.6% vs 2021).
- Worked example: $173 / 12 = $14.4167 -> $14.42 a month (about $14 a month).

## Real company numbers: Allstate 10-K, fiscal 2025
- Allstate's "Other personal lines" category includes renters, condominium, landlord, boat, umbrella and manufactured home policies (Allstate 10-K language, consistent across years).
- FY2025 Other personal lines: loss ratio 77.4, expense ratio 16.5, combined ratio 93.9 (2024 combined ratio: 97.6). Loss ratio fell 8.5 points vs 2024, mainly from higher premiums earned. Premiums written +6.4% (+$197 million) in 2025. Source: Allstate Form 10-K for FY2025 (filed Feb 2026), sec.gov.
- Worked example: per $1.00 of premium: 100 - 77.4 (claims) - 16.5 (expenses) = 6.1 cents of underwriting profit, before investment income. (Combined ratio 93.9 -> 100 - 93.9 = 6.1.)
- Caveat: this is a blended category, not renters alone.

## The other two profit levers (definitional)
- Float / investment income: insurers collect premiums up front and invest them until claims are paid; investment income is a standard second revenue source for P&C insurers (NAIC, Triple-I).
- Bundling: insurers offer multi-policy discounts (renters + auto); a low-priced renters policy is a common entry product (Allstate, State Farm, Lemonade marketing; Lemonade Q2 2026: premium per customer $433, up 8% y/y, 3,308,666 customers, IFP $1.43B; attributed to product mix shift and rate increases). Lemonade not used in script.

## Surprising fact
- The average renters premium was LOWER in 2023 ($173) than in 2014 ($190), per NAIC, even as homeowners premiums rose (NAIC: homeowners avg premium +10.5% 2021-2022).
