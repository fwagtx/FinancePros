## Where a card fee goes (flow)
Shopper's card -> merchant -> Stripe. From Stripe's fee: interchange goes to the card-issuing bank (largest piece), network/scheme fees go to Visa/Mastercard, Stripe keeps the rest (and pays fraud, risk, infrastructure from it).

## Worked example (our math)
$50 online order, US credit card:
- Stripe fee: 2.9% x $50 = $1.45; + $0.30 = **$1.75**.
- Using the 2024 average credit swipe fee of 2.35%: 0.0235 x $50 = $1.175, about **$1.18** mostly to the card's bank.
- Left: $1.75 - $1.18 = **$0.57**, which must also cover Visa/Mastercard network fees, fraud and Stripe's costs.
- Debit card (big bank): interchange about 22-24¢ (Fed, 2023), so far more of the $1.75 is left. Flat pricing means Stripe's margin depends on card mix.

## The surprising fact
Most of Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ fee on a credit card isn't Stripe's: roughly two-thirds goes straight to the bank that issued the card. Stripe wins by scale: $1.9 trillion of volume in 2025.
