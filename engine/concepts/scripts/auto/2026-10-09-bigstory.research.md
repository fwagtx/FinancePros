## Facts (University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers, preliminary October 2026, released Oct 9, 2026)
- Index of Consumer Sentiment: 46.3 (prelim Oct 2026), vs 48.1 final Sept 2026, vs 53.6 Oct 2025. Down 1.8 points (3.7%) m/m. Director Joanne Hsu called it "little changed" / "inching down a scant 1.8 index points".
- Lowest since May 2026 (record low 44.8). Benzinga headline: "second-lowest ever" (consistent with series: 44.8 May is only lower monthly reading). Use "lowest since May's record low".
- Economists expected roughly 47.3-48.0 (varies by source: Investing.com 47.5, Bloomberg 47.6, Seeking Alpha 48.0, Yahoo 47.3).
- Current Economic Conditions index: 44.7, down from 50.9 in Sept (-12.2%), the lowest on record (Benzinga; SCA table; Breitbart headline "views of current conditions hit all-time lows").
- Hsu: buying conditions for durables plummeted amid high prices and borrowing costs (paraphrase via CNN/Benzinga, not verbatim).
- Hsu: sentiment for lower-income consumers and those with smaller stock portfolios dropped steeply (paraphrase).
- Year-ahead inflation expectations: 4.7% (Oct prelim) vs 4.6% Sept; 3.4% in Feb 2026 before the Iran conflict. Long-run expectations 3.5% vs 3.4%; Hsu: "notably higher than their 2024 range of 2.8% to 3.2%."
- Final October reading: Fri Oct 23, 2026, 10 AM ET.

## Context
- 2026 sentiment slump tied to price pressures, fuel costs after the Iran conflict, borrowing costs. July bounce (55.2) came largely from interviews before the July 7 resumption of US strikes and later gas-price rise (Trading Economics).
- Sentiment measures attitudes; it is not spending data. Low sentiment readings have not reliably translated into spending pullbacks (correlation, not causation) — present as caution, not prediction.

## What it means for a normal person
- Survey says the hit is concentrated among lower-income households and those with small stock holdings.
- Big-ticket purchase conditions viewed worst — high prices + borrowing costs.

## What to watch
- Final October reading Oct 23; September CPI release; Q3 retail spending data.
