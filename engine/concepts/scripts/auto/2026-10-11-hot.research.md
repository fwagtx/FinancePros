# 2026-10-11-hot research brief: Scam alert, fake small business loan brokers

Angle: evergreen scam alert (no breaking news this week). Hook built on the SBA Office of Inspector General's own wording about upfront payments. Case study: FTC v. Seek Capital (final order announced November 2025).

## Red flags (official wording / attribution)
- SBA (Office of Inspector General, "Protect yourself from scams and fraud"): "If you are contacted by someone promising to get approval of an SBA loan but requires any payment up front or offers a high interest bridge loan in the interim, suspect fraud." (Confirmed in two separate search results quoting sba.gov; page itself blocked for direct fetch.)
- SBA OIG: loan agents (brokers/packagers) are legitimately paid to prepare SBA loan paperwork, but OIG investigations over ten years found loan-agent fraud schemes "in the hundreds of millions of dollars" (SBA Information Notice 9000-1793).
- SBA: someone claiming to be from SBA without an official SBA email address -> suspect fraud.
- FTC (advance-fee loans): scammers guarantee a loan but require payment before you get it; legitimate lenders don't guarantee a loan before you apply / before checking credit. It's illegal for companies doing business by phone in the US to promise you a loan and ask you to pay for it before they deliver (consumer loans, Telemarketing Sales Rule) -- NOT used in script because business-loan applicability is not confirmed.
- FTC business blog (2020-2021 small business financing posts): anyone contacting you out of the blue saying they need upfront money or personal info so you can get a government business loan is a scam; a salesperson rushing you through the process is a sign to slow down; some "lenders" just sell your info or commit identity theft.

## Loss figures
- FBI IC3 "Advanced Fee" category (all advance-fee schemes, not only business loans):
  - 2021: $98,694,137 (IC3 2022 report)
  - 2022: $104,325,444 (IC3 2022 report)
  - 2023: $134,516,577; 8,045 complaints (IC3 2024 report)
  - 2024: $102,074,512; 7,097 complaints (IC3 2024 report)
  - 2025: $155,910,852; 7,762 complaints (IC3 2025 report)
  - 2025 vs 2024: 155.91 / 102.07 = 1.527 -> up about 53%.
- FBI IC3 2025 total: 1,008,597 complaints, $20.877 billion losses.
- FTC Consumer Sentinel: $15.9 billion reported fraud losses in 2025 (3 million reports), up from $12.5 billion in 2024; $10 billion 2023; $8.8 billion 2022.

## Case study: FTC v. Seek Capital (allegations; settled)
- Nov 2024 FTC complaint: Seek Capital (California) and CEO Roy Ferman allegedly marketed business loans/lines of credit to new and aspiring small business owners, but instead applied for multiple credit cards, typically personal cards in the owner's name, and charged 10% of the total credit on the cards issued; FTC said it cost small business owners more than $37 million.
- March 2025: FTC won a preliminary injunction.
- November 2025: final order permanently bans Seek Capital and Ferman from business financing, debt relief and credit repair services; $48,280,328 monetary judgment, suspended in part due to inability to pay. Described by FTC as a settlement of allegations.

## Worked example (hypothetical, using the alleged 10% fee)
Cards issued with $50,000 total credit limit x 10% = $5,000 fee, and the owner still has no business loan, just personal card debt capacity.

## Surprising fact
The "business loan" in the Seek case allegedly turned out to be personal credit cards in the owner's name, with a 10% fee on the limits.
