# Research: How crypto is taxed (every trade can be a taxable event)

## Core rules (IRS)
- The IRS treats digital assets as PROPERTY, not currency; general property tax rules apply (IRS Notice 2014-21; irs.gov/filing/digital-assets).
- Taxable events: selling crypto for dollars, trading one crypto for another (e.g. bitcoin for ether), spending crypto on goods/services, and receiving crypto as income (mining, staking, pay). (IRS digital asset FAQ)
- Not taxable: buying crypto with dollars and just holding it; moving crypto between your own wallets. (IRS FAQ)
- Gain = fair market value received minus cost basis. In a crypto-to-crypto trade, the new coin's basis = its value at the moment of the trade.
- Held more than 1 year -> long-term capital gains rates 0% / 15% / 20%. Held 1 year or less -> short-term, taxed as ordinary income (10%-37%). (IRS Topic 409)
- Capital losses offset capital gains; up to $3,000 of net loss per year ($1,500 married filing separately) can offset ordinary income; the rest carries forward. (IRS Topic 409)
- Every Form 1040 filer must answer the digital asset Yes/No question. (IRS newsroom)

## Reporting: Form 1099-DA
- Brokers must report GROSS PROCEEDS for digital asset sales on or after Jan 1, 2025 (forms sent early 2026). Cost basis optional for 2025.
- For transactions from Jan 1, 2026, brokers also report cost basis for "covered" assets (bought on/after Jan 1, 2026 and held at the same broker); forms sent early 2027. (IRS; The Tax Adviser Mar 2026; H&R Block)

## Timely context (not law, as of Sept 30, 2026)
- House Ways and Means approved the Digital Asset Tax Certainty Act (H.R. 10357) 38-5 in mid-September 2026; includes a de minimis exemption for some transaction fees of $10 or less. Not law: needs House, Senate, President. Sen. Lummis' separate $300 per-transaction exemption proposal (July 2025) also not law. Decision: do not feature in the video (could change; keep evergreen).

## Worked example (exact math)
- Buy $1,000 of bitcoin. Months later it's worth $1,500. Swap it all for ether. No dollars ever hit your bank.
- Gain = $1,500 - $1,000 = $500 taxable gain.
- Held under a year -> short-term; in the 22% bracket: $500 x 0.22 = $110 federal tax.
- If held more than a year and in the 15% long-term bracket: $500 x 0.15 = $75.
- Coffee example: spend $5 of crypto that cost $2 -> $3 gain, reportable.

## The surprising fact
- Swapping one coin for another, or buying a coffee with crypto, is a taxable sale, even though you never cashed out to dollars.
