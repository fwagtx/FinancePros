# Research brief: 2026-12-20-hot. Money myth: "once the house is paid off, you live there for free"
Prepared Oct 21, 2026 for a post on Dec 20, 2026 (evergreen; every figure carries its year).

## Core idea
A paid-off home has no mortgage payment, but property tax, homeowners insurance and upkeep keep coming every year. Utilities and any HOA dues come on top (not quantified here). If property taxes go unpaid long enough, the home can be lost through tax foreclosure, even with no mortgage.

## Key facts (with sources)
1. Property tax, ATTOM 2025 annual property tax analysis (released April 9, 2026):
   - Average property tax on US single-family homes in 2025: $4,427, up about 3% from 2024.
   - Average estimated home value $494,231; national effective tax rate 0.9% (0.86% in 2024).
   - Total levy $396.8 billion across ~89.6 million single-family homes (+3.7%).
   - Highest effective rates: Illinois 1.84%, New Jersey 1.58%.
2. Homeowners insurance:
   - Insurance.com (2026 analysis): national average premium $2,872 a year, based on a $300,000 dwelling-coverage policy (Florida highest at $8,471).
   - Insurify (via Insurance Journal / Bloomberg, March 2026): average premium projected about $3,057 in 2026 after a 12% jump in 2025; premiums up 46% since 2021.
   - NAIC (released May 2025): average owner-occupied homeowners premium rose 10.5% from 2021 to 2022.
   - WEAK: national averages differ a lot by methodology (Insurance.com $2,872 vs Insurify ~$3,057 vs a Kin figure ~$2,424). Script uses only the Insurance.com number, attributed by name and year.
3. Maintenance: common rule of thumb is to budget about 1% (some sources say 1%-4%) of home value per year for upkeep (Bob Vila, HomeServe, Fulton Bank, LawnStarter). It is a rule of thumb, not an official standard. NerdWallet citing Census data: average maintenance spend was $950 (0.6% of value) in 2019.
4. Surprise: tax foreclosure. Tyler v. Hennepin County (US Supreme Court, decided May 25, 2023, unanimous, opinion by Chief Justice Roberts). Geraldine Tyler's Minnesota condo went unpaid on property taxes; about $15,000 in taxes, interest and penalties built up. The county seized and sold it and kept the roughly $25,000 left over beyond the debt. The Court ruled that keeping the surplus violated the Takings Clause. She still lost the condo; the ruling was about the surplus. (Sources: Ballard Spahr, Miller Canfield, Bloomberg Law, Casemine.)

## Worked example (hypothetical, stated assumptions)
Example home value: $400,000 (round number below ATTOM's 2025 average estimated value of $494,231). No mortgage.
- Property tax at ATTOM's 2025 national effective rate 0.9%: 400,000 x 0.009 = $3,600
- Insurance at Insurance.com 2026 national average: $2,872
- Upkeep at the 1% rule of thumb: 400,000 x 0.01 = $4,000
- Total: 3,600 + 2,872 + 4,000 = $10,472 a year; 10,472 / 12 = $872.67, about $873 a month
Not included: utilities, HOA dues (would push it higher).
