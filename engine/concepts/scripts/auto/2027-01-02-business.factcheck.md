## Issues (each with exact replacement)

1. **Line 0 hook / COVER / POST titles: misleading for a US audience (MUST FIX).** "Regulators found travel insurance pays back about 40¢ per dollar" makes US viewers think US regulators found this about US policies. The figure is EIOPA's (Europe, data from about 2015-2017, published 2019), and it is stated in the present tense ("pays"). The latest UK data (2025) is higher, at 44-48%. The hook is otherwise strong: specific, true when sourced, and it makes viewers want to know where the other 60¢ goes.
   - text: `Europe's regulator found travel insurance paid back about 40¢ per dollar.`
   - say (12 words): `Europe's regulator found travel insurance paid back about forty cents per dollar.`
   - scene title: `Europe: [40¢] back?`
   - COVER title: `40¢ Back in Europe?`
   - POST title: `In Europe, travel insurance paid back about 40¢ per dollar. Here's why`
   - ig line 1: `Europe's regulator found travel insurance paid back about 40 cents per premium dollar.`
   - tiktok: `In Europe, travel insurance paid back about 40¢ per dollar. Where's the rest? Not financial advice. #moneytok #personalfinance #travelinsurance #financeprostv`

2. **Line 1: "a 40% average claims ratio in 2019" is imprecise, and the 24% commission is never stated.** 2019 is the year the review was published, not the year of the data. The seller's 24% share is used in line 7 but never spoken, so the math looks unsourced. The line also repeats the hook.
   - text: `Its 2019 review: 40% went to claims, 24% to sellers.`
   - say: `Its twenty nineteen review: forty percent went to claims, twenty-four percent to sellers.`
   - scene: label `Claims ratio`, value `40%`, sub `EIOPA review, 2019` (unchanged otherwise)

3. **Line 2: the spoken line drops "percent" on the 2025 range and does not name the source.** Minor, but it reads better out loud with both fixed.
   - text: `UK regulator data: 31% to 37% in 2024, and 44% to 48% in 2025.`
   - say: `UK regulator data showed thirty-one to thirty-seven percent in twenty twenty-four, and forty-four to forty-eight percent in twenty twenty-five.`

4. **Line 3: "Still, Americans spent..."** "Still" links US spending to European claims data as if they were the same market, which repeats the cross-market confusion.
   - text: `In the US, travelers spent $5.56 billion on it in 2024.`
   - say: `In the US, travelers spent five point five six billion dollars on it in twenty twenty-four.`

5. **Lines 7-8: what is spoken does not match the screen, and the narration is awkward.** The screen shows $57.60 while the voice says "about fifty-eight". "Eighty-six forty" is hard to follow out loud. Use whole dollars that still add to $240 ($96 + $58 + $86):
   - Line 7 text: `At Europe's averages, about $96 pays claims and about $58 goes to the seller.`
   - Line 7 say: `At Europe's averages, about ninety-six dollars pays claims and about fifty-eight goes to the seller.`
   - Line 7 and 8 list items: `["Claims $96", "Seller $58", "Insurer $86"]`
   - Line 8 text: `The last $86 covers insurer costs and profit.`
   - Line 8 say: `The last eighty-six covers insurer costs and profit.`
   - POST ig/math line: `...at Europe's averages, about $96 to claims, $58 to the seller, $86 to the insurer.`

6. **Line 9: "paid in full" overstates the payout, and "cancelled" uses British spelling.** Policies reimburse only covered reasons, up to limits.
   - text: `Most trips get nothing back; a few canceled ones get reimbursed.`
   - say: `Most trips get nothing back; a few canceled ones get reimbursed.`
   - split right items: `["Get canceled", "Get reimbursed"]`

7. **POST sources: "UStiA market study (June 2025)"**. I could not confirm the month of release. Use `UStiA 2022-2024 market study`. Also add `FCA value measures (2025 data, pub. July 2026)` for clarity. yt_desc is accurate as written, but it could start with "In Europe," for consistency.

With these fixes, the spoken word count is 138 (original: 135). It stays within 115-140, the hook is 12 words, and every label and item stays within its limit.

VERDICT: FAIL
