# Research brief: Why insurance got so expensive in America (Explained, posts Oct 26, 2026)

## Car insurance (BLS CPI, motor vehicle insurance, Dec/Dec change)
- 2020 -4.8% (largest Dec/Dec drop since 1955); 2021 +4.1%; 2022 +14.2%; 2023 +20.3% (largest since 1976); 2024 +11.3%; 2025 +2.8% (smallest in 5 years). August 2026: -5.1% y/y (BLS, Sept 2026 release).
- Compounded Dec 2020 to Dec 2025: 1.041 x 1.142 x 1.203 x 1.113 x 1.028 = 1.636, about +64%. All-items CPI over the same period was about +25% (Dec/Dec: 7.0, 6.5, 3.4, 2.9, 2.7).
- Repairs: CPI motor vehicle maintenance and repair +43.6% from Jan 2019 to mid-2024. CCC: replacement parts per estimate +15% (2019-2024); parts >51% of repair cost; third-party bodily injury average payout $27,373 (2024); total loss frequency a record 23% (CCC 2026 report).
- Triple-I / AM Best, personal auto:
  - Combined ratio 112.2 (2022), 104.9 (2023), 95.3 (2024), 91.8 (2025).
  - 2022 underwriting loss $33.1B.
  - Net written premiums +14.4% (2023), +12.8% (2024).
- "Legal system abuse": Triple-I term for litigation-driven claim costs.

## Home insurance
- NOAA NCEI: 27 billion-dollar disasters in 2024, $182.7B total, second only to 28 in 2023.
- Swiss Re sigma: global insured nat-cat losses of $107B in 2025, the sixth straight year over $100B and down 24% from $141B in 2024; 83% from the US; a record 92% from secondary perils; LA wildfires about $40B, the largest insured wildfire loss on record.
- Guy Carpenter US property cat rate-on-line: +31.3% at Jan 2023, the largest one-year rise since 2006; six straight years of increases, nearly +100% cumulative.
- State Farm stopped new California homeowners applications (announced May 26, 2023; CNBC May 27), citing wildfire risk, construction costs and the reinsurance market. Sept 2026: filed underwriting guidelines that could reopen applications for fire-hardened homes.
- Insurify: average home premium $2,948 in 2025 (+12%); +46% since 2021 vs about 16% inflation.

## Health
- KFF EHBS 2025: average family premium $26,993 (+6%); worker contribution $6,850; wages +4%; inflation +2.7%.

## Levers
- Comparing quotes; deductibles (higher deductible, lower premium; a trade-off); bundling; discounts.
- Credit-based insurance scores are used in most states; California, Hawaii and Massachusetts ban them for auto.

## Worked example
- 1,000 drivers, 50 claims x $10,000 = $500,000, or $500 per driver; with 25% costs and profit, $500 / 0.75 = $667.
- Claims +30% ($13,000): $650 / 0.75 = $867.
