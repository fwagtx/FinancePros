## Core facts (with dates)
### What a share is / primary vs secondary market
- A share = a slice of ownership in a company. Market cap = share price x shares outstanding.
- IPO (primary market): company sells new shares and receives the cash. After that, trading is investor-to-investor (secondary market); the company gets nothing from those trades.
- 2025: 202 US IPOs raised $44.0 billion, a four-year high; largest was Medline at $6.3 billion (Renaissance Capital, US IPO Market 2025 Annual Review).
- Stock splits: Nvidia's 10-for-1 split, trading split-adjusted from June 10, 2024 - more shares, same company value (Nvidia 8-K/CNBC May 22, 2024).

### Who owns stock
- Fed Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF), families owning stock directly or indirectly (incl. funds, 401(k)s, IRAs): 2007 53.2%, 2013 48.8%, 2016 51.9%, 2019 52.6%, 2022 58.0% (record). Direct ownership of individual stocks: 15% (2019) -> 21% (2022), largest change on record. (Federal Reserve, Changes in US Family Finances 2019-2022, Oct 2023; 2013-2016 bulletin Sep 2017; Yahoo Finance/Axios/USAFacts.) 2025 SCF not yet published as of Oct 2, 2026.

### Size
- US equity market cap $68.9 trillion = 43.7% of global equity market cap ($157.8T) in 2025; US market 4.4x larger than China, the next largest (SIFMA 2026 Capital Markets Fact Book).

### Exchanges and history
- NYSE traces to the Buttonwood Agreement, signed by 24 stockbrokers on May 17, 1792 (NYSE history; History.com; CNBC 2017).
- Nasdaq began operations Feb 8, 1971 as the world's first electronic stock market (initially a quotation system) (Nasdaq; Wikipedia/encyclopedia).
- Regular hours 9:30 a.m.-4:00 p.m. ET; 2026: closed Thanksgiving Thu Nov 26, early 1 p.m. close Fri Nov 27 and Dec 24 (NYSE Group holiday calendar; Fidelity; Kiplinger).
- SEC conditionally approved 23x5 trading on five exchanges: 24X, Cboe EDGX, MEMX, Nasdaq, NYSE Arca (law firm alerts 2026).

### How a price is set
- Bid = highest price a buyer will pay; ask = lowest price a seller will accept; difference = spread (SEC Investor.gov).
- Market order: executes immediately, price not guaranteed; generally near the ask (buy) or bid (sell). Limit order: only at the limit price or better. Stop order: becomes a market order once the stop price is reached (SEC Investor.gov "Types of Orders"; FINRA "Order Types").
- Best execution: brokers must use reasonable diligence to get the best market for a customer's order (FINRA Rule 5310). Off-exchange trades are reported to FINRA trade reporting facilities (TRFs).
- Market makers/wholesalers continuously quote bids and asks and earn the spread; prices move when buyers are willing to pay more than the current ask (or sellers accept less than the bid).

### Indexes
- S&P 500 launched 1957; float-adjusted market-cap weighted. Minimum company market cap for ADDITION: $22.7 billion (effective July 1, 2025; previously $20.5B). Also needs US domicile, eligible US listing, positive GAAP earnings (latest quarter and sum of last four), adequate float and liquidity. Criteria apply to additions, not continued membership (S&P Dow Jones Indices press release Jul 1, 2025).
- Concentration: top 10 companies ~38-40% of S&P 500 weight in mid-2026 (about 26% at the 2000 dot-com peak). Nvidia was the largest weight (about 7-8%) in 2026 (Visual Capitalist 2026; Landmark Wealth Jul 2026; multiple index trackers). Use "about 40%" / "nearly 4 of every 10 dollars"; weaker sourcing, keep approximate.
- Long-run: S&P 500 average annual return about 10% a year since 1957 with dividends reinvested (about 10.3-10.6% depending on endpoints); about 6.5% after inflation; rarely returns the average in any single year (Fidelity, SmartAsset, Motley Fool).

### Rules and safety nets
- SEC created by the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. Mission (exact, SEC website): "to protect investors; maintain fair, orderly, and efficient markets; and facilitate capital formation."
- FINRA: nonprofit self-regulatory organization formed in 2007 (NASD + NYSE member regulation), oversees broker-dealers under SEC supervision (FINRA About; Cornell LII).
- Settlement: T+1 (one business day after trade date) since May 28, 2024; previously T+2 (SEC press release 2024-62 / Investor.gov bulletin; OCC Bulletin 2024-3; Davis Polk).
- Market-wide circuit breakers, based on S&P 500 drop vs prior close: Level 1 7% and Level 2 13% -> 15-minute halt if before 3:25 p.m. ET; Level 3 20% -> trading stops for the day (NYSE MWCB FAQ; Investor.gov).
- Level 1 triggered on March 9, 12, 16 and 18, 2020 (four times in 8 trading-day span); before that, the old Dow-based breakers tripped Oct 27, 1997 (NYSE; Optiver; Cleary Gottlieb Mar 2020).

## Worked examples (hypothetical, labeled)
1. Market cap: a hypothetical company with 1,000,000 shares at $50 -> 1,000,000 x $50 = $50,000,000 market cap. If the price moves to $51, market cap = $51,000,000 (+$1,000,000) even though no new money went to the company.
2. Spread and order types: hypothetical quote bid $49.98, ask $50.02 -> spread = $0.04.
   - Market buy 100 shares fills near the ask: 100 x $50.02 = $5,002.
   - Selling right back at the bid: 100 x $49.98 = $4,998. Round-trip cost = $5,002 - $4,998 = $4 = 0.08% of $5,000.
   - Limit buy at $50.00: pays at most 100 x $50.00 = $5,000, but fills only if a seller agrees to $50.00 or less; it may not fill at all.
3. Index weighting: if one company is 8% of the index, $100 in an S&P 500 index fund puts $8 into that one company ($100 x 0.08 = $8); if the top 10 are about 40%, about $40 of each $100 sits in 10 companies.
