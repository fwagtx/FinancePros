# Research brief: What a no-spend challenge actually teaches you about your spending
Slot: 2026-12-25-explainer (posts Fri Dec 25, 2026, 12 PM CT). Evergreen; no news hook. Light holiday/new-year framing is fine (January is a common time people try one).

## Definition (rule-type, not a law)
- A no-spend challenge is a self-imposed pause on non-essential (discretionary) spending for a set period, often a week or a month (30 days is the most common length per some guides). Essentials such as rent/mortgage, utilities, insurance, groceries, transportation/gas, medicine and minimum debt payments are still paid. Usually cut: dining out/takeout, online shopping, clothing, entertainment, impulse buys; many guides suggest reviewing subscriptions. (Bankrate; SoFi; Rocket Money; InCharge Debt Solutions.)

## Subscriptions (survey, labeled)
- C+R Research survey (2022, 1,000 US adults split across Gen Z/Millennial/Gen X/Boomer): offhand guess of monthly subscription spending averaged $86; when asked category by category, it averaged $219 (a $133 gap). 42% said they had stopped using a subscription but forgot they were still paying for it. 86% have at least some subscriptions on autopay. (Reported by CNBC/NBC, Fox stations, 2022.)

## Impulse spending (survey, weaker; research only)
- Slickdeals survey conducted by OnePoll (2023, 2,000 Americans): average $151 a month on impulse purchases, down from $314 in 2022 and $276 in 2021. Retailer-commissioned; methods vary year to year. Capital One Shopping's $254/month 2025 figure is an estimate/compilation. NOT used in script.

## Behavioral research ("the surprise")
- Prelec, D. & Loewenstein, G. (1998). "The Red and the Black: Mental Accounting of Savings and Debt." Marketing Science 17(1):4-28. Describes the "pain of paying" that can undercut enjoyment of purchases, and "coupling": cash payment tightly couples paying with buying, while credit cards weaken that coupling.
- Prelec & Simester (2001, Marketing Letters, "Always Leave Home Without It"): in sealed-bid auctions for Celtics tickets, participants told to pay by credit card bid substantially more (reported as up to roughly double) than those paying cash. Not used on screen (word budget).
- Takeaway framing (neutral): a no-spend stretch works like an audit; some people find it shows which purchases they actually miss and which were autopilot habits.

## Worked example (hypothetical, labeled)
- Three $15 takeout orders a week = 3 x $15 = $45 per week; 4 weeks x $45 = $180 in a four-week month. Example only.

## Surprising fact
- People in the C+R Research survey (2022) guessed $86/month on subscriptions, but itemized it was $219, about 2.5x their guess.
