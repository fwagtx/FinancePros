# Research brief: Why is rent so expensive in big cities? (Hot Take, posts Mon Dec 21, 2026, 6 PM CT)

Evergreen framing: every figure carries its period. No "this week/today". Neutral on policy: explain mechanisms only.

## Key facts (with dates and sources)
1. **Manhattan median rent, September 2026: $5,395/month (record)**, up 2% from August and 9% year over year (Corcoran September 2026 rental report, via 6sqft and Brick Underground). Note: Miller Samuel/Douglas Elliman (The Real Deal) uses a different method and reported $4,900 for September 2026; always label $5,395 as Corcoran's.
2. **National median asking rent (vacant-for-rent units), Census Housing Vacancy Survey:** Q2 2026 = $1,531 (Census release July 28, 2026). Q1 2026 = $1,579; Q2 2025 = $1,494.
3. **Housing shortage:** Freddie Mac estimated a national shortfall of about **3.7 million homes as of Q3 2024** (cited in Congressional Research Service Insight IN12628, Dec 2025). Other estimates differ widely (NAHB ~1.5M, Zillow ~4.5M, NAR 5.5M for 2021) - always attribute.
4. **Regulation cost:** a 2022 NAHB-NMHC survey of apartment developers estimated regulation accounts for an average of **40.6% of multifamily development costs** (NMHC press release, June 2022; NAHB Eye on Housing). Survey-based estimate by industry groups - attribute as such.
5. **Surprise - renter cost burdens (Harvard JCHS, State of the Nation's Housing 2026, released June 2026):** a record **22.7 million renter households (49%) were cost-burdened in 2024** (paying more than 30% of income on housing); about **12.1 million were severely burdened** (more than 50%). Units renting under $1,000/month fell 30% from 2014 to 2024. (via NLIHC, Novogradac, Harvard GSD)
6. Rental vacancy rate Q2 2026: 7.3% (Census, July 28, 2026) - supply has been catching up nationally; asking rents up ~2.5% year over year Q2 2025 -> Q2 2026 (own calc from Census figures).

## Mechanisms (explain, no advocacy)
- Jobs concentrate in big metros -> more people compete for homes near them (demand).
- Land near job centers is scarce and expensive; zoning rules limit how many homes can go on a lot (supply).
- Building costs (materials, labor, regulation) and higher interest rates raise the cost of construction loans, so fewer projects pencil out.
- Shortage + demand -> landlords can charge more.

## Worked example (30% rule, labeled as a rule of thumb)
Corcoran Manhattan median, Sept 2026: $5,395/month
$5,395 x 12 = $64,740 a year in rent
$64,740 / 0.30 = **$215,800 a year in gross income** to keep rent at 30% of income.
National comparison: $1,531 x 12 = $18,372; / 0.30 = $61,240.

## Surprise
About half of all US renter households (49%, a record 22.7 million in 2024) already pay more than 30% of income for housing (Harvard JCHS 2026).
