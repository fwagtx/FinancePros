# 2026-12-27-hot research: What happens if a stock gets delisted?

Format: Money Myth ("delisted = my shares are gone"). Evergreen; posts Dec 27, 2026. No news hook needed (no single timely event; rules below are current as of Oct 2026).

## Core facts
1. Delisting removes a stock from an exchange; it does not by itself cancel shares. Holders still own them unless the company cancels them (e.g., in a bankruptcy plan). Sources: Nasdaq.com "What Happens to Delisted Shares?"; Motley Fool; SEC Investor Bulletin "Bankruptcy for a Public Company" (notes a bankrupt company's stock may keep trading over the counter after delisting, with a "Q" added to the ticker).
2. Where it trades next: over-the-counter (OTC). OTC Link (OTC Markets Group) tiers per its SEC exemptive-relief request: OTCQX, OTCQB, Pink Open Market, Expert Market. Expert Market quotes are limited to broker-dealers and not shown to the public (can show $0.00 on finance sites). Typical OTC drawbacks: lower liquidity, wider bid-ask spreads, less disclosure (Nasdaq.com, SoFi, Motley Fool; SEC bulletin on OTC). 
3. Voluntary vs involuntary:
   - Voluntary: issuer files SEC Form 25 under Exchange Act Rule 12d2-2(c); must give the exchange written notice at least 10 days before filing and issue a press release; delisting effective 10 days after Form 25 is filed (SEC filings/rule text). Reasons: mergers/buyouts, going private, moving exchanges.
   - Involuntary: exchange removes it for failing continued-listing standards (price, equity, filings) or bankruptcy.
4. Nasdaq bid price rule (Rule 5550(a)(2), 5810(c)(3)(A)): deficiency when closing bid < $1.00 for 30 consecutive business days; automatic 180-calendar-day compliance period; cure = $1.00+ closing bid for at least 10 consecutive business days. Capital Market issuers may get a second 180 days. (Federal Register 2024-18911, 2025-01621; Norton Rose Fulbright; Manatt.)
5. NYSE Section 802.01C: average closing price below $1.00 over a consecutive 30-trading-day period; six-month cure; regain with ≥$1.00 close on last trading day of a month and 30-trading-day average ≥$1.00. NYSE: no cure period if the company did a reverse split in the past year (Norton Rose Fulbright, K&L Gates Jan 23, 2025).
6. Reverse splits are the common cure (combine shares to lift the price). Nasdaq 2025 changes (SEC approval Jan 17, 2025; Fed. Reg. Jan 23, 2025): no compliance period if the company did a reverse split in the prior one-year period, delisting goes straight to determination; hearing request no longer stays suspension after the 360-day (two 180-day periods) limit; stock trades OTC during appeal. Also cumulative ratio test (≥250:1 over two years) — related rule (Reviva DEF 14A 2025; weaker attribution as to which filing).
7. Nasdaq $0.10 rule (SR-NASDAQ-2025-065, SEC Release 34-104318, approved Dec 5, 2025, effective Jan 19, 2026): closing bid ≤ $0.10 for 10 consecutive business days → immediate delisting determination and suspension, no compliance period, appeal doesn't stay suspension (Robinson+Cole; policyrisk summary of Fed. Reg.). 
8. Bankruptcy: SEC Investor Bulletin (Bankruptcy for a Public Company) — common stock of a bankrupt company is likely to be worthless; shareholders are last in line after creditors; Chapter 11 plans often cancel existing shares.

## Examples
- Bed Bath & Beyond: filed Chapter 11 Apr 23, 2023; Nasdaq delisting notice Apr 25, 2023; trading suspended on Nasdaq at open May 3, 2023 (company release/Nasdaq.com/NJBIZ). Plan (filed July 2023) said equity "shall be canceled, released, and extinguished" with no recovery; plan confirmed Sept 2023; equity canceled when plan went effective ~Sept 29, 2023 (CNBC Aug 28, 2023 "shareholders left holding worthless stock"; TheDeepDive). Exact effective date secondary-sourced.
- Luckin Coffee (contrast): Nasdaq halted trading June 29, 2020; delisted July 1, 2020 (Form 25) after accounting fraud; ADSs kept trading OTC Pink as LKNCY (Luckin Form 20-F). Business later recovered while on OTC (20-F filings). Not used in script.

## Worked example (hypothetical reverse split)
1-for-10 reverse split: 1,000 shares at $0.50 = $500. After: 1,000 / 10 = 100 shares; $0.50 × 10 = $5.00 each; 100 × $5.00 = $500. Value unchanged; only the price per share moves.

## Chartable series (weakly sourced, scope varies)
Nasdaq-processed reverse splits (securities-law-blog citing Nasdaq): 2020: 94; 2021: 31; 2022: 196; 2023 YTD to June 23: 164. Not charted in script (2023 is partial; sources disagree).

## Surprising fact
Delisted doesn't mean deleted: you still own the shares. What really zeroes them out is a bankruptcy plan that cancels them.
