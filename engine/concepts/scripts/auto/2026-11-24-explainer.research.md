# Research: Exempt vs non-exempt: who gets overtime (2026-11-24-explainer)
Researched Oct 2, 2026.

## Facts
- FLSA: non-exempt employees get at least 1.5x their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek.
- White-collar (executive, administrative, professional) exemption generally requires: salary basis, salary of at least $684/week ($35,568/yr), and primarily exempt duties. HCE threshold $107,432/yr. (2019 rule, effective Jan 1, 2020.) Some jobs (e.g., outside sales, teachers, doctors, lawyers) are exempt without the salary test.
- History: $455/week (2004 rule); $684/week (effective Jan 1, 2020). 2024 Biden rule ($844 July 2024, $1,128 Jan 2025) vacated nationwide Nov 15, 2024 (E.D. Tex.). DOL formally rescinded it and restored $684 on May 14, 2026.
- No Tax on Overtime (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, July 2025): deduction of the FLSA-required overtime premium (the "half" of time-and-a-half), up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint), tax years 2025-2028; phases out above $150,000 MAGI ($300,000 joint); only FLSA non-exempt workers; available whether or not you itemize (IRS).

## Worked example
$20/hr, 50 hours: 40 x $20 = $800 + 10 x $30 = $300 -> $1,100 (vs $1,000 at straight time). Premium portion = 10 x $10 = $100 (deductible portion).
$684 x 52 = $35,568.
