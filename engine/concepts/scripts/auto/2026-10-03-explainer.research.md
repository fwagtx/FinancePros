# Research brief: Buy now, pay later: how it really works
Slot: 2026-10-03-explainer (vertical short, posts Saturday October 3, 2026). Researched September 30, 2026.

## News angle
No single breaking BNPL story to hook on for a Saturday evergreen post. Most recent official data: the Fed's
Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED) for 2025, released May 2026. Hook on the "zero interest, so who pays?" question instead.

## How it works (definition)
- CFPB definition of the classic "pay-in-four" product: a four-installment, no-interest loan, typically with a
  25 percent down payment at checkout and the remaining three installments due at two-week intervals (six weeks total).
  Source: CFPB, "Buy Now, Pay Later: Market Trends and Consumer Impacts," September 2022.
- Longer BNPL loans (monthly plans) can charge interest; this video is about pay-in-four only.

## Who pays (the business model)
1. Merchants: BNPL lenders earn most revenue from merchant fees ("merchant discount") charged to the store on each sale.
   Reported range: merchants typically pay about 3 to 6 percent of the purchase price, higher than typical credit card
   costs of roughly 2 to 3.5 percent (CFPB 2022 report and Fed/Richmond Fed analysis as summarized in search results).
   Script uses only the qualitative fact: "the store pays the lender a fee on each sale" (no exact percent on screen).
2. Late payers: late fees.
   - CFPB (December 2025 market report, 2023 data): average late fee assessed in 2023 was $9.99; 4.1% of BNPL loans were
     assessed a late fee in 2023 (down from 5.2% in 2022).
   - CFPB (Sept 2022): late fees were 6.9% of lender revenue in 2021.

## Key numbers
- 16% of US adults used BNPL in the prior 12 months in 2025 (Fed SHED 2025, released May 2026).
- 26% of BNPL users paid late at least once in 2025 (Fed SHED 2025); 17% of users were charged extra for paying late.
- Late share history: 18% (2023), 24% (2024), 26% (2025) (Fed SHED).
- 11% of BNPL users had a BNPL payment trigger a bank overdraft or NSF fee in 2025 (Fed SHED 2025).
- Top reasons for use: spreading out payments; only way to afford the purchase (Fed SHED 2025).
- CFPB Dec 2025 report: in 2023, six lenders originated 335.8 million BNPL loans totaling $45.2 billion; average loan $135.
- CFPB Sept 2022: loans from five lenders grew 970% from 16.8 million (2019) to 180 million (2021); $2B to $24.2B.
- FICO announced FICO Score 10 BNPL and 10 T BNPL on June 23, 2025, the first scores from a major provider to include BNPL data
  (FICO press release, Business Wire). On-time payment can help, late payment can hurt, once lenders use these scores.

## Worked example
$200 purchase, pay-in-four:
- $200 / 4 = $50 per payment.
- $50 at checkout (25% down), then $50 at week 2, week 4, week 6. Total paid on time: 4 x $50 = $200. Interest: $0.
- Miss one payment and a typical late fee (2023 average, CFPB) is $9.99.
  $9.99 / $50 = 19.98%, about 20% of that one payment. $9.99 / $200 = about 5% of the whole purchase.

## The surprising fact
"Zero interest" is not free money: the store pays a fee on every sale, and roughly one in four users (26% in 2025) paid late.
