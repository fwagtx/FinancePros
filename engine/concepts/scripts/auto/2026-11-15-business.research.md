# Research: How Chick-fil-A's franchise model works
Chick-fil-A is private (no 10-K). Official numbers come from its Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD, Operator Program), filed with state regulators each spring; the 2026 FDD covers fiscal 2025.

## Model (Chick-fil-A FDD Items 5-6; chick-fil-a.com/franchise)
- Initial franchise fee for an Operator: $10,000 (unchanged for decades).
- Chick-fil-A pays for / owns the restaurant, equipment and in most cases the real estate. Operators do not own the business, build no equity, and cannot sell it or pass it on.
- Ongoing: Base Operating Service Fee = 15% of gross sales; Additional Operating Service Fee = 50% of the restaurant's pre-tax profit (after the base fee and costs). (FDD; 1851 Franchise; Franchise Chatter)
- Operators are expected to run the restaurant full time, hands-on.

## Numbers (FDD, fiscal years)
- US systemwide sales: 2021 $16.674B; 2022 $18.814B; 2023 $21.586B; 2024 $22.746B; 2025 $23.918B. (FDDs via Restaurant Business, NRN, QSR Magazine, AJC Apr 2026)
- Chick-fil-A Inc. consolidated revenue: 2025 $10.343B; 2024 $9.063B; 2023 $7.888B.
- Average sales of freestanding restaurants open a full year in 2025: $9,161,239 (about 2,302 restaurants; 49% hit or beat the average). (2026 FDD via Franchise Chatter / QSR Magazine)

## Worked example (illustrative, hypothetical profit)
- Base fee on the 2025 average freestanding restaurant: $9,161,239 x 15% = $1,374,185.85 (about $1.37M) to Chick-fil-A off the top.
- Suppose the restaurant then has $400,000 of pre-tax profit. 50% split: operator $200,000, Chick-fil-A $200,000.

## Surprising fact
- It costs just $10,000 to become an operator, but you never own the restaurant: no equity, nothing to sell.
