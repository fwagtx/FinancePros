# Research brief: Credit utilization - why a maxed-out card hurts your score even if you pay it off
Posts Monday Jan 18, 2027 (evergreen). Researched Nov 19, 2026. Education only, no product promotion, no advice.

## News angle
None needed (evergreen). Seasonal note only: Experian says utilization is typically higher in December and falls in spring as holiday balances are paid down (Experian State of Credit Cards, 2026) - relevant to a mid-January post, but not used as "this week" news.

## Worked example (our math)
- $2,000 balance / $2,500 limit = 0.80 = 80% utilization (reported on the statement date, even if paid in full by the due date).
- To get under 30%: 0.30 x $2,500 = $750 reported balance. Under 10%: $250.
- 80% sits above Experian's March 2026 average for the Poor band (76.8%) and far above the Exceptional band (6.4%).

## Surprising fact
Paying in full every month does not guarantee low reported utilization: the snapshot is usually taken at statement close, before the due date. (And in classic FICO models the hit fades once a lower balance reports.)

## Uncertain / caveats
- Not every issuer reports on the statement closing date; "typically" / "usually" per myFICO and Experian.
- 800+ utilization differs by Experian article/date: 6.4% (March 2026) vs 7% (2026 article) vs 7.1% (Q3 2024). Script uses 6.4% March 2026 with spoken "about six percent".
- No CFPB page found directly addressing utilization timing or the 30% figure; attributed to myFICO/Experian instead.
- "No memory" applies to classic models (e.g., FICO 8, most scores lenders use); FICO 10T / VantageScore 4.0 use trended data (24 months). Per FICO, factor weights are for the general population and vary by person.
