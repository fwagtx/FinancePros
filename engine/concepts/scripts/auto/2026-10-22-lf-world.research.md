# Research brief: Why China's economy matters to your wallet (World Money, posts Oct 22, 2026)

## Timely angle
- Sept 28-29, 2026: the US and China agreed to cut tariffs on about $60B of goods ("30-for-30": ~$30B each way) after the second Trump-Xi summit of 2026, in Washington. The US list has 77 items, including toys, tableware, kitchen accessories, curtains, electric shavers and inflatable balls. China trims duties on US corn, wheat, sorghum, meat, dairy and vegetable oils. US soybeans were NOT included. (CNN Sept 28, 2026; Time Sept 29, 2026; ABC News Australia Sept 29, 2026)

## Trade (US Census Bureau)
- 2025 US goods imports from China: $308.7B (-29.9% vs 2024). Exports to China: $106.0B (-26.0%). Deficit: $202.7B (-31.8%, i.e. $94.4B smaller).
- Chartable series, US goods imports from China ($B, Census, rounded): 2018 539, 2019 450, 2020 433, 2021 505, 2022 536, 2023 427, 2024 440, 2025 309.
- China's share of US imports: peaked around 21.6% in 2017; 14.8% in Sept 2024; 7.1% in May 2025, the lowest since 2001; about 9% for 2025 as a whole.
- Per-household math: 2025 imports $308.7B / about 134.8M US households (Census via FRED TTLHH, 2025) = about $2,290 per household. Note: imports include business inputs, not only consumer goods.
- Fed (FEDS Notes, Aug 2, 2024): "As the U.S. is derisking from China, other foreign U.S. suppliers are relying more on Chinese imports". Countries like Vietnam and Mexico import more Chinese parts.
- De minimis: Executive Order 14256 ended the $800 duty-free exemption for goods from China and Hong Kong on May 2, 2025. About 4 million packages a day used to enter under de minimis (Fortune, NBC News, 2025).

## Tariffs: who pays
- Tariffs are paid to US Customs by the US importer of record.
- Amiti, Redding and Weinstein (Journal of Economic Perspectives, Fall 2019): near-complete pass-through of the 2018 tariffs into US import prices; the cost fell on US importers and consumers. By Dec 2018: $3.2B per month in added tax costs plus $1.4B per month in deadweight loss.
- Worked example (illustrative): a $10 import with a 25% tariff means $2.50 paid at the border, so the cost is $12.50. If a store prices at 2x cost: $20 becomes $25.

## China as a customer
- In 2024 US soybean exports to China were $12.84B, 51% of all US soybean exports ($24.58B) (USDA FAS; Farm Bureau).
- By Sept 2025, no Chinese buying of the new US soybean crop was in sight (farmdoc daily / Purdue, Sept 2025).

## China as a lender (Treasury TIC)
- China held $618B of Treasuries in July 2026, the lowest since 2008; June was $633.4B. Peak: about $1.32T in Nov 2013.
- In July 2026, Japan held $1,103.9B and the UK $998.3B; total foreign holdings were $9.248T. China ranks third.
- 618 / 9,248 = 6.7% of foreign-held Treasuries.

## China's own economy
- 2025 GDP +5.0% (NBS). By quarter: Q1 5.4, Q2 5.2, Q3 4.8, Q4 4.5. The 2026 growth target is 4.5-5%, the lowest on record.
- 2025 goods trade surplus: a record $1.189T (China customs, Jan 14, 2026). Exports were about $3.77T (+6.1%) and imports about $2.58T (+0.5%). Exports rose to Africa (+26%), Southeast Asia (+13%), the EU (+8%) and Latin America (+7%).
- CPI: +0.8% y/y in Aug 2026 (July 0.5%).
- PPI: +3.8% y/y in Aug 2026 (NBS, Sept 10, 2026), after a long deflation streak in 2023-2025. Drivers were energy costs and a memory-chip shortage.

## Surprising fact
- In May 2025, China's share of US imports fell to 7.1%, the lowest since 2001. Even so, Chinese parts often still arrive inside goods from other countries.
