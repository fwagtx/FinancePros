## The facts (NY Fed SCE, September 2026 survey, released Wednesday October 7, 2026)
- Median one-year-ahead inflation expectation: 3.9%, up 0.3 pt from 3.6% in August. Highest since May 2023.
  (NY Fed release "Short- and Medium-Term Inflation Expectations Increase; Labor Market Expectations Improve"; Bloomberg, CNBC, Quartz, Yahoo Finance, Reuters via Yahoo/KELO, FXStreet)
- Three-year-ahead: 3.3% (up 0.1 pt). Five-year-ahead: 3.0% (unchanged).
- Disagreement (dispersion) across respondents widened at all three horizons.
- Labor market expectations improved; perceptions of credit access and household financial outlook worsened (summary of release; not quantified here).
- Median expected household spending growth 5.5%, highest since May 2023 (investing.com headline + search summary; secondary -- NOT used in script).
- Category expectations (gas 4.8%, food 5.5%, medical care 9.2%, rent 6.8%) appeared in only one search summary and could not be cross-checked -- NOT used in script.
- Context: Fed's inflation goal is 2% (long-standing, Fed's statement on longer-run goals).
- Survey measures what households EXPECT, not actual inflation.

## Why it matters for a normal person
- Expectations can feed real inflation: workers ask for bigger raises, businesses feel freer to raise prices; the Fed watches these surveys when setting rates.
- Worked example (hypothetical): if prices rise 3.9% in a year, a $100 basket costs $103.90. A raise smaller than 3.9% would mean less buying power IF that expectation came true.
- Longer-term (5-year) view held at 3.0% -- still above the Fed's 2% goal.
