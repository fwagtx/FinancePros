# Research brief: How to reset your money in one weekend (new-year checklist)
Researched Nov 7, 2026 for posting Jan 6, 2027. Evergreen; every number dated. Note: irs.gov / federalreserve.gov fetches may be blocked from this environment; figures confirmed via search snippets of official pages plus reputable outlets.

## News angle
No single news event; the angle is the calendar (new year = new contribution limits, fresh statements). 2027 401(k)/IRA limits were NOT officially announced as of Nov 7, 2026 (Milliman projects $25,500 for 401(k); not official). USE 2026 FIGURES, LABELED "for 2026", and tell viewers to check the IRS's 2027 numbers.

## Key facts (with dates)
1. Emergency cushion (SHED): In the Fed's Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (fielded Oct 2025, published May 2026), 63% of adults said they would cover a $400 emergency expense with cash or its equivalent; unchanged from 2024; peak 68% in 2021; 50% in 2013. Ages 18-29: 45%. (Federal Reserve, "Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025", May 2026; InvestmentNews; MoneyTalksNews)
   - So about 37% could NOT (our math: 100 - 63).
2. Subscriptions: C+R Research survey (2022, 1,000 US adults): people guessed $86/month on subscriptions; itemized total averaged $219 = $133 more than they thought. 42% forgot they were still paying for a subscription they no longer used. (CNBC, Sept 6, 2022; NBC, Fox stations)
3. Credit card rate, accounts assessed interest (Fed G.19 / FRED TERMCBCCINTNS), August (Q3) readings:
   - 2022: 18.43% | 2023: 22.77% | 2024: 23.37% (record high) | 2025: 22.83% | 2026: 22.36%
   - Sources: Fed G.19 Dec 2023 table (18.43, 22.77); GuruFocus/TradingTools (23.37 Aug 2024); FRED (22.83 Aug 2025); G.19 Oct 7, 2026 release via govspending/thetrading.tools (22.36 Aug 2026).
   - CHARTABLE SERIES: x = 2022..2026, y = 18.43, 22.77, 23.37, 22.83, 22.36.
4. Retirement limits FOR 2026 (IRS, announced Nov 13, 2025; BenefitsPro, Fox Business, 401kSpecialist): 401(k) employee deferral $24,500; IRA $7,500. 2027 figures not yet announced as of Nov 7, 2026.
5. Employer match: Vanguard How America Saves 2026: average employer-promised match 4.7% of pay in 2025 (record level, same as 2024); median 4%. (PlanSponsor / Pensions & Investments coverage, 2026)
6. Free credit reports: weekly, free, permanently at AnnualCreditReport.com (made permanent Oct 2023; FTC consumer alert Oct 2023). Only authorized site for the free reports.
7. Credit freeze: free by federal law since Sept 21, 2018 (Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act; FTC). Must contact each of the three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion). Online/phone freeze done within one business day; lift is free too. Freeze doesn't affect your score. FTC: a "lock" may carry fees; a freeze is the free one guaranteed by law.
8. Beneficiaries: a beneficiary designation on a 401(k)/IRA/life insurance generally overrides a will; it doesn't update itself after marriage, divorce, birth (Kiplinger, Edward Jones, Chase).
9. Tax calendar: federal filing deadline for 2026 returns is April 15, 2027 (Thursday) (TaxAct, multiple; IRS to confirm in filing-season release).
10. Debt methods: avalanche = extra money to highest APR first (least interest); snowball = smallest balance first (quick wins) (Chase, Wells Fargo, Experian).

## Worked examples (our math)
A. Net worth: assets (checking $1,800 + savings $2,400 + 401(k) $9,300 = $13,500) minus debts (card $6,000 + store card $1,000 + car loan $9,000 = $16,000) = -$2,500. Point: the number is a starting line, not a grade.
B. One month of card interest: $6,000 x 28% / 12 = $140. $1,000 x 15% / 12 = $12.50.
C. Avalanche vs snowball (simulation, our math): Card A $1,000 at 15% (min $40), Card B $6,000 at 28% (min $120), $400/month total, interest monthly.
   - Avalanche (B first): paid off in 23 months, total interest $1,877.02.
   - Snowball (A first): paid off in 23 months, total interest $2,060.22; first card gone in month 4.
   - Difference: $183.20 (about $180) more interest with snowball.
D. Match: $50,000 salary, example plan matches 100% up to 4% of pay. Contribute 4% ($2,000) -> employer adds $2,000. Contribute 2% ($1,000) -> employer adds $1,000; $1,000 a year left on the table.
E. Subscriptions: $133/month underestimated x 12 = $1,596 a year (our math, from the 2022 survey average).

## Surprising fact
People guessed $86 a month for subscriptions; the real itemized total averaged $219 (2022 survey). Also: a beneficiary form beats your will.

## Steps (chapter plan)
1. Cold open: 63% could cover $400 with cash (2025) -> one weekend, eight steps.
2. Saturday morning: The snapshot (list accounts, net worth math).
3. Saturday afternoon: Follow the money (last month's statements, subscriptions $86 vs $219, cancel what you don't use).
4. Saturday night: Debt list (APR chart 2022-2026; avalanche vs snowball worked math).
5. Sunday morning: Automate savings + retirement (automatic transfer on payday; match math; 2026 limits $24,500 / $7,500; check 2027 IRS numbers).
6. Sunday afternoon: Protect (credit reports weekly, free freeze, insurance check, beneficiaries).
7. Sunday night: Calendar reminders (quarterly check, April 15, 2027 tax deadline) + common mistakes + one-screen checklist recap.

Common mistakes: trying to fix everything at once; cancelling nothing because "I use it sometimes"; paying for a "lock" instead of a free freeze; forgetting to raise the 401(k) % (contribution limits change; your % doesn't adjust itself); never checking beneficiaries; skipping the match.
