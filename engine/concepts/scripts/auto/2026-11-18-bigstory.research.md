## The story
Target (TGT) reported fiscal Q3 2026 (quarter ended Oct 31, 2026) before the open on Wednesday Nov 18, 2026.
- Net sales $27.1 billion, up 7.1% y/y.
- Comparable sales +5.6% (stores +4.6%, digital +9.8%); comparable traffic +5.0%. Best quarterly sales gain in five years (AP/News4Jax, CNBC). Year-ago Q3 2025 comps were -2.7%.
- Q3 includes $301 million of pretax tariff refund benefits (in gross margin / operating income) -> $228 million net earnings, $0.50 of GAAP and adjusted EPS.
- Diluted EPS $2.65 vs $1.51 a year ago. Excluding refunds about $2.15 vs ~$2.07 estimate (Benzinga).
- Operating income $1.7B vs $948M a year earlier.
- Guidance: FY2026 net sales growth raised to ~6%; adjusted EPS $10.65-$11.40, includes ~$2.14/share of tariff refund benefits recognized year to date; excludes future refunds.
- Q2 2026 (reported Aug 19, 2026): $994 million pretax tariff refund benefit -> $752M net, $1.65 EPS; Q2 comps +3.8%.
- OUR MATH: Q2 $994M + Q3 $301M = $1,295M, about $1.3 billion pretax refunds this year (Q1 had none: $1.65 + $0.50 ≈ $2.14 YTD).
- Stock: little changed / slightly lower premarket (reports differ: ~-1.1% to -3% early). Not used in script.

## Context: why refunds exist
- The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA ("emergency powers") tariffs; importers who paid them are owed refunds with interest.
- About $166 billion in IEEPA duties were paid by nearly 330,000 importers (Fortune Nov 12, 2026; Davis Wright Tremaine; government court filing). Over 75% refunded with interest as of Oct 2, 2026 (government filing, per DWT/Fortune). ~$100B paid by end of July (NBC).
- Court of International Trade certified an importer class Oct 8, 2026; Nov 10 class counsel asked the court to order refunds to all eligible importers; government says only importers who sue are entitled.
- Who can claim: only businesses that paid the tariffs (importers of record) and customs brokers who paid on their behalf, via CBP's CAPE portal. Consumers who paid higher prices are NOT eligible to file (CBS News).
- Some carriers say they pass refunds on: FedEx said it will refund shippers and consumers who bore the charges (CBS, NBC); ~$800M (NBC). UPS says it will refund payors once it receives the funds from CBP (CBS).
- CNN (Aug 22, 2026): most businesses won't pass refunds back to shoppers (one consultant: 15-20%) — single-outlet estimate, not used on screen.

## What it means for a normal person
- Tariff refunds go to the companies that paid the duty, not to shoppers, even if prices rose.
- The one direct route: if you paid a duty/brokerage tariff charge on an imported package through a carrier, the carrier may refund you once it gets paid.
- Watch: the trade court's ruling on universal refunds; whether retailers cut prices; more retailers (Walmart reports this week) booking refunds.
